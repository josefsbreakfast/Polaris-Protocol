## 📚 *The Fellowship of the Ring* --- Chapter 2 Reading Notes So Far

-   **Gandalf's "So...... Frodo..." problem**
    -   Gandalf spends much of *The Shadow of the Past* managing Frodo's
        epistemic state rather than simply giving him information.
    -   He asks questions, releases information incrementally, watches
        Frodo react, and then escalates.
    -   He already knows considerably more than Frodo throughout the
        conversation.
    -   This makes Gandalf's exposition function almost like controlled
        disclosure/interview technique.
    -   Also: he is an enormous drama queen.
-   **Gandalf communicates according to pyrotechnic principles**
    -   Gandalf cannot simply provide information; he has to **stage the
        reveal**.
    -   Example:
        -   Frodo: why doesn't Sauron have the Ring?
        -   Gandalf: **"IT WAS TAKEN FROM HIM."**
    -   Nobody asked him to do the trailer voice.
    -   Unfortunately, this is completely consistent with a man who has
        spent decades engineering fireworks for Hobbit parties.
    -   His oral exposition has the same architecture:
        -   establish anticipation;
        -   introduce suspense;
        -   light fuse;
        -   pause;
        -   **✨💥 LORE 💥✨**
    -   Gandalf has the energy of a DM who has 300 pages of
        world-building notes and has finally persuaded a player to ask
        the correct question.
    -   Tolkien was catastrophically early for D&D.
    -   Gandalf is effectively Tolkien's in-world mechanism for saying:
        -   *"Interesting question, Frodo. Fortunately I have the entire
            fucking Silmarillion in my head."*
-   **Saruman already sounds sketchy as fuck**
    -   Gandalf initially presents Saruman as the recognised expert on
        Ring-lore.
    -   Gandalf therefore has good institutional reasons to trust his
        assessment.
    -   Yet Saruman repeatedly provides conclusions that lower Gandalf's
        urgency around exactly the subject Saruman has reasons to
        control.
    -   Emerging information-flow problem:
        -   Saruman possesses specialist expertise.
        -   Gandalf consults him.
        -   Saruman says the Ring is effectively lost / not worth
            pursuing.
        -   Gandalf's investigation is delayed.
    -   This is potentially less about Gandalf being stupid than about
        **corruption of a trusted epistemic chokepoint**.

        -   small;
        -   provincial;
        -   conventionally powerless;

    -   Meanwhile Gandalf has spent decades hanging around them.
    -   Gandalf's revolutionary intelligence methodology:
        -   **talk to people who aren't important.**
    -   Yet Gandalf himself still calls them **"these charming, absurd,
        helpless hobbits."**
    -   This suggests Gandalf may also have a model-update problem.
    -   His observations repeatedly show Hobbits doing things the
        category *helpless Hobbit* shouldn't predict, but he keeps the
        label.
    -   Difference:
        -   **Saruman:** irrelevant → discard anomaly.
        -   **Gandalf:** irrelevant → "...but that's odd" → continues

    -   Sméagol wants it.
    -   Déagol refuses.
    -   Sméagol murders him and takes it.

        -   **Gollum isn't a species.**

-   **Later portrayals of Sméagol/Gollum need separate treatment**
    -   Jackson/Serkis strongly establishes the cultural model of:
        -   Sméagol = one personality;
        -   Gollum = another personality;
        -   visible switching between them.
    -   Tolkien's textual identity appears slipperier:
        -   Sméagol;
        -   Gollum;
        -   I;
        -   we;
        -   Precious;
        -   unstable boundaries among identity, possession and agency.
    -   Later visual portrayals also intensify the physical-monster
        coding:
        -   emaciation;
        -   crouching;
        -   huge eyes;
        -   teeth;
        -   animal movement;
        -   darkness.
    -   Gollum consequently becomes cultural shorthand for:
        -   ugliness;
        -   furtiveness;
        -   acquisitiveness;
        -   obsession;
        -   "my precious."
    -   That reception history risks stripping away the pity Tolkien
        deliberately preserves.
    -   Especially ironic because Gandalf explicitly challenges Frodo's
        disgust and eagerness to condemn him.
-   **Gollum as possible Ring-bearer outcome**
    -   Bilbo → Frodo → Sméagol can be treated almost as a natural
        experiment:
        -   similar object;
        -   different individuals;
        -   different exposure durations;
        -   different contexts;
        -   different degrees of resistance.
    -   Gollum is therefore not simply *the monster over there*.
    -   He is potentially **a future trajectory for the people currently
        holding the Ring**.
-   **Bilbo, trauma and altered behaviour**
    -   Earlier Bilbo material can be read through a trauma/dissociation
        comparator without claiming a clinical diagnosis.
    -   Bilbo:
        -   undergoes prolonged danger and violence;
        -   returns home changed;
        -   has unusual attachment to an object intimately associated
            with survival;
        -   becomes defensive around that object;
        -   shows restlessness and behavioural change;
        -   momentarily appears threatening toward someone he loves.
    -   This would have been culturally legible within a post-war
        environment familiar with men who **came home different**.
-   **Shell shock / combat PTSD comparator**
    -   The particularly important distinction is not simply
        *aggression*.
    -   Combat trauma can involve defensive responses learned in
        environments where rapid aggression was adaptive and
        deliberately trained.
    -   During severe re-experiencing/confusion, someone may respond to
        the situation they believe they are in rather than the situation
        actually occurring.
    -   This does **not** mean PTSD generally makes people violent.
    -   It means particular histories and presentations can create
        particular risk-management problems.
    -   Compassion for impaired control does not require pretending harm
        cannot occur.
    -   Recognising potential harm does not require turning the
        traumatised person into a moral monster.
-   **Confusion/orientation may be the stronger bridge**
    -   Night-terror comparator:
        -   actual situation: trusted person beside you;
        -   experienced situation: intruder;
        -   defensive response occurs before full waking
            orientation/recognition.
    -   The behaviour is intelligible relative to the **incorrectly
        perceived situation**.
    -   Useful distinction:
        -   not *"I know who you are and want to hurt you"*;
        -   but *"I am temporarily unable to understand that you are
            you."*
    -   This gives us useful language for Bilbo's apparent physical
        threat toward Frodo:
        -   **defensive action arising from confusion about present
            reality**.
-   **Dementia / Alzheimer's comparator**
    -   Gandalf's long-term observation of Bilbo can also evoke someone
        noticing cognitive/behavioural changes in an ageing friend.
    -   Gandalf has something clinically valuable:
        -   **longitudinal baseline**.
    -   He knows how Bilbo used to be strange and can therefore
        recognise that Bilbo is becoming strange **differently**.
    -   Dementia can create problems of orientation:
        -   Where am I?
        -   Who are you?
        -   Why are you touching me?
        -   Why are you taking my things?
    -   Apparently "combative" behaviour can arise from fear, confusion,
        pain, unfamiliarity or environmental stress rather than
        deliberate difficultness.
    -   Good care therefore depends heavily on:
        -   routine;
        -   familiarity;
        -   calm;
        -   communication;
        -   reducing unnecessary stress.
-   **Pre-existing psychiatric diagnosis + later dementia**
    -   This can become particularly complicated.
    -   New behavioural or cognitive symptoms may be interpreted through
        the person's existing diagnosis.
    -   Relevant concept:
        -   **diagnostic overshadowing**.
    -   Conversely, behaviour caused by dementia, delirium, confusion,
        pain or fear may be interpreted as intentional aggression or
        psychiatric deterioration.
    -   A person with an established trauma history may also lose some
        of the cognitive resources previously used to reorient:
        -   *that was then;*
        -   *this is now;*
        -   *this person is safe;*
        -   *that sound isn't the original threat.*
    -   This makes **orientation/confusion** a useful conceptual bridge
        between:
        -   trauma;
        -   cognitive impairment;
        -   Tolkien's supernatural Ring effects;
    -   without collapsing those things into one diagnosis.
-   **Gandalf and Bilbo under this reading**
    -   Gandalf doesn't simply conclude that Bilbo has become morally
        bad.
    -   He observes him longitudinally.
    -   He recognises behavioural change.
    -   He tries to understand its cause.
    -   He focuses increasingly on the object around which the disturbed
        behaviour is organised.
    -   This creates a surprisingly human underlying question:
        -   **What do you do when somebody you love suddenly responds to
            you as though you are the enemy?**

    -   This makes Frodo unexpectedly good at epistemic hygiene:
        -   What is your evidence?
        -   How do you know?

-   **Gollum and the "opium fiend" comparator**
    -   Gollum's relationship with the Ring resembles a cultural
        vocabulary Tolkien's generation would have known around
        opium/morphine dependence:
        -   compulsion;
        -   narrowed motivation;
        -   secrecy;
        -   social withdrawal;
        -   deterioration;
        -   inability to relinquish the object;
        -   distress following loss;
        -   life organised around reacquisition.
    -   Important distinction:
        -   not **"Tolkien wrote Gollum as an opium addict"**;
        -   but **Gollum's behaviour could be culturally legible through
            existing addiction/dependence imagery**.
    -   The Victorian/Edwardian "opium fiend" trope also carried
        racialised and Orientalist baggage, which may matter when later
        visual portrayals of Gollum are considered.
    -   Useful future separation:
        -   Tolkien's textual dependence grammar;
        -   Victorian/Edwardian opium-fiend imagery;
        -   later modern "junkie" performance vocabulary.
-   **The Ring crowds other wants out**
    -   Gandalf's description of Gollum after losing the Ring contains
        an extremely interesting detail:
        -   **suddenly he is really hungry.**
    -   Appetite returning can function as a bodily sign that other
        motivational systems are becoming active again.
    -   Under the Ring, Gollum's world increasingly narrows toward:
        -   Precious;
        -   survival;
        -   darkness;
        -   Precious.
    -   After separation, other things begin getting back in:
        -   hunger;
        -   outside;
        -   movement;
        -   curiosity;
        -   memory.
    -   The Ring therefore looks less like something simply **added** to
        Gollum and more like something that progressively **crowds
        everything else out**.
    -   Useful formulation:
        -   **The Precious does not merely become something Gollum
            wants. It becomes the thing around which wanting itself is
            organised.**
    -   Remove it and the organism starts making competing demands
        again:
        -   **"...actually I am fucking starving."**

-   **Gandalf is simply too powerful, unfortunately**
    -   Frodo quite reasonably asks why Gandalf cannot take the Ring.
    -   Gandalf's answer is essentially that the Ring would become
        catastrophically dangerous through someone with his existing
        power and capacity.
    -   Comedically, after repeatedly calling Hobbits helpless, this
        lands as:
        -   Hobbit: suitable containment vessel.
        -   Gandalf: **my power is simply too great and terrible.**
    -   Underneath the comedy is an important inversion:
        -   **susceptibility is not the same thing as weakness.**
    -   Gandalf's power, competence and desire to do good are themselves
        exploitable surfaces.
    -   The Ring can recruit an existing desire to improve, protect or
        order the world and convert it into domination.
    -   Conversely, the Hobbit characteristics Saruman discounts may be
        protective:
        -   low ambition;
        -   little conventional political power;
        -   ordinary pleasures;
        -   limited interest in domination;
        -   ability to exist below elite strategic attention.
-   **The unlikely hero is qualified by being apparently unqualified**
    -   Frodo is recognisably part of the *chosen one / unlikely hero*
        family of tropes, but Tolkien's construction is sideways.
    -   Frodo is not secretly the most conventionally powerful
        candidate.
    -   **Being conventionally powerful is actively disqualifying.**
    -   The qualities that make Hobbits look strategically insignificant
        are therefore part of what makes a Hobbit useful.
    -   This loops directly back to Saruman:
        -   **Saruman:** no power → no strategic significance.
        -   **Tolkien/Gandalf:** no power → a different kind of
            strategic significance.
    -   Tolkien is not the origin of the older mythic pattern of the
        overlooked or humble figure becoming decisive, but *The Lord of
        the Rings* is enormously influential on its modern fantasy form.
    -   Later fantasy frequently reverses the lesson back into:
        -   *the humble nobody was secretly the king / uniquely powerful
            all along.*
    -   Which rather misses the interesting part.
-   **Gandalf's suspiciously extensive interest in Hobbits**
    -   Gandalf remarks upon his pity and his particular interest in

    -   Meanwhile he has spent decades:
        -   visiting the Shire;

        -   keeping track of Bilbo;
        -   supplying bespoke fireworks for his parties;
        -   observing subtle changes in Bilbo's behaviour and ageing;
        -   investigating Bilbo's strange Ring;
        -   chasing down Gollum.
    -   At some point **"strategic interest in Hobbit resilience"**

    -   More seriously, his attachment may itself explain why he sees

-   **A queer-readable book is not the same claim as a secretly gay
    romance**
    -   It is unnecessary to claim that Tolkien secretly wrote Frodo and
        Sam as a canonical sexual or romantic couple.
    -   It is equally unnecessary to pretend the text offers no material
        for queer reading.
    -   *The Lord of the Rings* gives extraordinary moral and emotional
        weight to:
        -   fellowship;
        -   chosen companionship;
        -   same-sex devotion;
        -   service;
        -   bodily care;
        -   separation and reunion;
        -   vows and obligation;
        -   relationships outside the conventional nuclear household.
    -   Tolkien also permits men an unusual degree of emotional and
        physical tenderness without the prose constantly policing that
        tenderness back into a defensive performance of heterosexual
        masculinity.
    -   A queer reading can therefore coexist with a platonic reading.
    -   The useful distinction is:
        -   **"LOTR is secretly a gay romance."** --- unnecessary claim.
        -   **"Tolkien consciously intended modern queer coding."** ---
            historical claim requiring evidence.
        -   **"LOTR is extraordinarily productive under queer
            reading."** --- yes, very obviously.
-   **Frodo and Sam: function may be easier to define than emotion**
    -   Tolkien's own experience gives the Frodo/Sam relationship an
        important historical comparator in the First World War
        officer--batman relationship.
    -   The relationship can be described functionally through:
        -   class hierarchy;
        -   service;
        -   practical care;
        -   comradeship;
        -   domestic labour;
        -   bodily proximity;
        -   shared danger;
        -   mutual dependence.
    -   Those functions do not by themselves tell us whether a
        particular relationship is:
        -   platonic;
        -   romantic;
        -   sexual;
        -   familial;
        -   or something that does not map neatly onto those categories.
    -   This is useful because the relationship is **better defined by
        what the people do for one another than by a simple emotional
        label**.
    -   That was true of many historical relationships and remains
        surprisingly difficult to express in ordinary contemporary
        English.
-   **The class hierarchy contains practical dependence**
    -   The officer--batman / gentleman--servant structure is formally
        hierarchical, but the lived relationship can complicate that
        hierarchy.
    -   Elite masculine competence historically depended upon
        substantial service labour:
        -   clothes;
        -   boots;
        -   kit;
        -   food;
        -   packing;
        -   schedules;
        -   bodily and domestic maintenance.
    -   Put crudely:
        -   the socially powerful man may still need another human being
            to make sure he has his fucking socks before going to war.
    -   This is one reason the later fandom instinct of **"the rich guy
        is actually a total softboy"** can be doing more class analysis
        than it initially appears.
    -   Formal status and practical competence are not identical.
    -   **Elite masculinity can depend upon invisible service labour in
        order to appear effortlessly self-sufficient.**
    -   *Jeeves and Wooster* sits usefully adjacent to this dynamic:
        -   socially superior gentleman;
        -   formally subordinate servant;
        -   servant nevertheless possesses much of the practical
            competence, information and situational control.
-   **Queer theory is also useful for defining straightness**
    -   Modern English has a relatively impoverished everyday vocabulary
        for relationships whose functions cross categories such as:
        -   friendship;
        -   service;
        -   comradeship;
        -   domesticity;
        -   care;
        -   devotion;
        -   mentorship;
        -   eroticism;
        -   obligation;
        -   chosen kinship.
    -   Queer theory need not operate merely by locating a hidden queer
        identity.
    -   It can ask:
        -   What is the category *friend* doing here?
        -   What is *platonic* being asked to exclude?
        -   What assumptions are embedded in *straight*?
        -   Which forms of labour, dependence or bodily intimacy
            disappear when a relationship receives one simple label?
    -   A significant amount of queer theory therefore helps define
        supposedly **straight** relationships more precisely.
    -   For Frodo and Sam, the useful question is not necessarily *"are
        they secretly gay?"*
    -   It is:
        -   **what actually constitutes this relationship, and why are
            our available categories inadequate to describe all of it?**
-   **Historical categories should not simply be replaced with new
    binaries**
    -   The wider historical problem extends beyond Tolkien.
    -   British and other Western imperial systems repeatedly
        encountered forms of gender, kinship and sexuality that did not
        map neatly onto their administrative and moral categories.
    -   Colonial governance could criminalise, redefine, assimilate or
        erase local categories.
    -   The surviving archive may therefore reflect not merely missing
        information but the operations of the systems that decided what
        was legible enough to record.
    -   The same caution applies in reverse:
        -   recovering historical complexity does not require
            retroactively forcing every person into a modern binary of
            **straight / queer**.
    -   Queer analysis can instead recover the instability of the
        categories themselves.
-   **Historical sexual liberation also requires ethical
    discrimination**
    -   Late-Victorian and early-twentieth-century attempts to find
        vocabularies for same-sex desire sometimes drew upon Classical
        Greek models and elite intellectual or occult subcultures.
    -   Some of those efforts challenged genuinely oppressive
        criminalisation and social repression.
    -   Some also discussed age-asymmetric, class-asymmetric or

-   **Interpretive openness and the One Ring problem**
    -   Tolkien's text does not continuously adjudicate every
        affectionate gesture or intimate relationship for the reader.
    -   That leaves an interpretive commons in which multiple readings
        can coexist:
        -   Christian fellowship;
        -   wartime comradeship;
        -   officer--batman class relations;
        -   profound platonic love;
        -   queer possibility;
        -   combinations of the above.
    -   This does **not** mean every interpretation is equally
        persuasive.
    -   Readers can still argue from:
        -   textual evidence;
        -   history;
        -   philology;
        -   biography;
        -   reception.
    -   The important distinction is between disagreement and compulsory
        interpretive authority.
    -   One may argue:
        -   **"I think Frodo and Sam are platonic."**
    -   One may argue at considerable length:
        -   **"The text strongly supports a platonic reading."**
    -   The more Tolkien-shaped problem begins with:
        -   **"Other people must not queer-read this text; their
            interpretations should be excluded or suppressed in order to
            protect the proper cultural inheritance."**
    -   That is an attempt to convert interpretive conviction into
        coercive authority.
    -   Unfortunately Gandalf has already provided the relevant warning
        about the corruptive potential of power.
    -   The recursive joke therefore writes itself:
        -   **one interpretation to rule them all**
        -   is a spectacularly unfortunate governance model for *The
            Lord of the Rings*.

        ferociously while declining the Ring of Interpretive Authority.
