## 🎂 Frodo, Anniversaries, and Calendrical Memory

Frodo continues marking Bilbo's birthday after Bilbo's disappearance.

This is interesting through several overlapping lenses.

### Post-WWI loss

Tolkien was born in 1892 and served during the First World War.

His generation experienced extraordinary levels of:

- death;
- disappearance;
- unresolved absence;
- mass bereavement;
- bodily and psychological trauma;
- influenza deaths immediately following the war.

The relevant question is not whether Frodo is an allegorical WWI survivor.

It is whether a writer formed by this environment would naturally understand **absence as something which remains active rather than simply ending**.

### Catholic calendrical culture

Tolkien's Catholicism also matters.

Catholic culture is intensely recursive and calendrical:

- feast days;
- saints' days;
- vigils;
- anniversaries;
- requiem Masses;
- liturgical seasons;
- annual commemorations.

The absent or dead person remains situated in time.

A date comes around again, and something is done because it has returned.

This is particularly interesting beside the later importance of recurrence, circles, anniversaries, and potentially **rotatio**.

### Book-club question

> **Why does Tolkien keep giving loss a calendar?**

Watch anniversaries, birthdays, ages, genealogies, inherited dates, commemorations, and recurring historical events.

## 🌊 Bilbo and Narrative Closure

The Shire produces two folk endings for Bilbo.

### First disappearance: good ending

Bilbo disappears.

Then:

**adventure → return → treasure**

He comes home with jewels and gold.

The extraordinary event becomes a satisfying story.

### Second disappearance: bad ending

Bilbo disappears again.

This time:

**disappearance → prolonged absence → presumed drowning → closure**

Hence:

> **“drowned, probably”**

The community has difficulty maintaining the unresolved category:

> He is absent.  
> We do not know where he is.  
> He may still be alive.  
> We may never know.

Instead, uncertainty is converted into narrative.

### Working model

**event → memory → story → tradition → history → myth**

This may become extremely important in Tolkien.

His professional world as a philologist was full of precisely this problem:

something happened → people told it → people retold it → language changed → details changed → the surviving tradition must be interpreted.

### Book-club question

> **What happens when communal memory needs an ending more than the evidence provides one?**

## 👻 Spiritualism, Loss, and the Interwar Imagination

Frodo's response to Bilbo's disappearance has a recognisable post-WWI emotional texture:

**loss → conspicuous sociability → parties → wandering → unresolved attachment → slightly haunted interior life**

This sits alongside the enormous growth of interest in Spiritualism, séances, psychical research, and esoteric practices around and after WWI.

These practices long predated the war.

However, mass bereavement created a new social environment in which attempts to contact, understand, or remain connected with the dead acquired particular importance.

Arthur Conan Doyle is a useful contemporary comparison.

### Social position of occult practice

There is also an important class and legitimacy question.

Divination, fortune-telling, mediumship, folk magic, and related work could be:

- legally precarious;
- socially stigmatised;
- associated with itinerant or travelling communities;
- associated with women's informal labour;
- vulnerable to selective enforcement.

Meanwhile, wealthy and respectable people could participate in adjacent practices through:

- séances;
- Spiritualism;
- psychical research;
- private societies;
- drawing-room gatherings;
- intellectual discussion.

The symbolic and spiritual material could therefore possess considerable cultural importance while many of the people actually practising it remained socially vulnerable.

### Important distinction

This does **not** mean:

> Frodo = Spiritualist  
> Tolkien = occultist  
> LOTR = séance allegory

Rather:

> Tolkien belongs to a generation intensely concerned with how absent and dead people remain present to the living.

## 🧠 “Rather Cracked” and “Hobbit-Sense”

These phrases belong together.

### “Rather cracked”

This is social classification through understatement.

Frodo has departed sufficiently from Hobbit Normal for the community to notice.

But *cracked* is interesting:

- not destroyed;
- not replaced;
- still recognisably the same vessel;
- but something has fractured.

### “Grow some hobbit-sense”

This supplies the proposed cure.

The implied recovery script is:

**Bilbo is gone → time passes → Frodo grows up → Frodo settles down → normal life resumes**

The community expects disturbance eventually to become private and manageable.

### British trauma culture

This provides a useful historical comparison with British responses to trauma:

- emotional privacy;
- stoicism;
- understatement;
- suspicion of “making a fuss”;
- duty;
- endurance;
- humour and deflection;
- strong expectations about returning to normal.

These traits can be protective.

They can also become brutal when applied to experiences which cannot sensibly be “got over” on schedule.

### Working concept

**Social recovery script**

**“rather cracked” → “grow some hobbit-sense” → settle down**

One phrase labels deviation.

The other identifies the expected corrective.

## 🍻 “It Snowed Food and Rained Drink”

This sounds proverbial without necessarily being an inherited English proverb.

Tolkien constructs it from familiar English linguistic machinery:

**weather verb + abundant substance**

The parallelism is particularly neat:

**snow → food**  
**rain → drink**

Solid precipitation becomes solid provisions.

Liquid precipitation becomes liquid provisions.

It also resembles the kind of exaggeration that survives oral transmission.

Instead of remembering the catering inventory, people remember:

> There was so much that it snowed food and rained drink.

Tolkien therefore does not merely tell us that hobbits transmit events through communal storytelling.

He gives their memories **language designed to survive retelling**.

## 🛣️ The Roads Are an Information System

Alongside Frodo's subjective apprehension, Tolkien gives us a material one.

**The roads are changing.**

People are moving who did not previously move in these numbers or directions.

Travellers appear.

News changes.

Expected patterns of communication and movement become unreliable.

The hobbits are not strategically interested in the outside world, but the deviation becomes large enough that even they notice.

### Migration as early-warning sensor

This can be modelled as:

**external disturbance**  
↓  
**population movement**  
↓  
**altered road traffic**  
↓  
**peripheral observation**  
↓  
**rumour**  
↓  
**local interpretation**

The Shire possesses a surprisingly effective distributed sensor network.

Its weakness is the analytic layer.

### Working observation

> **The periphery detects the changing world before it understands the changing world.**

Frodo feels that the horizon is moving.

The roads demonstrate that it actually is.

## 🗺️ Interwar Europe as Experiential Grammar

Tolkien's rejection of allegory does not require Tolkien to have written without historical memory.

There is an important distinction between:

> **fictional thing X secretly equals historical thing Y**

and:

> **a writer who experienced particular historical conditions learns what instability looks and feels like.**

Tolkien lived through a period of extraordinary geopolitical transformation.

Among the relevant changes:

- First World War;
- collapse of the German monarchy;
- collapse of Austria-Hungary;
- collapse of the Ottoman Empire;
- Russian Revolution;
- Russian Civil War;
- creation and rearrangement of states;
- Italian Fascism;
- Spanish Civil War;
- rise of Nazism;
- mass displacement;
- Second World War.

For someone born in 1892, the political map was not a stable background.

**The background itself kept changing.**

## 🌲 Mirkwood, Mordor, and Different Kinds of Threat

- revolutionary/post-imperial Russia;
- Fascist Italy;

Mirkwood is particularly interesting as an initially opaque threat environment:

## 🕊️ Hobbits and the Psychology of Appeasement

The hobbits provide a surprisingly good teaching device for understanding part of the psychology behind British appeasement.

This does not require LOTR to be an allegory for appeasement.

The relevant pattern is:

**warning sign → alternative explanation → reassurance → another warning sign → stronger reassurance → delayed action**

People after WWI had extremely good reasons not to want another war.

The desire for reassurance was not necessarily stupidity or cowardice.

Accepting the frightening interpretation meant accepting that another catastrophe might be approaching.

### The central distinction

The healthy instinct:

> **Protect normal life by refusing unnecessary panic.**

The failure mode:

> **Protect the idea of normal life by discounting evidence that normal life is already being threatened.**

The hobbits' love of ordinary life therefore creates both:

- a genuine virtue;
- a possible vulnerability.

### Book-club question

> **How does the desire to avoid another catastrophe become a reason for failing to recognise the next one?**

Gandalf is structurally hilarious.

He:

- travels constantly across jurisdictions;
- maintains relationships among otherwise disconnected communities;
- collects information;
- knows obscure historical material;
- has contacts across the known world;
- notices apparently insignificant anomalies;
- disappears without explaining his destination;
- asks extremely specific questions about private artefacts;

- returns later with an intelligence assessment.

### Gandalf as information integrator

Hobbits:

> Roads seem funny.

Travellers:

> Bad things elsewhere.

Frodo:

> Weird dreams.

> Bilbo drowned probably.

> **I HAVE CORRELATED THE REPORTING.**

His important capability is not merely magical knowledge.

- archival investigation;
- pipe.

Occupation:

**Wizard**

Actual duties:

**liaison, HUMINT, archival research, threat assessment, intergovernmental coordination, occasional fireworks.**

## ⚗️ The Ring — Alchemical Behaviour

The strongest working hypothesis is **not**:

> The Ring is the Philosopher's Stone.

Nor necessarily:

> The Ring represents alchemy.

Instead:

> **The Ring behaves according to an alchemical grammar of transformation.**

The crucial feature is interaction.

**substance + vessel + time/process → transformation**

The Ring does not simply give every bearer identical Evil Points.

What happens appears to depend upon:

- bearer;
- disposition;
- desire;
- exposure;
- duration;
- use;
- resistance.

This resembles transformation of a substrate more than simple possession of a power token.

## 🔥 Gold, Fire, and Revelation

The Ring is:

- gold;
- unusually durable;
- apparently resistant to ordinary deterioration;
- tested through fire;
- made epistemically legible through heat.

Fire does not simply destroy.

**Fire reveals.**

That is strongly adjacent to alchemical and pre-chemical ideas of:

- testing;
- separation;
- purification;
- revelation of hidden properties;
- transformation through heat.

The interesting question is therefore not merely:

> Why is the Ring gold?

But:

> **Why does Tolkien make the Ring's truth materially discoverable through an operation performed upon it?**

## ⚗️ The Eight Alchemical Formations

The specific alchemical sequence being used as the comparative framework is:

1. **Calcinatio** — burning / reduction through fire
2. **Solutio** — dissolution / submersion
3. **Coagulatio** — solidification / embodiment / fixing into form
4. **Sublimatio** — elevation / refinement / movement upward
5. **Mortificatio** — death / decay / putrefaction
6. **Rotatio** — rotation / recurrence / cycling through the process
7. **Separatio** — division / discrimination / separation of components
8. **Coniunctio** — conjunction / union of separated elements

The important working hypothesis is **not** that Tolkien has deliberately encoded eight textbook alchemical stages in sequence.

Rather:

> **The Ring and the narrative around it repeatedly behave in ways recognisable through this grammar of transformation.**

The question is whether these formations provide useful structural scaffolding for understanding what the Ring does to people, places, and systems.

---

### 🔥 1. Calcinatio

**Fire, burning, testing, reduction.**

Already unusually conspicuous.

The Ring is deliberately placed into fire in order to determine its nature.

Fire does not destroy it.

Instead:

**fire → test → revelation**

Its hidden inscription becomes visible.

This makes fire epistemic as well as transformative.

The object survives the operation while its concealed nature becomes legible.

📌 Watch:

- furnaces;
- volcanic fire;
- burning;
- testing through heat;
- destruction versus purification;
- things which survive fire;
- things whose true nature fire reveals.

---

### 🌊 2. Solutio

**Dissolution, immersion, surrender of fixed form.**

This is where the earlier Bilbo material becomes particularly interesting.

The Shire imagines Bilbo's final fate as:

> **“drowned, probably.”**

Alongside:

> **“into the blue”**

This creates an early cluster of:

**departure → water/blue → presumed submersion → loss of previous social form**

The *rex marinus* / drowned-king comparison belongs particularly strongly here.

It does not establish direct borrowing.

But the morphology is worth retaining:

**established figure → immersion → dissolution/surrender → potential transformation**

📌 Watch:

- drowning;
- rivers;
- seas;
- immersion;
- washing;
- loss of boundaries;
- surrender;
- identities becoming less fixed.

---

### 🪨 3. Coagulatio

**Fixing, embodiment, solidification, taking material form.**

This may become particularly important because the Ring itself is extraordinarily **fixed**.

It:

- retains its form;
- resists deterioration;
- survives ordinary fire;
- appears materially stable while destabilising its bearer.

This raises the possibility of **pathological coagulation**.

Something which should participate in change instead becomes unnaturally fixed.

The bearer, meanwhile, can become increasingly organised around the fixed object.

📌 Watch:

- stone;
- bodily embodiment;
- materialisation;
- fixation;
- permanence;
- things becoming physically bound;
- healthy stability versus pathological rigidity.

---

### 🌬️ 4. Sublimatio

**Elevation, ascent, refinement, movement from lower to higher condition.**

Do not assume every mountain or high place is *sublimatio*.

Instead watch what happens when characters or substances move upward.

Questions:

- Does elevation produce perspective?
- Does it produce spiritual/intellectual clarification?
- Does someone attempt elevation without the necessary prior transformation?
- Are there counterfeit forms of transcendence?

Frodo's dreams of distant mountains may eventually become interesting here, but it is too early to assign them.

📌 Watch:

- mountains;
- towers;
- flight;
- ascent;
- air;
- high viewpoints;
- spiritual elevation;
- attempts to rise above ordinary limits.

---

### ☠️ 5. Mortificatio

**Death, decay, decomposition, putrefaction.**

This potentially intersects strongly with Tolkien's treatment of mortality.

The Ring's great peculiarity may be that it **interferes with proper mortificatio**.

It prolongs.

It preserves.

It stretches.

It prevents ordinary completion without necessarily producing genuine life.

This makes its apparent gift of longevity potentially **contra naturam**.

Instead of:

**life → ageing → death → transformation**

the Ring produces something more like:

**life → extension → thinning → fixation → inability to complete**

📌 Watch:

- corpses;
- decay;
- withering;
- things unable to die;
- things improperly preserved;
- acceptance versus refusal of mortality;
- death as necessary transformation.

---

### 🔄 6. Rotatio

**Rotation, repetition, recurrence, cycling through transformations.**

This one may connect particularly well with material already noticed independently:

- anniversaries;
- birthdays;
- repeated dates;
- historical recurrence;
- old dangers returning;
- stories retold;
- inherited objects;
- events becoming myth and then becoming relevant again.

Chapter 2 is literally **The Shadow of the Past**.

The past does not remain past.

It rotates back into the present.

This also connects with Catholic calendrical culture:

**date → recurrence → remembrance → renewed encounter**

📌 Watch:

- circles;
- wheels;
- rings;
- repeated journeys;
- anniversaries;
- historical recurrence;
- repeated mistakes;
- cycles which transform versus cycles which trap.

The Ring itself is, rather inconveniently, **a circle**.

🤨

### ✂️ 7. Separatio

**Separation, discrimination, division of what has become mixed or confused.**

Already extremely important.

Bilbo must become **separated from the Ring**.

That separation is difficult.

Gandalf recognises that Bilbo's ability to relinquish it matters.

This suggests that possession creates something approaching a coupled system:

**bearer ↔ Ring**

Separatio therefore becomes both material and psychological.

Can the bearer distinguish:

- self from object?
- desire from judgement?
- capability from entitlement?
- their own will from the Ring's influence?

📌 Watch:

- relinquishment;
- departures;
- cutting ties;
- discernment;
- distinguishing truth from appearance;
- separation of object and bearer;
- preservation of individual identity.

---

### 💍 8. Coniunctio

**Conjunction, union, bringing separated elements into relation.**

This may be the most important formation for understanding the Ring.

Possession is not merely:

> person has object.

It increasingly appears to be:

> **person enters into transformative relationship with object.**

The outcome depends upon both.

That makes the Ring less like a static symbol for power and more like a **reagent participating in conjunction with a bearer**.

Different bearer:

→ different desires  
→ different vulnerabilities  
→ different temptations  
→ potentially different transformation.

📌 Watch:

- joining;
- binding;
- possession;
- merging identities;
- shared wills;
- healthy union versus domination;
- conjunction without loss of self.

## 🧪 The Ring as the Eight Formations Gone Wrong

This is the particularly interesting hypothesis.

The Ring may not represent one formation.

It may behave like **a mechanism capable of driving the transformational process itself**.

But repeatedly:

> **the formation appears inverted, corrupted, premature, or contra naturam.**

| Formation | Transformative possibility | Ring / Mordor inversion to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcinatio** | testing / transformation through fire | fire used for domination; object resists destruction |
| **Solutio** | dissolution / surrender | loss of self rather than fruitful surrender |
| **Coagulatio** | embodiment / stable form | pathological fixation / inability to change |
| **Sublimatio** | elevation / expanded perspective | domination from above / counterfeit transcendence |
| **Mortificatio** | necessary death / decomposition | refusal or interruption of proper death |
| **Rotatio** | transformative recurrence | trapped repetition / history unable to resolve |
| **Separatio** | discrimination / healthy differentiation | inability to separate bearer from power |
| **Coniunctio** | integration / generative union | possession, domination, absorption |

This may explain why the Ring feels simultaneously **alchemical and wrong**.

It does not necessarily oppose transformation.

It produces **wrongly ordered transformation**.

## 🌱 Contra Naturam

The Ring appears to possess **contra naturam** qualities.

Its effects interfere with expected natural processes.

Most importantly:

**continued existence ≠ flourishing**

Bilbo receives prolonged life but becomes stretched and depleted.

The Ring offers something resembling preservation while producing the wrong kind of preservation.

### Working polarity

**secundum naturam**

Transformation which works with or fulfils proper nature.

versus

**contra naturam**

Transformation which distorts, fixes, corrupts, or violates the proper course of development.

This may make the Ring a form of **counterfeit perfection**.

Gold conventionally signifies perfected/incorruptible matter.

Tolkien gives us an apparently incorruptible golden object which produces corruption in living beings.

## 🧿 The Ring as Counterfeit Completion

This may be particularly important.

The Ring is already:

- gold;
- apparently incorruptible;
- resistant to ordinary fire;
- fixed in form;
- capable of extending the duration of its bearer.

In other words, it possesses several qualities symbolically associated with **successful completion**.

But it appears to possess them **without proper transformation**.

It is perfection-shaped.

Its consequences are not perfection.

That creates the possibility that the Ring represents something structurally nastier than an unfinished Work:

> **a false completed state**

or:

> **the end-state of transformation obtained contra naturam.**

This would explain why the Ring can look simultaneously:

- beautiful;
- simple;
- perfected;
- enduring;
- powerful;

while producing:

- thinning;
- stretching;
- dependence;
- fixation;
- corruption;
- loss of proportion.

The Ring may therefore embody an **inversion of alchemical completion** rather than merely participating in alchemical operations.

**king / established form**

**immersion**

**dissolution / surrender**

**loss of previous fixed condition**

**potential emergence in another form**

- relinquishment of gold;
- separation of bearer and Ring;
- inheritance by another bearer;
- unnatural preservation;
- fire revealing hidden properties;

it is sufficiently consonant with alchemical transformational imagery to remain on the watch list.

## 🌑 Jung and the Shadow — Historical Context, Not Proven Influence

Carl Jung was a major contemporary intellectual.

His engagement with alchemy became particularly important during the period in which Tolkien was developing LOTR.

Jung is therefore relevant as evidence that:

> **alchemy as a language for transformation of the person was culturally and intellectually live during Tolkien's period.**

This does **not** establish:

> Tolkien read Jung.

That requires evidence.

### Three separate evidential levels

**1. Direct source / influence**

Can we demonstrate Tolkien encountered a particular text, author, or idea?

**2. Cultural availability / resonance**

Was the material circulating in Tolkien's intellectual environment?

**3. Morphological similarity**

Does Tolkien's fictional structure behave in a way usefully comparable with that material?

Do not quietly turn Level 3 into Level 1.

### “Shadow”

Given:

- the chapter title;
- corrupted transformation;
- inversion;
- unresolved past;
- returning historical danger;

track Tolkien's use of **shadow**.

Do not automatically label it Jungian.

## 🧠 Memory and Intellectual Influence

Do not imagine influence as:

**Tolkien reads Text X → perfectly reproduces Symbol Y**

Human memory rarely works like that.

A more plausible route can be:

**source**  
↓  
**encounter**  
↓  
**memory**  
↓  
**partial forgetting**  
↓  
**recombination**  
↓  
**fiction**

What survives may be the **shape of the idea**, rather than its technical vocabulary.

For example:

- gold is special;
- fire reveals/tests;
- transformation occurs through formations or operations;
- different substances respond differently;
- preservation can become unnatural;
- dissolution can precede another state;
- separation and conjunction matter;
- transformation can move with or against nature.

Tolkien would not need to remember the original source accurately for those conceptual relationships to remain available.

### Important methodological question

Instead of asking only:

> **Which alchemical text is this from?**

also ask:

> **What might someone remember about alchemy twenty years after encountering it?**

Imperfect correspondence can itself be consistent with cultural transmission.

## ✍️ Writers Also Steal Cool Shapes

Do not over-intellectualise ordinary creative practice.

Sometimes a writer encounters a symbolic system and thinks:

> **That is extremely good story material.**

A pattern can become scaffolding.

Four stages might inspire four places.

Eight formations might provide eight useful kinds of transformation.

A number sequence might become structural architecture.

A colour sequence might become landscapes.

A striking symbolic death might become an unrelated narrative transformation.

Eventually the story develops its own internal logic and stops obeying the source.

Therefore:

> **80% correspondence followed by deviation does not necessarily disprove influence.**

It may simply mean:

> **the story now owns the machinery.**

This is particularly relevant to possible second-hand or long-remembered influence.

The author does not need to be deliberately “encoding” a system.

The system may simply have become part of the author's imaginative furniture.

## ✝️ Christian Metabolisation of Esoteric Material

Tolkien can use esoteric symbolic grammar without endorsing the esoteric system.

Christian traditions have long:

- absorbed;
- contested;
- repurposed;
- reinterpreted;

adjacent symbolic vocabularies.

Fire can represent:

- destruction;
- judgement;
- purification;
- divine presence;
- transformation.

Gold can represent:

- greed;
- worldly power;
- incorruptibility;
- glory.

Death can represent:

- defeat;
- passage;
- sacrifice;
- transformation;
- resurrection.

Therefore occult/alchemical-looking imagery does not automatically imply occult theological commitment.

### Working hypothesis

> **LOTR may metabolise parts of the early-twentieth-century esoteric revival through a strongly Christian moral imagination.**

Not:

**reject every symbol**

and not:

**endorse the occult system**

but:

**take symbolic material seriously, rearrange it, and distinguish properly ordered transformation from corrupted transformation.**

C.S. Lewis provides an obvious contemporary comparison for the broader British tradition of Christian fantasy freely using pagan, magical, and mythological material.

This also helps explain why later forms of “fantasy itself is occult corruption” panic fit awkwardly against parts of the British literary tradition.

British Christian fantasy has long been capable of saying:

> **Yes, there is a witch. No, the presence of the witch does not mean the author would like you to practise witchcraft. Please keep up.**

## 💍 The Ring Is Not Necessarily “Power”

A common reading is:

> Ring = power  
> power corrupts

This captures something but may be too static.

The Ring works better as a model of:

> **external capability interacting with internal desire and identity.**

Possible analogues can therefore include:

- political power;
- wealth;
- military capability;
- nuclear weapons;
- technology;
- surveillance;
- intelligence;
- charisma;
- esoteric knowledge;
- industrial capacity.

None needs to be **the** answer.

### General model

**external capability × internal desire × ego × justification × time**

The problem is not merely possession of capability.

It is what increased capability does to the bearer's relationship with their own will.

A dangerous progression:

> **I can**  
> ↓  
> **I should**  
> ↓  
> **only I can**

That structure can recur around almost any sufficiently powerful human tool.

This helps explain the Ring's extraordinary symbolic durability.

The Ring can resemble alchemy.

It can resemble a nuclear weapon.

It can resemble political authority.

It can resemble technology.

The particular analogue changes.

The human systems problem remains.

## 🧙 Gandalf and Discernment

Gandalf already displays something more interesting than generic wisdom.

He:

- notices anomalies;
- forms hypotheses;
- does not immediately confuse hypothesis with certainty;
- investigates;
- respects apparently trivial evidence;
- recognises gaps in his knowledge;
- exercises caution around powerful objects;
- appears reluctant to expose uncertain conclusions before he has discerned them properly.

Through a Catholic lens, this resembles **discernment**.

Not:

> I am good, therefore my intentions are trustworthy.

But:

> **The greater the consequence, the more carefully I must examine my own intentions.**

Relevant concepts include:

- humility;
- conscience;
- temptation;
- examination of motive;
- acceptance of limits;
- discernment;
- relinquishment.

### Central question

> **Who can relinquish power?**

Not merely:

> Who can possess the Ring?

Watch:

- who voluntarily gives up capability;
- who accepts limits;
- who believes goodness makes them safe;
- who recognises that good intentions can themselves become dangerous justification;
- who begins believing that only they can produce the desired outcome.

The particularly dangerous temptation may not be:

> **I want to rule everyone.**

It may be:

> **I could do so much good with this.**

## 🌱 Two Kinds of Transformation

This may become one of the largest questions in the book.

If Ring/Mordor transformation is **contra naturam**, what does Tolkien present as its positive opposite?

Watch for:

- organic growth;
- cultivation;
- healing;
- maturation;
- making without domination;
- voluntary relinquishment;
- acceptance of mortality;
- sacrifice;
- renewal;
- restoration;
- resurrection.

Possible larger structure:

**transformation through domination**

versus

**transformation through participation, humility, and proper relation**

Do not conclude yet.

Watch.

## 🧭 The Wider Point — Stop Looking for a Single Stand-In

There is a larger methodological issue running through all of this.

Readers often ask:

> What does the Ring symbolise?

and expect an answer of the form:

> **The Ring = X.**

That may be the wrong question.

The Ring appears deliberately nebulous enough to model a **recurring relationship between human beings and external capability**.

Whether the analogue is:

- alchemy;
- political authority;
- fascism;
- industrial power;
- nuclear weapons;
- wealth;
- technology;
- knowledge;

the deeper problem survives.

Humans acquire tools.

Humans then do weird shit with them.

The relevant moral question becomes:

> **Can a person retain a proportionate sense of self while possessing something which radically enlarges the reach of their will?**

This makes the Ring unusually timeless.

Its meaning does not expire when one historical technology or political system disappears.

Every generation can recognise a different dangerous capability in it because the Ring models **the human relationship with capability itself**.

## 🧪 Eight-Formation Watch

Keep these exact terms visible while reading:

**Calcinatio** — fire / burning / testing  
**Solutio** — dissolution / immersion  
**Coagulatio** — embodiment / fixation  
**Sublimatio** — ascent / elevation  
**Mortificatio** — death / decomposition  
**Rotatio** — recurrence / cycling  
**Separatio** — separation / discrimination / discernment  
**Coniunctio** — conjunction / integration

Do not force occurrences.

Do not require them to appear sequentially.

Do not assume direct textual borrowing.

Instead ask:

> **Does Tolkien repeatedly organise transformation using this family of relationships?**

And especially:

> **Does he repeatedly invert their constructive function?**

## 📍 Working Rules for the Read

Do not turn resemblance into attribution.

Do not turn historical comparison into allegory.

Do not assume symbolic consistency before the text demonstrates it.

Do not discard imperfect correspondences merely because Tolkien does not reproduce a source system exactly.

Keep separate:

**direct evidence of influence**

from:

**historical availability**

from:

**morphological similarity**

from:

**our own useful comparative reading.**

Remember that writers borrow because things are interesting.

Remember that people misremember things.

Remember that ideas circulate second-hand.

Remember that stories eventually develop their own internal machinery.

And above all:

> **WATCH, DON'T ASSIGN YET.**

For the alchemical hypothesis specifically:

> **FORMATIONS, NOT PHILOSOPHER'S STONE.**

For the Ring specifically:

> **THE RING DOES NOT CHANGE. THE RING MAKES CHANGE HAPPEN.**

For the wider thematic hypothesis:

> **THE QUESTION MAY NOT BE WHAT POWER IS. THE QUESTION MAY BE WHAT POWER DOES TO THE PERSON USING IT.**
