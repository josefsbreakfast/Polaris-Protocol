## 👀 Charles Fucking Williams

- Important social/intellectual bridge:
  - Charles Williams was one of Tolkien’s literary associates / an Inkling.
  - Williams had substantial involvement in A. E. Waite’s Fellowship of the Rosy Cross.
  - This was a Christian Rosicrucian initiatory system descended institutionally/intellectually from the Golden Dawn milieu.
- Therefore:
  - no need whatsoever for “Tolkien secretly practised ceremonial magic.”
  - Tolkien’s Catholic commitments make that simplistic reading particularly poor.
- More interesting:
  - Rosicrucian / Golden-Dawn-descended symbolic systems were not culturally remote from Tolkien.
  - One of the men in his actual literary environment had extensive working knowledge of them.
- Imagined exchange:
  - Edith: “What’s all this Golden Dawn business?”
  - Tolkien: “No idea, dear.”
  - Charles Williams: ✌️
- British-man supply chain:
  - “I was curious about one small thing.”
  - Mate: “I’ve got something you should read.”
  - Three days later an indefensible volume has migrated into the study.
- Important research distinction:
  - documented network exposure;
  - plausible ambient familiarity;
  - demonstrable textual influence;
  - conscious parody;
  are four different evidentiary categories.

## 🤨 Exhibit A: Tolkien opens with WHAT, exactly?

- Tolkien opens *The Fellowship of the Ring* with:

  > Three Rings for the Elven-kings under the sky,  
  > Seven for the Dwarf-lords in their halls of stone,  
  > Nine for Mortal Men doomed to die, One for the Dark Lord on his dark throne  
  > In the Land of Mordor where the Shadows lie.  
  > One Ring to rule them all, One Ring to find them,  
  > One Ring to bring them all and in the darkness bind them  
  > In the Land of Mordor where the Shadows lie.

- Immediate problem:

  **3 — 7 — 9 — 1**

- Sir.
- SIR.
- This is not yet evidence of a Rosicrucian source.
- It is, however, an extremely fucking conspicuous numerical construction to place at the threshold of the book.

### The numbers

- The aesthetically obvious reordered cluster is:

  **1 — 3 — 7 — 9**

- Properties immediately visible:
  - all four are odd;
  - `1 + 9 = 10`;
  - `3 + 7 = 10`;
  - therefore `1 + 3 + 7 + 9 = 20`;
  - two complementary pairs;
  - conspicuous absence of `5`;
  - extremely attractive material for traditions interested in numerical relationship and correspondence.
- Then there is the 3×3 Lo Shu / later Saturn magic-square structure:

        4 9 2
        3 5 7
        8 1 6

- Here:
  - **1, 3, 7, 9 surround 5**;
  - they form the orthogonal cross around the centre;
  - this square has a much older history than Western ceremonial occultism;
  - it later enters Western planetary magic as the square associated with Saturn;
  - therefore this is relevant to the *transmission history of numerical correspondences*, not evidence that Tolkien derived his sequence from the Lo Shu square.
- Important correction:
  - Rosenkreutz’s conventional legendary birth year is usually given as 1378, not 1379.
  - Do not preserve “1379 = Rosenkreutz” as a clean direct correspondence.
- Nevertheless Rosicrucianism remains a socially much more plausible route of exposure for Tolkien than a speculative direct Lo Shu route.

### But Tolkien does not give 1–3–7–9

- He gives:

  **3 — 7 — 9 — 1**

- The **One has been moved**.
- And where has Tolkien put it?
- At the end.
- With:
  - the Dark Lord;
  - the dark throne;
  - Mordor;
  - shadows;
  - rule;
  - finding;
  - bringing;
  - binding.
- Working hypothesis:

  > If **1–3–7–9** is being perceived as an ordered/correspondence-friendly cluster, Tolkien has rotated it so that **One**, rather than occupying the privileged beginning/top, becomes the terminal and morally degraded term.

- **HE IS MAKING A FUCKING POINT, EDITH.**
- Do not yet claim why.
- Just keep watching the One.

## 🌹 Exhibit B: what Waite’s Rosicrucian liturgy actually says

- Comparative text:
  - A. E. Waite / Watchers of the Holy House, *Ceremony of Consecration on the Threshold of Sacred Mystery* (1926).
  - link: https://iapsop.com/ssoc/1926__watchers_of_holy_house___ceremony_of_consecration_on_threshold_of_sacred_mystery.pdf  
- This matters because Charles Williams was directly involved in Waite’s Rosicrucian environment.
- We are therefore looking at an actual example of the ritual/literary language circulating within Tolkien’s extended intellectual circle, rather than generic modern descriptions of “occult symbolism.”
- Important:
  - not claiming Tolkien used this as a source;
  - examining whether the symbolic/liturgical grammar available in Williams’s Rosicrucian environment provides useful comparative material.

### The High Hymn of the Presence

- Waite:

  > THE HIGH HYMN OF THE PRESENCE  
  > I have passed through the ways without in the  
  > light of thesc,  
  > Amidst star-flowers, flowers of a bright star,  
  > The Herb of Silence and the Herb of Speech.  

- Immediate reaction:
  - **oh for fuck’s sake.**  
- This was one of the first points where the **timbre** became interesting.
- The surrounding hymn moves through:
  - star-flowers;
  - herbs;
  - speech and silence;
  - hills;
  - vales;
  - cornfields;
  - stars;
  - quest;
  - brush-wood;
  - sea;
  - rivers;
  - rain;
  - leaves;
  - moonlight;
  - sacred/hidden places;
  - secret words and voices.
- This is not uniquely Rosicrucian.
- It belongs to a much wider British Christian / mystical / Romantic / occult literary ecology.
- But it is striking beside Tolkien’s own use of:
  - landscape;
  - archaic diction;
  - plants;
  - speech;
  - song;
  - stars;
  - quests;
  - sacred and hidden things.

- And then Tolkien gives the Hobbits **pipe-weed** and treats smoking it as an art.

- Which is extremely funny beside:

  > The Herb of Silence and the Herb of Speech.

- Possible tonal inversion:
  - mystical lads:
    - **THE HERB OF SILENCE AND THE HERB OF SPEECH**
  - Tolkien:
    - **yes Charles, very good; Tobold Hornblower grows excellent pipe-weed.**

- Possible broader inversion:
  - esoteric system:
    - ordinary thing → correspondence → hidden significance → spiritual ascent.
  - Tolkien:
    - ordinary thing → particular place → particular people → ordinary pleasure → already meaningful.
- The herb does not need to unlock the cosmos.
- Sometimes the fucking herb is something you smoke after dinner.
- Crucially, Tolkien still gives pipe-weed:
  - history;
  - provenance;
  - cultivation;
  - regional expertise;
  - social practice;
  - aesthetic judgement;
  - trade;
  - lore.
- He is not rejecting meaning.
- He may be rejecting the need to make meaning **totalising**.

## 👑 Exhibit C: “one law alone, one rule and reign”

- The closest symbolic passage found in the Waite rite is:

  > one law alone, one rule and reign

- The same compact section then moves through the language of:
  - kingdom;
  - throne;
  - sceptre;
  - crown;
  - Kinghood;
  - King.

- Put directly beside Tolkien:

  > One Ring to rule them all

- And:

  > One for the Dark Lord on his dark throne

- This is the comparison worth preserving in its actual textual form.

### Waite

> one law alone, one rule and reign

→ kingdom  
→ throne  
→ sceptre  
→ crown  
→ King

### Tolkien

> One Ring to rule them all

→ Dark Lord  
→ dark throne  
→ bring  
→ bind  
→ darkness

- The interesting thing is not merely shared vocabulary.
- The **moral direction has apparently been reversed**.
- Waite’s One/rule/reign is associated with divine Love and legitimate spiritual unity.
- Tolkien’s One/rule is associated with coercive domination.
- Shared semantic cluster:

  **ONE → RULE → SOVEREIGNTY → THRONE → INTEGRATION OF THE MANY**

- But:
  - Waite:
    - One → Love → divine unity → fulfilment.
  - Tolkien:
    - One → domination → rule → gathering → binding.

- Another extremely important Waite line:

### Waite

- legitimate higher unity:
  - encompasses;
  - fulfils;
  - **does not destroy**.

### Tolkien

> One Ring to rule them all, One Ring to find them,  
> One Ring to bring them all and in the darkness bind them

- false unity:
  - rules;
  - finds;
  - brings;
  - **binds**.

- Possible distinction:

  **unity which preserves plurality**

  versus

  **unity which converts plurality into possession**.

- This may be the theological hinge.
- The One does not create the plurality.
- It attempts to possess it.
- **Many → one owner.**

## 7️⃣ Exhibit F: the numbered Hymn of Union

- Waite’s *Hymn of the Soul in Union* is organised into **seven numbered movements**.
- Its movement culminates in language of:

  > Unity

  and:

  > Oneness

- So within this actual ritual text we have:

  **numbered progression → Union → Oneness**

- Tolkien gives us:

  **3 → 7 → 9 → ONE**

- Followed immediately by:

  **rule → find → bring → bind → darkness**

- Important:
  - we have **not** found a Waite `1–3–7–9` sequence;
  - do not manufacture one.
- What we *have* found is:
  - numbered ritual progression;
  - One/Oneness as spiritually privileged;
  - One connected with rule/reign;
  - kingship/throne/crown vocabulary;
  - unity described as non-destructive.
- Tolkien appears to take that entire kind of symbolic grammar and make the terminal **One** the dangerous thing.
- Research question:

  > Is Tolkien taking a culturally available symbolic grammar of ascent toward divine unity and constructing a deliberately degraded/counterfeit version in which the One is dragged downward into domination?

## Tolkien on allegory: keep watching this

- Tolkien explicitly resists allegorical decoding.
- Important distinction:
  - this does **not** mean “symbols bad.”
  - obviously.
- Possible deeper aesthetic objection:
  - author determines mapping;
  - reader decodes mapping;
  - symbol becomes bound to authorised meaning.
- Tolkien famously contrasts this with applicability and uses the language of the:
  - **“purposed domination of the author.”**
- Extremely interesting phrase given the Ring’s function.
- Possible aesthetic/moral rhyme:
  - **do not bind everything to One.**
- Not:
  - political events;
  - symbols;
  - readers;
  - peoples;
  - meanings.
- Meaning may be allowed to proliferate.
- A symbol can resonate simultaneously with:
  - Christianity;

  - medieval literature;
  - folklore;
  - occult vocabulary;
  - contemporary politics;
  - personal experience;

## 🇬🇧 Catholic / British context

- Important not to flatten this into later evangelical:
  - “magic imagery = Satanism.”
- Tolkien’s British Catholic background potentially gives a different relationship to:
  - ritual;
  - sacrament;
  - mystery;
  - symbolic action;
  - hierarchy;
  - supernatural language.
- British Catholics also existed within a culture with a long history of:
  - anti-Catholic suspicion;
  - dual-loyalty allegations;
  - foreign-allegiance narratives;
  - ritual misrepresentation;
  - Catholicism being framed as politically suspect/un-English.
- Therefore Tolkien would have been culturally familiar with the fact that:
  - elaborate ritual can look sinister from outside;
  - formal similarity does not establish theological equivalence.
- Possible Catholic response to Rosicrucian/occult systems therefore need not be:
  - “ritual is weird and evil.”
- More interesting:
  - **what is the ritual directing the will toward?**
- Useful distinctions:
  - sacrament vs technique;
  - gift vs acquisition;
  - authority vs domination;
  - mystery vs secret knowledge as status;
  - surrender vs extension of personal will;
  - unity vs absorption.

## 🏴 Anti-fascism / anti-totalisation — spin out later

- Do **not** reduce this to:
  - Sauron = Hitler.
- Tolkien himself rejects that sort of mechanical war allegory.
- More interesting possibility:
  - responses to fascism/totalitarianism are encoded at the level of **social, linguistic and affective machinery**.
- Fascist movements historically exploit:
  - repetition;
  - slogans;
  - symbols;
  - mythic past;
  - sacred territory;
  - heroic archetypes;
  - mass synchronisation;
  - aestheticised violence;
  - belonging;
  - transcendence;
  - simple oppositions;
  - dissolution of individual identity into collective destiny.
- In other words:
  - **many becoming One.**
- Rosicrucianism, religion, mysticism, counterculture and fascism are obviously **not morally equivalent**.
- Relevant overlap is technological/psychological:
  - human beings respond powerfully to rhythm, repetition, synchronisation, patterned language, symbols and collective meaning.
- Ethical question:
  - **where is that machinery directing attention, identity and agency?**
- Tolkien’s opening verse itself is extraordinarily memorable:

  - repetition;
  - number;
  - escalation;
  - rupture;
  - One;
  - rule/find/bring/bind;
  - refrain;
  - darkness.
- It is seductive language describing seduction by domination.
- Potentially Tolkien recognises:
  - aesthetic power ≠ moral goodness.

## 🪄 But does it count as magic?

- Importantly:
  - **is it magic — does it count?**
  - **and/or is it satire?**
  - **and/or does it remain Christian in its core — and therefore an invocation/prayer?**
- Tolkien has written something that is, at minimum, **spell-shaped**.
- The Ring verse has:
  - patterned number;
  - repetition;
  - incantatory rhythm;
  - supernatural referents;
  - words associated with a magical object;
  - binding language;
  - invocation-like structure;
  - a linguistic form that appears to participate in the fictional world's magical reality.
- So:
  - **is it magic?**
  - **is it satire/parody of magical or esoteric forms?**
  - **can it be both?**
  - **and if its underlying moral/theological orientation remains Christian, what exactly distinguishes it from invocation, prayer, hymn, charm, or counter-liturgy?**

- This becomes particularly interesting because different traditions draw the boundary around “magic” differently.
- The question cannot be answered merely by asking whether the text:
  - rhymes;
  - invokes supernatural realities;
  - uses symbolic numbers;
  - sounds ritualistic;
  - or changes the reader’s affective state.
- Christian liturgy and prayer can also involve:
  - repetition;
  - sacred words;
  - patterned language;
  - number;
  - invocation;
  - material objects;
  - symbolic action;
  - altered attention;
  - communal recitation.
- Therefore the interesting theological distinction may lie somewhere else:
  - **what is the will doing?**
  - **where is agency located?**
  - **is power received or acquired?**
  - **is something being requested, commanded, manipulated, possessed, surrendered to, or participated in?**

### Magic / prayer / satire may not be mutually exclusive categories

- Possible readings of the Ring verse:
  1. **fictional magic**
     - within Middle-earth, these are words bound up with an actually magical object and its power;
  2. **literary imitation of magical language**
     - Tolkien deliberately constructs something recognisably incantatory;
  3. **counter-liturgy**
     - the form evokes sacred/esoteric language while its moral orientation is inverted;
  4. **satire/parody**
     - elevated claims to unity, hierarchy and hidden power are reproduced in order to expose their potentially ridiculous or dangerous endpoint;
  5. **Christian moral literature**
     - the entire construction can still operate within a recognisably Christian account of temptation, corrupted will, false transcendence and relinquishment.

- These possibilities do not necessarily cancel one another.
- A Christian author can write a fictional spell.
- A fictional spell can parody occult language.
- A parody can preserve the aesthetic power of the thing it criticises.
- And a text using magical forms can still direct the reader toward a profoundly Christian conclusion.

- Indeed, that ambiguity may be part of what makes the comparison interesting.
- If Tolkien has taken something aesthetically adjacent to:
  - hymn;
  - ritual;
  - invocation;
  - esoteric correspondence;
  - magical speech
  and made its central proposition:
  - **ONE WILL SHALL POSSESS EVERYTHING**
  then the theological criticism is not necessarily:
  - “look how spooky magic is.”
- It may instead be:
  - **look what happens when sacred-looking machinery becomes an instrument for extending the individual will.**

### The especially annoying possibility

- If the Ring verse is deliberately counter-liturgical, then asking:
  - “is this Christian or is this magical?”
  may itself produce the wrong binary.
- It could be **Christian writing about magic by constructing extremely good fictional magic**.
- It could even borrow or parody recognisable esoteric machinery precisely because Tolkien understands why that machinery is aesthetically compelling.
- The relevant distinction then becomes ethical/theological rather than merely formal:

  **prayer → relationship / petition / participation / surrender**

  **Sauronic magic → acquisition / command / possession / domination**

- Which returns us, annoyingly, to:

  > fulfilling and not destroying

  versus:

  > rule / bring / bind

- Research later:
  - Tolkien’s explicit writings on magic;
  - Catholic theological distinctions between miracle / sacrament / prayer / superstition / magic;
  - medieval Christian charms and protective formulae;
  - Tolkien’s professional familiarity with medieval texts where modern categories of “prayer,” “charm,” and “magic” become considerably less tidy;
  - whether Tolkien himself distinguishes different kinds of magic within the legendarium;
  - whether the Ring inscription is represented as descriptive, performative, constitutive, or some mixture of these.

- **Do not solve this yet.**
- It is much more useful as a live question:
  - **Does it count?**
  - **What would make it count?**
  - **Who gets to decide?**

## 🤔 Current extremely provisional thesis

- Tolkien may be doing something more interesting than allegorising twentieth-century authoritarianism.
- He may be constructing a literary environment that recognises **how totalising systems become emotionally and aesthetically compelling**, gives readers access to many of those pleasures, and then redirects them toward:
  - plurality;
  - restraint;
  - mercy;
  - ordinary life;
  - friendship;
  - locality;
  - relinquishment of power.
- Rosicrucian/Golden-Dawn-descended material matters here not necessarily as “the source,” but as one nearby example of the symbolic and ritual technologies circulating within Tolkien’s intellectual environment.
- Charles Williams makes that environment particularly difficult to treat as culturally remote.
- Possible formulation to preserve:

  > Tolkien’s anti-totalitarianism may operate below allegory, at the level of the narrative, linguistic and social technologies through which people experience power, belonging, transcendence and collective identity.

- Alternative formulation:

  **Sauron wants One. Tolkien gives you a world.**
