[I find it really interesting that this is still the operational strategy towards these protests.](https://vm.tiktok.com/ZN8eg26Ka/)  

I spoke a while ago about why blanket use of exclusion categories was an odd choice when dealing with GB News as an FOI stakeholder, and policing using officers who cannot change strategy for their arrest of people who are either solicitors, barristers, or very well briefed by them, is very odd. Not every legal strategy would be to extract clear damages in an unlawful arrest case; many solicitors would advise differently if the strategy was to challenge what they saw as a fundamental overextension of legal powers.  

In the context that now, the [“legal profession (is speaking out) in defence of silk facing contempt trial”](https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/kafkaesque-lawyers-rally-to-defence-of-silk-cited-for-contempt/5127485.article), it is curious that operational policing response has not yet changed.  

This is even more curious, in the context of the [“Met Police reconsidering decision not to probe war crimes claims against Britons who fought in IDF”](https://www.thejc.com/news/uk/met-police-reconsidering-decision-not-to-probe-war-crimes-claims-against-britons-who-fought-in-idf-wo3tdbw8).  

This starts to look a lot less like the Met themselves are leading, and more like the response to, for example, [previous Government failure to back up its senior policing leadership](https://www.youtube.com/live/spCwz8AO-Mc?is=mvpoKgcTmcprKZqx).  

The lack of respect for the relationship between the sitting Government of the day and senior policing leadership was grotesque, and the lasting effects will not help the police and the public to develop a less adversarial relationship, across the spectrum of reasons policing and public interface. The risk and pressure for what appears to be a political decision, appears to be being put squarely on the shoulders of policing; if we are serious about reduing security risks, this produces a number of secondary impacts, which include the public becoming unwilling to report to the police, which may sound like “less work”, but also lowers quality of community intelligence.  

But you know, I’m an abolitionist, so go off, I guess.  

Policing can be so much better than this, but it is essential for it to not be the Government’s risk dump, in order to serve better the communities who it relies upon for consent; Peel was being as philosophical as he was pragmatic.  

Policing does not have the same ability to lobby for its working conditions, in the same way as other professions in the UK, the Commissioners are incentivised against putting responsibility towards the Government even when this would be more honest as RCA – even more so after the Commissioner for West Mids and his colleagues were dragged in for a very publicised witch tr-… I mean, um, *subcommittee hearing*.
