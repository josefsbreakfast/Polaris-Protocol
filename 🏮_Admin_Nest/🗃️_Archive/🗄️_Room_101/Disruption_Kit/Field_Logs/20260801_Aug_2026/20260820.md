Since the IPT submission, everything was very harsh, then extremely systems-petty.

> Nintendo systems, especially modern Switch hardware/firmware, have a reputation for being unusually hardened compared with a generic consumer device, but “notoriously hard to hack” depends heavily on which Switch model, firmware version, access route, and what kind of compromise you mean.

> The original 2017-era Switch had serious vulnerabilities, including the famous Fusée Gelée bootROM exploit. But that was a hardware flaw in early Tegra-based units, and Nintendo/NVIDIA closed that route in later hardware revisions. ## 🧮 The Datamined Wish Counters

The strongest technical source for the beach-fragment system is IsomorphicBox's **Assortment of Rates and Information**, which states that its information was datamined from the ACNH executable and EventFlow files using tooling associated with Ninji's reverse-engineering work.

The relevant counters are:

~~~text
cPlayer:PrayStarToday
~~~

and

~~~text
cLand:VisitorPrayStarToday
~~~

### `cPlayer:PrayStarToday`

This records how many successful wishes the resident player made.

It is:

- **per resident/player profile**;
- capped at **20** for purposes of next-day star-fragment entitlement.

So:

~~~text
1 wish    → up to 1 own-wish fragment
10 wishes → up to 10 own-wish fragments
20 wishes → up to 20 own-wish fragments
80 wishes → still only 20 own-wish fragments
~~~

Extra personal wishes can still matter for achievements or enjoyment, but they do not continue increasing this particular next-day own-wish entitlement past 20.

### `cLand:VisitorPrayStarToday`

This records wishes made by **visitors on the island**.

It is:

- stored **per island** rather than per resident;
- capped at **100 visitor wishes**.

Visitor wishes are converted at:

~~~text
1 additional fragment per 5 visitor wishes
~~~

Therefore:

~~~text
5 visitor wishes   → +1 fragment
25 visitor wishes  → +5 fragments
50 visitor wishes  → +10 fragments
100 visitor wishes → +20 fragments
~~~

The visitor-derived contribution therefore tops out at **20 additional fragments per resident profile**.

## 🧮 The Next-Day Formula

During the game's daily `GrowUp` routine, ACNH sets another per-player counter:

~~~text
cPlayer:StarPieceGrowCount
~~~

The datamined formula is:

~~~text
StarPieceGrowCount
=
PrayStarToday
+
(VisitorPrayStarToday / 5)
~~~

subject to the caps already described.

In practical terms:

~~~text
E = min(personal wishes, 20) + min(visitor wishes, 100) / 5
~~~

where `E` is the resident's available fragment-spawn entitlement for that day.

Maximum:

~~~text
20 own-wish fragments
+
20 visitor-derived fragments
=
40 fragments per eligible resident profile
~~~

### Examples

| Own wishes | Visitor wishes | Expected counter |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 3 | 0 | 3 |
| 12 | 0 | 12 |
| 20 | 0 | 20 |
| 70 | 0 | 20 |
| 20 | 25 | 25 |
| 20 | 50 | 30 |
| 20 | 100 | 40 |
| 100 | 100 | 40 |

The important conceptual point is:

**the game first establishes an entitlement counter.**

It does not simply roll independently all day and maybe decide to give the player some stars.

If the counter was established correctly but only one component of the expected entitlement subsequently appeared, that observation cannot be explained by the entitlement equation alone.

That does **not** establish external interference. Possible explanations still include undocumented spawn constraints, beach-state limits, save/reload behaviour, profile interactions, EventFlow branches not captured by the simplified model, corrupted state, ordinary bugs, or errors in the reverse-engineered description itself.

But analytically, the distinction matters:

~~~text
expected state established
        ↓
one tranche realised
        ↓
another expected tranche absent
~~~

That means there is an additional variable somewhere.

The useful forensic question is not yet:

> **Who did it?**

It is:

> **What mechanism can produce this particular divergence?**

## 🧿 Why Do You Care?

Because this may be one of the most technically bounded anomalies in the record.

Most alleged systems interference is difficult to isolate. Search results have ranking systems. Telecoms have carriers, routing, congestion, handset software and network policy. Websites have CDNs, caches, recommendation layers and endless mundane failure modes.

A datamined game counter is different.

Here there is a comparatively small state machine:

~~~text
record wishes
    ↓
apply known caps
    ↓
calculate entitlement
    ↓
produce observable fragments
~~~

That makes a mismatch worth preserving even when its cause is unknown.

The evidential claim is deliberately narrow:

> **The observed output did not appear to match the documented computational model under the recorded conditions.**

It is **not**:

> somebody hacked Nintendo, therefore actor X did it.

Wi-Fi being formally disabled is likewise a useful environmental fact, not proof of complete technical isolation.

The reason to preserve the incident is therefore precisely that **attribution can wait**.

If a later investigation identifies a mechanism capable of manipulating this sort of Switch or ACNH state, this contemporaneous observation can be revisited against that mechanism.

And if deliberate manipulation of a modern Switch/ACNH execution state were eventually established, the capability would be sufficiently specialised that it would be reasonable to look for technical antecedents:

- vulnerability research;
- exploit-development knowledge;
- specialist tooling;
- personnel with the relevant expertise;
- testing infrastructure;
- access methodology;
- forensic artefacts.

That does not mean such a trail must presently be public.

It means the capability does not materialise from nowhere.

So the record can remain extremely simple:

> **Here is the expected state. Here is what happened instead. Here are the conditions under which it happened. Here is the technical basis for the expectation. I do not presently know why they diverged.**

And if disclosure later produces a mechanism capable of explaining it:

> *Please come back and look at the fucking stars.*
