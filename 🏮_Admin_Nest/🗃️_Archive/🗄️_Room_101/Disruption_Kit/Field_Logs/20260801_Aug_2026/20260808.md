The first time I saw an image which gave me the sensation that these ones, particularly the one on the left, do — I went looking, to check.  

To check if I was “being dramatic”.  

I am not suggesting that there is deliberate mimicry, so much as a universal understanding of what translates the sense of horror.  

The difference, being that these are photographs taken with the victims as Palestinians; the memories they were invoking, were of photographs and artistic renderings, of when that had been us.  

I burned out from repeating myself a long time ago; I do not longer know what to say, to anyone who still cannot see it.  

The first time I looked one up, and checked, it was as if it was a recreation exactly, of one of the more distressing panels of Maus.  

It is interesting to me, that we do not appear to openly acknowledge, that that in itself is an abusive recreation of imagery of the Holocaust. If you are in touch with the memory, you cannot see these images and not be emotionally activated in some way, even if that projects out as your own trauma speaking through a racist lens towards others.  

I worry what it normalises; violence never stays in boxes.  

One day this will end, and when it does, only we will know how much we resisted it. Let it be as much of an honour to the memories of our own losses and ancestors that we did as much as we could, as soon as we could, and that we did whatever was possible to be of some use to victims and survivors of this catastrophe.
