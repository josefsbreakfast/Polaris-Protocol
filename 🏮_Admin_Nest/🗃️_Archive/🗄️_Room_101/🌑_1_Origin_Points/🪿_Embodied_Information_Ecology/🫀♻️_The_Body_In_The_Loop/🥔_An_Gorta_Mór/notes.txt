# 🥔 An Gorta Mór — Cluster Plan

## README.md
**Job:** Orient the reader to the whole cluster.

- Why this cluster exists inside `🫀♻️_The_Body_In_The_Loop`.
- Establish the governing distinction:
  **the blight explains the failure of the potato crop; it does not by itself explain the human catastrophe.**
- Very short chronology.
- Explain that the cluster separates:
  - biological disturbance;
  - pre-existing political economy;
  - British knowledge;
  - British policy;
  - embodied consequences;
  - historical interpretation and memory.
- Acknowledge that this is personally difficult history for the author and will contain corrections and imperfections.
- Map/routes to every child node.
- Do NOT try to settle genocide, Gaza, capitalism, colonial responsibility or every historical dispute here.

---

## 🌱_before_the_blight.md
**Job:** Reconstruct the Ireland into which the organism arrived.

- Ireland before 1845 rather than beginning Irish history with the Famine.
- Anglo-Norman conquest and the Pale.
- Tudor reconquest.
- Munster and Ulster plantations as distinct processes.
- Cromwellian conquest/confiscation and later settlements.
- Protestant Ascendancy and Penal-era consequences.
- Acts of Union and Ireland's constitutional position inside the UK by 1845.
- Population growth, poverty and regional/class differences.
- Introduce nineteenth-century rural society.
- Make clear that none of this developed inevitably towards famine.

**Question:** *What was already there before the potatoes began to die?*

---

## 📐_english_land_controls.md
**Job:** Explain how conquest became an everyday system of property and economic power.

- Gaelic and English concepts of landholding without romanticising either.
- Confiscation, plantation, surveys and recognised title.
- Estate → lease → sublease → rent.
- Landlords, agents, middlemen, tenants, cottiers and labourers.
- Conacre, subdivision and tiny holdings.
- Land as both:
  - somewhere humans live and obtain food;
  - an abstractable income-producing asset.
- Commercialisation and marketisation.
- Modern concepts such as capitalism/colonial capitalism as analytical lenses, while distinguishing them from contemporary vocabulary.
- Formal legal rights versus practical access to remedies.
- Who could invoke law, against whom, at what cost?

**Question:** *How did control over land become control over the conditions under which people could survive?*

## 🕯️_the_lawful_catastrophe.md
**Job:** Examine how catastrophic outcomes can emerge through nominally lawful institutions.

- Property.
- contract.
- tenancy.
- rent.
- relief eligibility.
- Poor Law.
- Gregory/quarter-acre clause.
- formal rights versus effective remedies.
- legality versus survivability.
- state enforcement of some claims but not others.
- market rules themselves as political/legal arrangements.
- Avoid assuming legality means absence of coercion.
- Avoid assuming every harmful outcome required somebody individually breaking the law.

Central question:

**"Who has committed the legal wrong that gives the starving person a remedy?"**

And sometimes the answer may be: nobody has committed the particular recognised wrong required to activate one.

**Job:** Make the eventual cross-system comparison carefully.

- coercive labour.

- dependency.

## 🇬🇧_implications_for_unionism.md

**Job:** Examine An Gorta Mór from inside the political proposition of the Union.

- Ireland's constitutional position after the Acts of Union.
- What representation at Westminster actually meant in practice.
- Which responsibilities accompanied political incorporation.
- Central versus Irish/local responsibility for relief.
- The transfer of increasing financial responsibility onto Irish property/local Poor Law structures.
- Whether Ireland was treated as an integral part of the UK, a distinct administrative problem, a colonial possession, or different combinations of these depending upon the issue.
- Contemporary Unionist arguments and expectations — not merely modern ones.
- Irish responses to the apparent promises/failures of Union.
- The catastrophe's implications for later Home Rule and Union debates.
- Northern/Ulster complexity: avoid projecting twentieth-century political identities backwards.
- Modern British Unionism and historical memory.
- Distinguish:
    Union as constitutional fact
    ≠
    equal citizenship in practice
    ≠
    colonial relationships disappearing.

**Central question:**

> If Ireland was part of the United Kingdom, what did the Union oblige the United Kingdom to do when millions of its people lost their principal means of subsistence?

**Secondary question:**

> What does "Union" mean if political incorporation does not necessarily produce equivalent obligations, protection or material belonging?
