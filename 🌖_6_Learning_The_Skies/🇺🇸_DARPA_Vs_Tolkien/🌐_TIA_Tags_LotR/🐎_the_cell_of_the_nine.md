## 🛰️ Orientation

There are, unfortunately, nine of the fuckers.

They are dressed alike.

They move across jurisdictions.

They seek information about one person.

They pursue the same target.

They appear to share an objective.

They eventually prove to operate under the authority of a hostile power.

They are frightening, clandestine, coordinated, persistent, and
interested in a strategically important object.

America therefore gets one biscuit.

> 🇺🇸 **CELL?**
>
> 🇬🇧 **Do not get confident.**
>
> 🇺🇸 🥹

This node began as a joke about what happens when the Black Riders of
*The Lord of the Rings* are translated into security ontology before the
reader possesses the explanatory apparatus that makes them **Nazgûl**.

The joke survives.

The analytical problem has become larger.

The problem is not that a security system notices the Nine.

**It should notice the Nine.**

The problem is whether the same system can distinguish:

-   the dangerous organisation;
-   the person being hunted by it;
-   the person documenting it;
-   the person studying it;
-   the person criticising it;
-   the person writing fiction about it;
-   and the person who invented the entire fucking thing.

Because J. R. R. Tolkien possesses vastly more structured information
about the Nine than Frodo does.

He knows their number.

He knows their history.

He knows their hierarchy.

He knows their commander.

He knows their movements.

He knows their capabilities.

He knows their relationship to the Ring.

He knows their relationship to Sauron.

He knows their previous conflicts.

He knows what they are trying to do.

He has maps.

He has languages.

He has poems containing numbers.

He has notes.

He has, catastrophically, **documentation**.

So the central question is no longer merely:

> **Can a system correctly identify that the Nine look like a dangerous
> network?**

It is:

> **Can the system correctly identify the relationship between a person
> and the dangerous thing it has detected?**

The Ringwraiths are dangerous.

Tolkien is not a Ringwraith.

**A system insufficiently precise to distinguish those propositions has
not solved the security problem merely because it successfully found the
Nine.**

## 🧭 1. Nobody Gets Omniscience

The experiment has one important rule.

Nobody gets to know what the reader learns later.

No analyst gets:

`ENTITY = NAZGÛL`

before the Hobbits have enough information to know what a Nazgûl is.

No British cultural expert gets to announce:

> **Professor. Harmless. Next.**

merely because the subject will eventually become J. R. R. Tolkien.

No American classifier gets to begin with:

> **TERRORIST CELL.**

No folklore researcher gets to win automatically because a Black Rider
activates Black Shuck grammar.

No future reader gets to travel backwards carrying the completed
ontology.

At each information state, the question is:

> **What conclusion is justified by the information available now?**

That matters because retrospective recognition is cheap.

The Nine are easy once they are the Nine.

Tolkien is easy once he is **Tolkien**.

The difficult system has to operate before the label exists.

## 🌑 2. Start Where The Hobbits Start

At the beginning, the object is not:

`NAZGÛL`

It is closer to:

`UNRESOLVED PHENOMENON`

The early Black Rider is:

-   black-clad;
-   visually inaccessible;
-   mounted;
-   stalking rather than simply travelling;
-   interested in the Hobbits;
-   searching;
-   scenting or lowering itself in an animal-like way;
-   profoundly fucking wrong.

One feature is especially useful:

> **The horse behaves like a horse.**

The horse is not the anomaly.

The apparently human rider behaving unlike an ordinary human is.

That distinction matters.

A culturally uninformed observer might encode:

`HUMAN ACTOR + ABNORMAL TRACKING BEHAVIOUR`

A culturally informed British observer might instead ask:

> **Have we established that that's a bloke?**

That observer has not proved **Black Shuck**.

They have done something more useful.

They have challenged the ontology.

## 🗂️ 3. Build The Observation Log Before The Threat Model

The system should preserve observations before forcing them into a
completed explanation.

A useful record might contain:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Field                               Question
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  observer                            Who actually saw or reported this?

  time                                When did the observation occur?

  location                            Where?

  entity certainty                    What kind of entity have we
                                      actually established?

  observed behaviour                  What happened without
                                      interpretation?

  inferred relationship               What connection are we proposing?

  threat relevance                    Why might this matter?

  ordinary alternative                What non-threat explanation fits?

  cultural hypothesis                 Does situated knowledge suggest
                                      another ontology?

  disconfirming evidence              What would weaken the threat
                                      hypothesis?

  unresolved                          What do we simply not know?

  later knowledge                     What becomes known later but must
                                      not contaminate the earlier state?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The order matters.

Do not construct:

`TERRORIST NETWORK`

and then populate it with observations.

Construct the observations.

Then ask what networks they support.

## 🕸️ 4. When Does One Weird Rider Become A Network?

The escalation can be represented without pretending every stage proves
the next:

``` text
UNIDENTIFIED RIDER
        ↓
UNUSUAL BEHAVIOUR
        ↓
REPEATED PHENOMENON
        ↓
SECOND SIMILAR PHENOMENON
        ↓
MULTIPLE SIMILAR ENTITIES
        ↓
INFORMATION GATHERING
        ↓
COMMON TARGET
        ↓
APPARENT COORDINATION
        ↓
COMMON OBJECTIVE
        ↓
STRUCTURED GROUP
        ↓
NINE
        ↓
CELL?
```

Some of these joins may be correct.

That does not mean the final noun is correct.

> **The joins can be correct while the label on the joined object is
> wrong.**

This is one of the central problems of the case.

A system can become increasingly impressive at relationship extraction
while becoming increasingly confident about an inappropriate ontology.

## 🐕 5. Unfortunately Britain Has A Dog File

British cultural context does not produce:

`SAFE`

It produces additional hypotheses.

An ominous black thing encountered on roads, thresholds, churchyards,
coastlines or other liminal places can activate a pre-existing British
folklore repertoire around spectral or uncanny black dogs.

That does **not** establish:

`RIDER = BLACK SHUCK`

The useful intervention is:

> **Are we certain this phenomenon belongs in the human-actor ontology
> at all?**

That is a very different contribution.

The culturally informed observer may not possess a better answer.

They may possess a **better set of possible answers**.

## 🌉 6. Black Shuck Is Not The Innocent Hypothesis

This matters because cultural context is sometimes mistakenly treated as
a machine for producing reassuring explanations.

It isn't.

If the British observer says:

> **This has Black Shuck grammar.**

America is entirely entitled to reply:

> **Your alternative explanation is a supernatural death-dog?**

Yes.

Unfortunately.

The culturally situated explanation can be **more alarming** while still
correcting the ontology.

The point is not:

`CULTURAL CONTEXT → THREAT DOWN`

It is:

`CULTURAL CONTEXT → INTERPRETATION CHANGES`

Context changes the *kind* of information an observation produces.

## 🚧 7. The Hound At The Threshold

The useful folklore function here is not merely **dog**.

It is **threshold**.

The Riders arrive as Frodo is leaving one information environment and
entering another.

Home becomes road.

Known landscape becomes uncertain landscape.

Ordinary travel becomes pursuit.

The familiar countryside begins producing entities whose rules the
Hobbits do not understand.

The Black-Shuck comparison therefore works best as a reader-side
cultural activation:

> **what does this thing feel like before the explanatory apparatus
> tells us what it means?**

Not:

> Tolkien secretly wrote Black Shuck.

Not:

> Nazgûl are literally black dogs.

But:

> **A culturally situated reader may possess categories that change
> which aspects of an unresolved phenomenon become salient.**

## 🧠 8. Context Changes Salience

The most useful example may be the horse.

A classifier searching for unusual behaviour might notice:

-   black clothing;
-   concealed face;
-   nocturnal movement;
-   mounted travel;
-   pursuit;
-   sniffing;
-   repeated sightings.

But a culturally situated human may immediately redistribute the
anomaly:

`HORSE → NORMAL HORSE BEHAVIOUR`

`RIDER → WHY IS THE FUCKING MAN SCENTING US?`

The observations did not change.

Their informational weight did.

> **Cultural knowledge can change which feature carries the anomaly.**

That is a more precise claim than "humans understand context."

Sometimes they do.

Sometimes they understand the wrong context.

The design requirement is therefore not **human override**.

It is the ability to represent competing interpretations and their
provenance.

## 🐎 9. The Horse Is Doing Horse Correctly

This deserves its own rule because it is funny and because it is useful.

When a system contains multiple interacting entities, anomaly detection
should not automatically smear the anomaly across the whole scene.

The horse is not suspicious merely because its rider is.

The cloak is not suspicious merely because its wearer is.

The map is not suspicious merely because its owner is.

The poem is not suspicious merely because the person writing it is under
review.

Otherwise suspicion becomes contagious.

And once suspicion is contagious, every connected object becomes
evidence for the original suspicion.

That is how a network model can become a trap.

## 👤 10. Have We Established That's A Bloke?

Imagine the early ontology permits only:

``` text
civilian
criminal
terrorist
intelligence operative
military
unknown human
```

Then every subsequent feature answers:

> **What kind of human is this?**

Scenting becomes tracking technique.

Facelessness becomes concealment.

Cloak becomes disguise.

Horse becomes transport.

Repeated appearance becomes surveillance.

Multiple similar riders become coordinated actors.

Nine becomes cell.

The system may be reasoning coherently.

The problem occurred **before** the reasoning.

The entity was prematurely constrained to:

`HUMAN`

A better field is:

`ENTITY TYPE: UNRESOLVED`

Sometimes expertise contributes not a better answer but permission to
reopen the variable.

## 🧿 11. Phenomenon Before People

That gives us a useful discipline:

> **Phenomenon before people.**

Before deciding who an actor is, establish what kind of thing has
actually been observed.

Before deciding why a network exists, establish whether the
relationships are observed or inferred.

Before deciding that coded material is operational communication,
establish what activity produced it.

Before deciding that unusual cultural material is evidence of
ideological alignment, establish the person's relationship to the
material.

This will become extremely important when the system meets Tolkien.

## 🍪 13. America Gets One Biscuit

This creates a second problem.

Suppose the system makes several nested inferences:

1.  these observations concern related entities;
2.  the entities are coordinated;
3.  the coordination is relevant to threat;
4.  the entities are human;
5.  the correct ontology is a terrorist cell.

Imagine later evidence confirms propositions 1--3.

If the feedback signal is merely:

`THREAT DETECTION: SUCCESS`

the system can accidentally receive reinforcement for propositions 4--5
as well.

> **The reward was attached to the wrong abstraction.**

America got the relationship right and accidentally received
reinforcement for the ontology.

This matters far beyond hobbits.

A coarse success label can conceal which sub-inference actually earned
the success.

So:

> 🇺🇸 **I was right.**
>
> 🇬🇧 **You were right about one relationship.**
>
> 🇺🇸 **Therefore---**
>
> 🇬🇧 **NO. PUT THE BISCUIT DOWN.**

America gets exactly one biscuit.

It immediately develops a theory of biscuits.

## 🔢 14. AWOOOGA AWOOOGA: Coded Numbers

**Seven.**

**Nine.**

**One.**

> **AWOOOGA AWOOOGA.**


`OPERATIONAL CODE`

The poem contains compressed relational information.

It is not therefore clandestine operational communication.

> **A classifier can correctly detect that information is encoded while
> completely misunderstanding why it was encoded.**

Detecting structure is not the same as correctly identifying what the
structure means.

## 🧪 17. The Same Subject Matter Does Not Mean The Same Relationship

Once a system correctly detects a harmful organisation, it still has to
discriminate among people who produce information about that
organisation.

Possible roles include:

-   member;
-   supporter;
-   facilitator;
-   target;
-   victim;
-   witness;
-   investigator;
-   intelligence analyst;
-   journalist;
-   historian;
-   academic researcher;
-   critic;
-   artist;
-   satirist;
-   role-player;
-   archivist;
-   translator;
-   author.

These are not equivalent merely because the subject matter overlaps.

A journalist may know more about a violent organisation than many of its
supporters.

A historian may possess its propaganda.

A researcher may catalogue its symbols.

A victim may repeatedly search its terminology.

An artist may depict its violence.

An author may have invented every member of the fucking network.

The security question therefore cannot stop at:

> **Does this person possess information associated with the threat?**

It needs:

> **What relationship does this person actually have to the thing we
> correctly identified as dangerous?**

## 🧮 20. Correct Extraction, Wrong World

The case now gives us at least four distinct failure modes.

### False observation

The system says something happened that did not happen.

### False relationship

The observations are real, but the proposed connection is wrong.

### False significance

The observation and relationship are real, but their importance is
misjudged.

### False ontology

The observations and some relationships may be correct, but the system
misunderstands what kind of thing it is looking at.

The Tolkien case is particularly useful because it allows:

> **correct extraction + correct joins + wrong world**

That is harder than simple hallucination.

A system can be extremely accurate at the level of fields while being
wrong at the level of meaning.

## 🔄 22. The False Positive Has A Life

A false positive is not merely an incorrect label sitting quietly in a
database.

In a real information environment, classification can become an
intervention.

The exact consequences depend on the actual institution, legal
authority, technical architecture and safeguards involved. They require
evidence before being asserted as fact.

But the design question is unavoidable.

What happens if an ambiguous classification is:

-   retained;
-   joined to additional information;
-   propagated;
-   inherited by another system;
-   repeatedly surfaced during review;
-   treated as a reason for additional scrutiny?

The person may then experience friction.

That friction can change behaviour.

Changed behaviour produces new information.

The system is no longer merely observing the subject.

**It has entered the subject's information ecology.**

The feedback loop can become:

``` text
AMBIGUOUS SIGNAL
      ↓
SUSPICIOUS INTERPRETATION
      ↓
ADDITIONAL SCRUTINY
      ↓
MORE INFORMATION
      ↓
MORE COMPATIBLE FEATURES
      ↓
GREATER CONFIDENCE
      ↓
FRICTION
      ↓
SUBJECT CHANGES BEHAVIOUR
      ↓
NEW OBSERVATIONS
      ↓
SYSTEM INTERPRETS NEW OBSERVATIONS THROUGH EXISTING PRIOR
```

This is why false positives belong inside the security calculation.

## 🕰️ 23. Nobody Knows Tolkien Becomes Tolkien

There is another retrospective cheat we have to remove.

We know the books survive.

We know Tolkien becomes famous.

We know the enormous pile of languages, maps, histories and Ringwraith
notes becomes culturally legible as **literature**.

The system operating before publication does not.

Neither does the institution reviewing him.

Neither does the public.

Neither does the hypothetical civil-liberties defender.

That produces the harder question:

> **Would we defend Tolkien's ability to remain weird before we knew the
> weirdness would become valuable?**

Not:

> Would we protect a beloved world-famous author?

Easy.

Instead:

> Would we protect the unpublished professor producing an unreasonable
> quantity of extremely structured Elf Bullshit?

That is the prospective test.

## 🪞 24. Who Gets The Benefit Of Ambiguity?

This is where biography and history can matter without becoming destiny.

A system does not need an explicit rule saying:

`CATHOLIC = THREAT`

for prejudice to influence an outcome.

Bias can operate at the uncertainty boundary.

Two ambiguous cases can receive different treatment because one feels
more comfortable, respectable, ordinary, trustworthy or legible to the
people and institutions reviewing it.

The important research question is therefore not only:

> **Who gets classified?**

It is:

> **Who gets allowed to stop being unresolved?**

For Tolkien, any serious historical counterfactual needs research into:

-   Catholicism and Oxford;
-   the relatively recent removal of religious tests;
-   British anti-Catholicism;
-   Edith's conversion;
-   intermarriage and sectarian attitudes;
-   Irish and British political violence with careful periodisation;
-   Tolkien's actual political and religious commitments;
-   what contemporaries knew;
-   which institutions mattered;
-   and which prejudices were explicit, implicit, ordinary or contested.

The proposition is not:

> Tolkien was secretly on a modern watchlist before modern watchlists
> existed.

The proposition is:

> **If contemporary classification machinery were transplanted into an
> earlier social order, the biases of that order would become part of
> the classification environment too.**

## 🏝️ 28. It's Our Fucking Shared Culture

The Tolkien case also points outward.

Old English.

Old Norse.

Runes.

Folklore.

Medieval literature.

Archaeology.

Religious history.

Local ecology.

Manuscripts.

Stories.

None of these become the genetic property of whoever can construct the
longest ancestry claim.

Culture belongs to people through participation, custody, stewardship,
argument, transmission, reinterpretation and care.

Someone whose family arrived on these islands two months ago can learn
Old English, care about Lindisfarne, preserve local history and
participate in the culture.

Someone whose family has been here for centuries does not acquire the
right to make that culture racially exclusive.

The emotional formulation is deliberately stronger:

> **If you love this culture, come and fucking have some.**

Learn it.

Translate it.

Preserve it.

Question it.

Argue about it.

Make something new from it.

But:

> **you do not get to make it smaller so that you can call it yours.**

The **our** is the point.

## 🧬 29. Purity Would Dismantle The Thing Being Claimed

Tolkien is useful precisely because cultural synthesis is difficult to
reverse-engineer into pure boxes.

Languages encounter other languages.

Religions encounter older stories.

People migrate.

Texts travel.

Ideas are translated.

Landscapes change.

Wars happen.

Families move.

Scholars read things from elsewhere.

Artists recombine them.

The resulting cultural object is not contaminated by this process.

**The process helped produce the object.**

Trying to remove everything insufficiently native does not reveal a
pristine British culture underneath.

Eventually you discover that you have dismantled British culture.

Culture cultures.

## 🏴 30. When Extremists Want The Same Cultural Material

This produces another classifier problem.

Contemporary racist and far-right movements can deliberately appropriate
medieval, Germanic, Norse or other historical European symbols and
narratives.

That does **not** make the underlying cultural material inherently
extremist.

So:

`RUNE = RACIST`

is bad classification.

But:

`RUNE = JUST CULTURE, IGNORE`

can also be bad classification when an organisation is intentionally
using historical symbolism to construct a racialised political identity.

Again:

> **What is the person's relationship to the thing?**

Researcher?

Artist?

Religious practitioner?

Archaeologist?

Fantasy nerd?

Historian?

Extremist organiser?

The visible symbol is not enough.

The relevant information lies in context, relationships, behaviour,
organisation, stated ideology, mobilisation and the actual harm
hypothesis.

The same precision that protects harmless cultural weirdness is also
required to recognise harmful political use of cultural material.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## ♻️ 31. Did The Classifier Help Build The Correlation?

This is a hypothesis for research, not a settled causal claim.

Suppose a cultural symbol becomes associated with extremism.

Then:

``` text
SYMBOL ACQUIRES EXTREMIST ASSOCIATION
        ↓
HARMLESS USERS EXPERIENCE SUSPICION / SOCIAL COST
        ↓
SOME HARMLESS USERS WITHDRAW FROM VISIBLE USE
        ↓
IDEOLOGICALLY COMMITTED USERS REMAIN HIGHLY MOTIVATED
        ↓
VISIBLE USE BECOMES MORE CONCENTRATED AMONG THEM
        ↓
STATISTICAL ASSOCIATION STRENGTHENS
        ↓
CLASSIFIER APPEARS MORE CORRECT
```

At which point:

> **BRO. YOU MAY HAVE HELPED BUILD THE FUCKING CORRELATION.**

Classification is not always passive description.

It can alter the information environment from which later
classifications are made.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌱 32. The Weird Little Guardians

This is why protecting cultural weirdness is not merely sentimental.

Societies need people who care too much about:

-   manuscripts;
-   old languages;
-   folklore;
-   local history;
-   native plants;
-   vanished species;
-   historic landscapes;
-   traditional practices;
-   archaeological evidence;
-   the difference between attested practice and modern invention;
-   the difference between stewardship and bullshit branding.

These people keep cultural material available for everybody else.

They also contest bad claims about it.

If academics, artists, minorities, religious practitioners,
conservationists and gigantic nerds are pushed away from contested
cultural material while ideological actors remain intensely motivated to
occupy it, the society can lose interpretive capacity.

You do not defend shared culture by abandoning it to whoever shouts
**HERITAGE** the loudest.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌳 33. Custody Is Not Racial Ownership Or Commercial Capture

The same principle applies to ecological inheritance.

A serious conversation about these islands can include:

-   historical woodland;
-   lost species;
-   land use;
-   native flora;
-   conservation;
-   rewilding;
-   traditional European medicine;
-   historical remedies;
-   modern reconstruction;
-   evidence;
-   commercial appropriation;
-   ecological restoration.

That does not require:

> **RELEASE THE WOLVES TOMORROW.**

Nor does it require treating every claimed ancient remedy as medically
valid.

It means these are legitimate questions about inherited landscapes,
practices and knowledge.

There are at least three forms of capture worth watching:

``` text
RACIAL CAPTURE
this belongs to our bloodline

COMMERCIAL CAPTURE
this exists primarily as an exploitable asset

ADMINISTRATIVE ABANDONMENT
nobody values this enough to maintain the knowledge
```

None is stewardship.

A society should possess enough custody of its inheritance to argue
intelligently about what it wants to preserve, restore, change or leave
behind.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📦 34. Britain Has Put Some Things In A Box

Historical conflict belongs in the same information ecology.

Political settlement is not identical to:

-   historical understanding;
-   accountability;
-   memory;
-   trust;
-   emotional resolution;
-   shared narrative.

Britain can be exceptionally talented at:

> **That was terribly difficult.**

Yes.

> **Very complicated.**

Quite.

> **A sensitive period.**

Absolutely.

> **Have we processed it?**

**DO NOT TOUCH THE BOX.**

The Troubles are an obvious place where the project must be careful.

There has been enormous scholarship, testimony, community work,
peacebuilding, political negotiation, memorialisation and argument.

There is no single emotionally satisfying story that can be imposed on
everyone who lived through political violence.

But unresolved historical cleavages do not cease to exist merely because
institutions become uncomfortable discussing them.

Real wounds are information.

They can be exploited.

A resilient information environment needs enough historical capacity to
recognise the wound without requiring permanent reenactment of the
conflict.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🗣️ 35. Don't Silence The People You Need To Hear

Security systems also operate inside democratic legitimacy.

People who criticise their own government, allied governments, security
institutions or democratic failures can also be among the people willing
to criticise:

-   authoritarian states;
-   racist movements;
-   political violence;
-   foreign interference;
-   coercive political projects;
-   attacks on democratic participation.

Those functions are not opposites.

But credibility can be damaged when people experience institutional
mistreatment and are then expected to defend the same institutional
order without qualification.

This creates another research question:

> **Does a democratic security system preserve the independent critical
> voices whose credibility depends partly upon their willingness to
> criticise us too?**

Democratic criticism is not automatically evidence of hostility to
democracy.

Sometimes the ability to criticise your own side is precisely what makes
criticism of somebody else believable.

If a security architecture suppresses the people most willing to argue
publicly, document abuse, contest propaganda and defend democratic norms
in practice, it may weaken capacities it later expects to mobilise.

That is a legitimacy problem.

It is also an information problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🕊️ 36. What Are We Actually Securing?

The answer cannot be only:

`absence of detected threat`

A liberal-democratic security architecture is also operating around:

-   speech;
-   association;
-   political participation;
-   academic inquiry;
-   journalism;
-   religion;
-   art;
-   minority communities;
-   international exchange;
-   cultural inheritance;
-   ecological knowledge;
-   trust;
-   independent criticism;
-   intellectual eccentricity;
-   the ability to produce things nobody yet knows will matter.

False negatives matter.

People should be able to go to the concert without being bombed.

False positives matter too.

People should still be able to **make the fucking concert**.

And somewhere in the same information environment is an unpublished
professor producing a frankly unreasonable quantity of Elf Bullshit.

Nobody knows yet whether it matters.

That is precisely why the test matters.

## 🛠️ 38. What The Nine Ask Us To Build

The Nine do not ask us to stop doing security.

Quite the opposite.

They ask for systems capable of:

-   noticing genuine coordination;
-   preserving raw observations;
-   separating observation from inference;
-   representing unresolved entity types;
-   distinguishing subject matter from subject relationship;
-   selecting appropriate comparison populations;
-   testing culturally ordinary explanations;
-   recognising genuinely concealed meaning;
-   preserving alternative ontologies;
-   recording provenance;
-   requiring disconfirming evidence;
-   allowing confidence to decrease;
-   modelling false-positive consequences;
-   modelling false-negative consequences;
-   noticing when classification changes the environment;
-   accepting allied and multidisciplinary challenge;
-   and saying **we don't know yet** when that is the most accurate
    answer.

The goal is not:

> **stop noticing weird things.**

It is:

> **be precise enough to distinguish the dangerous weird thing from the
> harmless weird person documenting it.**

## 🧪 39. Evidence Discipline

For each future Tolkien case, preserve four passes.

### Pass A --- Detection

What did the system correctly notice?

### Pass B --- Discrimination

What relationship, comparison population or cultural context
distinguishes harmful from harmless explanations?

### Pass C --- Propagation

What happens if the classification remains wrong?

### Pass D --- Ecology

Does the classification alter the environment from which future
information, culture, criticism or threat behaviour emerges?

And preserve the evidence status:

-   **historical fact**
-   **documented security practice**
-   **technical capability**
-   **reasonable inference**
-   **counterfactual**
-   **bullying DARPA with hobbits**

Do not silently promote one into another.

The joke has to survive contact with the evidence.

## 🧭 41. Working Propositions

1.  **A classifier does not need to know what you are in order to
    discriminate against people who resemble you in observable
    dimensions.**

2.  **The dataset can know that a cluster exists without knowing what
    the cluster is.**

3.  **The joins can be correct while the ontology is wrong.**

4.  **Correct extraction can coexist with catastrophic semantic error.**

5.  **The existence of highly structured information is not evidence
    about why the information was structured.**

6.  **Detecting encoded structure is not the same as correctly
    identifying its purpose.**

7.  **The same subject matter does not imply the same relationship to
    the subject matter.**

8.  **Cultural context can change salience without making the situation
    safer.**

9.  **Sometimes expertise contributes not a better answer but a better
    set of possible answers.**

10. **Cultural context can mean choosing the correct comparison
    population before deciding that behaviour is anomalous.**

11. **A suspicious prior can make additional accurate information
    strengthen an inaccurate model.**

12. **The reward can be attached to the wrong abstraction.**

13. **A classification can become part of the information environment it
    claims merely to observe.**

14. **Bias can operate through how much evidence a person must produce
    before uncertainty is allowed to end.**

15. **No ally gets omniscience. Different observer positions are useful
    because their errors are not perfectly identical.**

16. **Shared culture is not genetic property.**

17. **You do not get to make a culture smaller so that you can call it
    yours.**

18. **Classification can potentially change the population distribution
    from which future correlations are learned.**

19. **A society cannot secure an inheritance it has abandoned
    understanding.**

20. **Democratic criticism is part of the information ecology a
    democratic security system is supposed to protect.**

21. **False positives and false negatives are not competing excuses for
    imprecision. Better discrimination reduces both.**

22. **The Ringwraiths can be dangerous and Tolkien can still not be a
    Ringwraith.**

And therefore:

> **Well done, America. The Ringwraiths are indeed extremely fucking
> concerning.**
>
> **Now build me a system good enough to know why the man who invented
> them wasn't.**
