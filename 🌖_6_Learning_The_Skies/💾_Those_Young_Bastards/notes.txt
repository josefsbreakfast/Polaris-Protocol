Yes. Before we disappear into individual systems, I’d build a small conceptual runway so the reader acquires the vocabulary for everything else.

For 🌖_6_Learning_The_Skies/💾_Those_Young_Bastards/, I think the starting map wants to look roughly like this:

Node	Job
README.md	Start here. Tech is an ecology, not a monolith; you do not need to be an engineer to understand its power structures.
🏘️_welcome_to_anatevka.md	The Fiddler on the Roof model. Butcher, blacksmith, matchmaker, rabbi: capability, dependency, dislike and cooperation can coexist.
🕸️_capability_maps_are_not_conspiracy_maps.md	The foundational distinction: mapping what a person/network could provide tells us about structure, not whether any particular capability was exercised.
🧰_the_boys_were_left_unattended_with_tools.md	Competitors fuck with competitors. Technical capability expands the repertoire; cybersecurity exists partly because this is utterly predictable behaviour.
💻_big_tech_is_not_one_fucking_computer.md	Break apart “Big Tech”: different firms, architectures, generations, markets, cultures, interests and dependencies.
🌉_the_people_who_speak_both_languages.md	Bridge figures. Why people who understand two otherwise alien networks acquire unusual routing value.
🔭_who_no_longer_needs_whom.md	The central diagnostic. Autonomy, dependency, disintermediation, gatekeeping and why losing the ability to say no matters more than simple wealth.
👴_those_young_bastards.md	Incumbent recognition of trajectory: sometimes the alarming thing isn’t how powerful the newcomers are, but recognising how they are becoming powerful.
📸_the_photo_where_you_can_see_it.md	The 2007 PayPal Mafia spread as the first major teaching object: a public visibility marker, not magical causal T₀. What can different observers reasonably see in the same photograph?
💰_meritocracy_until_it_fucking_works.md	“Anyone can succeed” meets autonomous capital formation, technical competence, networks and people who stop needing established permission structures.
⚙️_system_interest_is_not_incumbent_interest.md	Why something beneficial to an economy, industry, country or technology ecosystem can simultaneously threaten actors occupying privileged positions inside it.
🧠_billionaires_are_still_mammals.md	Money doesn’t install different cognitive software. Status anxiety, rivalry, loyalty, gossip, attraction, ageing, envy and embarrassment remain causal variables.
🪚_impossible_difficult_or_just_not_how_we_do_it.md	Technical literacy for non-technologists: distinguish impossible / infeasible / expensive / risky / unfamiliar / institutionally prohibited / “Dave says no.”
💩_the_boys_have_thrown_poo_everywhere.md	Tech’s credibility problem: decades of competitors describing one another as dangerous, monopolistic, insecure and incompetent have consequences for public trust.
🌌_you_do_not_need_to_understand_every_tree.md	Exit ramp into the deeper systems material: enough technical/network literacy to see the wood without knowing every tree.

💸_paypal_mafia_as_network.md
What resources existed across the cohort? Capital, code, entrepreneurship, venture finance, trust, recruitment, emerging platforms, etc.

🕸️_epstein_network_as_capability_map.md
Forget the grand unified conspiracy. What capabilities are publicly observable across that network? Brokerage, finance, law, politics, academia, technology, reputation, social access, information, etc. Where were its obvious strengths and limits?

🌉_when_two_villages_meet.md
This is where our Thiel/Epstein thought experiment belongs. Don’t assert hidden intentions. Ask which people would structurally be capable of understanding the value proposition of the other network.

📸_fortune_2007_as_visibility_event.md
The fun one. Different readers open exactly the same magazine:

ordinary reader: internet millionaires
investor: deal flow
recruiter: talent
politician: future donors/infrastructure
incumbent: competition
broker: new relationship graph
older entrepreneur: oh fuck, I recognise the trajectory

And importantly, none of these interpretations has to exclude the others.

I think there are three concepts we should teach before anything Epstein-related

Capability ≠ deployment.
Relationship ≠ agreement.
System ≠ conspiracy.

If those three are already installed in the reader’s head, we don’t subsequently have to spend seventeen paragraphs going “THIS DOES NOT MEAN BILL GATES SECRETLY CONTROLLED THE BLACKSMITH.”

They know.

Then we can actually have fun.

And I think 🔭_who_no_longer_needs_whom.md should probably be the conceptual heart of the entire folder. That’s the question that connects PayPal, Epstein, Microsoft, Apple, venture capital, DEI, incumbent preservation, infrastructure, sanctions, the fucking bonds, and eventually ordinary people’s relationship to technological power without pretending they’re all manifestations of one phenomenon.

It asks something much simpler:

What can I do without you?

What can you prevent me doing without your cooperation?

What happens when that balance changes?

That’s systems literacy in three questions.
