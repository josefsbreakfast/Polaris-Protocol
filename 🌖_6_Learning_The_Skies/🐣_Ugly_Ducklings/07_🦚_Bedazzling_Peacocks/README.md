# 🦚 Bedazzling Peacocks
**First created:** 2026-08-23 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-23  
*Concentrated attribution, hidden supply chains, receiver ignorance, optimisation after adequacy, and the rather obvious question of whether the peacock needed bedazzling at all.*

>
> They had been arranged around somebody else.
>
> The wearer already had feathers of his own.
>
> Quite good ones, actually.
>
> A Keeper stood beside him looking extremely pleased.
>
> “Isn't he magnificent?”

## 🛰️ Orientation

The crude answer would be:

> The peacock is fake.

That is usually wrong and analytically lazy.

The receiver may already be intelligent, skilled, charismatic, creative, experienced, hardworking, strategically capable, well connected, or genuinely exceptional.

The system may be augmenting a person who was already doing perfectly well.

That changes the question.

> **Why are you plucking ducklings to bedazzle a fucking peacock?**

## 🎭 Distributed Production, Concentrated Attribution

Modern institutions legitimately compress many people's work into visible performance.

Leaders have staff. Writers have editors. Ministers have civil servants. Commanders receive intelligence. Researchers collaborate.

The problem is not collective production.

It is hidden exploitative provenance inside collective production.

## 🪶 The Suit Is Composite

```text
Source A ─┐
Source B ─┼→ selection / synthesis ─┐
Source C ─┘                         ├→ role augmentation
receiver's own capacities ─────────┘
```

The suit can combine many sources and substantial genuine capacity from the wearer.

This is tailoring, not cloning.

## 🪑 Sometimes The Peacock Is A Chair

The optimisation target may be a role.

The current occupant determines the tailoring.

That can make the process look intensely personal while the organisational objective remains role performance.

## ✨ The Superfluous Sparkle Problem

Optimisation systems do not necessarily contain an internal instruction saying:

> good enough; stop.

They can continue:

```text
excellent
→ optimise

more excellent
→ optimise

exceptional
→ optimise

marginal gain still measurable
→ optimise again
```

If the cost of improvement is externalised onto somebody else, the optimisation target can keep improving while the system never asks whether the next increment is proportionate.

This is the central problem of **Bedazzling Peacocks**.

```text
already capable peacock
+ ordinary legitimate support
= magnificent peacock

already capable peacock
+ opaque extracted augmentation
= marginally shinier peacock
+ harmed source
+ provenance risk
+ dependency risk
+ reputational risk
+ institutional liability
```

> **Value created is not the same thing as value justified.**

## 🦚 The Peacock May Not Have Ordered The Suit

Receiving benefit does not establish knowledge of provenance.

A receiver may experience unusually apt briefings, introductions, recommendations, polished support, or repeated steering and simply think:

> **Why are people being weird with me?**

Keep separate:

```text
receives benefit
≠ knows provenance

knows provenance
≠ understands apparatus

understands apparatus
≠ consented to it

consented to support
≠ consented to exploitative sourcing

benefits from system
≠ controls system
```

The receiver may therefore be beneficiary, participant, manipulated person, or some combination of these at different times.

## 🧿 The Receiver Needs Assurance, Not A Moral Guess

A receiver cannot inspect a hidden supply chain merely by being more virtuous.

A legitimate organisation should provide assurance about the provenance of material supplied to senior or high-leverage roles.

That can require:
- accountable commissioning,
- provenance controls,
- auditable transformation chains,
- conflict-of-interest controls,
- protected internal challenge,
- independent review,
- and a route for the receiver to say:

> **I did not order this. Find out where it came from.**

without having to destroy their own career to ask the question.

## 🪤 The Magnificent Cage

Hidden augmentation can also harm the receiver.

It may:
- create dependency,
- contaminate legitimate achievement,
- obscure genuine weaknesses that would be better addressed directly,
- create reputational exposure,
- entangle the receiver in harm they did not consent to,
- and give intermediaries leverage over them.

The discovery may therefore be not:

> I am a fraud.

but:

> **Why the fuck did you do that? I was fine.**

## 📈 Prestige Is A Feedback System

```text
augmentation
→ better performance
→ greater prestige
→ more authority
→ more resources
→ more access to augmentation
```

Meanwhile the source can experience reduced recognition and bargaining power.

The resulting asymmetry can widen even if the original input was small.

That is why downstream value and proportionality both matter.

## 🚫 Leave The Peacock Alone

There are legitimate ways to support people: good teams, transparent research, coaching, therapy where appropriate, training, editors, analysts, advisers, mentoring, and professional development.

But the first question is not always how to replace an exploitative augmentation system with a nicer augmentation system.

Sometimes the correct intervention is:

> **none.**

The peacock may already be capable.

The system may be optimising because optimisation is what the system has been built and paid to do.

That is not the same thing as need.

## 🌘 The Duckling Looks Away

The peacock really was magnificent.

Some of the feathers were his.

Some were not.

He may not have known the difference.

The duckling stopped asking whether she wanted a suit.

Something else was missing.

And somewhere beyond the loading bay, a mother was looking for her child.

## 🏮 Footer

*Bedazzling Peacocks* examines how distributed inputs can concentrate prestige around a visible person or role without assuming the receiver is fake, informed, consenting, or even in need of augmentation. Its central proportionality question is whether harm to a vulnerable source can ever be justified by making an already capable receiver marginally shinier.

*Survivor authorship is sovereign. Containment is never neutral.*
