# 🦚 ਫੁੱਲਾਂ ਦੇ ਪੈਂਦੇ / Phullā̃ De Painde / The Flowers Around His Neck
**First created:** 2026-08-28 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-30  
*An interior-perspective model of regrown plumage, multiple embodiment, selective display, relational power, model lag, and what changes when the classifier is no longer the cultural centre.*

## 🛰️ Orientation — The Whole Bird

There is a point in the Ugly Duckling story after the feathers begin to grow back.

The bird has not merely survived.

It has acquired plumage.

That creates a new problem.

Having feathers is not the same thing as knowing what they mean.

Knowing what they mean is not the same thing as wanting everybody to see them.

And wanting somebody, specifically, to see them is not the same thing as surrendering ownership of them.

This node uses Diljit Dosanjh's **ਮੋਰਨੀ / Morni / Peahen** as a cultural and relational rose for thinking about that problem.

The song is not being treated as a deposition, diagnostic instrument, or universal account of Punjabi masculinity, South Asian femininity, Sikh identity, Hindu philosophy, romance, danger, or attraction.

It is a song.

It is also a very good fucking song.

And songs can carry conceptual structures that become useful elsewhere.

The important operator here is:

# **AND**

```text
swagger
AND femininity

defence
AND beauty

arrogance
AND vulnerability

displaying
AND being displayed for

Peacock
AND Peahen
```

The older Swan model asks what happens when a living organism outgrows inherited representations.

*Morni* adds another question:

> **What happens when the organism discovers that apparently contradictory aspects were never mutually exclusive in the first place?**

The answer is not:

> woman becomes feminine after she stops doing the swagger.

It is:

> **she discovers she was the Morni while doing the swagger.**

Caution.

Swans aggressive.

Peacocks displaying.

## 🦚 ਮੋਰਨੀ / Morni / Peahen

The dictionary answer is simple.

```text
ਮੋਰਨੀ
morni
peahen
```

The relational answer is richer.

Within the song, **ਮੋਰਨੀ / morni** is repeatedly rendered in the English translation as **princess**.

That is not a literal zoological translation.

It does, however, catch something useful about the position the woman occupies inside the courtship grammar.

For this node:

```text
ਮੋਰਨੀ
morni
peahen

symbolically:
princess

longer relational gloss:
the Peahen for whom the Peacock
is prepared to put the whole fucking plumage on display
```

That last line matters.

The Peacock is not merely a powerful male object standing beside a beautiful female object.

The Peacock is performing.

Displaying.

Expending.

Making his condition visible.

The Peahen is partly made legible through what happens to the Peacock around her.

And for a woman listening, the positions do not have to remain neatly gender-segregated.

She can inhabit the masculine-coded swagger **from inside the song**.

She can also hear herself addressed as the Morni.

She does not have to choose which bird she is.

## 🔫 ਤੇਰੀ ਅੱਖ ਨੇ ਕਰਾ ਤੇ ਕਾਰੇ / Teri Ankh Ne Kara Te Kaare / Your Look Caused Trouble

The song makes the collision immediately.

> **ਕਿ 40 ਦਾ Glock ਫੜਿਆ, ਤੇਰੀ ਅੱਖ ਨੇ ਕਰਾ ਤੇ ਕਾਰੇ, ਮਾਂ ਦੀਏ ਮੋਰਨੀ ਐ**  
> *ki 40 da Glock fadheya, teri ankh ne kara te kaare, maa diye morni ae*  
> **Holding a .40 Glock, your look caused trouble, princess.**

The two pieces of information are allowed to coexist.

```text
Glock
AND
Morni

weapon
AND
gaze

swagger
AND
disruption
```

For a female listener, that creates a particularly interesting double identification.

She can hear the masculine-coded display and go:

> **Fuck yes. Bail. Flowers. Boys. Look at us.**

AND simultaneously receive:

> **ਤੇਰੀ ਅੱਖ ਨੇ ਕਰਾ ਤੇ ਕਾਰੇ**  
> *teri ankh ne kara te kaare*  
> **your look caused trouble**

That is different from merely being:

> the woman a dangerous man finds attractive.

She is participating imaginatively in the swagger and then being told that her own presence has disruptive force **inside precisely that swaggering world**.

Hence:

> Oh.
>
> My look did what?
>
> Oh right.
>
> **You REALLY like this.**

That is the first major update.

Not:

> I have discovered that men desire women.

Not even:

> I have discovered that I am desirable.

More interestingly:

> **I thought these aspects competed with each other. Apparently you don't.**

## 💐 ਫੁੱਲਾਂ ਦੇ ਪੈਂਦੇ / Phullā̃ De Painde / The Flowers Around His Neck

This is where the title comes from.

> **ਹੋ ਨਿਕਲਿਆ ਜੱਟ ਬੇਲ ਤੇ, ਹਾਰ ਗਲ 'ਚ ਫੁੱਲਾਂ ਦੇ ਪੈਂਦੇ, ਬਦਲੇ ਜੱਟਾਂ ਦੇ ਪੁੱਤ ਨੀ**  
> *ho nikleya jatt bail te, haar gal 'ch phullan de painde, badle jattan de putt ni*  
> **This Jatt got out on bail, with flower garlands around his neck, the sons of the Jatts take revenge.**

Then:

> **ਗਿਣ ਗਿਣ ਕੇ, ਸੋਹਣੀਏ, ਲੈਂਦੇ, ਕਿ 40 ਦਾ Glock ਫੜਿਆ**  
> *gin gin ke, sohniye, lainde, ki 40 da Glock fadheya*  
> **We're counting one by one, beautiful, we take revenge, holding a .40 Glock.**

Then again:

> **ਕਿ 40 ਦਾ Glock ਫੜਿਆ, ਤੇਰੀ ਅੱਖ ਨੇ ਕਰਾ ਤੇ ਕਾਰੇ, ਮਾਂ ਦੀਏ ਮੋਰਨੀ ਐ**  
> *ki 40 da Glock fadheya, teri ankh ne kara te kaare, maa diye morni ae*  
> **Holding a .40 Glock, your look caused trouble, princess.**

For a reader unfamiliar with South Asian visual vocabularies, the garland matters.

Flower garlands can function as forms of welcome, honour, celebration, ceremonial reception, and public recognition. In the video's own staging, the man emerging from the bail/court sequence into garlands reads as a triumphant return to social life.

The analytical point is not that South Asian cultures possess one universal rule about garlanding.

It is what **this song and video do with the image**.

```text
bail
AND
flowers

trouble
AND
welcome

revenge remains in the vocabulary
AND
celebration remains available
```

There is no theoretical denunciation first.

No compulsory purification ritual before somebody is allowed back into the fucking huddle.

The flowers do not erase the bail.

The bail does not make the flowers meaningless.

Both pieces of information remain attached to the same organism.

That is already an information model.

## 🔥 Nobody Asked The Peacock To Denounce His Feathers

Before the cybernetics:

the sear works.

The swagger is enjoyable.

That is allowed to be first-order information.

A woman can enjoy masculine-coded potency without thereby consenting to masculine domination.

She can enjoy:

- bravado,
- danger as aesthetic vocabulary,
- communal swagger,
- display,
- money,
- clothes,
- boys being ridiculous together,
- somebody coming out of court with flowers around his neck,
- and the sheer theatrical pleasure of **LOOK AT US**.

The analytical response does not have to be:

> first remove every morally difficult element from the fantasy, then permission to enjoy the remainder may be considered.

Nor does feminine power require the masculine figure to be symbolically castrated before it becomes safe to acknowledge her.

The song does something more interesting.

It leaves the Peacock's feathers on.

Then it lets the Morni affect him anyway.

## 🪷 Aspects — AND, Not Cancellation

A comparative religious vocabulary can be useful here, provided it is kept properly bounded.

Diljit Dosanjh is Sikh.

This node is **not** claiming that *Morni* is an encoded work of Hindu theology, that its artists intended a Shiva–Shakti reading, or that South Asian religious traditions can be flattened into one interchangeable symbolic system.

They cannot.

“Hinduism believes X” is usually inadequate language for traditions containing enormous internal diversity of philosophy, deity, ritual, region, lineage, practice, and interpretation.

The comparison belongs to the **listener's reception**.

Shiva–Shakti offers one vocabulary for thinking about differentiated masculine and feminine potency in relation without requiring one to annihilate the other.

The Chamunda thought works differently.

It is not:

> Chanda + Munda = united masculine/feminine deity.

Rather, fierce-goddess traditions can provide a useful vocabulary of **aspect and embodiment**: the feminine divine does not have to collapse into one affective presentation in order to remain feminine.

Tenderness does not invalidate ferocity.

Beauty does not invalidate defensive capacity.

Sexuality does not invalidate sovereignty.

One form does not have to expose another as counterfeit.

The useful operator remains:

# **AND**

```text
swagger AND tenderness
masculine-coded identification AND feminine embodiment
beauty AND defensive capacity
arrogance AND vulnerability
being admired AND remaining sovereign
```

A classifier that insists these must be separate organisms may simply have an impoverished state space.

## 🦚 Fuck Yes. Look At Us.

The collective swagger matters too.

The song does not give us only one man and one woman floating in a romantic vacuum.

> **ਉਹ ਜੱਟ ਤੋਂ ਨਾ ਬਿਨਾਂ ਪੁੱਛਿਆ, ਕਦੇ hood 'ਚ ਉੱਡੇ ਨਾ ਪੰਛੀ, ਦੁਸਾਂਝਾਂ ਆਲਾ ਨਾਲ ਖੜ੍ਹੇ ਆ**  
> *oh jatt ton na bina pucheya, kade hood 'ch udde na panchi, dosanjhan aala naal khadhe aa*  
> **Without asking the Jatt, no bird flies in the hood, Dosanjh is by your side.**

Then:

> **ਤੇਰਾ ਚੰਨੀ ਨੱਤਾਂ ਕਰੇ ਸਰਪੰਚੀ, ਨੀ Bagatelle bag ਫੂਕਤਾ**  
> *tera channi nattan kare sarpanchi, ni Bagatelle bag phookta*  
> **Channi Nattan is the leader, we dropped some cash on a Bagatelle bag.**

And:

> **ਨੀ Bagatelle bag ਫੂਕਤਾ, ਤੇਰੀਆਂ ਸਹੇਲੀਆਂ ਨੂੰ ਕਰਨੀ ਆ ਰੋਟੀ, ਨੀ ਪੀੜ੍ਹੀਆਂ 'ਚ ਖਾਊ ਬੱਲੀਏ**  
> *ni Bagatelle bag phookta, teriyan saheliyan nu karni aa roti, ni peedhiyan 'ch khaau balliye*  
> **We dropped some cash on a Bagatelle bag, we're gonna buy your friends dinner, generations will live off this, baby.**

Then:

> **ਕਰੀ ਜੱਟ ਨੇ ਕਮਾਈ ਬੜੀ ਮੋਟੀ, ਖ਼ਬਰਾਂ 'ਚ ਯਾਰ ਆ ਗਿਆ**  
> *kari jatt ne kamai badi moti, khabran 'ch yaar aa gaya*  
> **The Jatt made a lot of cash, our homie is in the news.**

And:

> **ਖ਼ਬਰਾਂ 'ਚ ਯਾਰ ਆ ਗਿਆ, ਬੋਲ ਬੋਲਦਾ ਐ ਜੱਟੀਏ ਕਰਾਰੇ, ਮਾਂ ਦੀ ਮੋਰਨੀ ਐ**  
> *khabran 'ch yaar aa gaya, bol bolda ae jattiye karare, maa di morni ae*  
> **Our homie is in the news, we speak sharp words, girl, princess.**

The exact grammatical and social positions matter.

Jatt.

Dosanjh.

Channi Nattan.

We.

Friends.

Generations.

Morni.

The song's masculine world is collective.

There is status, provision, leadership, notoriety, money, friendship, continuity and somebody standing beside somebody else.

The swagger therefore contains something beyond individual dominance:

```text
we have each other
we have resources
we can provide
we have continuity
we take up space
we are fucking here
```

A female listener can imaginatively occupy **that we** too.

She is not required to stand outside Punjabi masculinity and observe it solely as the female object of its courtship.

She can go:

> **Fuck yes. Look at us.**

And then the song turns toward her.

That is the double position.

## 🌹 ਕੇਸ ਜਿੱਤ ਗਏ, ਤੇਰੇ ਤੋਂ ਦਿਲ ਹਾਰੇ / Case Jitt Gaye, Tere Ton Dil Haare / We Won The Cases, But Lost Our Hearts To You

Then comes the refrain.

> **ਮੋਰਨੀ ਐ, ਕੇਸ ਜਿੱਤ ਗਏ ਤੇਰੇ ਤੋਂ ਦਿਲ ਹਾਰੇ, ਮਾਂ ਦੀਏ ਮੋਰਨੀ ਐ**  
> *morni ae, case jitt gaye tere ton dil haare, maa diye morni ae*  
> **Beautiful, we won the battle, but we lost our hearts to you, princess.**

Again:

> **ਕੇਸ ਜਿੱਤ ਗਏ ਤੇਰੇ ਤੋਂ ਦਿਲ ਹਾਰੇ**  
> *case jitt gaye tere ton dil haare*  
> **We won the battle, but we lost our hearts to you.**

The English source renders **ਕੇਸ / case** here as *battle* in this line, while the word and surrounding imagery also permit the obvious resonance with *cases*.

That ambiguity is worth leaving visible rather than forcing the Punjabi through one English aperture.

The important thing is not merely:

> tough man secretly has feelings.

The song has already established that the swagger is real.

His ordinary vocabulary contains winning.

Cases.

Money.

Authority.

Revenge.

Public notoriety.

Capacity.

The courtship turn does not reveal all of that to have been fraudulent.

It says that **winning is not the appropriate operation here**.

He can remain formidable and still lose his heart.

She does not need to defeat him by force.

He does not need to become harmless for her to possess relational power.

Another aspect becomes available.

```text
formidable
AND
vulnerable
```

## 🖤 Piece At My Side, Love Happening

The song makes the coexistence even less subtle.

> **ਹੋ ਸਾਈਡ ਉੱਤੇ piece ਲੱਗਿਆ, ਚਾੜ੍ਹੇ ਜੱਟੀਏ track suit ਕਾਲੇ, ਤੇਰੇ ਨਾਲ ਪਿਆਰ ਚੱਲਦਾ**  
> *ho side utte piece lageya, chaadhe jattiye track suit kaale, tere naal pyaar chalda*  
> **My gun is on my side, but close to that girl in a black tracksuit, our love is happening.**

It does not give us:

```text
weapon
→ moral purification
→ weapon disappears
→ romance may begin
```

It gives:

```text
piece at my side
AND
girl in black tracksuit
AND
love happening
```

The coexistence is the information.

This is why *Morni* works so well as a rose for high-information organisms.

One state does not exhaust the person.

One context does not exhaust the state.

One relationship does not delete the rest of the social world.

The song is not frightened of holding more than one thing at once.

## 🪶 The Feathers Cost Something

This matters particularly inside **Ugly Ducklings**.

A bird can lose feathers.

A bird can have feathers plucked from it.

A bird can learn that conspicuous plumage attracts the wrong kind of attention.

A bird can regrow them.

Those are not psychologically equivalent states.

If the plumage has been hard-won, regrowing it may make it **more precious**, not easier to expose.

There is therefore a developmental difference between:

```text
I have feathers
```

and:

```text
I am opening them
```

The existing Swan vocabulary contains:

> **PLEASE FUCKING UNDERSTAND ME.**

and:

> **I KNOW WHAT I AM AND I WILL NOT LET YOU DEFINE ME.**

*Morni* adds a third possibility:

> **I WOULD LIKE YOU, SPECIFICALLY, TO SEE ME.**

That is selective display.

Not compulsory visibility.

Not:

> I have healed, therefore everybody is entitled to my interior.

Not:

> I have become beautiful, therefore observation is harmless.

Rather:

> **these feathers are mine.**
>
> **I know what they cost.**
>
> **I decide when they open.**
>
> **And sometimes I would very much like you to look.**

The Peacock metaphor becomes especially useful here.

Courtship display is not the abandonment of power.

Display itself can be an expenditure.

A willingness to put the whole fucking tail out can communicate:

> **you are worth the risk of being seen trying.**

And from the Peahen position:

> Wait.
>
> You're showing me **all of that**?
>
> For me?
>
> Oh.
>
> **OH.**

Regrowing the feathers is one achievement.

Learning that one may choose when, where, and for whom to display them is another.

## 👀 Oh. You Like This?

Only now does the model-lag problem properly enter.

Imagine a person who has repeatedly received feedback that some combination of her characteristics is:

```text
too loud
too arrogant
too masculine
too sexual
too difficult
too conspicuous
too much
```

Those responses become data.

Repeated data become priors.

Then another environment encounters substantially the same organism and produces:

```text
holy shit
```

That is not merely flattering information.

It is **model-breaking information**.

```text
same organism
same characteristic

observer / environment A:
TOO MUCH

observer / environment B:
HOLY SHIT

therefore:
the classifier was not universal
```

The update is not:

> I was ugly and now I am desirable.

Nor:

> every instance of attention must therefore be admiration.

It is:

> **the social valuation attached to this aspect of me was never universal.**

That can take time to incorporate.

Negative power may be cognitively cheaper to believe.

If attention has historically predicted correction, punishment, ridicule, danger, extraction, or evidence that one has once again done something wrong, then:

> I frighten them.

may fit the inherited model more cheaply than:

> They appear to really fucking like this.

Positive information can produce model error too.

The task is not more flattering classification.

It is better classification.

## 🪞 The Swan Has A Ledger Too

Institutions carry stale representations.

Cultures carry stale representations.

Other people carry stale representations.

The Swan can carry them too.

A self-model is still a model.

It can contain:

- old environments,
- old punishments,
- old mirrors,
- old hierarchies,
- old attempts to survive,
- and old conclusions that were once locally rational.

That does not make the ledger stupid.

It makes it historical.

The problem begins when an inherited representation quietly becomes more authoritative than current evidence.

```text
old environment
→ repeated feedback
→ internal model
→ new environment
→ anomalous response
→ model resists update
```

Sometimes the correct response is to retain the old warning.

Sometimes the new environment is genuinely safer.

Sometimes the attention is neutral.

Sometimes it is hostile.

Sometimes it is curiosity.

Sometimes it is recognition.

Sometimes it is admiration.

Sometimes it is attraction.

Sometimes there is not enough information yet.

The point is not:

> assume flowers.

It is:

> **become capable of recognising flowers when the evidence actually supports flowers.**

## 🦢 Caution Swans Aggressive

A Swan that has repeatedly been misclassified may become increasingly forceful about classification.

This can look disproportionate if the observer begins the story at iteration thirty-seven.

```text
quiet correction
→ ignored

clearer correction
→ ignored

strong correction
→ pathologised

stronger correction
→ entered as evidence
   that original adverse classification was correct
```

That loop is dangerous.

The correction signal becomes evidence against the correcting organism.

The more urgently the Swan attempts to communicate:

> **THAT IS NOT WHAT I AM**

the more the system may record:

> unusually reactive Swan.

The ecology disappears.

The behaviour remains.

Caution.

Swans aggressive.

## 📣 Explaining Yourself Harder

There is a related failure mode.

If a person has repeatedly been misunderstood, she may respond by increasing resolution.

More words.

More context.

More examples.

More explicit distinctions.

More plumage.

Sometimes this works.

Sometimes the receiver has already classified the additional signal as part of the problem.

```text
misclassification
→ increased explanation
→ explanation classified as excess
→ excess used to reinforce misclassification
→ still more explanation
```

That is not proof that every explanation is correct.

It is a warning about recursive systems.

And one distinction matters here:

**swagger is not automatically a symptom.**

It may perform defensive work.

It may also be:

- joy,
- personality,
- humour,
- style,
- culture,
- gender play,
- aesthetic pleasure,
- confidence,
- or simply enjoying one's own fucking feathers.

Do not pathologise the Peacock merely because the Swan sometimes uses plumage defensively.

## 🧫 Behavioural Ecology

Behaviour is produced inside environments.

Change the environment and the same organism may produce different behaviour.

This is elementary behavioural ecology and surprisingly easy to forget in administrative systems.

A person may become:

- guarded in one room,
- funny in another,
- combative under coercion,
- expansive among trusted people,
- silent under observation,
- flamboyant where flamboyance is welcomed,
- and tender where tenderness is safe.

The correct inference is not that one state must be the authentic person and the others disguises.

A high-information organism may have a large state space.

The governance problem begins when one local state is captured, detached from the conditions that produced it, and treated as the organism's timeless essence.

## 🍶 Fermentation Is Local Optimisation

A system can become very good at producing the wrong thing.

That is fermentation.

A local environment rewards one behaviour.

The behaviour becomes adaptive.

The environment then changes.

The adaptation remains.

```text
environment A
→ behaviour X is useful
→ X strengthens

environment B
→ X is expensive
→ organism still predicts A
```

This is not peculiar to individuals.

Institutions do it.

Cultures do it.

Models do it.

States do it.

Classifiers do it.

The fact that a representation was once useful does not establish that it remains proportionate.

The fact that an adaptation once kept an organism safe does not establish that it should govern every later room.

But neither should the later room demand that the organism forget why the adaptation existed.

L'Chaim is not amnesia.

It is life with a larger state space.

## 🗃️ The Box Has A History

Classification systems are not culturally empty.

Categories emerge from particular histories, institutions, professions, languages, political arrangements, commercial incentives, legal structures, and administrative needs.

That does not make every category illegitimate.

It means categories have provenance.

A system can therefore make two errors at once:

```text
reduce a living organism into a useful box

AND

forget that the box itself
was made somewhere
by somebody
for something
```

The box can then begin masquerading as nature.

This matters particularly where the classified population has historically had less power to define the categories through which it was represented.

A classified population acquiring the ability to describe itself changes the information environment.

Not because self-description is automatically infallible.

Not because every external classification is malicious.

Not because every historical hierarchy persists unchanged.

Because the classifier is no longer the only participant producing information about the classified.

That changes the feedback loop.

## 🌍 The Classifier Is Not The Cultural Centre

This is where the personal and cultural problems converge.

At the individual scale:

> **this characteristic was not universally too much.**

At the cultural scale:

> **this symbolic vocabulary does not need the dominant classifier's permission to signify.**

Marginalised cultures are often asked to become legible by becoming easier to translate into dominant categories.

The alternative is not isolation.

It is **plural legibility**.

A person, community, or culture can enter a shared environment without accepting that the dominant participant possesses automatic epistemic supremacy.

Diljit Dosanjh's international and North American success is useful to the node precisely because Punjabi cultural material can occupy enormous stages without first becoming culturally smaller.

**ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਆ ਗਏ ਓਏ.**

Punjabi arrived.

The stadium can be enormous.

It does not therefore have to remain the interpretive centre.

And the same principle applies on the page.

This node does not erase:

> **ਤੇਰੀ ਅੱਖ ਨੇ ਕਰਾ ਤੇ ਕਾਰੇ**

and replace it with:

> *your look caused trouble*

as though the English sentence were the object and the Punjabi merely its inconvenient source code.

The reader gets:

```text
Gurmukhi
↓
Latin transliteration
↓
English translation
```

The English is an access layer.

It is not the original object.

Readers who do not know Punjabi are allowed not to know Punjabi.

They are also allowed to encounter a language they do not know and move toward it.

The language does not have to disappear before the reader is permitted to continue.

That is not an obstacle to the node's argument.

It is part of it.

Not:

That works at cultural scale.

It also works for the Swan.

## 🦚 The Peacock Is Not An Apology

The adult Swan can therefore appear to contain contradictory instructions.

```text
please see me accurately

AND

look at my fucking feathers
```

They are not necessarily contradictory.

The vulnerability says:

> Being inaccurately perceived has had consequences.

The arrogance says:

> Your previous authority to classify me does not make your classification correct.

And the Peacock adds:

```text
these feathers are mine

I know what they cost

I decide when they open

AND

sometimes I would very much
like you to look
```

That is not the same thing as universal availability.

It is not compulsory visibility.

It is not:

> everybody who notices me acquires interpretive rights over me.

It is selective relational display.

The former Ugly Duckling can therefore have several developmental tasks:

```text
survive the plucking

regrow the feathers

recognise the feathers as hers

learn what different environments do with them

stop treating one classifier as universal

choose when to display

learn that being seen can sometimes be pleasurable

retain the right to close the fucking tail
```

The endpoint is not better armour.

It is greater freedom over state.

## 🪶 High-Information Organisms

Extractive classification tends to work by reduction.

All models reduce.

The governance question is what happens when the reduction becomes more authoritative than the thing being reduced.

*Morni* offers another possibility inside the rose.

He does not have to become powerless for her to have power.

She does not have to become harmless for him to feel safe.

He can be dangerous in one context and vulnerable in another.

She can identify with swagger and remain feminine.

She can be vulnerable in one context and formidable in another.

The collective can remain present while courtship occurs.

Affection does not require social isolation.

Display does not require surrender.

Neither state exhausts the person.

Neither person needs to become the other's database entry.

Two complicated subjects can coexist.

Several complicated subjects can huddle.

This sounds obvious.

Many systems are surprisingly bad at it.

## 🛠️ Diagnostic Questions

When apparently contradictory Swan behaviour appears, ask:

- What does the person think attention predicts?
- Which parts of that prediction were previously adaptive?
- Is attention independently established, merely perceived, or still ambiguous?
- What evidence distinguishes threat, neutrality, curiosity, recognition, admiration, affection, attraction, or another explanation?
- Is the observer treating one aspect as the person's “real” state and the others as contradictions?
- Has behaviour that may be pleasurable, cultural, expressive, humorous, or aesthetic been explained away as purely defensive?
- Is swagger being mistaken for pathology because it is unfamiliar, gender-nonconforming, culturally unfamiliar, or inconvenient?
- Is a relational response being treated as information solely about the person receiving it?
- Is the behaviour of the Peacock being treated as atmospheric background rather than data?
- Who is actually being asked to account for their conduct?
- Is the current behaviour an initial signal or iteration thirty-seven?
- Were quieter correction attempts available, heard, recorded, or acted upon?
- Has increased signalling itself been incorporated into an adverse classification?
- Is an inherited representation being treated as more authoritative than current evidence?
- Does the person have access to the information required to understand why others are responding to them?
- Are possible positive social effects being rejected because an older negative model cannot easily represent them?
- Are possible positive interpretations being assumed without sufficient evidence?
- Is the system optimising a proxy whose improvement can coexist with deterioration in human outcomes?
- Which categories have historical or cultural assumptions embedded within them?
- Has translation preserved the original object, or replaced it?
- Is the classifier confusing its own cultural unfamiliarity with absence of meaning?
- Can the classified person contest the box?
- Can the box itself be changed?
- Who is responsible for noticing when the living bird has outgrown the model?
