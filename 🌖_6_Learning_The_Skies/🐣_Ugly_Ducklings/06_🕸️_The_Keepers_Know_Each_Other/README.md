## 🕸️ Network Proximity Is Not Coordination

Specialised ecosystems contain dense networks of founders, investors, directors, advisers, contractors, former officials, researchers, vendors, customers, and mobile personnel.

Useful maps distinguish:

```text
same industry
↓
professional proximity
↓
shared personnel / investor / adviser
↓
formal commercial relationship
↓
technical integration
↓
documented information exchange
↓
documented coordination
```

Do not collapse the levels.

Every important edge needs a type, source, and time.

## 🧩 The Full Stack Can Exist Between Organisations

A capability bundle can be distributed across identity resolution, relationship mapping, classification, collection, analysis, allocation, reputation, influence, and discoverability systems.

The question is not only:

> Which single company does everything?

It is:

> **Which interfaces would have to exist for these capabilities to operate together, and is there evidence those interfaces exist?**

Capability adjacency is a lead, not proof of transfer.

## 🪟 Everybody Has A Window

Compartmentalisation can be necessary.

The problem appears when compartmentalisation of information becomes compartmentalisation of responsibility.

```text
Keeper A → collection
Keeper B → enrichment
Keeper C → analysis
Keeper D → recommendation
Keeper E → action
Keeper F → oversight of E
```

Every Keeper may possess a legitimate window.

Nobody may be responsible for the whole building.

> **Everyone has a remit. The bird has an outcome.**

## 🪖 Local Compliance, System Harm

Every component can be locally compliant while the assembled system produces a harmful cumulative result.

This is the same overlap-accountability problem treated elsewhere in Polaris: distributed authorisation, fragmented knowledge, procedural insulation, attribution gaps, and mutual exoneration.

The answer is not to tell the bird to navigate the remits more skilfully.

## 🧭 Someone Has To Own The Joins

```text
distributed capability
→ named system-level owner

compartmentalised knowledge
→ authorised whole-system audit

multiple legal regimes
→ explicit interface governance

outsourced components
→ retained commissioning responsibility

restricted operators
→ protected escalation routes

cumulative harm
→ cumulative-effect review
```

A system-level owner needs enough authority and independent oversight to answer:
- what the whole system is intended to do,
- which interfaces exist,
- where responsibility transfers,
- how errors propagate,
- which risks accumulate,
- who can stop the process,
- and who is answerable for the cumulative outcome.

> **Someone has to own the joins.**

## 🛡️ Push Accountability Upstream

Where operators possess only fragments, scrutiny should reach authorisers, commissioners, procurement decisions, policy architecture, information design, and oversight structures.

This protects ordinary operators from being made scapegoats for architecture they could not see, while preventing the architecture itself from becoming nobody's responsibility.
