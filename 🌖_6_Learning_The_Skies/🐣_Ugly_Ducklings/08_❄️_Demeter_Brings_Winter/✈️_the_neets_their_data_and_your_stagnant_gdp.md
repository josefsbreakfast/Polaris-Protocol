# ✈️ The NEETs, Their Data, and Your Stagnant GDP
**First created:** 2026-08-28 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-28  
*An Ugly Ducklings hypothesis about participation-depleted populations, informational extraction, data sovereignty, and the possibility that some private productivity gains have been purchased with uncounted losses elsewhere in the human economy.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Governments keep asking why portions of their populations are not producing enough conventional economic value.

Technology companies keep asking how much additional economic value can be extracted from those same populations' data, attention, language, culture, behaviour, relationships, creative work, and cognitive output.

Has anybody reconciled these two fucking spreadsheets?

This node proposes a **slice**, not a universal explanation.

It does not claim that surveillance capitalism causes NEET status.

It does not claim that all economically inactive people share one developmental history.

It does not claim that artificial intelligence, private technology firms, intelligence agencies, governments, finance, or any other single actor secretly coordinates the entire information economy.

It asks a narrower question:

> **Could some apparently unused human productive capacity already have been consumed elsewhere?**

If an individual's behaviour, language, attention, social relationships, cultural production, creative work, preferences, and cognitive output become economically valuable inputs to somebody else's productive system, while the originating person experiences reduced bargaining power, cognitive load, lost opportunity, institutional distrust, or withdrawal from conventional participation, then a national economy may be recording the two sides of the event in different places.

One side appears as:

```text
innovation
productivity
data asset
platform growth
model capability
advertising efficiency
firm valuation
```

The other may appear as:

```text
economic inactivity
underemployment
NEET
poor engagement
skills deficit
low confidence
institutional avoidance
stagnant productivity
```

The question is not whether every item in the second list was caused by the first.

The question is whether **some of the first list has been purchased with costs hidden inside the second**.

> **A person can be economically extractable while being economically excluded.**

## 📊 NEET Is A Measurement Window, Not A Species

**NEET** — *not in education, employment, or training* — is an administrative and statistical category.

It is not a personality type.

It contains people with radically different circumstances, including combinations of:

- unemployment,
- temporary transition,
- disability or illness,
- caring responsibilities,
- deprivation,
- educational exclusion,
- parenthood,
- weak local labour markets,
- housing insecurity,
- skills mismatch,
- and many other factors.

This node does not propose to replace those explanations.

Nor is the population of interest confined to the formal NEET age range.

There are older people who have become detached from conventional economic participation.

There are people who are technically employed but severely underused.

There are people cycling through unstable work.

There are people doing large amounts of unpaid care, creative production, digital participation, defensive administration, or informal work which conventional measures only partially recognise.

There are people whose capacity to participate has become substantially smaller than the plausible counterfactual of what they might otherwise have done.

For this node, **NEET-like** or **participation-depleted** therefore describes an analytical territory rather than a new official category.

The question is broader:

> **Where has human productive capacity gone?**

## 🐣 The Bird That Learned Not To Use The Door

Some participation loss may begin with repeated adverse encounters with the institutions through which participation is supposed to occur.

```text
developmental difference / vulnerability
→ misclassification
→ repeated adverse institutional encounters
→ attempts at correction fail
→ participation becomes increasingly costly
→ vigilance / defensive behaviour / avoidance
→ pathways narrow
→ withdrawal becomes locally adaptive
```

**Adaptive does not mean desirable.**

A person may desperately want:

- education,
- work,
- professional identity,
- community,
- independence,
- money,
- colleagues,
- or meaningful contribution,

while simultaneously having learned that the available institutional gateways predict humiliation, danger, inexplicable friction, loss of control, or another round of being represented incorrectly.

From the dashboard:

> disengaged person.

From inside the bird:

> Every fucking door keeps hurting me.

These are not the same description.

## 🚪 Have You Asked What Happened The Previous Thirty-Seven Times?

Institutions often encounter the endpoint.

They see:

- missed appointments,
- distrust,
- excessive documentation,
- hostility toward professionals,
- reluctance to disclose,
- inconsistent participation,
- refusal to follow ordinary process,
- intense boundary-setting,
- apparently disproportionate reactions to administrative errors,
- or somebody who simply stops coming back.

Those observations can then become explanations.

```text
Why are they economically marginal?

Because they are difficult to engage.
```

Maybe.

But:

> **Why are they difficult to engage?**

Some present-day predictors may also be **outputs of the trajectory requiring explanation**.

A poor employment history can reduce employment prospects.

A damaged educational trajectory can reduce later opportunity.

Institutional distrust can make future institutional participation harder.

Low confidence can inhibit re-entry.

All true.

But if those characteristics were themselves produced or intensified by earlier interaction with the system, treating them only as individual deficits quietly deletes the causal history.

## 🔄 The Remediation Can Reproduce The Injury

This is where the Caution Swan enters.

```text
institutional injury
→ withdrawal
→ inactivity classification
→ deficit-oriented intervention
→ "fix the person"
→ person recognises the old misclassification
→ stronger defensive signalling
→ "hard to engage"
→ intensified intervention
```

The attempted remedy can reproduce the relationship that helped create the problem.

A person who believes:

> You have misunderstood me again.

may respond by explaining more.

Then documenting more.

Then objecting more strongly.

Then refusing to cooperate with a process they believe is structurally incapable of representing them accurately.

At iteration thirty-seven, the resulting behaviour can look like the cause.

The system may never have recorded iterations one through thirty-six.

## 🧠 Cognitive Bandwidth Is An Economic Input

Human cognition is not infinitely elastic.

People possess finite:

- attention,
- working memory,
- executive capacity,
- emotional regulation,
- decision bandwidth,
- time,
- creative energy,
- and tolerance for uncertainty.

Modern economic systems routinely treat portions of that capacity as effectively free infrastructure.

The costs may include:

- managing dozens of digital interfaces,
- constant notifications,
- behavioural targeting,
- privacy management,
- unwanted accessibility,
- identity verification,
- administrative repetition,
- reputational management,
- platform dependency,
- information overload,
- insecure work,
- defensive documentation,
- and continual adaptation to systems whose rules change faster than the people using them can reasonably track.

Not every such burden is caused by surveillance capitalism.

Not every burden produces economic inactivity.

But cognitive load does not stop being economically relevant because it occurs outside paid working hours.

> **If the information economy consumes human cognitive bandwidth, that bandwidth is an input whether or not anybody bothered to price it.**

## 🪶 Peter's Feathers Made Paul More Productive

Consider a deliberately simplified accounting model.

### Peter supplies

```text
behavioural information
language
creative work
attention
preferences
social information
cultural production
feedback
relationships
tacit knowledge
```

These become inputs into systems capable of producing:

```text
data assets
advertising products
recommendation systems
models
financial products
platform intelligence
automation
market advantage
firm productivity
valuation
```

Paul receives the productive asset.

The gain is visible.

But Peter's side of the ledger may contain:

```text
lost attention
reduced bargaining power
market displacement
privacy costs
cognitive load
loss of control over output
administrative burden
reduced opportunity
institutional distrust
```

Some of those costs are difficult to measure.

Some are distributed across millions of people.

Some occur years later.

Some are recorded under entirely different policy headings.

Some are never recorded at all.

So:

> **Did we count Paul's productivity gain and classify Peter's loss as an unrelated social problem?**

This is the central accounting question of the node.

## 💰 Gross Extracted Productivity Is Not Net Social Productivity

A firm becoming more productive does not automatically establish that the surrounding society experienced an equivalent net productivity gain.

These are different objects:

```text
firm productivity
worker productivity
national productivity
GDP
wealth
welfare
labour-force participation
distribution
counterfactual opportunity
```

They interact.

They are not interchangeable.

A technology can produce genuine efficiency gains while simultaneously:

- displacing opportunity,
- concentrating returns,
- externalising costs,
- degrading job quality,
- increasing dependency,
- or consuming inputs whose original suppliers are poorly compensated.

The appropriate question is therefore not:

> Did the technology create productivity?

It is:

> **Whose productivity increased, whose capacity decreased, who captured the resulting value, and which costs were omitted from the calculation?**

Or, in shorter Polaris:

> **Gross extracted productivity is not net social productivity.**

## 🤖 AI Makes The Accounting Problem Difficult To Ignore

Artificial intelligence did not invent informational extraction.

It makes the accounting problem unusually visible.

The productive inputs can include enormous accumulations of:

- language,
- code,
- images,
- writing,
- artistic conventions,
- behavioural feedback,
- preferences,
- cultural knowledge,
- and other forms of human-produced information.

Questions about copyright, data protection, consent, provenance, compensation, fair use, competition, and data sovereignty are legally distinct.

Economically, however, they repeatedly return us to one awkward question:

> **If the informational ecology was valuable enough to help produce an extraordinarily valuable asset, where was the value of the original input recorded?**

This does not establish that every input was unlawfully acquired.

It does not establish that every contributor is owed the same form of compensation.

It does establish that **availability and economic valuelessness are not synonyms**.

The fact that an input was easy to acquire does not mean it was economically irrelevant.

## 🕸️ Surveillance Capitalism Eats Its Workers Too

The familiar critique of surveillance capitalism concerns privacy, autonomy, consent, manipulation, and power.

This node adds another question:

> **Can sufficiently aggressive informational extraction degrade the productive ecology upon which the extractor ultimately depends?**

If people become:

- cognitively depleted,
- less trusting,
- more defensive,
- more precarious,
- less able to monetise their own work,
- less willing to participate in institutions,
- or increasingly occupied with managing the systems extracting from them,

then an information economy can create a strange accounting outcome.

The population remains extremely useful as:

```text
data source
attention source
training source
behavioural source
cultural source
```

while becoming less successful as:

```text
worker
student
creator with bargaining power
entrepreneur
professional
institutional participant
```

> **Economically extractable. Economically excluded.**

That should worry capitalists as much as anybody else.

## 🍶 The Fermentation Problem

A locally successful process can degrade the environment required for its own continuation.

Fermentation offers the deliberately simple analogy.

A biological process can produce outputs which eventually make the surrounding environment less hospitable to further activity.

No conspiracy is required.

No organism needs a five-year strategic plan.

The cybernetic lesson is:

> **local optimisation is not system viability.**

So:

```text
platform engagement ↑
human attention quality ↓

behavioural extraction ↑
felt privacy / autonomy ↓

firm efficiency ↑
worker bargaining power ↓

classification confidence ↑
human ability to correct classification ↓

private informational capital ↑
individual informational sovereignty ↓
```

The existence of the left-hand gain does not prove the right-hand cost.

The point is that **systems capable of producing both require measurement of both**.

## 🏭 Industrialised Misclassification

Ugly Ducklings does not require a universal machine.

Some adverse outcomes may be deliberate.

Some may emerge from incentives.

Some may arise from administrative fragmentation.

Some may be commercial.

Some may be algorithmic.

Some may result from twenty organisations inheriting one another's representations.

Some may have nothing whatsoever to do with the mechanisms proposed here.

The governing methodological rules remain:

```text
capability ≠ deployment
relationship ≠ coordination
anomaly ≠ attribution
benefit ≠ knowledge
representation ≠ person
```

This matters because industrial processes can produce patterned population effects without requiring central coordination.

A system does not need to agree upon:

> Let us create participation-depleted people.

It only needs to repeatedly optimise local objectives while failing to account for the cumulative human externality.

## 🗝️ Data Sovereignty Is A Property Question

The modern debate often treats data sovereignty as a niche concern belonging to privacy specialists.

That is increasingly difficult to sustain.

Data can be:

- economically valuable,
- predictive,
- tradeable,
- aggregatable,
- licensable,
- securitised indirectly through company value,
- transformed into proprietary models,
- and used to generate durable competitive advantage.

Yet individuals often possess much weaker functional control over data generated by or about their lives than the rhetoric of private property might lead them to expect.

Functional sovereignty would involve meaningful questions such as:

- Can I refuse?
- Can I know what was taken?
- Can I understand what was inferred?
- Can I correct it?
- Can I withdraw it where law permits?
- Can I control onward use?
- Can I contest the transformation?
- Can I obtain meaningful remedy?
- Can I share in value where the economic structure says I should?
- Does my right survive once my information has been converted into somebody else's asset?

This is not identical to saying that personal data should legally behave exactly like a house, car, or copyright.

It is saying that an economy committed to private property has a serious conceptual problem if **property-like protection becomes strongest only after information has crossed the corporate boundary**.

> **Private property cannot begin at the point where somebody else's information becomes yours.**

## 🧮 Peter To Pay Paul

The simplest version of the hypothesis is:

> **We may have stolen from Peter to pay Paul in productivity units.**

Paul's organisation receives:

- automation,
- information,
- predictive capacity,
- market intelligence,
- cheaper inputs,
- or increased productive leverage.

Peter's contribution is dispersed across:

- data,
- cognition,
- creative work,
- cultural production,
- attention,
- behavioural information,
- and opportunity costs.

Paul's gain is booked as productivity.

Peter's depletion may later be booked as:

- inactivity,
- poor labour-market attachment,
- health expenditure,
- skills failure,
- welfare expenditure,
- low productivity,
- social disengagement,
- or nothing measurable at all.

Then policymakers ask why Peter is not contributing enough.

Peter may reasonably respond:

> **You already spent me.**

## 🗃️ The Box Has A Political Economy

Informational extraction does not occur outside history.

Modern states and markets inherited administrative traditions shaped by:

- empire,
- industrialisation,
- colonial classification,
- financialisation,
- bureaucratic legibility,
- census-making,
- policing,
- welfare administration,
- advertising,
- intelligence,
- and twentieth-century mass communications.

No contemporary data system becomes colonial merely because colonial administrations also collected information.

The relevant warning is structural:

> **Systems inherit categories, incentives, institutional habits, and assumptions about who is entitled to describe whom.**

For marginalised populations, the question of data sovereignty therefore intersects with older questions:

> Who made the category?
>
> Who benefits from legibility?
>
> Who becomes suspicious by default?
>
> Whose description is treated as authoritative?
>
> Who may extract?
>
> Who may refuse?
>
> Who owns the downstream value?

The box has a history.

So does the hand holding it.

## 🧲 The Cybernetic Horseshoe

The conventional horseshoe theory claims that political extremes can resemble one another.

That is not the claim here.

The more useful proposition is:

> **Political-economic systems with very different ideological origins can converge upon similar failure dynamics when information, allocation, and corrective power become sufficiently concentrated.**

Call it a **cybernetic horseshoe**.

Possible convergent failure modes include:

```text
concentrated informational power
→ privileged intermediaries
→ increasingly discretionary allocation
→ weak individual sovereignty
→ elite insulation
→ surveillance
→ suspicion of dissent
→ degraded correction channels
→ official model diverges from lived conditions
→ legitimacy declines
```

This is a systems comparison.

It is not a claim that the systems are historically or ideologically identical.

## 🟥 This Is What You Told Us Socialism Would Do

There is nevertheless an emotional comparison worth making directly.

For generations, anti-socialist political rhetoric warned that corrupt socialist states would:

- weaken meaningful private ownership,
- subordinate individual interests to institutional priorities,
- reward political insiders,
- centralise information,
- surveil their populations,
- become paranoid about dissent,
- protect bureaucracies from corrective feedback,
- and eventually become unable to reconcile official claims with ordinary lived reality.

So there is a legitimate question for political movements which define themselves through opposition to socialism:

> **Why are some of your failure modes beginning to resemble the ones you taught everybody else to fear?**

This does **not** make contemporary American capitalism equivalent to the Soviet Union.

It does not make private technology companies socialist ministries.

It does not erase the enormous differences in law, ownership, political institutions, violence, elections, markets, speech, or historical context.

The comparison is **phenomenological and cybernetic**.

To a European political memory shaped by the late Eastern Bloc and its aftermath, there is an uncomfortable familiarity in systems which proclaim freedom while ordinary people experience:

- shrinking practical control over valuable personal resources,
- politically connected economic actors,
- pervasive observation,
- institutional opacity,
- suspicion of internal dissent,
- and increasingly weak mechanisms for making powerful institutions acknowledge error.

The response cannot simply be:

> But we are capitalists.

A label does not immunise a system against a failure mode.

## 🧱 1989 Is A Warning, Not A Prediction

This node does not predict a Soviet-style collapse.

The late 1980s are useful because they provide a memorable example of what can happen when a system's internal representation of its own health diverges too far from the population living inside it.

The dangerous threshold is not:

> the government becomes socialist.

It is:

> **telling the system that it is wrong becomes more dangerous than allowing the system to remain wrong.**

At that point:

```text
citizen:
the model is wrong

system:
resistance to model detected

citizen:
that is what I am telling you

system:
hostility increasing
```

Caution Swan has entered the economic policy department.

A system which treats corrective information as attack progressively destroys its own ability to learn.

That is dangerous under any ideology.

## 🏷️ You Cannot Defend Private Property Only After Extraction

A serious defence of capitalism normally places substantial weight upon:

- ownership,
- voluntary exchange,
- predictable rules,
- contract,
- competition,
- price,
- liability,
- and the ability to refuse transactions.

Those principles become incoherent if informational resources are treated as effectively communal while they belong to ordinary people and fiercely proprietary after they have been accumulated by firms.

The provocation is therefore deliberately internal to capitalist rhetoric:

> **You cannot defend private property only after somebody else's property-like asset has been transformed into yours.**

Or:

> **You do not need to abolish private property in law if ordinary people have already lost meaningful sovereignty over the asset class through which increasing quantities of modern economic value are created.**

Whether data should legally constitute property in every context is a separate and difficult legal question.

The economic sovereignty problem remains.

## 🐦 The Missing Worker May Not Be Missing

The NEET-like population now looks different.

Some people who appear conventionally unproductive may still be producing enormous quantities of value through:

- data,
- attention,
- cultural production,
- language,
- platform activity,
- social networks,
- behavioural information,
- unpaid care,
- informal work,
- community knowledge,
- and creative fragments.

The system can therefore observe the same person as:

```text
commercial system:
valuable source

labour-market system:
low-participation person
```

That contradiction should be investigated.

Perhaps the missing worker is not missing.

Perhaps portions of their productive life have simply been routed somewhere the labour-market statistics do not recognise.

And perhaps some of the process of routing it there has made ordinary participation harder.

## 📉 Your Stagnant GDP

This is where the accusation reaches the macroeconomy.

If:

1. human informational and cognitive inputs generate private productive gains;
2. some costs of generating those gains are externalised onto the originating population;
3. those costs reduce conventional productive participation for some people;
4. the private gain and distributed loss are recorded separately;

then a country can simultaneously experience:

```text
spectacularly productive firms

and

disappointing national productivity
```

without either observation being false.

That does not prove this mechanism explains current GDP stagnation.

It establishes a question worth testing:

> **Are we systematically overstating the social productivity of extractive digital business models because their concentrated private gains are easier to measure than their distributed human costs?**

And:

> **How much human productive capacity has been pushed out of legible economic participation while remaining available for extraction elsewhere?**

Those are empirical questions.

They deserve empirical answers.

## 🔬 How Would We Test It?

A serious hypothesis must expose itself to failure.

Potential research could examine relationships between informational/institutional exposure and:

- labour-force participation,
- NEET status,
- long-term economic inactivity,
- employment stability,
- earnings,
- occupational exit,
- educational trajectory,
- creative-market displacement,
- administrative burden,
- institutional trust,
- perceived control over personal data,
- digital cognitive load,
- platform dependency,
- bargaining power,
- and ability to monetise one's own productive output.

Important confounders would include:

- age,
- disability and health,
- deprivation,
- education,
- region,
- housing,
- caring responsibilities,
- family resources,
- local labour-market conditions,
- migration status,
- macroeconomic shocks,
- and pre-existing differences in digital exposure.

Useful research designs might distinguish:

```text
correlation
from
causation

selection
from
effect

individual vulnerability
from
population ecology

firm productivity
from
national productivity

economic output
from
welfare
```

Disconfirming evidence matters.

If the proposed extraction mechanisms do not meaningfully predict participation loss after stronger explanations are controlled for, the theory should shrink.

That is what models are for.

## ⚖️ If The Hypothesis Is Even Partly True

The answer cannot be:

> Peter needs more resilience training.

Potential structural implications would instead include examining:

- stronger practical data sovereignty,
- meaningful consent and refusal,
- provenance duties,
- correction and challenge rights,
- limits on unnecessary behavioural extraction,
- competition and interoperability,
- creator and worker bargaining power,
- compensation or revenue-sharing where justified,
- administrative simplification,
- protection against inherited misclassification,
- assessment of cognitive and participation externalities,
- and productivity measures capable of distinguishing private gain from distributed social cost.

The source may participate in remedy.

The source should not have to manufacture remedy alone.

> **Never turn an institutional capability problem into an individual coping assignment.**

## 🧿 The Accounting Question

The node ultimately asks something deliberately simple.

> **What if we have been calling people unproductive after somebody else already consumed part of the productivity?**

Not every NEET.

Not every economically inactive person.

Not every worker.

Not every technology company.

Not every dataset.

Not every AI system.

A slice.

But if Peter's feathers made Paul more productive, then Peter's subsequent lack of feathers cannot automatically be treated as an inexplicable moral, behavioural, or economic failure of Peter.

Before designing another intervention for the bird, reconcile the fucking spreadsheets.

## 🏮 Footer

*✈️ The NEETs, Their Data, and Your Stagnant GDP* is a living node of the **Polaris Protocol**.

It sits within **Caution Swans Aggressive** as a political-economy hypothesis about apparently unused human capacity. It asks whether some conventional participation loss may coexist with — or in a narrower subset be intensified by — systems which continue extracting informational, behavioural, creative, and cognitive value from the same people they later classify as economically marginal.

The node does not propose a universal explanation for NEET status, economic inactivity, productivity stagnation, or contemporary political-economic instability. Its purpose is to expose a potentially missing accounting relationship: concentrated gains from informational extraction may be measured more readily than distributed losses in human capacity, sovereignty, trust, opportunity, and participation.
