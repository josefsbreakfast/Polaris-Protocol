## 🕰️ Event Time / Information Time / Evidence Time / Court Time

- Keep four clocks distinct.

### Event time

- When the underlying action occurred.

### Information time

- What each actor knew or had available at that moment.

### Evidence time

- When material documenting the event became available.

### Court time

- When that material entered the litigation / public judicial record.

- A later witness statement can describe an earlier event.
- It does not automatically prove every participant possessed the later account at the earlier moment.
- A later disclosed message can change our understanding of an earlier event.

## 🎭 Litigation Makes Institutions Perform

- Explain what adversarial proceedings do to institutional narratives.
- Litigation requires:
  - defined parties;
  - relevant facts;
  - legal grounds;
  - evidence;
  - chronology;
  - remedies.
- Complex institutions therefore acquire unusually coherent narrated positions.
- This is analytically useful.
- It also compresses.
- Pleadings select for:
  - legal relevance;
  - strategy;
  - procedural requirements;
  - disputed issues.
- Therefore:
  - claimant pleading ≠ neutral institutional history;
  - defence pleading ≠ neutral institutional history.
- Neither proposition means the pleading is false.
- Read each as:
  - **an argument about evidence constructed for a legal purpose.**
