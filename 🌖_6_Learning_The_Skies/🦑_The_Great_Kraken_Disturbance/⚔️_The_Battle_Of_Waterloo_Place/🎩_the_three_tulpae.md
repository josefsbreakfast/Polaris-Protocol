## 🎩 What The Fuck Is A Tulpa Doing In A Procurement Dispute?

- Define the Polaris usage carefully.
- “Tulpa” here is an analytical/narrative metaphor.
- It refers to the process by which a complicated institution acquires an apparent body in public understanding.
- Institutions are materially/formally real:
  - statutes;
  - offices;
  - employees;
  - budgets;
  - contracts;
  - buildings;
  - databases;
  - policies;
  - powers;
  - procedures.
- But they also acquire accumulated representations:
  - names;
  - logos;
  - reputations;
  - press identities;
  - political associations;
  - public narratives;
  - institutional memory;
  - assumptions about motives;
  - imagined personalities.
- These accumulated representations can make a large institution appear to behave like a single person.
- That apparent person can be useful for narration.
- It becomes dangerous when we forget that the body was constructed.
- Preserve:
  - **The tulpa gives the institution a body. The investigation opens the body back up.**
- Distinguish:
  - institution;
  - representation of institution;
  - individual actors inside institution;
  - relationships between those actors;
  - formal authority;
  - actual action.
- The tulpa is not:
  - imaginary in the sense that the institution does not exist;
  - supernatural;
  - evidence of institutional consciousness;
  - evidence that every member shares one motive.

## 🔺 Govern / Guard / Make

- Put the three heuristic verbs together.
- **Govern**
  - authorise;
  - oversee;
  - account;
  - legitimate.
- **Guard**
  - operate;
  - police;
  - secure;
  - identify operational need.
- **Make / build / own**
  - supply;
  - engineer;
  - integrate;
  - commercialise;
  - retain relevant private rights/capabilities.
- Ask what happens when modern public capability requires all three.
- Central structural problem:
  - public authority may possess lawful power without possessing every technical capability needed to exercise it;
  - operational institutions may possess expertise without possessing final democratic authority;
  - private suppliers may possess capability without possessing public sovereignty.
- Do not turn this into three perfectly separate boxes.
- Investigate overlaps and dependencies.
- Preserve:
  - **Who governs when government buys capability?**

## 🎭 Archetype Is Not Institution

- Make the danger explicit.
- “Governing man”, “guarding man” and “making man” are narrative archetypes.
- They are **not findings about institutional character**.
- An institution can:
  - contain competing departments;
  - change position;
  - misunderstand itself;
  - hold inconsistent records;
  - contain people with different incentives;
  - act through delegated authority;
  - behave differently at different moments.
- The apparent unity of the body must never increase evidential confidence about institutional unity.
- Preserve:
  - **The apparent unity of the bronze body must never increase evidential confidence about institutional unity.**
- The narration may use:
  - “MOPAC said”;

  - “Palantir challenged”.

  - who exactly?
  - when?
  - under what authority?
  - using what information?
