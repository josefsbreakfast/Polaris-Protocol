# 🎩 The Three Tulpae

*Working bullet-point scaffold. Not finished narrative prose.*

## 🎩 Core device

- The Guards Crimean War Memorial gives us **three Guardsmen**.
- The legal situation gives us **three parties**.
- Polaris temporarily maps the three legal/institutional parties onto the three figures.
- They become **three tulpae**:
    - constructed bodies through which much larger systems of institutional power can temporarily become visible.
- This is an imaginative and analytical device.
- It is **not** a claim that:
    - each institution is internally unified;
    - each represents an organised political faction;
    - the wider systems behind them act as single coordinated entities;
    - the litigation consists of three metaphysical “sides”.

---

## 🪖 A Guard of Three

- Together, the three tulpae form:
    - **a Guard of Three**.
- This deliberately echoes the three Guardsmen already standing at Waterloo Place.
- “Guard” is useful because guarding can mean:
    - protecting;
    - watching;
    - controlling access;
    - maintaining a boundary;
    - holding a position;
    - keeping something safe;
    - preventing something from passing.
- Useful question for each tulpa:
    - **What is this Guard actually guarding?**
- Possible things to investigate rather than assume:
    - authority
    - operational independence
    - public safety
    - democratic control
    - institutional legitimacy
    - information
    - data
    - technological capability
    - commercial position
    - procurement power
    - the King's Peace.

## 🏛️ Tulpa One — MOPAC

- Concrete legal/institutional body:
    - **Mayor's Office for Policing and Crime — MOPAC**.
- Through MOPAC, the dispute provides a sensor into a much larger ecology involving:
    - elected political authority;
    - democratic accountability;
    - mayoral government;
    - Labour political power;
    - statutory governance of policing.
- Do not collapse:
    - **MOPAC ≠ Labour ≠ London government ≠ British democratic government.**
- Map the actual relationships.
- Identify:
    - officeholders;
    - statutory authority;
    - decision-making powers;
    - relevant political relationships;
    - resources;
    - constraints.
- Masculinity/archetype being provisionally explored:
    - **the governing man**
    - administrator
    - statesman
    - political officeholder
    - committee room
    - mandate
    - rule through legitimate public authority.

## 🔺 A triangle of masculinities of old power

- The three Guardsmen allow us to imagine a **triangle of masculinities of old power**.
- Provisional threefold grammar:
    - **govern**
    - **guard**
    - **make / build / own**
- These are not claims about the individual men involved.
- They are cultural/institutional archetypes used to make different forms of power visible.
- One corner can contain extremely modern software while still reproducing a very old performance of authority.
- Technologies change.
- Institutional arrangements change.
- The underlying cultural performances of:
    - command;
    - governance;
    - protection;
    - ownership;
    - construction;
    - mastery;
    - may have considerably longer histories.
- Research this rather than declaring an uninterrupted historical genealogy.

## 🫥 Why “tulpa”?

- Each party looks singular when written as a name:
    - MOPAC
    - Metropolitan Police
    - Palantir.
- But none is actually one human mind or body.
- Each apparently singular actor contains:
    - people;
    - offices;
    - procedures;
    - legal authorities;
    - internal hierarchies;
    - information systems;
    - professional relationships;
    - resources;
    - potentially competing interests.
- The tulpa gives this complicated system a temporary body.
- That makes institutional power cognitively manageable enough to inspect.
- But the analytical job is then to **open the body back up**.

## 🎩 The conjuring trick

- Narrative move:
    - conjure each institution into one Guardsman.
- Analytical move:
    - immediately investigate what is actually underneath the hat.
- Possible central formulation:
    - **The tulpa gives the institution a body. The investigation opens the body back up.**
- Or:
    - **Conjure the three men so we can see power. Then take the hats off and find out what is actually underneath.**
- The apparent unity of the bronze body must never increase evidential confidence about institutional unity.

## 🔬 What to examine inside each tulpa

- For each:
    - actor
    - relationship
    - resource
    - authority
    - capability
    - pathway
    - observable consequence.
- Preserve:
    - capability ≠ access
    - access ≠ use
    - use ≠ attribution
    - attribution ≠ causation
    - relationship ≠ coordination
    - network ≠ conspiracy.
- Ask:
    - Who actually speaks?
    - Who decides?
    - Under what authority?
    - Who controls money?
    - Who controls information?
    - Who possesses technical capability?
    - Who can say no?
    - Who can compel?
    - Who can delay?
    - Who can litigate?
    - Who can disclose?
    - Who can withhold?
    - Who can change the practical situation?
    - Who merely appears to possess power because we have drawn them as one enormous bloke?

## ⚔️ The important analytical tension

- The three-tulpa device deliberately simplifies.
- Polaris then investigates the simplification.
- Therefore:
    - **archetype and evidence must remain separate.**
- “Governing man”, “guarding man” and “building/owning man” are narrative lenses.
- MOPAC, the Met and Palantir remain concrete institutions whose actual powers and conduct must be established from evidence.
- The battle grammar helps us see.
- It does not establish facts.
