# 🗺️ A Missing Battle Map?

*Working bullet-point scaffold. Not finished narrative prose.*

## 🗺️ Basic idea

- A **Data Protection Impact Assessment — DPIA** can be explained here without immediately making everybody want to die of governance documentation.
- For Polaris purposes, imagine it as something like a **battle map for proposed information processing**.
- This is an analogy:
    - a DPIA is **not literally** a systems map;
    - it does not necessarily contain a diagram;
    - its legal function is broader than mapping data flows.
- But before high-risk processing begins, somebody needs to understand:
    - what is being done;
    - why it is being done;
    - what information is involved;
    - where information comes from;
    - how it will be processed;
    - what systems or datasets interact;
    - what risks arise;
    - who may be affected;
    - what safeguards reduce those risks.
- In other words:
    - **someone needs to understand the terrain before the operation begins.**

---

## ⚔️ A list of armies is not a battle map

- A historical battle map does not become useful merely because it tells us:
    - Regiment A exists.
    - Regiment B exists.
    - Artillery Battery C exists.
- What matters is also:
    - where they are;
    - where they move;
    - where they encounter one another;
    - what routes become possible;
    - what changes when previously separate forces meet.
- Apply the same distinction to information systems.
- Knowing that several databases individually exist is not necessarily the same thing as understanding:
    - what happens when they are connected;
    - what can be queried across them;
    - what relationships become visible;
    - what new inferences become possible;
    - what actions those inferences can trigger.
- **A list of the databases is not necessarily a map of the processing.**

---

## 🔗 The connection may be the important bit

- This question does not inherently depend upon Palantir.
- The same conceptual problem could arise with:
    - another supplier;
    - another integration platform;
    - APIs;
    - data warehouses;
    - analytics systems;
    - internally developed software.
- The important distinction is between:
    - risks associated with individual datasets;
    - risks associated with the **new processing created by connecting them**.
- Dataset A may already have been assessed.
- Dataset B may already have been assessed.
- Dataset C may already have been assessed.
- That does not automatically establish that:
    - **A + B + C + cross-system querying + analytics + inference**
    - has been adequately assessed.

---

## 🧭 What terrain should the map describe?

- Research against the actual DPIA requirements.
- Useful questions include:
    - What processing is proposed?
    - What is its purpose?
    - What categories of data are involved?
    - What categories of people are affected?
    - What systems feed into it?
    - What systems receive information from it?
    - What relationships between information become newly discoverable?
    - What profiling or inference becomes possible?
    - Who can access the resulting information?
    - What decisions or operational actions can follow?
    - What are the risks to people's rights and freedoms?
    - What safeguards exist?
    - What risks remain after those safeguards?
    - Can the design be changed to reduce them?
- A DPIA should not merely document a technological fait accompli.
- Research the extent to which it is supposed to influence project design **before** the relevant high-risk processing occurs.

---

## 🚔 Where might the responsibility sit?

- Do not begin with:
    - **Palantir privacy scandal.**
- First establish:
    - who is controller;
    - who determines purposes;
    - who determines the relevant means;
    - who designed the intended implementation;
    - who was responsible for conducting and maintaining the DPIA;
    - what responsibilities belonged to any processor/supplier.
- Possible evidential outcomes include:
    - supplier deficiency;
    - Met specification deficiency;
    - Met assessment deficiency;
    - implementation-design deficiency;
    - governance/sequencing deficiency;
    - no material deficiency at all.
- These are different findings.
- Do not merge them.

## 🎩 Why this matters to the Three Tulpae

- If an implementation or DPIA problem existed primarily at Met level, it could expose a relationship that the case title obscures.
- The litigation may formally present:
    - **Palantir ↔ MOPAC**
- But the underlying governance system also contains:
    - **MOPAC ↔ Met**
    - **Met ↔ Palantir**
- A problem discovered in one relationship may have consequences in another.
- Therefore:
    - **The lawsuit has parties. The power system has relationships.**

## 🏛️ The MOPAC question

- If the Met sought approval for a proposed capability, ask:
    - what exactly was MOPAC being asked to approve?
    - how mature was the proposed implementation?
    - what assurance material accompanied it?
    - what did the relevant DPIA actually assess?
    - what remained unresolved?
    - what did MOPAC know at decision time?
- Possible central question:
    - **Was MOPAC being presented with a sufficiently mapped proposition to cross the authorisation boundary?**
- Do not answer until the documents support an answer.

## ⏱️ Event time / information time

- Reconstruct separately:
    - when technical decisions were made;
    - when datasets/integrations were proposed;
    - when DPIAs were drafted;
    - when DPIAs were revised;
    - when risks were identified;
    - when safeguards were proposed;
    - when the Met knew each thing;
    - when MOPAC received each thing;
    - what MOPAC knew on 21 May 2026.
- A later document cannot automatically establish what an earlier decision-maker knew.
- **Later information ≠ earlier information.**

## 📋 Evidence job

- Obtain the relevant DPIA or DPIAs.
- Do not assess them merely by searching for the word “Palantir”.
- Identify:
    - title;
    - version;
    - date;
    - authorship/ownership;
    - processing covered;
    - systems covered;
    - datasets covered;
    - data flows described;
    - integrations described;
    - analytics/inferences described;
    - risks identified;
    - mitigations;
    - residual risks;
    - references to other documents;
    - review/update arrangements.
- Then compare that scope against what UOA was actually intended to enable.
- Distinguish:
    - vague drafting;
    - missing information;
    - information incorporated elsewhere;
    - genuine assessment gap.
- **Do not complete the pattern.**

## ❓ The missing battle map?

- The title remains a question.
- We have **not established that the map was missing**.
- Possible outcomes:
    - the DPIA adequately mapped the proposed processing;
    - the necessary detail existed across several linked governance documents;
    - the DPIA existed but was insufficiently specific;
    - it adequately assessed existing systems but not the consequences of integration;
    - relevant assessment happened too late;
    - some other assurance mechanism addressed the apparent gap.
- Therefore:
    - **missing map is the research question, not the finding.**
