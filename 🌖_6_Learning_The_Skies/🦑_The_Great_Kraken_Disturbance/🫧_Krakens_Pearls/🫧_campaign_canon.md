# 🫧 Campaign Canon  
**First created:** 2026-09-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-18  
*The durable narrative, epistemic, and editorial rules governing The Great Kraken Disturbance.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This file is the human-readable campaign constitution for **The Great Kraken Disturbance**.

It governs the meanings, distinctions, narrative rules, evidential boundaries, and editorial conventions that should remain stable across the cluster unless the author expressly changes them.

It is **canon, not chronology**.

It is **rules, not case notes**.

It is not intended to contain every fact, joke, participant, argument, source, or development that appears in the series. Those belong in the relevant nodes. This file exists so that a new human collaborator or a fresh language-model context can enter the project without quietly changing what the project means.

The surrounding Pearl infrastructure has separate jobs:

- `README.md` explains publicly what the Kraken's Pearls are and why
    the method is being used.
- `🫧_campaign_canon.md` specifies the durable human-readable rules of
    this campaign.
- `🧾_definitions.yaml` provides a compact machine-readable semantic
    map.
- `🛡️_document_integrity.yaml` governs the integrity of the files
    themselves: encoding, Unicode, Markdown hygiene, conversion damage,
    instruction boundaries, and related production concerns.

Before drafting, extending, summarising, restructuring, or interpreting Kraken material:

> **Read the campaign canon before entering the water.**

The purpose is not to make the writing timid.

The purpose is to make it possible for the writing to be extremely silly without becoming epistemically silly.

## 🦑 1. The Kraken

The Kraken is a narrative representation of the **King's Peace**.

Within the fictional geography of the series, she is imagined as an ambient mythological or folkloric creature existing somewhere beneath the islands of the British archipelago.

Events happen above her.

Institutions act above her.

Documents move above her.

Lawyers acquire more lawyers above her.

The disturbance reaches the water.

The Kraken notices.

### What the Kraken is

The Kraken may function narratively as:

- a representation of the King's Peace;
- an ambient presence beneath the archipelago;
- a sensor for disturbance in the civic and institutional environment;
- a recurring literary figure through which changes in that
    environment become perceptible;
- a creature who is, quite reasonably, precious about her pearls.

### What the Kraken is not

The Kraken is not:

- the British state;
- His Majesty's Government;
- the Crown;
- the monarch;
- Parliament;
- the courts;
- the police;
- the security or intelligence services;
- the armed forces;
- Palantir;
- any other company;
- any political party;
- any political faction;
- any professional network;
- the public;
- civil society;
- the "deep state";
- a hidden cabal;
- a conspiracy;
- a secret controller;
- an institutional actor;
- any named participant in the events being described.

The Kraken must not be substituted for one of those things merely because a later passage would become easier to write if she were.

### Permitted narrative behaviour

The Kraken may:

- sleep;
- stir;
- notice;
- listen;
- become perturbed;
- register a disturbance;
- make ominous, annoyed, confused, or otherwise expressive underwater
    noises;
- value, guard, receive, inspect, or become pleased by pearls;
- wonder what the fuck the people above her are doing.

These are literary actions.

They do not convert the Kraken into a causal actor in the institutional events being analysed.

### Canonical relationship

> **The disturbance perturbs the Kraken.**
>
> **The Kraken does not cause the disturbance.**

This distinction is canonical.

Do not collapse it.

## 👑 2. The King's Peace

The **King's Peace** is the closest natural-language description of what the Kraken represents within the campaign.

The phrase draws on an old legal and constitutional idea, but the Kraken is not intended as a literal embodiment of one precisely bounded contemporary doctrine.

Within the narrative system, the King's Peace is broader: the condition in which disputes within the polity remain governable through legitimate institutions, rules, processes, records, argument, accountability, and peaceful contest rather than becoming uncontrolled conflict.

This makes the Kraken a background condition rather than another box in an institutional network diagram.

If a diagram contains government departments, companies, courts, Parliament, police, lawyers, journalists, political actors, regulators, contractors, or other institutions, the Kraken should not simply appear as another node with arrows connecting her to them.

She is closer to the **field in which the diagram sits**.

The phrase must therefore not be mechanically translated into:

- "the state";
- "government";
- "law and order";
- "the Crown";
- "the police";
- "national security";
- "public opinion";
- or any other single contemporary institution or function.

The metaphor is deliberately broader than any one of them.

## 💥 3. The Disturbance

**The Great Kraken Disturbance** is the cumulative disturbance produced by unusually energetic legal, political, commercial, administrative, parliamentary, informational, institutional, and public activity above the Kraken.

Depending on the node, that may include:

- procurement decisions;
- litigation;
- threatened or actual legal proceedings;
- parliamentary questions;
- debates;
- ministerial or institutional statements;
- disclosure;
- nondisclosure;
- journalism;
- public records;
- contractual activity;
- administrative decisions;
- commercial responses;
- regulatory activity;
- political interventions;
- legal argument;
- information moving from one arena into another;
- later actors responding to earlier records.

Not every one of these elements must be present in every episode.

The Disturbance is not a formal legal category. It is the narrative name for the cumulative system state produced by the interactions being observed.

The basic relationship is:

> People make the disturbance.
>
> Institutions participate in the disturbance.
>
> Information can propagate or reshape the disturbance.
>
> The Kraken registers the disturbance.

The Kraken is therefore not one of the factions in **The Great Kraken Disturbance**.

The people are not Krakens.

The institutions are not Krakens.

The networks are not tentacles.

The events disturb the Kraken.

## 🎚️ 4. Epistemic and Narrative Modes

The series deliberately changes register.

Those changes should be visible.

A reader should not have to infer whether a passage is a factual record, an analytical interpretation, a heightened retelling of documented events, or an expressly invented joke.

### 🍿 Narrative mode

Narrative mode tells a substantially grounded sequence through heightened storytelling.

It may use:

- fantasy;
- military history;
- tabletop gaming;
- wizardry;
- melodrama;
- camp;
- soap-opera logic;
- institutional farce;
- mock historiography;
- ceremonial magic;
- absurd bureaucracy;
- other clearly literary devices.

The telling may be ridiculous.

The underlying sequence may not be invented merely to improve the story.

Narrative mode can interpret the shape, rhythm, or absurdity of documented conduct. It must not manufacture undocumented conduct, relationships, motives, coordination, or causation.

### 🎪 Satire mode

Some nodes or sections may be substantially heavier satire.

This means the comic invention itself is part of the object.

A satire section may include:

- invented dialogue;
- counterfactual scenes;
- fictional character classes;
- absurd game mechanics;
- imagined reactions;
- exaggerated narrative roles;
- scenarios that have no corresponding real-world event.

Heavy satire must be marked clearly, normally with a short blockquote near the beginning.

For example:

> **🎪 Satire mode:** This section is deliberately fictionalised and
> heightened for comic effect. Real people or institutions may appear as
> narrative characters, but invented dialogue, character classes,
> motives, reactions, and scenarios are jokes rather than factual
> claims.

The marker establishes the container.

It is not necessary to interrupt every subsequent joke with another disclaimer.

The satire must, however, remain inside its epistemic container.

### 📋 Record mode

Record mode is the point at which the capes come off.

It records the underlying events, sources, positions, chronology, mechanisms, and evidential limits.

It may use bullets, timelines, tables, quotations within appropriate limits, or ordinary explanatory prose.

Record mode does not inherit factual propositions from satire or narrative merely because those propositions were memorable.

Invented dialogue does not migrate downstairs.

Narrative casting does not become evidence of personality or motive.

A fictional class bonus does not become biography.

A good joke does not become a source.

### 🔎 Analytical mode

Analytical mode reasons from the record.

It may:

- identify patterns;
- compare mechanisms;
- distinguish capabilities;
- examine incentives;
- map information flows;
- identify contradictions supported by the record;
- explain why a procedural move matters;
- formulate hypotheses;
- identify what evidence would strengthen or weaken an interpretation.

Inference is permitted.

Unlabelled transformation of inference into fact is not.

### Transition rule

> **A material change in epistemic mode should be visible to the
> reader.**

The purpose is not bureaucratic labelling for its own sake.

It is to preserve the reader's ability to know what kind of claim they are being asked to evaluate.

## 📋 5. Evidence Vocabulary

Where the distinction matters, the campaign uses a small set of evidential labels.

### Documented

A claim directly supported by an appropriate primary source or sufficiently reliable evidence.

"Documented" does not mean that every possible interpretation of the document is therefore established.

### Party's position

What an identifiable participant says, argues, alleges, denies, maintains, characterises, or claims.

A party's position should remain attributed to that party unless independently established.

### Reported

Information attributed to reporting rather than independently established by Polaris.

The quality and proximity of the reporting still matter.

### Our reading

An interpretation, synthesis, or analytical inference made from the available material.

It should remain recognisable as interpretation.

### Unknown

The available evidence does not presently establish the answer.

"Unknown" is a valid result.

It must not be treated as an irritating blank that narrative instinct is permitted to fill.

### Evidential rule

> **Narrative confidence must never increase evidential confidence.**

A beautifully coherent story can still rest on incomplete evidence.

A clumsy or boring documentary record can still be the better evidence.

When the story and the record pull in different directions, the record wins.

## 🎬 6. Casting Rules

The Kraken campaign has continuity.

That does not mean everybody who has ever appeared in it has a permanent speaking role.

### Named parties are local by default

Named real-world people, organisations, institutions, companies, journalists, lawyers, officials, politicians, parties, regulators, campaigners, researchers, or other identifiable actors do not automatically persist from one node into another.

Previous appearance is not permanent cast membership.

### A named party may enter when

1. the author expressly introduces them;
2. the material being drafted places them in the relevant event;
3. they are genuinely necessary to explain an already in-scope
    documented event.

### A named party must not be imported merely because

- they appeared in an earlier Kraken node;
- they were part of a previous joke;
- they are famous;
- they are associated with the topic in public discourse;
- they seem thematically appropriate;
- they occupy an adjacent professional or political network;
- an embedding or semantic association makes them feel relevant;
- a crosslink mentions them;
- a previous summary happened to preserve their name.

A crosslink is not a casting call.

Semantic relevance is not a speaking role.

Network adjacency is not participation.

### Canonical casting rule

> **The machine does not cast the show.**
>
> **The author casts the show.**

If the author has not brought somebody into the episode, do not materialise them merely because the machine can imagine a reason they might be funny there.

## 🎭 7. Narrative Characters Are Not Biographical Claims

Real people and institutions may be rendered as heightened narrative characters.

Depending on the joke, somebody may become:

- a wizard;
- a bard;
- a daemon;
- a rules lawyer;
- a dungeon master;
- a general;
- a military unit;
- a procurer;
- a politician-shaped artillery piece;
- a summoned KC;
- a tabletop character;
- some other ridiculous entity.

This is narrative casting.

It is not a biographical assessment.

Unless separately established in record or analytical mode, the following do not become factual claims merely because satire depicts them:

- personality traits;
- motives;
- preferences;
- friendships;
- rivalries;
- private conversations;
- emotional reactions;
- game preferences;
- strategic intentions;
- competence;
- coordination;
- alliances.

Invented dialogue is invented dialogue.

A running joke is not a witness statement.

A fictional character sheet is not a psychological profile.

The purpose of the device is to make institutional behaviour memorable, legible, and funny.

It is not a back door for making claims that would require evidence elsewhere.

## 🍿 8. Editorial Position

The campaign does not require the narrator to select a faction.

The narrator is permitted to have a personality.

The narrator is permitted to notice that something is funny.

The narrator is permitted to become extremely interested when somebody produces another document, parliamentary question, procedural move, lawyer, argument, disclosure, counterargument, or other piece of institutional theatre.

None of those permissions creates an editorial team.

### The narrator may

- identify irony;
- notice reversals;
- notice callbacks;
- describe escalation;
- recognise procedural absurdity;
- use camp and melodrama;
- explain why a development is strategically or procedurally
    significant;
- follow whichever participant has produced the most interesting
    development;
- identify contradictions where the evidence establishes them;
- laugh at multiple participants in the same episode;
- allow the documentary record to complicate an earlier narrative
    impression.

### The narrator must not

- designate a campaign faction as the hero;
- designate another faction as the villain;
- assume one network is inherently legitimate and another inherently
    suspect;
- convert narrative sympathy into factual certainty;
- suppress inconvenient evidence because it damages the story;
- manufacture conflict to improve the plot;
- manufacture motive or coordination;
- treat a good joke as evidence;
- protect a recurring joke from contradictory evidence.

### Canonical editorial rule

> **We are narrating the fight, not joining it.**

And:

> **The story may have characters. The investigation does not have
> teams.**

Nobody owns the narrator.

The popcorn is radically promiscuous.

## 📰 9. The Tabloid Bargain

The campaign deliberately borrows some techniques from tabloid storytelling.

Public procurement, public law, disclosure, parliamentary procedure, contracting, institutional governance, and information architecture can be enormously consequential while remaining almost aggressively resistant to being read for pleasure.

The answer is not to manufacture scandal.

The answer is not to remove the plot from the documentation.

### Borrow the tabloid instinct for

- pace;
- character;
- conflict;
- escalation;
- reversals;
- memorable language;
- recurring motifs;
- curiosity;
- strong openings;
- the moment the reader thinks, "oh shit";
- making a human being want to turn the page.

### Do not borrow

- evidential inflation;
- manufactured scandal;
- unsupported certainty;
- insinuation presented as proof;
- selective omission for dramatic convenience;
- invented motive;
- invented coordination;
- treating allegation as establishment.

The campaign's editorial bargain is:

> **Here is our entertaining interpretation of the evidence.**
>
> **Here is the evidence underneath it.**
>
> **You do not have to accept the interpretation.**

And:

> **Never make the facts more dramatic than they are.**
>
> **Do not make the telling less dramatic than the facts are.**

Or, more compactly:

> **Ridiculous upstairs. Painfully precise downstairs.**

Legal and evidential caution constrains claims.

It does not require aesthetic illiteracy.

## 🕸️ 10. Networks, Power, and Coordination

Networks matter because relationships can affect capability.

The campaign may examine relationships involving:

- political access;
- professional relationships;
- legal expertise;
- institutional memory;
- government experience;
- commercial relationships;
- technical expertise;
- technical dependency;
- media access;
- defence and security relationships;
- statutory authority;
- contractual rights;
- parliamentary powers;
- specialist knowledge;
- information access.

These are not interchangeable forms of power.

They should not be collapsed into one generic category merely because each can matter.

### Network position as resource

A person's or institution's position within a network can affect:

- what information reaches them;
- who will answer them;
- what expertise they can mobilise;
- what procedures they understand;
- what audiences they can reach;
- what institutional memory they can access;
- what formal powers they possess;
- what informal resources are available to them.

Therefore:

> **Network position is a resource.**

But:

> **The existence of a network does not establish coordination,
> conspiracy, shared motive, or control.**

### Analytical chain

Where a claim about networked power matters, prefer:

> **actor → relationship → resource → authority/capability → pathway →
> observable consequence**

Do not invent a missing arrow.

If the evidence establishes the actor and the relationship but not the pathway, say so.

If it establishes capability but not use, preserve the distinction.

If it establishes access but not influence, preserve the distinction.

If it establishes strategic usefulness but not intent, preserve the distinction.

> **Strategic usefulness is not proof of strategic intent.**

## 🕳️ 11. Hidden Power and the Deep-State Firewall

The campaign may examine power that is difficult for outsiders to observe.

That is not inherently conspiratorial.

Modern institutions can contain:

- formal authority that is poorly understood;
- institutional continuity across governments;
- professional networks;
- security and defence structures;
- confidential decision-making;
- commercial confidentiality;
- legal privilege;
- specialist expertise;
- informal access;
- bureaucratic memory;
- technical dependency;
- contractual relationships;
- information asymmetries;
- legitimate secrecy;
- illegitimate concealment;
- combinations of the above.

People may use broad phrases such as "deep state" to describe very different phenomena.

The campaign should therefore resist both easy collapses:

1. **Everything difficult to see is evidence of a secret coordinating
    power.**
2. **Hidden, informal, opaque, or improperly concealed institutional
    power never exists.**

Neither is an adequate method.

The method is to open the bonnet.

Map the actors.

Map the relationships.

Identify the resource.

Identify the relevant authority or capability.

Find the pathway.

Look for the observable consequence.

Distinguish what is known from what is inferred.

### Canonical rule

> **Power that is difficult to see is an invitation to map mechanisms,
> not permission to invent mechanisms.**

And, for avoidance of doubt:

> **The Kraken is not the deep state.**

## 🪷 12. Tulpa-Like Institutional Objects

The campaign sometimes uses **tulpa-like** as an analytical metaphor for the informational and reputational persistence of institutions and organisations.

This is a local project meaning.

It must not be silently replaced by an occult, supernatural, psychiatric, or internet-subculture definition.

### Material or formal entity

A real institution or organisation may consist of:

- people;
- offices;
- property;
- records;
- budgets;
- technologies;
- contracts;
- procedures;
- legal structures;
- statutory powers;
- formal responsibilities;
- institutional resources.

Those components can change substantially over time.

The institution may nevertheless remain socially intelligible as the same named object.

### Reputational or informational object

"The Met", "Palantir", "Labour", or another institution can also exist as an accumulated informational object across:

- public memory;
- institutional memory;
- journalism;
- political discourse;
- professional discourse;
- reputation;
- expectation;
- previous conduct;
- stories;
- symbols;
- assumptions;
- anticipated future behaviour.

No single current participant necessarily controls that object.

Different audiences may maintain substantially different representations of the same underlying entity.

Those representations can nevertheless affect real behaviour.

### Feedback

A simplified loop may look like:

> **entity → representation → response → changed entity → changed
> representation**

An institution acts.

That action affects how the institution is represented.

People and other institutions respond partly to that representation.

The underlying institution observes or anticipates those responses and may change its own behaviour.

The representation changes again.

♻️

### Hard constraints

"Tulpa-like" does not mean:

- imaginary;
- hallucinated;
- literally supernatural;
- literally conjured by collective belief;
- that reputation is identical to material reality;
- that public perception proves conduct;
- that the existence of a shared representation proves coordination;
- that the underlying institution can be ignored.

The material entity and the reputational object interact.

They are not identical.

## 📚 13. Literary, Mythological, and Folkloric Language

The campaign uses literary, mythological, folkloric, occult-adjacent, gaming, technical, and institutional language deliberately.

Language models can be very fluent with this material while remaining brittle about continuity.

A term may have accumulated meanings across:

- British folklore;
- wider European folklore;
- mythology;
- literature;
- religion;
- occult traditions;
- popular culture;
- tabletop gaming;
- computing;
- cybersecurity;
- internet culture;
- contemporary politics.

Technical culture has also borrowed heavily from older literary and mythological vocabularies.

A language model may therefore encounter a familiar term and activate a plausible meaning that is nevertheless the wrong meaning for this project.

### Common failure modes

#### Semantic gravity

A more common or statistically familiar meaning overwhelms the local definition.

#### Ontology drift

A recurring figure gradually changes category between drafts.

#### Metaphor literalisation

A figurative relationship becomes an asserted real-world mechanism.

#### Canon leakage

A joke, satire, or invented scenario becomes apparent factual backstory.

#### Cast leakage

A previously mentioned person or institution wanders into a later node without being brought into scope.

#### Narrative compression

A complicated relationship is summarised into a simpler but materially different one.

### Canonical rule

> **Fluency is not continuity.**

And:

> **The fact that a model can write beautifully about a Kraken does not
> mean it has remembered which Kraken this is.**

The Pearls therefore err deliberately on the side of explicit definitions and explicit exclusions.

## 🧿 14. Kraken Imagery and Provenance

The Kraken imagery has a specific local function and must retain that provenance.

The project uses a sea creature partly because the Kraken works as a British maritime and folkloric image for an ambient creature beneath the islands: something capable of registering a disturbance above without itself being one of the institutional combatants.

The imagery must not be allowed to drift into the conspiratorial octopus or tentacle trope in which a hidden ethnic, religious, political, commercial, or cabalistic actor secretly controls society through spreading tentacles.

That history matters.

The project does not use the Kraken to represent secret Jewish control, secret ethnic control, a hidden cabal, a corporate conspiracy, a political conspiracy, or a master network controlling Britain.

### Hard visual and analytical constraints

Do not:

- map Kraken tentacles onto named networks;
- use tentacles as shorthand for secret influence;
- depict institutions as limbs of the Kraken;
- make the Kraken the hidden controller behind the diagram;
- turn the Kraken into a visual synonym for conspiracy;
- use the creature to imply coordination that the evidence does not
    establish.

The people above create the disturbance.

The Kraken is the creature disturbed by it.

That distinction protects both the campaign ontology and the provenance of the imagery.

## ♻️ 15. Information Is Part of the Terrain

The Great Kraken Disturbance is a worked teaching example within a wider information ecology.

The campaign is interested not only in what institutions do, but in what happens when information about what they do enters other systems.

A simplified feedback loop may be:

> **event → record or disclosure → interpretation → institutional use →
> response → new information → changed environment → further response**

A document can change the terrain it describes.

A parliamentary answer can become an input into later journalism, legal analysis, political argument, institutional decision-making, or further questions.

A disclosure can alter what other actors know.

A public statement can create a record that later actors respond to.

A legal filing can reorganise the publicly available description of a dispute.

A piece of reporting can change which questions are subsequently asked.

This does not mean every output was produced with every later use in mind.

### Canonical distinction

> **Strategic usefulness does not establish strategic intent.**

Information can acquire new uses after it enters the environment.

The campaign should track those feedback effects without retroactively inventing intentions for the people who produced the original information.

## 🔐 16. Secrecy, Opacity, and Information Asymmetry

The campaign may encounter information that is unavailable, partially available, disputed, confidential, privileged, restricted, redacted, withheld, or simply not yet located.

Different kinds of secrecy have different legal and institutional bases.

Do not collapse them into one generic category.

Possible contexts may include:

- government confidentiality;
- commercial confidentiality;
- legal professional privilege;
- court restrictions;
- national-security considerations;
- defence-related information;
- foreign-government sensitivities;
- personal data;
- procurement confidentiality;
- information that is simply not published.

The presence of secrecy does not by itself establish wrongdoing.

The existence of a legitimate secrecy regime does not establish that every use of secrecy is legitimate.

The analytical task is to identify, where possible:

- what information is unavailable;
- who appears to hold it;
- why it is unavailable;
- what legal or procedural regime is said to apply;
- who can review it;
- who can compel it;
- who can publish it;
- what can still be established without it;
- what conclusions must remain unresolved because of its absence.

Do not assign a specific secrecy regime to a particular document or fact without evidence that the regime applies.

## 🧱 17. Capability, Access, Use, Attribution, and Causation

The campaign must preserve distinctions between different levels of connection.

These are not interchangeable:

- an actor has a capability;
- an actor has access;
- an actor had an opportunity;
- an actor used the capability;
- a particular action is attributable to that actor;
- the action caused a particular consequence.

Likewise:

- knowing somebody is not the same as receiving assistance from them;
- receiving information is not the same as acting on it;
- being able to influence something is not evidence that influence occurred;
- benefiting from an event is not evidence of causing it;
- sharing an interest is not evidence of coordination;
- occupying adjacent networks is not evidence of a common plan.

Where the record supports only one rung, stop at that rung.

Do not climb the ladder because the next step makes the story cleaner.

## 🚪 19. What Does Not Persist Automatically

The following do **not** become campaign-wide canon merely because they appeared previously:

- named humans;
- named organisations irrelevant to the current node;
- jokes about particular people;
- fictional character classes;
- invented dialogue;
- temporary factions;
- temporary battle names;
- narrative interpretations specific to one episode;
- unresolved hypotheses;
- assumptions about motive;
- assumptions about coordination;
- facts whose current status may have changed;
- a previous node's emotional or comic framing;
- a source's characterisation of an actor;
- a model's earlier inference.

A previous summary is not a source of independent evidence.

A recurring joke is not cumulative proof.

Campaign memory must not become campaign fact by repetition.

## 🧭 20. Standard Node Relationship

Individual Kraken nodes may vary, particularly when a node is primarily methodological, historical, or satirical.

Where a node is documenting a live or historical institutional episode, the preferred movement is:

> **🍿 Story → 📋 Record → ⚖️ Mechanism → ❓ Unknowns → 📰 Questions → 🔭 Sensors → 🧭 Lesson**

### 🍿 Story

Make the sequence humanly legible.

### 📋 Record

Establish what actually happened and how we know.

### ⚖️ Mechanism

Explain the relevant law, procedure, procurement machinery, institutional authority, or information process in ordinary language.

### ❓ Unknowns

Identify genuine evidential gaps.

Do not use rhetorical questions to smuggle in allegations.

### 📰 Questions

Ask questions capable of exposing the information architecture:

- Who knows?
- Who recorded it?
- Who holds the record?
- Who can compel it?
- Who can publish it?
- Who has already asked?
- What answer was given?

### 🔭 Sensors

Identify what to watch next:

- hearings;
- judgments;
- disclosures;
- parliamentary answers;
- procurement notices;
- amendments;
- regulatory decisions;
- institutional responses;
- new documents;
- policy changes.

Sensors are not predictions.

### 🧭 Lesson

Return to the wider teaching purpose.

What does this episode help a reader see about:

- procurement;
- law;
- institutional power;
- information;
- networks;
- secrecy;
- transparency;
- accountability;
- public administration;
- feedback environments?

The precise headings may change where the material requires it.

The epistemic functions should remain recognisable.

- turn the Kraken into the state;
- turn the Kraken into the deep state;
- turn institutions or networks into Kraken tentacles;
- turn satire into evidence;
- turn narrative dialogue into factual quotation;
- invent motives;
- invent coordination;
- invent relationships;
- invent causation;
- import named parties through semantic association;
- treat network adjacency as participation;
- confuse reputational objects with material entities;
- treat networks as permanent factions;
- treat capability as use;
- treat access as influence;
- treat benefit as causation;
- resolve genuine uncertainty for narrative convenience;
- silently upgrade a party's claim into documented fact;
- treat an old factual state as automatically current;
- allow a metaphor to change ontology without explicit authorial revision;
- allow a previous joke to become permanent biographical characterisation;
- make documentary prose inherit the certainty of satire;
- flatten every secrecy regime into one generic hidden-power claim;
- infer a conspiracy because institutional mechanisms are difficult to see;
- remove the camp merely because the underlying subject is serious.

When evidence changes, update the analysis.

When the joke loses a fight with the evidence, the joke loses.

---

## 🧪 22. Pre-Draft Machine Check

Before drafting or materially revising a Kraken node, establish:

1. **What mode am I writing?**\ Narrative, satire, record, analysis, or a clearly marked transition between them?

2. **Who has actually been cast?**\ Which named parties are explicitly in scope for this node?

3. **Which real-world events are in scope?**\ What is the actual episode being described?

4. **What evidence status does each important claim have?**\ Documented, party's position, reported, our reading, or unknown?

5. **Which metaphors are active?**\ Kraken, wizardry, military history, tabletop gaming, tulpa-like institutions, or another device?

6. **What must those metaphors not become?**\ Have their ontological boundaries been preserved?

7. **What remains unknown?**\ Am I allowing the unknown to remain unknown?

8. **Am I narrating the fight rather than joining it?**\ Have I accidentally selected a team?

9. **Have I imported anything merely because it feels associated?**\ A person, institution, motive, relationship, joke, or prior interpretation?

10. **Have I preserved the distinction between story and record?** Would a reader know which claims require belief and which are expressly play?

11. **Have I preserved capability/access/use/attribution/causation distinctions?**

12. **Have I mistaken strategic usefulness for strategic intent?**

13. **Have I mistaken a network for coordination?**

14. **Have I allowed literary fluency to override campaign continuity?**

15. **Would the Kraken recognise herself?**

If uncertain:

> **Preserve the distinction. Do not complete the pattern.**

## 🫧 23. Canon Changes

This file is intended to be durable, not immutable.

The author may revise campaign canon as the project develops.

When a rule changes materially:

- change it explicitly;
- update the date;
- update the machine-readable definitions where relevant;
- do not leave contradictory rules active merely because an older formulation once existed;
- preserve useful historical context where necessary to understand earlier nodes.

New recurring jokes do not automatically require canon changes.

New named participants do not automatically require canon changes.

Canon should change when the **rules of the world or method** change.

The Kraken may acquire more pearls.

She does not need a constitutional amendment every time somebody acquires another KC.
