	
# 🫧 Kraken's Pearls

**First created:** 2026-09-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-18  
*Public methodology, continuity infrastructure, and shiny things for
keeping The Great Kraken Disturbance coherent across humans, machines,
jokes, evidence, and time.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The Kraken likes pearls.

This folder contains hers.

`🫧_Krakens_Pearls` is the continuity and rules layer for **The Great
Kraken Disturbance**. It holds the definitions, editorial rules,
narrative conventions, evidential boundaries, recurring metaphors,
taxonomies, and other pieces of campaign canon that a human collaborator
should know --- and that an AI collaborator should be given --- before
working on the series.

The Pearls exist because a long-running project develops its own
language.

A word can become a character. A character can become a metaphor. A
metaphor can acquire rules. A joke can recur often enough to look
suspiciously like canon. A real institution can exist simultaneously as
a legal or material entity, a public reputation, a media object, and a
narrative character. If those categories are allowed to bleed together,
the writing may remain fluent while becoming conceptually wrong.

The Pearls are where the borders live.

They are also deliberately public.

## 🫧 Why Pearls?

Partly, this is ordinary methodological transparency.

Readers should be able to see how **The Great Kraken Disturbance** distinguishes between evidence, interpretation, narrative, satire, unresolved questions, and things that are simply very funny.

Partly, it is an experiment in information architecture.

Writers, journalists, researchers, archivists, investigators, and other people working with contemporary language models increasingly face the same problem: a model may have an enormous amount of context available to it while still misunderstanding the structure of that context.

More information is not necessarily better information.

**Context has architecture.**

A useful working environment tells the model:

-   what a term means here;
-   what it expressly does **not** mean;
-   which distinctions must survive summarisation;
-   what persists between documents;
-   what is local to one document;
-   which people are actually in the current cast;
-   when a metaphor is being used;
-   when a section is expressly fictional or satirical;
-   when the capes come off and the documentary record begins;
-   and where uncertainty must remain uncertainty.

The Pearls are therefore not a giant bag of background material.

They are labelled containers.

## 🤖 Fluency Is Not Continuity

Large language models can work impressively with literary, poetic, mythological, folkloric, technical, and satirical language.

They can also produce beautifully fluent prose while quietly changing what something **is**.

This becomes particularly noticeable in long-running literary or analytical projects built around recurring symbolic figures.

Words such as *Kraken*, *tulpa*, *ghost*, *daemon*, *wizard*, *network*, *apparition*, and *deep state* arrive with large semantic neighbourhoods. Some meanings come from folklore, mythology, religion, occult traditions, literature, politics, or popular culture. Others have been borrowed into computing, gaming, security, systems language, or internet culture.

The model does not automatically know which inheritance this project intends to activate.

That can produce several kinds of drift:

-   **semantic gravity** --- a more familiar meaning overwhelms the
    local project definition;
-   **ontology drift** --- a recurring thing gradually changes category;
-   **metaphor literalisation** --- a figurative relationship becomes an
    asserted mechanism;
-   **canon leakage** --- a joke or satirical invention becomes apparent
    factual backstory;
-   **cast leakage** --- somebody previously mentioned wanders into a
    later episode without being invited;
-   **narrative compression** --- a complicated relationship is
    summarised into a simpler but materially different one.

This is why the Pearls sometimes look unusually strict.

They specify not only meanings, but relationships between meanings.

The Kraken may stir when the King's Peace is disturbed.

That does **not** mean the Kraken caused the disturbance.

Those sentences use almost identical vocabulary. They describe completely different relationships.

**Fluency is not continuity.**

The fact that a model can write beautifully about a Kraken does not mean it has remembered which Kraken this is.

🦑 *She is extremely particular about this.*

## 🦑 The Precious Pearl Principle

Within **The Great Kraken Disturbance**, the Kraken is a narrative representation of the **King's Peace**: an ambient creature or force imagined somewhere beneath the islands of the British archipelago.

She is not a substitute label for an institution, government, company, political faction, professional network, secret controller, or conspiracy.

The disturbance happens above her.

She notices.

She may sleep. She may stir. She may become perturbed. She may make deeply unimpressed underwater noises about what the men are doing.

That is quite enough responsibility for one squid.

The same principle applies throughout this folder: recurring terms receive local definitions, and those definitions should not be silently replaced by something that happens to be linguistically adjacent.

When the Pearls say that a distinction matters, **keep the distinction**.

## 🎭 The Writing Has Modes

The series deliberately uses more than one register.

A reader should not have to guess which one they are currently reading.

### 🍿 Narrative

Narrative sections tell a real sequence with heightened storytelling: military history, fantasy, wizard fights, tabletop mechanics, melodrama, institutional farce, camp, or whatever other device makes consequential but frequently impenetrable machinery intelligible to an ordinary human being.

The telling may be ridiculous.

The underlying sequence must remain grounded.

### 🎪 Satire

Some nodes or sections go substantially further and are expressly written as **lol, wouldn't this be funny**.

Those sections are clearly marked, normally with a short blockquote near the beginning.

> **🎪 Satire mode:** This section is deliberately fictionalised and heightened for comic effect. Real people or institutions may appear as narrative characters, but invented dialogue, character classes, motives, reactions, and scenarios are jokes rather than factual claims.

A satire container means we do not need to interrupt every joke with a tiny lawyer carrying a disclaimer.

It also means the joke stays in its container.

### 📋 Record

Then the capes come off.

Documentary sections distinguish the underlying events, sources, positions, interpretations, mechanisms, and unresolved questions. Invented dialogue does not migrate downstairs. Narrative casting does not become evidence of motive. A good joke does not become a source.

Where useful, the record distinguishes:

-   **Documented**
-   **Party's position**
-   **Reported**
-   **Our reading**
-   **Unknown**

The governing bargain with the reader is simple:

> Here is our entertaining interpretation of the evidence.
>
> Here is the evidence underneath it.
>
> You do not have to accept the interpretation.

## 🍿 We Are Narrating the Fight, Not Joining It

**The Great Kraken Disturbance** does not require the narrator to select a team.

The series borrows a useful instinct from the tabloid press: notice characters, conflict, escalation, reversals, absurdity, memorable details, callbacks, and the moment somebody appears to have acquired another lawyer.

It does **not** borrow tabloid epistemology.

Narrative excitement is not evidence.

Comic sympathy is not a factual finding.

One participant producing a spectacular counter-move does not make that participant the hero. A later document complicating their position does not make somebody else the hero instead.

The camera can follow whoever has just made the story more interesting.

The documentary record gets the final say about the documentary record.

**The story may have characters. The investigation does not have teams.**

---

## 🎬 The Machine Does Not Cast the Show

Definitions and campaign rules can persist.

Named parties do not automatically persist.

A real person, organisation, institution, company, journalist, lawyer, official, political actor, or other identifiable party should not be imported into a node merely because they appeared elsewhere in the campaign, once participated in a running joke, or happen to be semantically associated with the subject.

Named parties enter when the author brings them in, when the material being drafted brings them in, or when they are genuinely necessary to explain an already in-scope documented event.

A cross-link is not a casting call.

Semantic relevance is not a speaking role.

**The machine does not cast the show. The author casts the show.**

## 🪷 Tulpa-Like Things and Other Local Language

Some Pearls define terms that Polaris uses in a deliberately local or extended way.

For example, **tulpa-like** can be useful for thinking about the informational and reputational persistence of institutions.

A real institution has material and formal components: people, property, records, budgets, powers, contracts, technologies, offices, procedures, and legal structures.

It can also persist as a socially maintained object in public and institutional imagination: a name carrying accumulated memories, expectations, associations, narratives, reputations, and assumptions that no single current participant fully controls.

Those layers can interact.

That does not make the institution imaginary.

It does not mean collective belief literally conjures a supernatural
entity.

And it certainly does not mean somebody is about to summon Palantir as a psychic egregore because the machine found the occult definition first.

Local terminology exists because it helps us think.

The definitions exist because we would quite like the machine to think about the **same thing**.

## 🕸️ Networks Are Not Conspiracies

The series frequently examines networks because relationships matter.

Political relationships, professional relationships, institutional memory, statutory authority, legal expertise, commercial relationships, technical dependency, media access, government experience, defence and security relationships, and contractual rights can all affect what an
actor is capable of doing.

A network position can therefore be a resource.

The existence of a network does not establish coordination, common motive, conspiracy, or control.

Where power is difficult to see, the method is to map the mechanism:

**actor → relationship → resource → authority or capability → pathway → observable consequence**

If a link cannot be established, leave the link unestablished.

**Power that is difficult to see is an invitation to map mechanisms, not permission to invent mechanisms.**

The Kraken herself is not the hidden network behind the diagram.

She is trying to sleep underneath it.

## 🧾 Human Layer, Machine Layer

The Pearls use two complementary forms.

### Markdown

Markdown holds the rich explanation:

-   why a rule exists;
-   examples and counterexamples;
-   edge cases;
-   narrative conventions;
-   methodological reasoning;
-   and the human meaning that would be flattened by turning everything
    into fields.

### YAML

YAML provides a compact canonical layer for definitions and constraints.

It can state, in deliberately unromantic terms, that:

``` yaml
kraken:
  ontological_type: narrative_figure
  represents: kings_peace
  causes_disturbance: false
  detects_disturbance: true
  is_institution: false
  is_network: false
  is_faction: false
  is_secret_controller: false
```

Sometimes the most useful thing we can tell a language model is:

**stop doing literary association for thirty seconds; these are the borders.**

Then we can give her back her poetry.

## 🧰 What the Pearls Are For

This folder is intended to make long-running human - AI collaboration more legible and less fragile.

It can help preserve:

-   terminology;
-   narrative ontology;
-   evidential boundaries;
-   casting discipline;
-   recurring jokes without converting them into facts;
-   distinctions between material entities and reputational objects;
-   local meanings of literary and folkloric language;
-   editorial position;
-   continuity between nodes;
-   and explicit uncertainty.

It can also make mistakes easier to diagnose.

If a later draft suddenly decides that the Kraken is a government agency, that a satirical character sheet is biographical evidence, or that somebody mentioned three months ago has inexplicably materialised in a scene, there is somewhere canonical to point and say:

**No. Pearl violation. Put it back.**

---

## ⚠️ What the Pearls Are Not

This is not a claim that careful prompting solves AI safety.

It does not substitute for:

-   source checking;
-   research;
-   verification;
-   legal or specialist review where required;
-   editorial judgement;
-   responsible model development;
-   safety engineering;
-   or a human noticing that the machine has confidently produced
    bollocks.

The Pearls are simply an information-architecture technique.

Language models generally have an easier working environment when important categories are explicit, terminology is stable, boundaries are legible, and the task tells them what must not be collapsed.

That is useful.

It is not magic.

Despite the squid.

---

## 🛠️ Borrow This Method

Nothing about the underlying method is Kraken-specific.

A writer could maintain definitions for characters, chronology, point of view, fictional canon, and deliberate ambiguities.

A journalist could separate sourced facts, attributed claims, working hypotheses, unanswered questions, protected material, and language that must not be strengthened beyond the evidence.

A researcher could define contested terminology, evidential grades, scope conditions, exclusions, dataset boundaries, and the difference between an observed relationship and an inferred mechanism.

An archivist could specify naming conventions, provenance states, version rules, uncertain dates, and relationships between copies.

The precise taxonomy will change.

The principle does not:

> **Do not merely give the model context. Give the context structure.**

Or, in Kraken:

> 🦑 Give her shiny, well-labelled pearls and she is considerably less likely to start throwing the furniture around.
