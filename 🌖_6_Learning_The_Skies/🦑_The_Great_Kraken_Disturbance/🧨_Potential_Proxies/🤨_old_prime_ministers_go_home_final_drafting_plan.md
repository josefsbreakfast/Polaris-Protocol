# 🤨 Old Prime Ministers Go Home — Final Drafting Plan
**First created:** 2026-09-19 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-19  
*Pre-drafting scaffold for a Potential Proxies node about former prime ministers, elite social topology, parliamentary scrutiny, protective security, information environments, and the difference between an interesting network and an invented arrow.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

### Working destination

`🌖_6_Learning_The_Skies/🦑_The_Great_Kraken_Disturbance/🧨_Potential_Proxies/🤨_old_prime_ministers_go_home.md`

This is **not** a claim that Boris Johnson, Palantir, Russia, the Metropolitan Police, MOPAC, Jonathan Powell, Cambridge Analytica, Peter Mandelson, George Osborne, Peter Thiel, the BBC, or anybody else belongs to one coordinated structure.

It belongs in **Potential Proxies** because the Battle of Waterloo Place has made a series of adjacent networks, historical relationships, unresolved parliamentary questions, information-environment effects, and institutional seams newly worth preserving.

### Central question

> **What is a former prime minister after he stops being prime minister?**

The office ends. The accumulated address book does not.

Former prime ministers may retain:

- international recognisability;
- political and symbolic significance;
- relationships formed through government, media, finance, diplomacy, security and business;
- access to current and former office-holders;
- public-duty and commercial activity;
- protective-security needs;
- the ability to attract extraordinary media attention;
- and a continuing capacity to intersect with the state without being part of the government of the day.

The node asks what happens when those retained networks and capacities meet **public accountability, national security, foreign policy, commercial power and computational attention systems**.

### Methodological rule

The node should repeatedly distinguish:

- **Documented** — established by primary records or reliable reporting;
- **Reported** — attributed claim requiring source preservation;
- **Our reading** — analytical interpretation;
- **Unknown** — not established from the public record.

The narrative may be funny. The evidential layer must not become funny about what it knows.

## 🤵 2. They Know A Bloke Too

Use class as a way of **demystifying elite networks**, not as an evidential shortcut.

The social mechanism is ordinary:

- people know people through school;
- university;
- work;
- family;
- holidays;
- journalism;
- political parties;
- professional advisers;
- clubs;
- business;
- shared friends.

In a working-class community, social capital might mean knowing a plumber, mechanic, roofer, union rep, care worker, electrician or somebody who can get a decent second-hand washing machine.

At the top of British political life, the same ordinary mechanism can produce acquaintances who are:

- billionaires;
- newspaper proprietors;
- serving and former ministers;
- diplomats;
- defence executives;
- financiers;
- intelligence veterans;
- foreign political intermediaries;
- senior civil servants;
- technology executives.

### Teaching line

> **They know a bloke too. Their bloke may simply have considerably more geopolitical metadata.**

The fact of elite sociability is not itself suspicious.

The relevant question is what happens when an ordinary social relationship intersects with:

- ministerial responsibility;
- classified or sensitive information;
- public appointments;
- procurement;
- political donations;
- hospitality;
- national-security exposure;
- foreign-state interests;
- public money.

### Important topology rule

> **Party boundaries are political boundaries. They are not reliable social-network boundaries.**

Do not infer social separation from parliamentary seating.

## 🫀 4. The Joke Stops Here

The node needs an explicit moral floor.

Politicians are allowed:

- friends;
- private lives;
- dinners;
- holidays;
- humour;
- careers;
- ridiculous decorating preferences.

But public power is not merely a career experience for the people downstream of it.

Policy and institutional decisions can affect whether people have:

- housing;
- healthcare;
- adequate income;
- safe public services;
- functioning schools;
- protection from violence;
- economic opportunity;
- viable futures for their children.

### Core line

> **The fact that politics contains ridiculous people does not make political power ridiculous.**

And:

> **We use the soap opera because humans remember stories. We scrutinise the people in those stories because the consequences of their decisions are not stories to the people living with them.**

Avoid monocausal claims such as “poverty causes extremism”. Instead preserve the narrower proposition: material insecurity, institutional failure, alienation, distrust and information manipulation can interact in ways that damage democratic legitimacy and create opportunities for anti-system or extremist mobilisation.

Scrutiny is one mechanism for repairing the democratic feedback loop.

## 🇺🇦 7. Why Was Boris In Ukraine?

- Why was Johnson in Ukraine?
- Who invited him?
- In what capacity was he travelling?
- Was the activity official, public-duty, political, conference-related, privately organised, or some combination?
- Who organised his travel?

- What caused the early departure?
- What warnings were received?
- What happened at Yahodyn?
- What did Ukrainian Railways assess?

> **Whose problem was Boris Johnson that weekend?**

## 🛡️ 8. A Walking Security Hazard

Translate the conversational `shit magnet` into an institutional problem.

A former prime minister can retain:

- extreme recognisability;
- symbolic importance;
- hostile-state interest;
- political salience;
- unusual international mobility;
- relationships with current officials;
- personal agency to undertake activity the government may not control.

This creates a governance problem even if everyone involved is behaving properly.

### The paradox

```text
             BORIS JOHNSON
                  │
         extreme recognisability
            /             \
           ↓               ↓
communications value    security cost
           │               │
instant public signal   conspicuous person
           │               │
           └──── ATTENTION ─┘
```

The same feature that makes a former PM useful as a public symbol can make them complicated to move safely.

### Karp / Mosley comparison

Use the Palantir-book episode as a **protective-security comparison**, once the exact passage/page is retrieved.

The useful question is not whether Alex Karp and Boris Johnson are equivalent.

It is:

> **How do organisations reason about moving conspicuous, strategically significant civilians through an active war zone, and who owns the consequences if it goes wrong?**

Mark the remembered Mosley passage as **book-derived — exact citation to retrieve** until verified.

## 🔁 11. Nobody Needs To Make A Phone Call

This is the information-environment teaching section.

Separate at least five mechanisms.

### A. Direct coordination

```text
actor
  ↓
recipient
  ↓
requested behaviour
```

### B. Professional/network transmission

```text
consultants / advisers / organisations
          ↓
techniques and assumptions travel
          ↓
different political actors
```

### C. Deliberate environmental perturbation

```text
state / campaign / influence actor
          ↓
injects or amplifies material
          ↓
platform systems
          ↓
altered information environment
          ↓
people make their own decisions inside it
```

### D. Endogenous algorithmic feedback

```text
content performs
      ↓
algorithm increases exposure
      ↓
audience expectations adapt
      ↓
producers adapt
      ↓
similar content performs
      ↺
```

### E. Convergent adaptation

Two actors can arrive at similar behaviour because the same environment rewards the same behaviour.

No interpersonal transmission is required.

### Central methodological pair

> **Similarity is not evidence of coordination.**

> **Absence of direct coordination is not evidence of absence of influence.**

### Russia / foreign interference

When discussing Russian interference, make clear that modern influence can involve:

- direct covert relationships;
- money;
- cyber operations;
- information operations;
- amplification of existing grievances;
- bots or coordinated accounts;
- manipulation of recommendation systems;
- opportunistic exploitation of already-occurring events.

Do not imply that every right-wing narrative, algorithmic effect or political similarity is Russian activity.

Russia can exploit an information ecology without creating every belief circulating inside it.

## 🎭 12. Is Boris The Instrument Or Just Boris?

Keep three evidential levels separate.

### Documented

Potentially measurable:

- Johnson's recognisability;
- frequency with which he becomes headline protagonist;
- prominence relative to less famous serving officials;
- timing of coverage.

### Our reading

Johnson can function as an **attention sink**.

His presence can lower the contextual cost of encoding a complicated event:

```text
complex institutional event
        ↓
BORIS enters frame
        ↓
instant recognition
        ↓
cheapest narrative encoding becomes BORIS
        ↓
coverage becomes Boris-shaped
        ↓
Boris-shaped coverage performs
        ↓
news environment learns USE BORIS
        ↺
```

### Unknown

Whether anybody deliberately deploys Johnson to distract from another story in any particular case.

### Important insight

> **Instrumentalisation does not always require an identifiable instrumentaliser.**

A computational attention system can repeatedly make somebody useful as an instrument because their name, face and existing cultural metadata reliably capture attention.

### Empirical test

Rather than assuming displacement, build a small dataset:

- Palantir / MOPAC / procurement developments;
- parliamentary-question answers;
- Johnson stories in the same windows;
- outlet;
- timestamp;
- prominence;
- headline;
- article volume;
- subsequent follow-up.

Possible outcomes:

1. no displacement pattern → kill the hypothesis;
2. persistent attention effect without coordination evidence → information-ecology finding;
3. outlet-specific effect → investigate editorial/system differences;
4. actor-specific or pressure-point-specific effect → earn another research question.

## 🏛️ 13. “It’s Complicated.” “Okay. Why?”

This is the anti-intimidation section for MPs who do not consider defence, intelligence or foreign affairs their specialist territory.

### Core rule

> **Complexity can be real without being mystical.**

If the answer is:

> “It's complicated.”

Ask:

> **“Okay. Why?”**

Then decompose.

```text
"It's complicated."
        ↓
What makes it complicated?
        ↓
multiple departments?
different legal duties?
security classification?
international partners?
operational risk?
unclear responsibility?
        ↓
name the components
        ↓
ask the responsible body about its component
```

MPs do not need to conduct intelligence assessments.

They can ask ordinary governance questions:

- Who was responsible?
- In what capacity was this person acting?
- Which department owned the decision?
- What procedure applied?
- Was it followed?
- What records exist?
- Was advice provided?
- Was that advice followed?
- Was public money used?
- Was a review conducted?
- Did guidance change?
- Is the government satisfied that the appropriate process operated?

A legitimate national-security refusal is not necessarily a failed question. It may establish:

- jurisdiction;
- the responsible authority;
- the existence of a procedure;
- the existence of a review;
- the boundary of permissible disclosure.

Then the next question can be narrower.

## 🪵 14. Do A Mike Wood — Five-Second Guide To Parliamentary Questions

This should be a practical worked module.

### The principle

> **Do not ask Parliament to confirm your theory. Ask the responsible minister for one missing fact. Then let that fact determine Question Two.**

### Construction machine

```text
THING I HAVE NOTICED
        ↓
What part touches government?
        ↓
Which department owns that part?
        ↓
What ONE missing fact would distinguish
between plausible explanations?
        ↓
Ask for that fact neutrally.
        ↓
READ THE ANSWER LITERALLY.
        ↓
What does it establish?
What does it NOT establish?
        ↓
What new distinction did it create?
        ↓
QUESTION TWO
```

### Why this works for opposition ecosystems too

A Labour MP does not need jurisdiction over Boris Johnson's private life in order to learn about the state's interaction with him.

Ask the **public half of the interface**.

```text
external / private actor
          │
          │ interaction
          ▼
       GOVERNMENT
          │
          ├── correspondence
          ├── meeting records
          ├── procurement
          ├── expenditure
          ├── guidance
          ├── reviews
          ├── approvals
          └── departmental responsibility
          │
          ▼
 PARLIAMENTARY QUESTION
```

Government records can illuminate the interface without Parliament pretending to govern an external actor's entire life.

### Worked example: Boris / Ukraine

Research thought:

> **What the fuck was Boris doing on that train with Jonathan Powell?**

Do **not** submit that.

Possible first question:

> To ask the Minister for the Cabinet Office, whether the Cabinet Office was notified in advance of the visit to Ukraine by the Rt Hon Boris Johnson in September 2026.

If **yes**:

> whether that visit was considered under the normal arrangements applicable to overseas travel by former Prime Ministers.

If the answer invokes security confidentiality:

> whether the Government has reviewed the general protective-security guidance applicable to former Prime Ministers travelling in active conflict zones following the strike at Yahodyn on 13 September 2026.

Separately:

> whether Jonathan Powell was travelling in Ukraine in his official capacity as National Security Adviser on 13 September 2026.

### Worked example: responsibility

If an answer says:

> “The Cabinet Office was not involved.”

Do not write **COVER-UP**.

Ask:

> **Which department or public body, if any, held responsibility?**

### Worked example: Mike Wood method

Use verified Palantir PQs to show the sequence:

```text
Who invited X?
        ↓
answer changes map
        ↓
Did Y correspond with Z?
        ↓
answer changes map
        ↓
Does department hold document?
        ↓
yes / no becomes evidence
        ↓
next narrow question
```

The point is **question chaining**, not question volume.

### Pocket card

> **What fact am I missing?**  
> **Who in government should possess it?**  
> **Can I ask for it without embedding my conclusion?**  
> **What does the answer establish?**  
> **What does it not establish?**  
> **What is the smallest useful next question?**

## ⚖️ 15. Scrutiny Is Not Accusation

This should sit immediately after the PQ guide.

An MP can ask about:

- a former minister's interaction with government;
- a security review;
- a procurement process;
- a foreign visit;
- an appointment;
- a donor relationship;
- a meeting;
- departmental records;

without first deciding that misconduct occurred.

Useful formulation:

> **Something consequential happened. Please demonstrate that the machinery designed for this situation operated properly.**

A boring answer can be a very good answer:

- procedure engaged;
- appropriate authority notified;
- advice provided;
- arrangements adapted as threat changed;
- incident reviewed;
- no systemic deficiency identified.

The purpose is not to make the interesting theory survive.

The purpose is to find out what happened.

## 🧭 16. Government Is Not The Same Thing As Party

Make the distinction explicit for newer MPs.

A governing-party MP asking their own government a question about an opposition politician or former office-holder is not necessarily “attacking their own side”.

Government may hold:

- the records;
- the correspondence;
- the security responsibility;
- the procurement file;
- the appointment paperwork;
- the review;
- the public-money trail.

So the route can be:

```text
BORIS JOHNSON
former Conservative PM
        │
interaction with present state
        ▼
LABOUR GOVERNMENT
holds relevant public records
        │
        ▼
LABOUR MP asks minister
        │
        ▼
answer illuminates
state ↔ Johnson interface
```

This is ordinary parliamentary scrutiny.

Likewise, a Conservative MP can ask a Conservative government questions that reveal facts about Labour-linked actors, and vice versa.

### Teaching line

> **Ask the institution that owns the record, not the political tribe you imagine owns the person.**
