# 🍉 From the West Bank to Boston

## 🛰️ Orientation

- Investigate the international circulation of surveillance, policing and border-control technologies.
- Examine the occupied Palestinian territories as environments in which Israeli security technologies and operational practices have been deployed and developed.
- Investigate the relationship between military occupation, technology development and commercial security markets.
- Examine how technologies and operational knowledge circulate between:
  - Military institutions.
  - Intelligence agencies.
  - Border authorities.
  - Police departments.
  - Immigration-enforcement agencies.
  - Private security companies.
  - Technology manufacturers.
  - Research institutions.
- Investigate documented connections between Israeli security industries and American institutions.
- Use Boston as a geographical case study rather than assuming every American police department has identical relationships.
- Distinguish technological transfer from operational training, commercial sales and independently developed similarities.
- Central question: How do technologies and practices developed or deployed within one system of population control enter other policing and border-enforcement environments?

## 🗺️ 1. Palestine as a Security-Technology Environment

- Examine the development of Israeli surveillance and security infrastructure in the occupied Palestinian territories.
- Distinguish:
  - The occupied West Bank.
  - East Jerusalem.
  - Gaza.
  - Israel within its internationally recognised pre-1967 boundaries.
- Examine differences in legal status and governing authorities.
- Investigate:
  - Checkpoint systems.
  - Movement permits.
  - Identity documentation.
  - Biometric databases.
  - Facial recognition.
  - Camera networks.
  - Communications surveillance.
  - Predictive and automated analysis.
  - Military surveillance systems.
- Examine the relationship between military occupation and civilian technology development.
- Investigate how Palestinians experience these technologies.
- Include Palestinian testimony, journalism, legal challenges and human-rights documentation.
- Examine the absence or limitations of meaningful consent.
- Central question: What changes when a population subjected to surveillance has limited power to refuse, challenge or leave the system?

## 🧪 2. Testing, Deployment and Commercialisation

- Distinguish:
  - Research and development.
  - Laboratory testing.
  - Field trials.
  - Operational deployment.
  - Product refinement.
  - Commercial marketing.
  - International export.
- Investigate which technologies were developed specifically for military or occupation-related applications.
- Identify products whose manufacturers advertise operational experience.
- Examine the commercial significance of claims that products are "battle-tested" or operationally proven.
- Establish whether such claims concern Palestine specifically.
- Investigate the relationship between government procurement and private-sector product development.
- Examine the role of former military and intelligence personnel in commercial security industries.
- Avoid assuming every Israeli technology company has the same relationship with the occupation.
- Central question: When does experience gained through coercive state operations become a commercial advantage?

## 👁️ 3. Biometric Surveillance and Population Classification

- Examine biometric identification technologies used in the occupied Palestinian territories.
- Investigate facial-recognition systems reported in the West Bank.
- Examine the relationship between biometric data and movement permissions.
- Investigate the use of identity databases at checkpoints.
- Examine how identification systems distinguish authorised and unauthorised movement.
- Investigate documented error rates and discriminatory effects.
- Examine whether affected populations can:
  - Access their records.
  - Correct errors.
  - Challenge classifications.
  - Refuse data collection.
  - Obtain independent review.
- Investigate comparable biometric systems used in American immigration enforcement.
- Establish whether specific technologies or suppliers connect the systems.
- Central question: How does biometric identification become a mechanism for controlling movement?
