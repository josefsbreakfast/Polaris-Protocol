# ⚖️ Oversight and Prevention Failures

## 🛰️ Orientation

- Investigate the historical development of American immigration enforcement and its relationship to national security.
- Distinguish the legal authority to enforce immigration law from the effectiveness of particular enforcement strategies.
- Examine whether enforcement institutions achieve their stated preventive objectives.
- Examine the mechanisms available to identify and correct institutional failures.
- Separate:
  - Statutory authority.
  - Constitutional limits.
  - Institutional accountability.
  - Operational effectiveness.
  - Political responsibility.
  - Individual misconduct.
- Avoid treating the existence of a legal power as proof that exercising it produces a security benefit.

## 🏛️ Legislative inheritance

- Immigration enforcement powers predate ICE.
- Examine the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952.
- Trace the development of exclusion, detention and deportation authorities.
- Examine the 1965 immigration reforms.
- Examine the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986.
- Examine the Immigration Act of 1990.
- Examine the 1996 immigration reforms.
- Identify which authorities ICE inherited rather than created.
- Distinguish changes to immigration eligibility from changes to enforcement powers.

## ⚖️ Civil immigration law and criminal law

- Unlawful presence is generally a civil immigration matter.
- Improper entry can constitute a criminal offence under 8 USC § 1325.
- Re-entry following removal can constitute a criminal offence under 8 USC § 1326.
- Immigration detention is generally classified as civil rather than criminal detention.
- Civil classification does not eliminate constitutional protections.
- Investigate detention without criminal conviction.
- Investigate mandatory detention provisions.
- Examine the legal standards governing arrest, detention and removal.
- Distinguish administrative immigration warrants from judicial warrants.
- Examine judicial review and access to legal representation.
- Central question: What safeguards exist when the state exercises coercive powers through civil rather than criminal procedures?

## 🛂 The creation of ICE

- 2001: September 11 attacks.
- 2002: Homeland Security Act.
- 2003: Department of Homeland Security became operational.
- 2003: Immigration and Customs Enforcement established.
- Investigate the division of responsibilities between:
  - ICE.
  - Customs and Border Protection.
  - US Citizenship and Immigration Services.
  - Department of Justice.
  - Department of State.
- Examine the functions inherited from the Immigration and Naturalization Service.
- Examine the functions inherited from the US Customs Service.
- Investigate why immigration enforcement and customs investigations were combined.
- Investigate how these organisational choices related to documented security failures.

## 🧊 September 11 and prevention

- Examine the findings of the 9/11 Commission.
- Investigate failures concerning:
  - Intelligence sharing.
  - Watchlisting.
  - Visa screening.
  - Interagency coordination.
  - Aviation security.
  - Threat assessment.
- The hijackers entered the United States through legal admission processes.
- Some subsequently violated immigration conditions; avoid treating all nineteen as having identical immigration histories.
- Distinguish failures to detect known threats from failures to remove people lacking lawful immigration status.
- Investigate which 9/11 Commission recommendations directly concerned immigration administration.
- ICE was created before publication of the Commission's final report in July 2004.
- Central question: Did the restructuring of immigration enforcement address the specific failures that enabled the attacks?

## 🔍 Intelligence versus enforcement

- Distinguish intelligence collection from enforcement operations.
- Examine the relationship between broad immigration enforcement and targeted security investigations.
- Investigate whether enforcement volume is used as a proxy for preventive effectiveness.
- Examine false positives and resource allocation.
- Examine whether community trust affects the reporting of threats.
- Investigate whether indiscriminate enforcement can discourage cooperation with authorities.
- Examine the possibility that sophisticated actors exploit weaknesses in information-sharing systems.
- Do not assume that increasing arrests or removals necessarily increases national security.
- Central question: What evidence demonstrates that a particular enforcement practice reduces a defined security threat?

## 🏢 ICE's institutional structure

- Examine Homeland Security Investigations.
- Examine Enforcement and Removal Operations.
- Examine the Office of the Principal Legal Advisor.
- Examine the Office of Professional Responsibility.
- Examine internal management and administrative functions.
- Investigate the relationship between criminal investigations and civil immigration enforcement.
- Examine overlapping jurisdiction with:
  - FBI.
  - IRS Criminal Investigation.
  - FinCEN.
  - Other DHS agencies.
- Investigate why ICE's jurisdiction includes customs, excise and gambling-related authorities.
- Identify the precise statutory basis of those authorities.
- Avoid assuming the organisational rationale without legislative or administrative evidence.
- Central question: Are ICE's responsibilities organised around coherent preventive objectives or inherited enforcement jurisdictions?

## 🧾 Oversight and accountability

- Examine congressional oversight.
- Examine Senate confirmation of ICE leadership.
- Investigate periods without a Senate-confirmed ICE director.
- Distinguish acting leadership from an absence of institutional authority.
- Examine DHS inspector-general oversight.
- Examine internal professional-responsibility investigations.
- Examine judicial review of detention and removal.
- Examine compliance with federal court orders.
- Investigate mechanisms for reporting misconduct.
- Examine transparency and public access to enforcement data.
- Examine the practical consequences of oversight findings.
- Central question: Which institutions can identify unlawful or ineffective conduct, and which can actually compel correction?

## 💰 Funding and incentives

- Trace changes in ICE funding.
- Distinguish appropriations from expenditure.
- Examine detention contracts and private-sector involvement.
- Examine incentives associated with detention capacity.
- Investigate performance metrics and operational targets.
- Examine whether funding decisions are linked to independently evaluated outcomes.
- Investigate the relationship between institutional expansion and political incentives.
- Central question: What behaviour does the funding and performance system reward?

## 🚨 Contemporary enforcement

- Examine changes in enforcement policy across successive administrations.
- Distinguish statutory changes from executive policy changes.
- Examine expansions of detention and removal operations.
- Investigate allegations of unlawful detention or enforcement conduct.
- Identify specific court findings rather than generalising from allegations.
- Examine the Laken Riley Act of 2025.
- Examine relevant 2025 budget legislation and its effects on ICE.
- Investigate reported enforcement-related deaths and the subsequent accountability processes.
- Verify dates, responsible agencies, legal findings and procedural outcomes.
- Distinguish established facts from ongoing litigation and contested claims.

## 🌍 International accountability

- Examine the relationship between domestic immigration enforcement and international human-rights obligations.
- Distinguish the International Criminal Court from the International Court of Justice.
- Examine the jurisdictional limits of international criminal law.
- Investigate whether particular alleged conduct could meet relevant international-law thresholds.
- Avoid treating political comparisons as established legal classifications.
- Examine domestic remedies before considering international mechanisms.
- Investigate the principle of complementarity where relevant.
- Central question: What happens when domestic accountability mechanisms are unable or unwilling to address serious alleged abuses?

## 🪿 Questions to carry forward

- What problem was ICE originally intended to solve?
- Which powers were inherited from earlier immigration legislation?
- Which failures of 9/11 were specifically addressed by ICE's creation?
- What evidence demonstrates ICE's effectiveness in preventing terrorism?
- How are enforcement outcomes measured?
- What happens when enforcement activity undermines intelligence gathering?
- Why are criminal-investigation and immigration-removal functions housed within the same agency?
- How effective are congressional, judicial and internal oversight mechanisms?
- What happens when an agency repeatedly fails to comply with legal constraints?
- How can ineffective or harmful powers be reduced, revised or withdrawn?
- What institutional arrangements could better distinguish immigration administration, criminal investigation and genuine national-security prevention?
