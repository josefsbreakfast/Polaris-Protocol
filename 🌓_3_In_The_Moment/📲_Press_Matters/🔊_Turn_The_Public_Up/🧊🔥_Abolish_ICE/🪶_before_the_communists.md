# 🪶 Before the Communists

## 🗺️ Historical foundations

- American immigration history does not begin with the establishment of the United States.
- Indigenous nations already possessed territories, political institutions, diplomatic relationships and systems governing movement.
- European colonisation introduced competing claims to territorial sovereignty.
- Distinguish between:
  - Indigenous movement and displacement.
  - European settler migration.
  - Enslavement and forced transportation.
  - Indentured servitude.
  - Voluntary labour migration.
  - Refugee movement.
  - Territorial annexation.
- These forms of movement involved very different degrees of agency, legal protection and coercion.
- Investigate how territorial expansion changed who was classified as an immigrant, foreigner, citizen or subject.
- Central question: Who possessed the authority to decide who could enter, remain or belong?

## 🏛️ Citizenship before modern immigration enforcement

- 1790: Naturalization Act restricted eligibility for naturalisation to free white persons meeting statutory requirements.
- Citizenship, residence and permission to enter were not interchangeable legal categories.
- Investigate the relationship between slavery, citizenship and constitutional personhood.
- 1857: Dred Scott decision denied federal citizenship to people of African descent within its reasoning.
- 1868: Fourteenth Amendment established constitutional birthright citizenship, subject to its jurisdictional language.
- Investigate how citizenship law interacted with Indigenous sovereignty.
- 1924: Indian Citizenship Act extended US citizenship to Indigenous people born within US territorial limits who were not already citizens.
- Indigenous citizenship and tribal citizenship require separate treatment.
- Central question: How did the United States define political membership while expanding across territories inhabited by people it did not initially recognise as equal citizens?

## 🧳 Migration, labour and economic development

- Examine European migration during industrialisation.
- Examine Irish migration, particularly during and after the Great Famine.
- Examine Jewish migration from Europe, including responses to persecution and antisemitism.
- Examine Chinese migration and labour recruitment.
- Examine Mexican migration and the relationship between labour demand and territorial history.
- Examine Caribbean migration and changing racial classifications.
- Distinguish between employers' demand for migrant labour and political campaigns against migrants.
- Investigate whether immigration restrictions were accompanied by continued economic dependence on migrant workers.
- Investigate how class, occupation, wealth, gender and race affected admission and citizenship.
- Central question: Why did industries recruit migrant workers while political institutions increasingly restricted their entry and belonging?

## ⚖️ The emergence of exclusion

- 1875: Page Act restricted entry of certain Asian migrants, particularly affecting Chinese women.
- Investigate the interaction between racial prejudice, gender, sexuality and claims about morality.
- 1882: Chinese Exclusion Act established explicit restrictions on Chinese labour immigration.
- 1882: Federal immigration legislation also introduced exclusions concerning specified categories of migrants.
- 1891: Immigration Act expanded federal immigration administration and exclusion.
- 1892: Ellis Island opened as a federal immigration station.
- Investigate the development of immigration inspection, detention and deportation.
- Examine the relationship between immigration enforcement and racial classification.
- Investigate how courts interpreted federal authority over immigration.
- Include the development of the plenary-power doctrine.
- Central question: How did immigration control become an area in which the federal government exercised unusually broad powers?

## 🏝️ Territory, borders and jurisdiction

- 1830: Indian Removal Act.
- Examine forced Indigenous displacement and its relationship to federal territorial policy.
- 1848: Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo transferred extensive territories from Mexico to the United States.
- Examine the legal status of Mexican residents following territorial annexation.
- 1862: Homestead Act encouraged settlement through federal land distribution.
- Investigate how federal land policy intersected with Indigenous dispossession.
- Examine the changing legal position of Puerto Rico and other US territories.
- Distinguish territorial incorporation, citizenship, immigration and colonial administration.
- Central question: How did American territorial expansion complicate the distinction between internal and external populations?

## 🚨 Political exclusion before the Cold War

- Investigate nineteenth-century fears concerning anarchism and revolutionary movements.
- 1901: Assassination of President William McKinley.
- 1903: Immigration Act included provisions excluding anarchists.
- Examine the relationship between political violence and immigration restrictions.
- 1917: Immigration Act introduced additional restrictions, including literacy requirements and the Asiatic Barred Zone.
- 1919–1920: First Red Scare.
- Investigate the Palmer Raids and deportations of suspected radicals.
- Examine the relationship between immigration enforcement, labour organising and political dissent.
- Distinguish documented criminal conduct from political association.
- Central question: How did immigration law become a mechanism for excluding or removing people because of their political beliefs?

## 🧬 National origins and racial hierarchy

- 1921: Emergency Quota Act introduced nationality-based numerical quotas.
- 1924: Immigration Act established a more restrictive national-origins quota system.
- The system strongly favoured immigration from northern and western Europe.
- Examine the influence of eugenics and racial pseudoscience on immigration policy.
- Investigate congressional debates concerning the desired composition of the American population.
- Examine how immigration law treated Asian populations.
- Examine the distinct legal position of migration from the Western Hemisphere.
- Investigate the relationship between immigration restrictions and contemporary racial segregation.
- Central question: How did demographic preferences become embedded in federal immigration legislation?

## 🕎 Refugees, persecution and exclusion

- Examine restrictions affecting Jewish refugees fleeing Nazi persecution.
- Investigate the 1939 voyage of the MS St. Louis.
- Examine the relationship between immigration quotas and humanitarian protection.
- Distinguish refugee admissions, immigration eligibility and asylum as legal concepts.
- Investigate American responses to displacement during and after the Second World War.
- Examine Japanese American incarceration during the war, distinguishing citizens from non-citizens.
- Investigate how wartime security claims affected racialised populations.
- Central question: What happened when immigration restrictions conflicted with the need to protect people fleeing persecution?
