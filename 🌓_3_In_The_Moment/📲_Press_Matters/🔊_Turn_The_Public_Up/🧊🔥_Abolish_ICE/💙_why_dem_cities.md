# 💙 Why Dem Cities?

## 🛰️ Orientation

- Investigate why immigration-enforcement operations and political confrontations may become particularly visible in Democratic-led American cities.
- Examine the relationship between:
  - Federal immigration enforcement.
  - Municipal government.
  - State government.
  - Electoral politics.
  - Immigration demographics.
  - Sanctuary policies.
  - Public demonstrations.
  - Media coverage.
  - Institutional accountability.
- Distinguish actual enforcement concentration from disproportionate publicity.
- Investigate whether particular cities are selected for operational, demographic, administrative or political reasons.
- Avoid assuming partisan targeting solely because a city is Democratic-led.
- Central question: Why do certain cities become focal points for immigration-enforcement disputes, and what evidence explains their selection?

## 🏙️ 1. The Geography of American Immigration

- Examine the historical concentration of immigrant communities in major metropolitan areas.
- Investigate the relationship between immigration and urban labour markets.
- Examine the role of established family and community networks.
- Investigate differences between:
  - Long-established immigrant destinations.
  - Newer immigrant destinations.
  - Border cities.
  - Interior metropolitan areas.
- Examine the political composition of major American cities.
- Investigate whether Democratic governance correlates with larger immigrant populations.
- Distinguish population size from immigration-enforcement rates.
- Compare enforcement activity using appropriate population-adjusted measures.
- Central question: Does the apparent concentration of enforcement in Democratic cities partly reflect where immigrant populations live?

## ⚖️ 2. Federalism and Immigration Authority

- Examine the federal government's constitutional authority over immigration.
- Investigate the relationship between federal immigration law and state or municipal authority.
- Distinguish federal immigration enforcement from local criminal policing.
- Examine the anti-commandeering principle.
- Investigate the extent to which federal authorities can require local governments to assist immigration enforcement.
- Examine the limits of local government authority to obstruct federal operations.
- Investigate disputes over information sharing, detention requests and access to facilities.
- Distinguish lawful non-cooperation from unlawful interference.
- Central question: What can the federal government require local authorities to do, and what can local authorities lawfully refuse?

## 🛟 3. What Are Sanctuary Cities?

- Investigate the history of sanctuary movements in the United States.
- Examine the relationship between religious sanctuary organising and Central American refugee movements during the 1980s.
- Distinguish grassroots sanctuary movements from contemporary municipal sanctuary policies.
- Examine the absence of a single nationwide legal definition of "sanctuary city".
- Investigate local policies concerning:
  - Immigration detainers.
  - Police cooperation with ICE.
  - Information sharing.
  - Access to municipal facilities.
  - Immigration-status inquiries.
  - Local expenditure on federal immigration enforcement.
- Distinguish restrictions on voluntary cooperation from prohibitions on federal enforcement.
- Examine the stated objectives of sanctuary policies.
- Investigate legal challenges concerning those policies.
- Central question: What do sanctuary policies actually prevent, permit or require?

## 🚓 4. Federal Enforcement and Local Policing

- Examine how local police interact with federal immigration authorities.
- Investigate immigration detainer requests.
- Examine the legal status of administrative immigration warrants.
- Distinguish judicial warrants from administrative warrants.
- Investigate 287(g) agreements.
- Examine the Secure Communities programme.
- Investigate fingerprint and biometric information sharing.
- Examine the relationship between local arrests and federal immigration databases.
- Investigate the consequences of mistaken identity.
- Examine local police policies concerning immigration-status inquiries.
- Central question: How does ordinary local policing become connected to federal immigration enforcement?

## 🧊 5. Why Federal Authorities Might Prioritise Particular Cities

- Investigate operational explanations for city selection.
- Examine:
  - Immigration population size.
  - Existing removal cases.
  - Outstanding immigration warrants.
  - Federal investigative priorities.
  - Availability of enforcement personnel.
  - Transportation infrastructure.
  - Detention capacity.
  - Local cooperation arrangements.
  - Intelligence concerning particular investigations.
- Investigate whether non-cooperation by local authorities changes federal operational requirements.
- Examine whether additional personnel are deployed to compensate for limited local assistance.
- Distinguish plausible operational explanations from explanations supported by documentary evidence.
- Central question: Which operational factors explain the selection of particular cities?

## 🗳️ 6. Why Political Targeting Is a Research Question

- Examine whether federal officials publicly identify Democratic-led cities as enforcement priorities.

## 🕸️ 13. The Cybernetic Problem

- Identify the possible feedback loop:
  - Federal authorities increase enforcement activity.
  - Local communities document and challenge operations.
  - Local officials criticise federal policy.
  - Federal officials portray local resistance as obstruction.
  - Political attention increases.
  - Additional enforcement resources or publicity follow.
  - Public resistance becomes more visible.
- Investigate whether this pattern is observable in specific cities.
- Distinguish documented decisions from inferred political incentives.
- Examine whether the conflict generates useful oversight.
- Examine whether political confrontation diverts attention from detention conditions and legal accountability.
- Investigate whether escalating conflict reduces institutional cooperation.
- Central question: Can federal–local confrontation become self-reinforcing even when it undermines the practical objectives of immigration administration?
