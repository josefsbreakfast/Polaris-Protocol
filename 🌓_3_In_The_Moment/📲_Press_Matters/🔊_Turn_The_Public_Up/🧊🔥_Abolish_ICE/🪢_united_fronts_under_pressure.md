# 🪢 United Fronts Under Pressure

- Investigate how communities, organisations and institutions form coalitions in response to coercive state policies.
- Examine the relationship between shared interests, political disagreement and collective action.
- Explore the historical development of united-front organising.
- Distinguish:
  - Political alliances.
  - Civil-rights coalitions.
  - Labour organising.
  - Religious sanctuary networks.
  - Legal advocacy.
  - Community mutual aid.
  - Professional resistance.
  - Institutional oversight.
- Examine how coalitions respond to immigration enforcement, detention and discriminatory policy.
- Investigate why political pressure can simultaneously encourage cooperation and intensify internal divisions.
- Consider how different organisations can pursue shared institutional objectives without adopting identical political positions.
- Central question: How can groups with different identities, interests and political commitments cooperate to defend shared rights and accountability mechanisms?

## 🪿 12. Practical Questions for Coalition Design

- What is the shared problem?
- Which facts are established?
- Which claims remain disputed?
- What institutional responsibilities are involved?
- Which organisations have relevant expertise?
- What does each participant actually agree to do?
- What disagreements can remain unresolved?
- How are decisions made?
- How are affected people represented?
- How is evidence protected?
- How are mistakes corrected?
- How are conflicts resolved?
- What happens when one participant withdraws?
- How is success measured?
- What institutional changes would demonstrate progress?

## 🕯️ 13. What United Fronts Cannot Solve

- Cooperation does not eliminate ideological disagreement.
- Shared objectives do not erase unequal power.
- Coalitions cannot substitute for functioning courts or regulatory institutions.
- Public solidarity does not automatically provide legal remedies.
- Organisational diversity can complicate decision-making.
- Some disagreements concern fundamental rights and cannot be resolved through compromise.
- Coalitions can reproduce the exclusions they seek to challenge.
- Examine how these limitations affect coalition design.
- Central question: How can cooperation remain useful without becoming an excuse to avoid difficult disagreements or institutional responsibilities?
