
# ⛓️ Historical Parallels

## 🛰️ Orientation

- Investigate historical and international parallels involving:
  - Immigration enforcement.
  - Citizenship and nationality.
  - Population registration.
  - Internal movement restrictions.
  - Detention and deportation.
  - Political and ideological exclusion.
  - Racial and ethnic classification.
  - National-security legislation.
  - Administrative discretion.
  - Institutional accountability.
- Examine how governments have used administrative systems to distinguish between populations entitled to protection and populations subjected to coercion.
- Include democratic, colonial, authoritarian and totalitarian systems.
- Distinguish historical comparison from claims of equivalence.
- Compare specific mechanisms, legal authorities, institutional structures and documented outcomes.
- Do not assume identical intentions, severity or historical circumstances.
- Examine how apparently ordinary administrative functions can become instruments of political repression.
- Central question: Under what conditions do systems designed to administer populations become systems for controlling, excluding or persecuting them?

## ⚖️ 16. Comparative Mechanisms

- Population registration:
  - Who is recorded?
  - Which characteristics are recorded?
  - Who controls the records?
  - How are records used by enforcement agencies?

- Citizenship and legal status:
  - Who is recognised as a citizen?
  - Who can lose citizenship or residency?
  - What rights depend on legal classification?

- Movement restrictions:
  - Are restrictions imposed at international borders?
  - Are internal movements regulated?
  - Are permits or identity documents required?

- Detention:
  - Is detention criminal, civil, military or administrative?
  - What legal standard authorises detention?
  - Is individualised evidence required?
  - What judicial review is available?

- Deportation and forced displacement:
  - What legal authority permits removal?
  - Is removal individual or collective?
  - Where are people sent?
  - What safeguards exist against wrongful removal?

- National-security justification:
  - What threat is identified?
  - What evidence supports the threat assessment?
  - Are restrictions proportionate to the stated threat?
  - Are emergency powers temporary or enduring?

- Institutional accountability:
  - Who authorises enforcement?
  - Who investigates misconduct?
  - Who can suspend unlawful practices?
  - What remedies exist for affected people?
