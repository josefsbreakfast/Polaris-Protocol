# 🍎 Kids in Cages

## 🛰️ Orientation

- Investigate the history of migrant-child detention in the United States.
- Establish why immigration detention and the treatment of children were controversial long before Donald Trump's first presidency.
- Trace the development of child-detention policy across successive administrations.
- Distinguish:
  - Unaccompanied children.
  - Children arriving with parents or guardians.
  - Children separated from their families by government policy.
  - Children temporarily held in border-processing facilities.
  - Children held in longer-term immigration detention.
  - Children transferred into federal child-welfare custody.
- Examine the distinction between the existence of detention facilities and the policies determining who was placed inside them.
- Investigate the legal protections governing detained children.
- Examine the relationship between immigration enforcement, child welfare and family unity.
- Central question: How did the United States develop a system in which children could be detained because of immigration proceedings, and why did successive reforms fail to resolve the underlying controversy?

## ⚖️ 1. Before ICE: The Flores Litigation

- 1985: Jenny Lisette Flores, a Salvadoran teenager, was detained by the Immigration and Naturalization Service.
- Examine the conditions of her detention and the restrictions on release.
- Investigate the class-action litigation challenging federal treatment of detained migrant children.
- 1993: Reno v. Flores reached the US Supreme Court.
- Distinguish the Supreme Court's ruling from the subsequent settlement.
- 1997: Flores Settlement Agreement.
- Examine its requirements concerning:
  - Release of children without unnecessary delay.
  - Placement with parents, relatives or other appropriate custodians.
  - Minimum standards for detention conditions.
  - Appropriate placement in licensed facilities when release is not possible.
- Investigate the continuing enforcement of the settlement.
- Central question: Why were federal authorities already facing litigation over migrant-child detention in the 1980s?

## 🏛️ 2. The Clinton Administration

- Examine immigration enforcement under President Bill Clinton.
- 1996: Illegal Immigration Reform and Immigrant Responsibility Act.
- Investigate expansions of detention and removal authorities.
- Examine the relationship between increased enforcement capacity and family detention.
- 1997: Flores Settlement Agreement.
- Investigate how federal authorities implemented the agreement.
- Examine criticisms raised by immigration lawyers and children's advocates.
- Distinguish legislative expansion of immigration enforcement from specific child-detention practices.
- Central question: How did the federal government respond when courts and advocates challenged the treatment of detained children?

## 🛂 3. The Bush Administration

- 2002: Homeland Security Act.
- 2003: Creation of the Department of Homeland Security.
- Immigration enforcement responsibilities transferred from the former INS into the new departmental structure.
- Responsibility for the care of unaccompanied migrant children transferred to the Office of Refugee Resettlement within the Department of Health and Human Services.
- Examine the division between immigration enforcement and child-welfare responsibilities.
- 2006: Hutto family detention facility opened in Texas.
- Investigate conditions at Hutto and subsequent litigation.
- Examine the use of family detention as an immigration-management strategy.
- Investigate whether family detention complied with the Flores Settlement Agreement.
- Central question: Did the post-9/11 restructuring improve protections for migrant children, or create new problems of institutional coordination?

## 🧳 4. The Obama Administration

- Examine immigration enforcement and child detention under President Barack Obama.
- 2014: Significant increase in arrivals of unaccompanied children and families at the southern border.
- Examine conditions in temporary border-processing facilities.
- Investigate the use of fenced holding areas and overcrowded facilities.
- Examine the expansion of family detention.
- Investigate facilities at Artesia, Karnes and Dilley.
- Examine the administration's stated reasons for expanding detention capacity.
- Investigate legal challenges concerning family detention.
- 2015: Federal court rulings concerning the application of the Flores Settlement Agreement.
- Examine criticism from immigrant-rights organisations, legal advocates and medical professionals.
- Distinguish detention of families together from systematic separation of children from parents.
- Investigate photographs of children held in fenced processing areas and how those images were subsequently circulated.
- Central question: Why were migrant-child detention conditions already the subject of sustained criticism before the 2016 presidential election?

## 🚨 5. The Trump Administration and Family Separation

- 2017: Examine changes in immigration enforcement priorities.
- 2018: Implementation of the administration's zero-tolerance prosecution policy.
- Investigate how criminal prosecution of parents for improper entry resulted in children being separated from them.
- Examine the roles of:
  - Department of Justice.
  - Department of Homeland Security.
  - Customs and Border Protection.
  - Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
  - Department of Health and Human Services.
- Distinguish agency responsibilities rather than attributing every action to ICE.
- Examine failures in systems intended to track separated family members.
- Investigate the number of children affected and the limitations of official records.
- June 2018: Executive order addressing family separation.
- June 2018: Ms. L v. ICE family-reunification litigation.
- Investigate court-ordered reunification requirements.
- Examine the difficulties of identifying and reuniting separated families.
- Investigate subsequent government investigations and litigation.
- Central question: What distinguished the 2018 family-separation policy from earlier detention practices?

## 🧾 7. Administrative Responsibility

- Examine how children move between federal agencies.
- Distinguish:
  - CBP processing and short-term custody.
  - ICE immigration enforcement and detention.
  - HHS/ORR care and placement of unaccompanied children.
  - Immigration courts.
  - Federal judicial oversight.

## 🧊 8. Why the Anger Predates 2016

- The Flores litigation began decades before Trump's presidency.
- The federal government had been challenged over child-detention conditions since the 1980s.
- The 1997 Flores Settlement Agreement established protections that remained the subject of litigation.
- Family detention expanded under multiple administrations.
- Conditions in border-processing facilities attracted criticism before 2016.
- Children's advocates, immigration lawyers and civil-rights organisations repeatedly challenged detention practices.
- The 2018 family-separation crisis intensified public attention to an already contested system.
- Distinguish longstanding criticism from the specific policies and decisions of individual administrations.
- Investigate how media attention changed over time.
- Examine whether public debate focused disproportionately on presidential personalities rather than institutional structures.
- Central question: Why did problems documented across several decades remain unresolved?

## 🕸️ 9. Detention as an Institutional Response

- Investigate why detention became a recurring response to immigration-management pressures.
- Examine whether detention was used for:
  - Identity verification.
  - Processing.
  - Ensuring attendance at proceedings.
  - Deterrence.
  - Managing operational capacity.
  - Preparing for removal.
- Distinguish the government's stated objectives from independently demonstrated outcomes.
- Investigate alternatives to detention.
- Examine community-based case management.
- Examine the relationship between legal representation and immigration-court attendance.
- Investigate the costs of different approaches.
- Examine whether increased detention capacity created incentives to continue using detention.
- Central question: Was detention selected because it was necessary, or because the institutional machinery already existed?

## ⚖️ 10. Law, Oversight and Accountability

- Examine the Flores Settlement Agreement and subsequent litigation.
- Examine constitutional protections applicable to children in immigration custody.
- Investigate federal standards for detention conditions.
- Examine judicial enforcement of settlement requirements.
- Investigate congressional oversight.

## 🕯️ 11. Historical Memory and Political Responsibility

- Examine how responsibility for immigration detention has been discussed across administrations.
- Avoid presenting child detention as originating with Trump.
- Avoid treating earlier detention practices as equivalent to the 2018 systematic family-separation policy.
- Examine the role of bipartisan legislation and institutional continuity.
- Investigate how political debates obscure differences between:
  - Creating legal powers.
  - Expanding detention capacity.
  - Establishing enforcement priorities.
  - Ordering particular practices.
  - Failing to correct known abuses.
- Examine how public memory changes when photographs or reports are detached from their original dates.
- Central question: How can historical accountability distinguish institutional continuity from responsibility for specific policy decisions?
