# 🔥 Jeunesse en Feu
**First created:** 2026-10-09 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-09  
*French youth mobilisation, the schools young people inhabit, the institutions answering them, and the evidence of what changes.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

A school can be open without providing every lesson it ought to provide. A government can publish a response without having repaired the problem that prompted it. And a pupil can have an opinion about either of these things without first securing permission from a suitably accredited adult.

*Jeunesse en Feu* follows the reported French student and education-sector mobilisation of September–October 2026. It begins with educational conditions and young people's accounts of them, then follows the argument outward: demands, school staffing, public spending, unions, demonstrations, policing, parliamentary scrutiny, cultural memory and the media. These are connected subjects, but they are not interchangeable evidence. A protest photograph does not establish the condition of a classroom; a budget announcement does not establish that the classroom has acquired a teacher.

The constellation belongs to **Polaris Protocol**, which asks how information moves through systems of power and whether the people experiencing a problem receive the repair they asked for. Here the first witnesses to educational provision are the people trying to learn and work within it. The story should not begin only when the adults become alarmed by the noise.

**Coverage:** principally September–9 October 2026, with earlier history for context. **Editorial and evidence checkpoint:** 9 October 2026. The linked drafts contain source leads, reporting and interpretation of different strengths; their presence here is not independent confirmation of every contemporary claim. For dates and unresolved details, start with the chronology.

## 4. 🧪 How to Read the Evidence

A reader should not need a law degree, a French union membership card and three years of forensic training to work out what sort of claim is being made. The basic distinctions are these:

| Evidence type | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Contemporary reporting | A named outlet reported something on a date | That every reported detail is independently proven |
| First-person testimony | Someone describes what they saw or experienced | That everyone experienced the same thing |
| Organisation statement | A group publicly adopted a position or issued a notice | That all members agreed or acted on it |
| Official record | An institution said, proposed or recorded something | That a promise was implemented |
| Allegation | A claim was made and can be attributed | That responsibility has been established |
| Analysis | A reasoned reading of specified material | An additional eyewitness or a demonstrated causal chain |
| Unresolved | The current record cannot settle a question | Permission to invent the missing piece |

Particular distinctions matter throughout this branch: **a strike notice is not a confirmed stoppage; a proposed budget is not money spent; a consultation is not an agreement; a reported use of force is not a legal finding; reopening a school is not proof that every staffing gap has been filled.**

French quotations should retain their original wording, with translations identified and explained where a pun or local idiom will not travel neatly. An attributed quotation is not automatically a slogan used everywhere. Nor should the words of a minor be treated as an invitation to publish identifying details unrelated to the public issue.

The evidence registers and source shelves within the nodes are the place to check particular claims. Cross-referencing five Polaris drafts that repeat the same report does **not** create five independent sources. That would be a very efficient machine for manufacturing certainty, which is not what we are building.

## 5. 🧑‍🎓 Whose Movement Is This?

Young people are political actors, not merely the picturesque foreground of an adult argument about public spending. Some may have organised, spoken publicly or joined blockades; others may have disagreed with a tactic, been unable to participate or wanted to get back to lessons. None of those positions makes their educational interests less real.

Similarly, a teachers' union, student association, parent, firefighter or political party may support a grievance without acquiring authority to speak for everyone affected. [🤝 Syndicats et solidarités](./🤝_syndicats_et_solidarites.md) distinguishes sympathy, endorsement, coordination, joint action and shared decision-making. The distinctions matter because representation is not a souvenir one picks up at a demonstration.

The planned gender node will ask whose labour makes mobilisation possible and whose speech is remembered. Until it exists, that question remains visible here rather than quietly disappearing from the directory.

## 6. ♻️ Why Polaris Is Looking at This

Polaris approaches institutions through **Embodied Information Ecology**: information is not simply an abstract statement circulating between official accounts. It begins somewhere, in somebody's conditions of life. A pupil knows when a lesson has not happened. A teacher knows which workloads can be sustained. A family knows whether support is actually available. Their knowledge may be local, partial and differently situated, but it is not rendered meaningless because it has yet to be translated into a ministerial spreadsheet.

To travel, those experiences must change form. A missing lesson becomes a complaint; complaints become demands; demands become placards, interviews, union statements and statistics; those become headlines, parliamentary questions, consultation categories and budget lines. Each translation can make an experience legible to a new audience. Each can also drop something important. A national staffing average may not tell you why one pupil has gone without a subject teacher. A dramatic image of a confrontation may travel further than a careful explanation of how disability support has been rationed.

This is a cybernetic question because a functioning response requires **feedback**. The institution receives information about a problem, acts, and then needs to learn whether its action altered the condition that produced the complaint. A letter acknowledging frustration is information about acknowledgement. A timetable with restored teaching is information about repair. They are not the same measurement.

The useful audit is therefore: **experienced problem → articulated demand → institutional response → decision → implementation → experienced change.** Not every link is documented. Not every response succeeds. The framework does not prove deliberate concealment or bad faith by any particular official; it tells us where to look for losses, delays, mismatched incentives and missing feedback.

**Information has travelled. Has the repair?**

## 7. 🕰️ Scope, Updates and Open Work

This README describes a **working constellation**, not a closed verdict on the autumn mobilisation. Its substantive evidence checkpoint is **9 October 2026**. Events after that date, including proposed deadlines and negotiations, belong in the future or pending register until contemporary evidence establishes what happened.

The [master chronology](./⏱️_chronologie_de_la_revolte.md) is the first place to record a new event or correct a date. Changes affecting claims about staffing, official promises, strike participation or policing should then be carried through to the relevant specialist nodes. A correction should preserve the distinction between what an earlier source reported and what later evidence established.

Two specialist drafts remain **forthcoming**: *Les filles aussi sont en colère* and *Qui raconte la révolte ?*. They are deliberately not linked to nonexistent files. Other drafts still require source-by-source editorial review before they can be presented as independently verified research. This is particularly important for precise incident claims, alleged slogans, numerical budget comparisons and descriptions of organisational coordination.

The aim is not to freeze a movement into a tidy narrative while it is still happening. It is to keep an intelligible record, with the uncertainty left where it belongs.

## 8. 📚 Sources and Corrections

The nodes draw on different source families: attributed student testimony; student-organisation programmes; school and education-authority records; parliamentary proceedings; union notices and statements; contemporary local and national reporting; and legal or historical material for the policing cluster. Each category answers a different question. A parliamentary transcript can establish what was said in parliament. It cannot, without further evidence, establish that the underlying claim was true.

For the working source trails, begin with [the chronology](./⏱️_chronologie_de_la_revolte.md), [the demands](./🎓_ce_que_reclame_la_jeunesse.md), [the student words](./📣_les_mots_de_la_colere.md), and the relevant specialist node. These are research routes, **not** a blanket assertion that every link or quotation was independently rechecked on 9 October.

Corrections should identify the affected event or passage, the new source, the difference it makes and the date of revision. A particularly good correction may be less dramatic than the original story. That is not a reason to leave the dramatic version standing.

The Republic may have an elaborate organisational chart. The pupils are still entitled to ask whether anyone has found the teachers.
