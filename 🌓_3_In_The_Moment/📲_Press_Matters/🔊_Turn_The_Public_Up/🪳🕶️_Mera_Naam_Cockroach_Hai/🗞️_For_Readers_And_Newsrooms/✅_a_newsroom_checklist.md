# ✅ A Newsroom Checklist  
**First created:** 2026-07-30 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-30  
*A compact workflow for commissioning, reporting, editing, publishing and correcting coverage of the Cockroach case without outrunning the evidence, flattening participants or exposing the people who made the story visible.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

This checklist is the practical exit point for the newsroom-facing folder.

It does not replace:

- source tracing;
- translation;
- participant interviews;
- visual verification;
- diaspora positioning;
- regional-media analysis;
- headline discipline.

It turns those methods into a sequence that can be used:

- before commissioning;
- while reporting;
- before publication;
- after publication.

The central question is:

> Can the newsroom show exactly what happened, who says so, what the source establishes, what remains uncertain and whether publication creates avoidable harm?

The minimum distinction is:

```text
event
≠ claim
≠ source
≠ interpretation
≠ inference
≠ uncertainty
```

---

## 1. 🧭 Before Commissioning  

### Define The Event  

- What exactly happened?
- On what date?
- In which place and jurisdiction?
- Which institution is responsible?
- Which part remains ongoing?
- Is this a single event, a sequence or a live process?

### Define The Claim  

Write the proposed claim before assigning the story.

- Is it descriptive?
- Causal?
- Comparative?
- Transferable?
- Is the claim narrower than the pitch?
- Does the pitch already assume motive or causation?

### Map The Likely Sources  

Identify:

- participants;
- organisers;
- families;
- local journalists;
- regional-language outlets;
- lawyers;
- officials;
- institutional documents;
- visual evidence;
- diaspora interpreters;
- neighbouring press.

### Ask Who Is Missing  

- Which region is absent?
- Which language is absent?
- Which caste or class position is absent?
- Are disabled students visible?
- Are women present only through invisible labour?
- Are people facing active cases too exposed to speak?
- Is one spokesperson standing in for the whole movement?

### Set The Risk Floor  

Before contact:

- could publication affect education?
- employment?
- professional registration?
- family safety?
- legal status?
- police attention?
- digital harassment?
- future automated indexing?

### Commission For Time, Not Just Output  

Allow time for:

- explanation;
- translation;
- correction;
- follow-up;
- participant hesitation;
- regional-source comparison.

Do not commission a culturally dense case on a deadline that structurally guarantees flattening.

---

## 2. 🧾 Before Reporting Begins  

### Create A Claim File  

For each major claim, record:

```text
CLAIM:
SOURCE:
SOURCE CLASS:
ORIGINAL LANGUAGE:
CONFIDENCE:
LIMIT:
MISSING EVIDENCE:
ASSESSMENT DATE:
```

### Create A Chronology  

Record:

- earliest known event;
- earliest known wording;
- earliest known image;
- earliest known public post;
- earliest known private circulation;
- later reinterpretation;
- official response;
- implementation.

### Separate Original And Derivative Sources  

Ask:

- Which outlet did original reporting?
- Which article rewrote another?
- Which social post repeated a headline?
- Are several sources actually one source chain?

### Prepare Translation Support  

Identify:

- original script;
- transliteration;
- literal translation;
- natural translation;
- register;
- joke structure;
- cultural reference;
- alternative reading.

### Prepare Consent And Exposure Questions  

Decide before the interview:

- name or anonymity?
- image?
- voice?
- location?
- international syndication?
- archive?
- future reuse?

---

## 3. 💕 While Reporting  

### Ask Participants Before Assigning Theory  

Start with:

- What happened?
- What did the grievance mean to you?
- What did the cockroach mean?
- What did you want changed?
- What did you believe had been won?
- What remained?

Do not begin by asking participants to confirm the newsroom’s preferred theory.

### Distinguish Roles  

Label accurately:

```text
organiser
≠ participant
≠ supporter
≠ observer
≠ critic
```

### Ask About Invisible Labour  

- Who handled transport?
- food?
- legal support?
- translation?
- accessibility?
- documentation?
- moderation?
- family negotiation?
- emotional support?

### Ask Who Could Not Be Visible  

- Who stayed away because of risk?
- Who supported privately?
- Who could not be photographed?
- Who left before press arrived?
- Who could not speak English?
- Who was missing because of disability, cost or care duties?

### Preserve Disagreement  

Do not force one account of:

- victory;
- settlement;
- symbolism;
- tactics;
- leadership;
- trust.

### Return For Clarification  

Ask again when:

- chronology is unclear;
- translation is uncertain;
- a quote may be ambiguous;
- two accounts conflict;
- legal status changes;
- publication risk becomes clearer.

---

## 4. 🥭 Translation Check  

For every significant phrase:

- preserve the original;
- preserve the script;
- preserve participant-used transliteration;
- provide literal meaning;
- provide natural meaning;
- explain register;
- identify film, song or political reference;
- preserve uncertainty;
- credit the translator or explainer where safe;
- pay cultural labour where appropriate.

Ask:

- Is the joke translated?
- Is the emotional instruction translated?
- Has hostile language been mistaken for reclamation?
- Has reclamation erased the original hostility?
- Has one regional reading become national meaning?
- Has anti-Brahminical critique been flattened into hostility to all Brahmins?
- Has diaspora reception been mistaken for local participant meaning?

---

## 5. 🗺️ Source-Position Check  

For every source, ask:

- What are they in a position to know?
- What evidence do they hold?
- What incentives shape their account?
- What risk do they carry?
- What can they not establish?

Check whether the newsroom is privileging:

- elite English;
- institutional title;
- professional calm;
- prestige;
- ease of access;
- familiarity to power.

Keep separate:

```text
professionalism
≠ neutrality

social prestige
≠ evidentiary quality

visibility
≠ representativeness
```

Ask whether the source is being treated as credible because the evidence is strong or because they are institutionally comfortable.

---

## 6. 🌍 Diaspora Check  

For diaspora sources, record:

- region;
- language;
- migration history;
- time depth;
- current local contact;
- political position;
- family or professional network;
- host-country frame.

Ask:

- Are they recognising a reference or representing participants?
- Are they helping locate local sources?
- Is historical memory being presented as current fact?
- Is host-country racial language displacing caste, region or examination structure?
- Is diaspora racialisation being used to deny caste power?
- Is structural anti-caste critique being mistaken for collective abuse?
- Is collective abuse being excused as structural critique?

Use diaspora knowledge to improve:

- questions;
- searches;
- translation;
- source maps.

Do not use it as a substitute for local reporting.

---

## 7. 📱 Private-Circulation Check  

For every screenshot, forwarded message, image, audio or video:

- who supplied it?
- direct, relayed or unknown?
- original file available?
- earliest version?
- caption history?
- date?
- location?
- speaker?
- editing?
- earlier copies?
- exposure risk?

Keep separate:

```text
evidence of circulation
≠ evidence the claim is true

several copies
≠ several independent sources

private circulation
≠ national representativeness
```

Do not publish:

- phone numbers;
- profile images;
- group names;
- school details;
- contact chains;

unless strictly necessary and safe.

---

## 8. 🌈 Visual-Evidence Check  

For every image or video:

- what is visibly established?
- what is outside the frame?
- has it been cropped?
- has audio changed?
- is the caption original?
- has location been verified?
- has chronology been verified?
- is the image synthetic or materially edited?
- does publication identify anyone?
- is the person a minor or facing active proceedings?

Ask:

> Does the evidentiary value justify the exposure?

Keep separate:

```text
image
≠ complete causal theory

authentic media
≠ accurate caption

already online
≠ safe to amplify
```

Where necessary:

- blur;
- crop;
- paraphrase;
- delay;
- withhold;
- preserve only in restricted archive.

Do not reproduce surveillance.

---

## 9. 🌐 Regional And Neighbouring-Press Check  

For each neighbouring or regional report:

- is it original reporting?
- what is the underlying source?
- what evidence does it add?
- what framing does it impose?
- what political use may follow?
- how is the territory named?
- what does it omit?
- what domestic reporting omitted does it reveal?

Use neighbouring press as a hostile auditor.

Do not dismiss a claim because of its passport.

Do not accept it because of hostility.

### Hold Two Realities At Once  

Where India-side abuse and violent protest suppression in Pakistan-administered Kashmir are both active:

- keep separate timelines;
- keep separate casualty records;
- keep separate legal contexts;
- keep separate source maps;
- compare only where justified;
- do not let one government’s abuse become the other’s escape route.

Keep separate:

```text
attention to one abuse
≠ denial of another

comparison
≠ rebuttal

divided attention
≠ balanced reporting
```

---

## 10. 📰 Before Publication  

### Headline Test  

Can the newsroom answer:

1. What exact claim does the headline make?
2. Which source supports each verb?
3. Is attribution visible?
4. Has possibility become proof?
5. Has sequence become causation?
6. Has a promise become implementation?
7. Has one spokesperson become the whole movement?
8. Has local circulation become national virality?
9. Has a translated phrase become an exact English quote?
10. Does the body support the same certainty?

### Standfirst And Social Copy  

Audit:

- standfirst;
- deck;
- pull quotes;
- captions;
- alt text;
- newsletter subject;
- push notification;
- social post;
- social card;
- metadata;
- URL slug.

The strongest compressed wording may become the permanent machine-readable version of the story.

### Claim-Source Match  

For every major sentence:

- exact source?
- source class?
- confidence?
- limit?
- assessment date?
- contradiction?
- missing evidence?

### Exposure Test  

Before publishing names, images or contact material:

- what public-interest value does identification add?
- can the same point be made with less exposure?
- will republication increase risk?
- what will this mean in five years?

### Translation Test  

- original language preserved?
- scripts correct?
- transliteration checked?
- literal and natural meaning separated?
- joke and political reference explained?
- translator credited?
- disagreement preserved?

---

## 11. 🚩 Red Flags  

Stop and review if:

- the headline uses “proved”, “forced”, “admitted”, “targeted” or “used” without direct support;
- several outlets repeat identical wording;
- no one can locate the original source;
- one screenshot is carrying a national-scale claim;
- a public post is being treated as blanket consent;
- the most visible spokesperson is quoted as the whole movement;
- an official statement is treated as proof of implementation;
- a participant is asked to prove hidden motive;
- English fluency is being treated as expertise;
- a diaspora source is being treated as locally representative;
- a neighbouring outlet is dismissed solely because it is hostile;
- India–Pakistan comparison interrupts scrutiny of both;
- casualty counts are presented without source and date;
- anti-Brahminical critique is flattened into universal ethnic hostility;
- collective abuse is excused as structural critique;
- the image is more dramatic than the evidence;
- uncertainty appears only in paragraph twelve;
- correction cannot reach social cards or metadata.

---

## 12. ✅ Editor Sign-Off  

Before publication, the editor should be able to state:

### Event  

- We know what happened.
- We know the date and jurisdiction.
- We know what remains live.

### Claim  

- We have separated description, causation, comparison and inference.
- Every major claim has a source.
- Every source has a limit.

### People  

- Participants were spoken to before theory was imposed.
- Visible spokespeople were not treated as the whole movement.
- Invisible labour and absent people were considered.
- Consent and future risk were assessed.

### Language  

- Original language and scripts were preserved.
- Translation and cultural explanation were checked.
- Disagreement was not edited out.

### Sources  

- Source dependency was mapped.
- Prestige was not mistaken for evidence.
- Regional, local and diaspora positions were distinguished.
- Missing voices were recorded.

### Images And Private Material  

- Visuals were verified.
- Captions were checked separately.
- Contact networks were protected.
- Publication does not reproduce surveillance.

### Headline And Distribution  

- The headline does not outrun the source.
- Social cards and metadata match the evidence.
- The story can stand alone without misleading.
- A correction route exists.

---

## 13. 🔁 After Publication  

### Monitor The Record  

Track:

- official response;
- participant correction;
- legal change;
- implementation;
- compensation;
- case withdrawal;
- new casualty evidence;
- corrected translation;
- newly located original media.

### Correct Traceably  

When evidence changes:

1. preserve the earlier version;
2. record the new source;
3. state what changed;
4. update confidence;
5. correct headline, body, captions and metadata;
6. notify syndication partners where possible;
7. keep a visible correction note.

### Follow The Outcome  

Do not stop at:

- resignation;
- announcement;
- settlement;
- proscription;
- compensation promise;
- inquiry launch.

Ask:

- Was it implemented?
- Who received relief?
- Which cases remained?
- Which records were corrected?
- Did the blackout end?
- Were deaths investigated?
- Did participants remain exposed?
- Did the public need to re-enter?

### Preserve Attention  

Do not let:

- a new scandal;
- a neighbouring-state allegation;
- a political reshuffle;
- a viral image;

erase the implementation record.

```text
publication
≠ completion
```

---

## 14. 🧷 Ten Questions Before Publish  

Before publishing, can the newsroom answer:

1. What exactly happened?
2. Who says so?
3. What kind of source is that?
4. What does the source actually establish?
5. What remains interpretation or inference?
6. Has the language or joke been translated responsibly?
7. Whose knowledge is missing?
8. Could publication expose participants?
9. Does the headline outrun the evidence?
10. Would a correction remain traceable?

If not, the story is not ready.

## 🚧 What This Checklist Does Not Claim  

This checklist does not claim that:

- every story requires every source class;
- uncertainty prevents publication;
- official sources are inherently weak;
- participant accounts replace documents;
- diaspora sources are secondary;
- neighbouring press is neutral;
- images should never be published;
- anonymity is always safer;
- all comparisons are distractions;
- every headline must contain every caveat;
- one workflow can remove editorial judgement;
- correction eliminates downstream harm.

It does claim that:

- the newsroom should know what each claim is;
- every significant claim needs a traceable source and visible limit;
- translation, consent and source position belong inside reporting;
- private and visual material require provenance and exposure assessment;
- regional and neighbouring press belong inside the source environment;
- the headline must remain inside the evidence;
- corrections must follow the claim through the whole publication pathway;
- implementation and follow-up are part of the story.

## 🧷 Compact Finding  

A newsroom covering a culturally dense, fast-moving political case should be able to show:

- the event;
- the claim;
- the source;
- the confidence;
- the limit;
- the translation;
- the missing voices;
- the exposure risk;
- the headline;
- the correction path.

Turning the public up does not mean turning verification down.

It means building reporting strong enough to hear people who are usually flattened, ignored or made legible only after they perform institutional comfort.
