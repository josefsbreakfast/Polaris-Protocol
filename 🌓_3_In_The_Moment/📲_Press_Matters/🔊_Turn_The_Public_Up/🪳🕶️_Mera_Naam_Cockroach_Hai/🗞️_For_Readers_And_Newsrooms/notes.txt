✅ a_newsroom_checklist.md

Include:

* a compact practical checklist for commissioning, reporting, editing and publication;
* before reporting:
  * identify the exact event and date;
  * locate original-language material;
  * identify local and participant sources;
  * map official actors;
  * distinguish allegations from confirmed facts;
* while reporting:
  * ask participants what the symbol means;
  * ask who was not visible;
  * trace images and claims to earliest available sources;
  * record uncertainty;
  * verify translations;
  * check whether several outlets share one source;
  * assess publication risk;
* before publication:
  * test the headline against the strongest available source;
  * verify quotes;
  * verify image chronology and consent;
  * check names, scripts and transliteration;
  * separate fact, interpretation and inference;
  * include unresolved questions;
  * link or cite primary material where safe;
* after publication:
  * monitor corrections;
  * preserve archived sources;
  * update implementation claims;
  * correct social cards and metadata;
  * avoid treating silence as confirmation;
* a quick red-flag list:
  * “viral” used without evidence of scale;
  * “the students” used as a single voice;
  * one English-language source treated as complete;
  * an image published without exposure assessment;
  * an official quote stronger than the located source;
  * analogy presented as equivalence;
  * procurement presented as proof of deployment;
  * capability presented as proof of use;
* a final editor sign-off section.

Working principle for all eleven mega-nodes

Each should contain:

* a clear practical purpose;
* one central reporting or reading problem;
* examples anchored to the Cockroach case;
* explicit evidence boundaries;
* careful separation of:
  * source material;
  * participant meaning;
  * newsroom interpretation;
  * analytical inference;
* attention to translation, provenance, social position and publication risk;
* a compact finding;
* cross-references;
* no branching until a section becomes independently useful, evidentially distinct or too large to navigate.

Core newsroom test across the whole subsection:

```text
Before publishing, can the newsroom answer:

1. What exactly happened?
2. Who says so?
3. What kind of source is that?
4. What does the source actually establish?
5. What remains interpretation or inference?
6. Has the language or joke been translated responsibly?
7. Whose knowledge is missing?
8. Could publication expose participants?
9. Does the headline outrun the evidence?
10. Would a correction remain traceable?
```
