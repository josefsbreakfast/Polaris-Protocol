# 📱 The WhatsApp Test  
**First created:** 2026-07-30 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-30  
*How to report political material circulating through private networks without mistaking screenshots for provenance, forwarding for scale or open-web invisibility for social absence.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

A movement can be socially widespread before it becomes publicly searchable.

Political material may circulate through:

- family groups;
- student groups;
- workplace groups;
- regional-language groups;
- alumni networks;
- professional associations;
- neighbourhood chats;
- campaign groups;
- informal voice-note chains.

The newsroom may encounter:

- screenshots;
- forwarded audio;
- cropped video;
- compressed images;
- changed captions;
- messages with missing dates;
- content detached from the original sender.

The central reporting problem is:

> How can a newsroom recognise private circulation as part of the information environment without treating unverifiable forwarding as proof of origin, reach, organisation or representativeness?

Keep separate:

```text
not publicly searchable
        ≠
not circulating

circulating widely
        ≠
representative

forwarded many times
        ≠
independently corroborated
```

“WhatsApp” is used here as shorthand for ordinary private-network circulation.

The same method may apply to:

- Signal;
- Telegram;
- iMessage;
- SMS;
- closed Facebook groups;
- Discord;
- private Instagram accounts;
- email lists;
- local messaging platforms.

The point is not one company.

The point is the evidentiary structure of private circulation.

---

## 📱 Why The WhatsApp Test Matters  

Open-web research privileges material that is:

- public;
- indexed;
- searchable;
- persistent;
- available in dominant languages;
- published by established accounts.

Private-network circulation is often:

- socially dense;
- relational;
- fast;
- fragmented;
- ephemeral;
- difficult to verify;
- invisible to newsroom search.

This creates two opposite errors.

### Error one: invisibility  

The newsroom assumes the material did not matter because it cannot be found publicly.

### Error two: inflation  

The newsroom sees several screenshots and assumes the material reached an entire country.

Both are failures.

```text
search invisibility
        ≠
social irrelevance

private circulation
        ≠
proof of national scale
```

---

## 🕸️ Private Networks Carry Social Trust  

People may trust material because it arrives from:

- a family member;
- a classmate;
- a doctor;
- a teacher;
- a religious leader;
- a colleague;
- a local organiser;
- a known journalist.

The trust may attach to the relationship, not the content.

A message forwarded by a known person can feel more credible than an anonymous public post.

This can help circulate:

- warnings;
- practical information;
- legal updates;
- mobilisation details;
- translation;
- emotional reassurance.

It can also circulate:

- rumour;
- fabricated media;
- decontextualised clips;
- false certainty;
- hostile framing.

The newsroom should ask not only:

> Is the content true?

but:

> Through which trusted relationship did it become believable?

---

## 🧵 Family Groups Are Political Infrastructure  

Family groups may carry:

- examination updates;
- travel advice;
- legal warnings;
- requests for money;
- rumours about arrests;
- political argument;
- translated articles;
- emotional pressure;
- instructions not to participate;
- encouragement to join.

They may connect:

- candidates;
- parents;
- relatives abroad;
- local institutions;
- professional networks.

Family circulation can reveal:

- how a grievance enters ordinary life;
- how risk is interpreted;
- why some people remain invisible;
- how diaspora attention forms;
- how political language changes between generations.

It does not automatically establish:

- who originated the information;
- whether the claim is accurate;
- how many distinct people saw it;
- whether the family view represents the movement.

---

## 🧑‍🎓 Student Groups Can Become Operational Space  

Student groups may be used for:

- transport;
- meeting points;
- legal updates;
- document sharing;
- accommodation;
- safety warnings;
- slogan circulation;
- poster files;
- rapid correction;
- emotional support.

This may be genuine movement infrastructure.

It may also be ordinary coordination that later observers overinterpret as central command.

Keep separate:

```text
coordination
        ≠
central organisation

shared file
        ≠
formal strategy

active group
        ≠
movement-wide leadership
```

A screenshot showing coordination in one group does not establish the architecture of the whole movement.

---

## 📸 A Screenshot Is A New Object  

A screenshot is not the original message.

It is a new object produced by:

- a particular phone;
- a particular crop;
- a particular time;
- a particular sender;
- a particular privacy choice.

A screenshot may remove:

- date;
- sender;
- reply chain;
- preceding context;
- later correction;
- group name;
- forwarding label;
- media metadata.

It may add:

- crop;
- markup;
- highlight;
- translation;
- hostile caption;
- false attribution.

```text
screenshot of message
        ≠
original message record
```

The newsroom should treat the screenshot as evidence that someone captured this display.

It should not automatically treat it as proof that every visible field is authentic.

---

## 🎙️ Forwarded Audio Needs A Chain  

Voice notes can appear especially credible because they contain:

- a human voice;
- urgency;
- local accent;
- apparent insider detail;
- emotional conviction.

The speaker may be:

- direct witness;
- second-hand narrator;
- impersonator;
- mistaken;
- deliberately deceptive;
- speaking in another context.

Ask:

- Who recorded it?
- When?
- In which language?
- Was the speaker identified independently?
- Is this the original file?
- Has it been edited?
- Was it forwarded with a new caption?
- Is there a longer recording?
- Does the speaker consent to publication?

A recognisable accent is not proof of origin.

A confident voice is not corroboration.

---

## 🎞️ Cropped And Compressed Video  

Private platforms often compress media.

Compression can remove or degrade:

- metadata;
- resolution;
- audio quality;
- frame detail;
- editing traces.

Cropping can remove:

- date;
- signage;
- location;
- speaker;
- police presence;
- surrounding crowd;
- source watermark.

The newsroom should seek:

- original file;
- earliest available copy;
- uncropped version;
- longer sequence;
- uploader;
- date;
- location;
- prior versions.

A compressed forwarded clip may still contain important evidence.

Its limitations should remain visible.

---

## 🏷️ Captions Change Faster Than Files  

The same image or video may travel with several captions.

Example:

```text
original:
student protest at location A

forward 1:
police attack students

forward 2:
government finally falls

forward 3:
this happened today in another city
```

The media file may remain unchanged.

The claim attached to it changes.

The newsroom should archive:

- media;
- caption;
- sender;
- date;
- group context;
- later variants.

Do not publish the file with the latest caption as though they originated together.

---

## ⏳ Reverse Chronology  

Private circulation often presents the newest forwarded copy first.

The newsroom should work backwards.

Ask:

1. Who sent this copy?
2. Where did they receive it?
3. Is the previous sender available?
4. Is the original file available?
5. Are there earlier captions?
6. Does another version preserve more metadata?
7. When did the item first enter this network?
8. Is there a public or regional source predating it?

```text
latest screenshot
        ↓
earlier forward
        ↓
earlier caption
        ↓
original file
        ↓
original event
```

The aim is not always to identify the first person in the movement.

It is to identify the earliest defensible source presently available.

---

## 🪜 Direct, Relayed And Unknown  

Classify the source relationship.

### Direct  

The sender:

- created the media;
- witnessed the event;
- wrote the message;
- received the document from the institution.

### Relayed  

The sender knows who supplied it but did not directly create or witness it.

### Unknown  

The chain has broken.

The source may still be useful.

But the copy should say:

- circulated in student groups;
- shared by several participants;
- original sender not established;
- date or location not independently verified.

Do not turn an unknown chain into a direct claim.

---

## 📊 What Private Circulation Can Establish  

Private-network material may help establish:

- that an item circulated in a particular network;
- how participants explained an event;
- which rumours required correction;
- how language changed;
- what practical information was shared;
- how diaspora or family attention formed;
- how a symbol moved between publics.

It may not establish:

- national scale;
- representativeness;
- original authorship;
- central coordination;
- factual accuracy of the underlying claim;
- how many unique people saw it;
- government response.

Keep separate:

```text
evidence of circulation
        ≠
evidence that circulated claim is true
```

---

## 📏 Reach Is Difficult To Measure  

A message may be forwarded through:

- overlapping groups;
- duplicate memberships;
- screenshots;
- copied text;
- re-uploaded video.

Apparent reach may count the same people repeatedly.

A group with hundreds of members may contain:

- inactive accounts;
- observers;
- journalists;
- opponents;
- duplicated participants.

The newsroom should avoid unsupported claims such as:

- went viral across India;
- reached millions;
- spread everywhere;
- dominated student groups.

Better wording may be:

- circulated in several student and family groups reviewed by the newsroom;
- appeared in groups spanning multiple regions;
- was independently supplied by several participants;
- exact reach could not be established.

---

## 📣 Virality Is Not Representativeness  

A message may spread because it is:

- funny;
- shocking;
- emotionally simple;
- visually memorable;
- endorsed by a prestigious sender;
- hostile;
- false;
- easy to forward.

Virality may reveal:

- recognisability;
- emotional charge;
- network access.

It does not reveal:

- agreement;
- participation;
- demographic balance;
- national opinion;
- organisational control.

```text
virality
        ≠
representativeness

circulation
        ≠
endorsement
```

People forward material to:

- support;
- criticise;
- mock;
- verify;
- warn;
- document.

The act of forwarding needs context.

---

## 🗳️ Private Groups Are Not Polls  

A family, student or professional group is not a representative sample.

Membership may be shaped by:

- institution;
- caste;
- class;
- language;
- geography;
- age;
- profession;
- political alignment;
- social trust.

The newsroom should not infer:

- “Indian students believe”;
- “the diaspora thinks”;
- “parents demanded”;

from a small set of private groups.

Describe the network actually observed.

---

## 🕳️ Open-Web Absence Is Not Absence  

The newsroom may fail to find a phrase publicly because:

- it circulated privately;
- it was deleted;
- it appeared in regional language;
- accounts were locked;
- participants feared exposure;
- moderation removed it;
- search did not index it;
- screenshots replaced original posts.

Do not conclude:

> There is no evidence it circulated.

Say:

> No public version has yet been located.

That preserves the search limit.

---

## 🔍 Search Blind Spots  

Newsroom search may privilege:

- English;
- large platforms;
- national outlets;
- verified accounts;
- high-ranking domains.

It may miss:

- transliteration variants;
- spelling variants;
- regional scripts;
- image text;
- audio;
- closed groups;
- deleted content;
- small local pages.

The WhatsApp test asks:

> What might be socially present but technically invisible to our search method?

This is not permission to believe unevidenced claims.

It is a reason to widen the method.

---

## 🔤 Search Across Language And Script  

A phrase may appear in:

- Devanagari;
- Urdu script;
- Latin transliteration;
- regional script;
- emoji;
- image text;
- phonetic misspelling.

Search:

- exact phrase;
- spelling variants;
- transliteration variants;
- translated meaning;
- key names;
- visual symbol;
- relevant hashtags.

Ask participants which spelling they actually used.

Formal transliteration may not match private-group language.

---

## 🧾 Verification Questions  

Before relying on private-network material, ask:

1. Who supplied this copy?
2. Are they direct, relayed or unknown?
3. When did they receive it?
4. Which group or network did it appear in?
5. Is the original file available?
6. Are there earlier versions?
7. Has the caption changed?
8. Can date and location be independently checked?
9. Is the sender identifiable?
10. Is publication safe?
11. What does this prove?
12. What does it not prove?

The final two questions matter most.

---

## 🧬 Shared Source Dependency  

Several participants may send the same item.

That can mean:

- wide circulation;
- one popular group;
- one influential sender;
- one screenshot copied repeatedly.

Map dependency.

```text
original file
        ↓
group A
        ├── participant 1
        ├── participant 2
        └── participant 3
```

Three newsroom sources may still represent one circulation route.

This is evidence of reach within a network.

It is not three independent confirmations of the underlying event.

---

## 🛡️ Protect The Network  

Private material may expose:

- phone numbers;
- profile images;
- names;
- group titles;
- institutions;
- locations;
- family relationships;
- political affiliations;
- contact chains.

Do not publish screenshots without reviewing every visible field.

Possible protections include:

- crop;
- blur;
- redraw;
- transcription;
- paraphrase;
- removal of timestamps;
- withholding the screenshot;
- restricted archival storage.

The newsroom should not convert a private group into a target list.

```text
verification need
        ≠
publication need
```

A newsroom may need to inspect identifying information.

The public may not need to see it.

---

## 📷 Profile Images And Group Names  

Profile images can reveal:

- face;
- family;
- religion;
- workplace;
- politics;
- location.

Group names can reveal:

- school;
- course;
- movement role;
- region;
- legal strategy;
- private affiliation.

Blur or remove them unless they are necessary to the evidentiary claim and publication is safe.

Do not assume that a small thumbnail is harmless.

---

## 👶 Minors And Vulnerable Participants  

Private groups may include:

- minors;
- students under family control;
- people facing active cases;
- disabled participants;
- people in crisis;
- bereaved families.

Use heightened caution.

Do not publish:

- phone numbers;
- faces;
- unique biographies;
- medical details;
- school identifiers;
- family messages;

without clear public-interest need and meaningful consent.

A screenshot that appears ordinary may become searchable evidence against someone later.

---

## 🧭 Consent In Private Circulation  

A person who sends material to a group may consent to:

- group circulation.

They may not consent to:

- national publication;
- international publication;
- naming;
- archival preservation;
- facial recognition;
- use by police;
- use by AI systems.

```text
shared in group
        ≠
consent to mass republication
```

Where possible, contact the original creator or speaker.

Where impossible, assess:

- public interest;
- identifiability;
- harm;
- necessity;
- alternatives.

---

## 🗂️ Preserve Originals Securely  

Where lawful and safe, preserve:

- original file;
- supplied screenshot;
- date received;
- source identity;
- source relationship;
- original caption;
- later captions;
- hashes or file details;
- verification notes.

Store:

- public copy;
- restricted evidentiary copy;

separately where needed.

Do not preserve unnecessary personal data merely because it may later disappear.

Archival value does not cancel privacy.

---

## 📝 Quote The Network Precisely  

Useful wording includes:

- “The message circulated in three student groups reviewed by the newsroom.”
- “Two participants independently supplied the same screenshot, although both copies appear to derive from one earlier post.”
- “The original sender could not be established.”
- “The audio was widely forwarded, but its speaker and recording date remain unverified.”
- “The clip is authentic to the event, but the attached caption was added later.”
- “No public upload predating the private copies has been located.”

Avoid:

- everyone was sharing;
- the whole country saw;
- WhatsApp proved;
- students universally believed.

---

## 🪳 Applying The Test To The Cockroach  

For Cockroach material, ask:

- Was the first located version public or private?
- Did the symbol circulate as image, text, audio or video?
- Which scripts and transliterations appeared?
- Did captions change the meaning?
- Did family groups interpret it differently from student groups?
- Did diaspora groups add geopolitical framing?
- Did hostile groups repost it?
- Did later public visibility obscure earlier private circulation?
- Did private circulation help with logistics, recognition or both?
- Is there evidence of coordination, or only evidence of repetition?

Do not reduce the question to:

> Did it go viral on WhatsApp?

The stronger questions are:

> Through which networks did it travel?

> What changed as it travelled?

> What can those records actually establish?

## 📋 Private-Circulation Record  

For each significant item:

```text
ITEM:
Message, screenshot, image, audio or video.

DATE RECEIVED:
When the newsroom obtained it.

SOURCE:
Named source or internal identifier.

SOURCE RELATION:
Direct / relayed / unknown.

NETWORK:
Family / student / workplace / regional / diaspora / other.

ORIGINAL FILE:
Available / unavailable.

EARLIEST LOCATED VERSION:
Date, sender and platform where known.

CAPTION HISTORY:
Original and later captions.

LANGUAGE:
Script, transliteration and translation status.

VERIFICATION:
Date, location, speaker, media integrity and corroboration.

CIRCULATION EVIDENCE:
Which groups or independent routes supplied it.

WHAT IT ESTABLISHES:
Narrow supported finding.

WHAT IT CANNOT ESTABLISH:
Scale, representativeness, origin, organisation or truth of claim.

EXPOSURE RISK:
Names, numbers, faces, group titles and relationships.

PUBLICATION DECISION:
Publish, redact, paraphrase, describe or withhold.
```

Within this folder, the record should remain anchored to Cockroach-specific material.

The generic private-network protocol belongs later in `🧭_Reusable_Findings/`.

## 🚧 What This Node Does Not Claim  

This node does not claim that:

- WhatsApp is the only important private platform;
- private groups are inherently unreliable;
- forwarded material is necessarily false;
- open-web material is automatically stronger;
- screenshots have no evidentiary value;
- compression proves manipulation;
- every private message can be traced to an origin;
- virality can never be estimated;
- group members endorse what they receive;
- private circulation proves organisation;
- publication is justified whenever verification succeeds;
- all identifying details must be retained;
- open-web absence proves private circulation occurred.

It does claim that:

- private networks are part of the political information environment;
- searchable visibility and social circulation are different;
- screenshots and forwarded media require provenance work;
- captions may change while files remain the same;
- repetition is not independent corroboration;
- scale and representativeness require separate evidence;
- newsroom search methods have language and platform blind spots;
- network protection is part of verification;
- material shared privately is not automatically available for mass republication.

## 🧷 Compact Finding  

The open web is not the whole public.

A message may be socially important while remaining technically invisible to newsroom search.

But private circulation is difficult evidence.

A screenshot proves that a screenshot existed.

A forwarded clip may prove that a clip circulated.

Neither automatically proves:

- origin;
- truth;
- scale;
- endorsement;
- organisation;
- representativeness.

The newsroom must trace the chain, protect the network and describe only what the material can actually establish.
