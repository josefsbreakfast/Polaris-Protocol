# 🍉 Connections To Other Oppressions  
**First created:** 2026-07-29 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-29  
*How one population’s repression can become another institution’s supplier relationship, doctrine, precedent, revenue stream or political protection—without making different histories interchangeable.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

Oppressions are connected.

They are not identical.

That distinction is the governing rule of this node.

A movement may recognise that the systems confronting it are linked to:

- occupation;
- militarised borders;
- surveillance;
- detention;
- policing;
- censorship;
- biometric control;
- population classification;
- platform restriction;
- arms production;
- diplomatic protection;
- counterterrorism doctrine;
- commercial markets built around repression.

But recognition must not become flattening.

```text
connected
        ≠
equivalent
        ≠
interchangeable
```

The task is not to search for one master oppression and fit every population inside it.

The task is to trace the relationships through which:

- products travel;
- doctrine travels;
- legal arguments travel;
- commercial credibility travels;
- political protection travels;
- records of operational use become sales material;
- one institution learns what another population has already been forced to endure.

Palestine is the first substantial lens because Indian procurement, military cooperation, public solidarity and the politics of occupation create a concrete route into the analysis.

Palestinians must remain the subjects of that original oppression.

They are not merely evidence in somebody else’s supply-chain investigation.

## 🍉 Why Palestine Comes First Here  

Palestine occupies a distinct place in the movement’s wider political environment.

Possible connections include:

- Indian military procurement from Israeli companies;
- joint development and domestic manufacture;
- surveillance and security technology;
- diplomatic alignment;
- university and industry partnerships;
- repression or narrowing of Palestine solidarity;
- the political recognition of occupation by people with their own anticolonial histories;
- commercial claims built around operational experience.

These connections do not prove that:

- every CJP participant held the same view;
- Palestine was the movement’s central demand;
- every Israeli-linked product was used against Palestinians;
- every Indian contract strengthened a specific act of repression;
- every technology used in India originated in occupation;
- every participant consciously connected the cases.

They make Palestine a necessary analytical route.

The movement existed inside a country whose institutions had material relationships with a state exercising prolonged control over Palestinian land, movement, population records and political life.

That fact should be traced carefully rather than treated as atmosphere.

## 🧍 Palestinians Are Not A Metaphor  

Comparative political writing can turn Palestinians into a warning label.

The structure becomes:

> This technology was used on Palestinians, and therefore everyone else should be worried.

That framing is inadequate.

Palestinians are not important only because a system may later travel elsewhere.

They are people subjected to:

- military occupation;
- displacement;
- settlement expansion;
- restrictions on movement;
- permit systems;
- surveillance;
- detention;
- lethal force;
- destruction of homes and infrastructure;
- interference with political, economic and family life.

UN reporting has repeatedly documented the layered administrative, physical and digital systems governing Palestinian movement and daily existence in the occupied territory.

The ethical order matters:

```text
Palestinian harm
        is not
a product demonstration

Palestinian life
        is not
a warning supplied for others
```

The export question comes afterwards.

## 🧱 Occupation Is A System, Not One Weapon  

Occupation is not reducible to military hardware.

It can operate through:

- territorial control;
- checkpoints;
- permits;
- roads and barriers;
- settlement infrastructure;
- population registries;
- land classification;
- detention;
- surveillance;
- administrative orders;
- courts;
- communications control;
- economic dependency;
- restrictions on assembly and movement.

UNCTAD has described the West Bank as subject to a multilayered system of administrative and physical impediments, including permit regimes, checkpoints, gates, roadblocks, trenches, the wall and settlements.

A 2023 UN Special Rapporteur report described a wider carceral continuum involving physical, bureaucratic and digital confinement beyond prison walls.

This matters because a product does not need to be a weapon in order to support coercive control.

Possible enabling systems include:

- databases;
- cameras;
- biometric tools;
- cloud services;
- communications infrastructure;
- mapping;
- analytics;
- identity systems;
- checkpoint technology;
- maintenance and technical support.

The analysis must therefore examine infrastructure as well as force.

## 👁️ Surveillance Is Part Of Territorial Power  

Surveillance in occupied territory is not only about observing individuals.

It can help govern:

- movement;
- access;
- identity;
- association;
- work;
- travel;
- permits;
- protest;
- detention;
- family unification;
- entry into specific spaces.

A camera or database becomes politically meaningful through the system into which it is integrated.

```text
identity data
        +
territorial control
        +
movement permission
        +
enforcement
        ↓
surveillance becomes
a method of governing life
```

This is why product-level tracing matters.

The question is not simply whether a technology can recognise a face.

It is:

> What institutional decision becomes possible after the recognition occurs?

## 🏭 The Security Market Converts Experience Into Value  

Security industries sell more than objects.

They sell:

- credibility;
- expertise;
- integration;
- training;
- operational confidence;
- claims that the product has worked under difficult conditions.

Companies may market technology as:

- proven;
- operational;
- field-tested;
- combat-proven;
- battle-tested.

These terms can convert experience in conflict or occupation into commercial value.

But the claim must be traced precisely.

For any product, ask:

- Did the company use the term?
- Which product was named?
- What kind of operation was referenced?
- Was deployment independently documented?
- Was the exported version technically the same?
- Was the relevant configuration used?
- Did the operational claim influence the procurement?

Do not apply “battle-tested” to an entire national industry without product-specific evidence.

Do not treat a company’s marketing statement as independent verification.

The wider political mechanism remains important:

```text
coercive deployment
        ↓
operational knowledge
        ↓
commercial credibility
        ↓
export opportunity
```

## 💰 Repression Can Become Revenue  

A public or private institution may benefit financially from technologies developed, refined or marketed through systems of coercive control.

Possible revenue routes include:

- direct sale;
- licence fees;
- royalties;
- maintenance;
- upgrades;
- training;
- joint ventures;
- local manufacture;
- technical support;
- cloud services;
- consultancy;
- export finance.

This does not make every employee, investor or customer equally responsible.

It creates a chain that can be investigated.

```text
original site of coercion
        ↓
technology or doctrine acquires value
        ↓
foreign procurement
        ↓
revenue and institutional growth
        ↓
greater capacity for future export
```

Where that chain is documented, one population’s repression can become part of another institution’s capability and another company’s income.

Examples include:

- DRDO–IAI joint development of MRSAM;
- production for Indian military users;
- BEL manufacture in collaboration with IAI;
- BEL–IAI lifecycle-support arrangements;
- a dedicated BEL–IAI joint venture;
- further agreements on air-defence systems;
- collaboration and technology-transfer language involving Elbit-linked systems.

These relationships can create:

- Indian industrial capacity;
- employment;
- military capability;
- domestic manufacture;
- long-term maintenance arrangements;
- continuing technical dependence;
- shared commercial interests;
- diplomatic constituencies invested in continuity.

This node does not treat those benefits as imaginary.

It asks what else the relationship contains.

A defence partnership can be:

- commercially valuable;
- strategically important;
- domestically productive;
- politically compromising;
- connected to a system of occupation.

Several truths can exist at once.

- royalties;
- maintenance rights;
- transfer agreements;
- operational history;

The domestic product may be real.

The connection may also be real.

```text
Indian manufacture
        +
foreign technology lineage
        +
shared commercial benefit
        +
continuing support
        ↓
a transnational capability

```

- based on;

## 🔗 Material Connection Is Stronger Than Rhetorical Similarity  

The strongest connections are traceable.

A useful ladder is:

```text
shared rhetoric
        ↓
similar technique
        ↓
shared supplier
        ↓
shared product
        ↓
shared training
        ↓
documented transfer
        ↓
documented use
```

Examples of material connection may include:

- the same company;
- the same product family;
- the same software;
- the same maintenance provider;
- the same training programme;
- the same legal consultant;
- the same cloud infrastructure;
- the same export licence;
- the same doctrine cited in policy.

A general resemblance between two systems can be politically useful.

It is not the same as evidence that one caused or trained the other.

## ⚖️ Legal Arguments Travel Too  

States observe how other states justify coercive practices.

Recurring legal or political frames include:

- national security;
- terrorism;
- extremism;
- public order;
- border integrity;
- misinformation;
- communal harmony;
- emergency necessity;
- prevention of incitement;
- protection of critical infrastructure.

These categories may address real dangers.

They can also widen the zone in which:

- secrecy increases;
- evidence is withheld;
- association becomes suspicious;
- protest becomes security data;
- ordinary safeguards weaken.

The connection does not require identical laws.

It may appear through a shared governing logic:

```text
identify an exceptional threat
        ↓
move the target into a special category
        ↓
reduce ordinary procedural protection
        ↓
present coercion as technical necessity
```

## 🛂 Borders Are Laboratories Of Exception  

Border systems often normalise capabilities that later appear elsewhere.

These may include:

- biometric identity;
- watchlists;
- automated risk assessment;
- device searches;
- movement databases;
- facial recognition;
- data sharing;
- detention without ordinary access;
- private contractors;
- broad security discretion.

Occupation and border control are not interchangeable.

But both can create spaces in which movement becomes conditional and identity becomes an administrative permission.

The important question is:

> Which capabilities first become acceptable when used against people classified as external, dangerous or exceptional?

Once normalised, the same techniques may become available for:

- migration;
- public order;
- policing;
- welfare enforcement;
- education;
- protest management.

This requires evidence of transfer, not merely fear.

But the institutional pathway is politically important.

## 🧑‍💻 Platforms Connect Different Sites Of Repression  

Global platforms operate across many jurisdictions.

They may face:

- blocking orders;
- law-enforcement requests;
- content rules;
- national-security demands;

- political advertising;
- requests to preserve or disclose data.

The same platform infrastructure may therefore mediate:

- diaspora solidarity;
- state requests from several governments;
- content moderation across multiple languages.

UN experts have raised concerns about surveillance, harassment and alleged systematic censorship affecting Palestinian and pro-Palestinian voices on major platforms.

A platform’s treatment of one movement does not automatically prove the treatment of another.

But shared infrastructure creates a transnational question:

```text
one company
        +
many governments
        +
unequal legal pressure
        +
unequal language support
        ↓
different movements encounter
the same private gatekeeper
```

## 📡 Diplomatic Protection Is Part Of The System  

Material connection is not limited to sales.

States may protect partners through:

- diplomatic statements;
- abstentions or votes;
- resistance to sanctions;
- trade continuation;
- military cooperation;
- intelligence relationships;
- export licensing;
- shielding companies from political consequences;
- narrowing domestic criticism.

Diplomatic positions should be traced over time.

One vote does not define an entire relationship.

India has historically supported many UN resolutions concerning Palestinian rights while also deepening defence and strategic cooperation with Israel.

That combination matters.

It shows that states can hold:

```text
formal support for Palestinian rights
        +
material partnership with Israeli institutions
```

The relationship is not captured by a single label.

It should be examined through:

- votes;
- contracts;
- statements;
- military cooperation;
- trade;
- domestic political practice.

## 🗳️ Voting Records Are Evidence, Not Essence  

UN voting records provide a traceable part of diplomatic conduct.

They do not reveal every motive.

A vote may reflect:

- legal position;
- alliance management;
- domestic politics;
- diplomatic compromise;
- wording disputes;
- strategic ambiguity.

India’s voting pattern has varied across resolutions and years.

For example, official UN records show India voting in favour of some resolutions concerning settlements and Palestinian rights, while abstaining on some accountability resolutions.

The analytical rule is:

```text
vote
        =
documented diplomatic act

vote
        ≠
complete foreign policy
```

Use the act.

Do not turn it into a permanent national essence.

## 🧬 Oppressions Can Interlock Without Sharing One Cause  

Caste oppression, occupation, colonialism, anti-Muslim politics, class exploitation and racialised border control have different histories and structures.

They may still interact.

Examples include:

- dominant-group nationalism using security language against minorities;
- caste hierarchy shaping who bears the cost of surveillance;
- anti-Muslim politics making occupation easier to defend;
- class inequality determining who can avoid coercive systems;
- colonial administrative forms surviving inside postcolonial states;
- global firms selling the same capability into different hierarchies.

The connection is relational.

```text
different history
        +
different target population
        +
shared supplier, technique or political logic
        ↓
interlocking systems
without historical equivalence
```

This is more precise than saying every oppression is the same.

political affinity

## 🧾 Corporate Connection Requires Its Own Ladder  

For each company, distinguish:

```text
incorporated in a country
        ↓
supplies a state institution
        ↓
supplies a capability used in occupied territory
        ↓
specific product documented in use
        ↓
product marketed through that use
        ↓
same product exported elsewhere
        ↓
documented use in the second context
```

Do not jump from company nationality to product-level responsibility.

Do not assume that every product in a corporate portfolio shares one operational history.

Do not assume that a supplier’s connection to occupation establishes CJP-specific use.

At the same time, do not allow corporate complexity to erase:

- ownership;
- revenue;
- maintenance;
- operational support;
- documented enabling roles.

## 🧷 Compact Finding  

The connection was not that every struggle was the same.

The connection was material.

A technology could be developed inside one system of control, gain commercial credibility there, be purchased or manufactured elsewhere, create revenue across borders and become available to another institution confronting another public.

Palestinians were not a rehearsal for everybody else.

They were people subjected to the original violence.

The export chain mattered because their repression could be converted into capability, profit and political protection elsewhere.
