# 💰 Follow The Money  
**First created:** 2026-07-29 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-29  
*How surveillance, security and administrative capability is purchased, owned, integrated, maintained and converted into long-term institutional dependency.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

A state does not need to invent every capability it uses.

It can buy one.

It can:

- commission a service;
- licence software;
- purchase hardware;
- hire an integrator;
- outsource operation;
- train staff through a supplier;
- obtain foreign technology through a domestic partner;
- fund local manufacture under licence;
- depend on maintenance, updates and replacement parts after the first sale.

Procurement therefore does more than move public money.

It decides:

- what an institution can do;
- which technologies become normal;
- who understands the system;
- who can inspect it;
- who receives candidate or citizen data;
- which suppliers become difficult to replace;
- which commercial relationships acquire political importance.

The first contract is rarely the whole system.

```text
policy objective
        ↓
contracting authority
        ↓
prime contractor
        ↓
subcontractors and technology owners
        ↓
licensing, integration and training
        ↓
maintenance and upgrades
        ↓
institutional dependency
        ↓
financial and political beneficiaries
```

The central question is:

> Who paid for the capability, who supplied it, who owned it, who operated it and who continued to benefit after the first invoice was paid?

## 💰 Procurement Creates Capability  

Procurement is where an institutional intention begins to acquire technical form.

An authority may want to:

- prevent impersonation;
- monitor examination centres;
- manage crowds;
- detect prohibited devices;
- authenticate faces;
- analyse CCTV;
- run computer-based tests;
- preserve examination records;
- extract data from devices;
- map public activity;
- coordinate incident response.

That intention becomes capability through:

- a specification;
- a tender;
- bidder qualification;
- technical evaluation;
- contract award;
- deployment;
- integration;
- access control;
- training;
- maintenance.

The National Testing Agency’s tender archive shows procurement activity across computer-based testing, examination-management services, security and surveillance, live CCTV, biometric and face authentication, frisking, examination monitoring, image processing, CCTV analytics, digital infrastructure, command-centre work and outsourced manpower.

That archive does not prove that every tender was awarded, that every service was deployed or that any named supplier caused a particular examination failure.

It does show that the examination experienced by the candidate is assembled through a substantial procurement environment.

## 🧾 Start With The Contracting Authority  

Before following the supplier, identify the public body that created the purchase.

Ask:

- Who defined the need?
- Who wrote the specification?
- Who approved the budget?
- Who issued the tender?
- Who evaluated the bids?
- Who signed the contract?
- Who received the service?
- Who inspected performance?
- Who authorised renewal?
- Who could impose a penalty?
- Who could terminate the arrangement?

These roles may sit with different bodies.

Possible actors include:

- a ministry;
- the National Testing Agency;
- a state government;
- a police force;
- a public-sector undertaking;
- a university;
- an examination owner;
- another contracting agency acting on behalf of the end user.

The authority named on the tender may not be the only public body shaping the requirement.

The final user may not be the body that signed the contract.

Procurement analysis should therefore distinguish:

```text
policy owner
        ≠
budget holder
        ≠
contracting authority
        ≠
operator
        ≠
beneficiary
```

## 📑 A Tender Is Not An Award  

A tender establishes that an authority sought a service.

It does not automatically establish:

- that bids were received;
- that the tender was not cancelled;
- which supplier won;
- what final price was agreed;
- whether the scope changed;
- whether the contract was signed;
- whether the system was deployed;
- whether the authority accepted the work;
- whether the supplier performed adequately.

The NTA archive itself includes annulment notices, corrigenda, expressions of interest, pilot procurements and repeated versions of similar requirements.

The evidence ladder should therefore begin:

```text
request for information
        ↓
expression of interest
        ↓
published tender
        ↓
bid
        ↓
award
        ↓
signed contract
        ↓
deployment
        ↓
acceptance
        ↓
renewal or maintenance
```

Do not jump from tender language to operational fact.

## 🏢 The Prime Contractor Is Not The End Of The Trail  

The company signing the public contract may assemble technology owned by other organisations.

A prime contractor may rely on:

- software vendors;
- equipment manufacturers;
- cloud providers;
- data processors;
- local centre operators;
- temporary-workforce suppliers;
- telecommunications companies;
- biometric-system providers;
- foreign original equipment manufacturers;
- specialist subcontractors.

The prime may be responsible for the whole service while possessing only part of the underlying technology.

This creates two different maps.

### The contractual map

Who owes duties to the public authority?

### The capability map

Who owns, understands, controls or maintains the technology?

The maps overlap.

They are not identical.

```text
named contractor
        ↓
subcontractors
        ↓
original equipment manufacturer
        ↓
software or model owner
        ↓
cloud or infrastructure provider
        ↓
maintenance and support chain
```

A public authority may know the prime contractor well while remaining dependent upon actors farther down the chain.

## 🧠 Who Owns The Technology?  

Ownership should be traced at the level that matters operationally.

Ask who owns or controls:

- source code;
- patents;
- model weights;
- biometric templates;
- proprietary databases;
- encryption keys;
- device firmware;
- update servers;
- system documentation;
- training materials;
- integration tools;
- export permissions;
- replacement components.

A system can be physically located in India while remaining dependent upon intellectual property controlled elsewhere.

A domestic contractor may operate the product without being able to:

- alter its core logic;
- inspect every component;
- repair it independently;
- migrate the data;
- issue updates;
- continue support after the licence ends.

This is why the visible location of manufacture does not settle the dependency question.

---

## 🪆 Made In India, Foreign IP  

Domestic production can be real and valuable.

It may create:

- manufacturing capacity;
- engineering skill;
- employment;
- supply resilience;
- local testing;
- adaptation to Indian requirements;
- export potential;
- bargaining power.

But “made in India” may describe several different arrangements.

```text
designed and owned in India

assembled in India from imported systems

manufactured under licence

built through transferred technology

co-developed with a foreign partner

domestically integrated around foreign core IP
```

These are not interchangeable.

The analysis should ask:

- Who designed the core system?
- Was technology transferred?
- What rights were transferred?
- Was the transfer permanent or licensed?
- Could the Indian partner modify the design?
- Who receives royalties?
- Who supplies critical components?
- Who controls updates?
- Who authorises export?
- Who provides post-warranty support?

Local manufacture may reduce one dependency while creating or preserving another.

---

## 🔁 Transfer Of Technology Is Not Transfer Of Sovereignty  

“Transfer of technology” can refer to very different levels of access.

It may include:

- manufacturing drawings;
- assembly instructions;
- testing procedures;
- tooling;
- training;
- limited production rights;
- repair rights;
- software access;
- design knowledge;
- permission to modify;
- export rights.

A transfer allowing assembly does not necessarily transfer:

- ownership of the design;
- source code;
- independent upgrade rights;
- control over future versions;
- unrestricted export;
- the ability to replace the original supplier.

The correct question is not only:

> Was technology transferred?

It is:

> Which layer of capability became independent, and which remained controlled by the original owner?

---

## 🧩 The Integrator Can Become The Real Centre Of Knowledge  

Complex systems need integration.

The integrator may connect:

- hardware;
- software;
- identity records;
- examination-centre systems;
- CCTV;
- communications;
- data storage;
- command dashboards;
- reporting;
- incident workflows.

That role can create enormous practical influence.

The integrator may understand:

- how systems exchange data;
- which fields are matched;
- where logs are held;
- which failures are visible;
- which failures remain hidden;
- how one vendor’s output affects another.

The public authority may own the policy.

The integrator may own the operational picture.

This is another route to the hollow centre:

```text
public authority defines the mission
        while
private integrator understands
how the mission actually runs
```

Integration should therefore be treated as a distinct capability, not an incidental service.

---

## 🪪 Procurement Determines Data Access  

A contract may create lawful access to candidate or citizen information.

Possible recipients include:

- the prime contractor;
- biometric providers;
- centre operators;
- cloud providers;
- technical-support staff;
- subcontractors;
- auditors;
- platform or communications providers.

For each actor, record:

- which data they receive;
- why they receive it;
- whether they can copy it;
- where it is stored;
- how long it is retained;
- whether access is logged;
- whether subcontractors can see it;
- what happens at contract end;
- whether the authority can verify deletion.

Data access should not be inferred merely from supplier involvement.

It should be traced through:

- tender requirements;
- privacy notices;
- data-processing clauses;
- system architecture;
- access-control records;
- audit reports;
- incident reports.

The relevant distinction is:

```text
providing a service
        ≠
access to every dataset
```

But where access exists, it forms part of the procurement finding.

---

## 🔐 Proprietary Systems Can Weaken Public Audit  

A supplier may protect:

- trade secrets;
- source code;
- technical design;
- security architecture;
- pricing;
- model logic.

Some confidentiality is legitimate.

Public disclosure of every security detail can create real vulnerability.

But proprietary protection can also make it difficult to determine:

- why a system made a decision;
- whether it performed as promised;
- whether bias or error occurred;
- whether logs were complete;
- whether the authority could test the supplier independently;
- whether the public can challenge an automated outcome.

The balance is not:

```text
complete public disclosure
        versus
complete supplier secrecy
```

Possible safeguards include:

- confidential independent audit;
- regulator access;
- contractual inspection;
- source-code escrow;
- test datasets;
- performance reporting;
- incident disclosure;
- human review;
- court-supervised examination.

Commercial secrecy should not become immunity from public accountability.

---

## 🔧 Maintenance Is Part Of The Purchase  

A capability does not end when it is delivered.

It may require:

- patches;
- security updates;
- model retraining;
- replacement components;
- licence renewal;
- cloud hosting;
- calibration;
- certification;
- technical support;
- staff retraining;
- data migration.

The maintenance relationship may last longer than the procurement controversy that created it.

That matters because the supplier can become embedded in routine administration.

```text
initial purchase
        ↓
integration
        ↓
staff training
        ↓
data accumulation
        ↓
maintenance
        ↓
renewal
        ↓
switching becomes expensive
```

This is lifecycle dependency.

---

## 🔒 Vendor Lock-In Is Institutional Power  

A supplier becomes difficult to replace when:

- data is stored in a proprietary format;
- staff are trained only on one system;
- other systems depend on its interfaces;
- replacement requires service interruption;
- historical records cannot be migrated easily;
- warranties depend on approved maintenance;
- the authority lacks internal technical expertise;
- no alternative supplier has access to the design.

Lock-in can be commercial.

It can also become political.

A government may find that changing a diplomatic or commercial relationship threatens systems already embedded in:

- defence;
- policing;
- examinations;
- identity management;
- infrastructure.

This does not mean procurement mechanically determines policy.

It creates constituencies, costs and dependencies that shape what rupture would require.

---

## 📈 Renewal Can Escape The Attention Given To The First Purchase  

The first contract may receive:

- a public tender;
- political scrutiny;
- media attention;
- competitive bidding.

Later expenditure may appear as:

- maintenance;
- support;
- extension;
- upgrade;
- compatibility work;
- additional licences;
- replacement units;
- change requests.

These later purchases may be operationally necessary.

They may also deepen dependency without reopening the original policy question.

Ask:

- Was renewal competitive?
- Was the incumbent the only technically viable supplier?
- Did the authority assess alternatives?
- Was performance reviewed?
- Were earlier failures corrected?
- Did the scope expand?
- Did the data use change?
- Did the public receive notice?

A capability can grow through maintenance budgets more quietly than through a headline procurement.

## 📱 Digital Forensics Requires Product-Level Tracing  

“Digital forensics” is not one capability.

It may include:

- device extraction;
- password recovery;
- deleted-file recovery;
- cloud-data acquisition;
- messaging analysis;
- image and video analysis;
- location reconstruction;
- malware analysis;
- social-network analysis.

For each product or supplier, record:

- the product name;
- the contracting authority;
- the date;
- the intended purpose;
- the operator;
- the legal framework;
- the data it can reach;
- the training supplied;
- the maintenance relationship;
- documented deployment;
- documented use;
- any CJP-specific evidence.

Do not infer that a police force possessing one forensic tool can perform every function associated with the sector.

Do not infer that national possession means local access.

Do not infer that possession proves use against the movement.

## 📷 Facial Recognition Is Several Procurement Markets  

“Facial recognition” may refer to:

- examination face authentication;
- one-to-one identity verification;
- airport systems;
- police identification;
- live facial recognition;
- retrospective CCTV search;
- attendance systems;
- access control;
- private venue security.

These systems have different purposes and architectures.

An examination system may ask:

> Does this face match the enrolled candidate?

A police system may ask:

> Does this face match anyone in a watchlist or database?

Those are not the same operation.

The NTA tender archive shows active procurement in 2026 for CUET(PG) and NEET(UG) face authentication, alongside CCTV, frisking and examination monitoring.

That establishes an examination-security procurement environment.

It does not establish police access, protest identification or CJP-specific use.

## 📹 CCTV Is Not One Capability Either  

CCTV procurement may involve:

- camera hire;
- permanent installation;
- live feeds;
- recording;
- command-centre monitoring;
- video retention;
- image processing;
- automated analytics;
- post-event review.

The NTA archive includes older tenders for IP-based CCTV systems, live CCTV surveillance, image processing and CCTV video analytics, as well as current examination-specific CCTV procurements.

For each system, ask:

- Who owned the cameras?
- Who watched the feed?
- Where was video stored?
- Was audio captured?
- What analytics were used?
- How long was footage retained?
- Could footage be exported?
- Who could request it?
- Was deletion verified?

The word CCTV does not answer those questions.

## 🇨🇳 Replacing One Foreign Supplier With Another Is Not Independence  

Governments may seek to reduce dependence on suppliers from a particular country.

That may be justified by:

- cybersecurity;
- sanctions risk;
- geopolitical tension;
- supply resilience;
- industrial policy;
- intelligence concerns.

But replacement raises a second question:

> What replaced it?

Possible outcomes include:

- genuine domestic design;
- domestic assembly of another foreign system;
- licensed manufacture;
- a joint venture;
- dependence on a different foreign original equipment manufacturer;
- a proprietary system with improved political alignment but similar audit problems.

Supply-chain diversification can reduce risk.

It does not automatically produce public control.

## 🇮🇳 Atmanirbhar Bharat Has Several Layers  

Self-reliance can refer to:

- domestic manufacture;
- domestic employment;
- domestic integration;
- domestic maintenance;
- domestic design;
- domestic intellectual-property ownership;
- independent export rights;
- sovereign control of critical components.

These are different achievements.

Official BEL and DRDO materials provide a useful example.

DRDO describes MRSAM as jointly developed with Israel Aerospace Industries for Indian military users, including production as part of the joint-development programme.

BEL describes MRSAM as manufactured in collaboration with IAI.

In September 2024, BEL and IAI incorporated BEL IAI AeroSystems Private Limited to provide post-warranty lifecycle support in India, including technical and maintenance support.

This is not a story of simple foreign purchase.

It includes:

- joint development;
- Indian production;
- public-sector manufacturing;
- foreign partnership;
- lifecycle support;
- a dedicated joint venture.

That is precisely why procurement analysis must be layered.

The system can be substantially Indian and still contain durable transnational dependencies.

Official company and government materials document several forms of India–Israel defence cooperation.

Examples include:

- joint development between DRDO and IAI;

- domestic manufacture of the IAI LORA system;
- BEL technical collaboration with an Elbit subsidiary for electro-optical payloads and lifetime maintenance support;

The relevant procurement categories include:

```text
direct purchase
        +
licensed manufacture

joint development

joint venture

maintenance
        +
export partnership
```

These arrangements can:

- deepen domestic capacity;

## 🛩️ Adani–Elbit Shows How A Foreign Platform Can Become An Indian Product  

Elbit materials have described the Hermes 900 in connection with the Adani Elbit Advanced Systems partnership.

Later Elbit product materials describe India’s Drishti-10 Starliner as a variant of the Hermes 900 integrated for Indian naval use.

The analytical point is not that the Indian product is unreal.

It is that several identities coexist:

- foreign platform lineage;
- Indian manufacture or integration;
- Indian product name;
- Indian military customer;
- continued relationship with the original technology company.

A product should therefore be described at the level supported by the evidence.

Possible wording may include:

- based on;
- variant of;
- manufactured through;
- integrated by;
- developed with;
- licensed from;
- jointly produced.

Avoid using “Indian” or “Israeli” as though one adjective must erase the other.

## 🔄 Lifecycle Support Is A Political Relationship  

BEL and IAI explicitly describe their joint venture as a single point of contact for long-term or lifecycle support for MRSAM systems.

Lifecycle support can include:

- repair;
- maintenance;
- technical assistance;
- spare parts;
- upgrades;
- troubleshooting;
- post-warranty work;
- configuration management.

That support is operationally valuable.

It also means the relationship continues after procurement, delivery and domestic deployment.

The relevant dependency is not simply:

> Can India purchase this system?

It is:

> Can India operate, repair, modify and replace it across its useful life without the original relationship?

The answer may differ by component.

## 📦 Export Rights Reveal Who Controls The Product  

A domestically manufactured system may still require permission for export.

Ask:

- Who owns export rights?
- Which government must approve?
- Are there territorial restrictions?
- Can the Indian partner market independently?
- Are components controlled by foreign export law?
- Does the original company retain veto power?
- Who receives export revenue?
- Who provides support to the foreign customer?

Export rights reveal where commercial sovereignty sits.

A product that can be built domestically but not sold, modified or supported independently remains only partly sovereign.

## 🌍 Procurement Can Become Foreign Policy  

A long-term technology relationship creates:

- contracts;
- joint ventures;
- factories;
- employment;
- training;
- service obligations;
- military interoperability;
- executive relationships;
- export ambitions;
- investor expectations.

These interests do not dictate every diplomatic decision.

They affect the cost of change.

```text
commercial relationship
        +
operational dependency
        +
industrial constituency
        +
diplomatic partnership
        ↓
rupture becomes materially expensive
```

This is why procurement is foreign policy with invoices.

The phrase is sharp.

The evidence underneath it must remain exact.

## 🧪 Capability, Access, Use And Attribution  

Every case-specific claim should be placed on this ladder:

```text
procurement
        ↓
contract award
        ↓
delivery
        ↓
technical integration
        ↓
institutional access
        ↓
operational deployment
        ↓
use against CJP
        ↓
specific consequence
```

These stages must remain distinct.

```text
procurement
≠ operational access
≠ implementation
≠ use against the movement
≠ causation of a specific outcome
```

Examples:

- A tender establishes that a service was sought.
- An award establishes a supplier relationship.
- Acceptance records may establish delivery.
- system documentation may establish integration.
- access-control evidence may establish who could use it.
- logs, orders or testimony may establish deployment.
- named records may establish use against a participant.
- a later decision may establish consequence.
- causation requires a supported connection between them.

## 🚨 “Battle-Tested” Requires Product-Specific Evidence  

Security companies may market products through operational credibility.

But the phrase “battle-tested” should not be applied merely because:

- the company is Israeli;
- the company supplies the Israeli state;
- the company operates in a sector shaped by occupation;
- another product from the same company was used in conflict.

For a specific product, ask:

- Did the company make the claim?
- Which product was named?
- Where was it deployed?
- Was deployment documented independently?
- Was the configuration the same?
- Did the claimed experience influence the sale?
- Was the Indian product a direct version, derivative or locally modified system?

The commercial relationship may still be politically relevant without this claim.

Precision protects the larger argument.

## 🧱 The Public Authority Remains Responsible  

A supplier may:

- design;
- manufacture;
- integrate;
- maintain;
- operate;
- process data.

The public authority still chose the arrangement.

It remains responsible for:

- necessity;
- legality;
- proportionality;
- due diligence;
- data protection;
- inspection;
- audit;
- remedy;
- termination;
- explaining the public purpose.

A government cannot answer scrutiny by saying:

> The vendor supplied the system.

The vendor did not create the public duty.

The state did.

## 🧷 Compact Finding  

The first invoice did not reveal the whole relationship.

A capability could begin with a public tender and continue through a prime contractor, foreign intellectual property, local integration, staff training, proprietary updates, maintenance and eventual renewal.

“Made in India” could describe real domestic capacity without answering who owned the design, controlled the upgrade or profited across the product’s life.

The correct question was never only:

> Who sold it?

It was:

> Who remained necessary after it had been bought?

They do not, by themselves, establish:

- the award or operational use of every NTA tender;
- access to a named examination or protest dataset;
- use of any capability against CJP;
- responsibility for a specific examination outcome;
- that every referenced product was deployed in occupation;
- that commercial integration dictated a particular diplomatic decision.
