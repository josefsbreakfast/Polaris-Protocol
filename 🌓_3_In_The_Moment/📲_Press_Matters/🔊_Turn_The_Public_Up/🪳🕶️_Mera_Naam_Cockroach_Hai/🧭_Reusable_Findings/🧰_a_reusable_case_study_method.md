# 🧰 A Reusable Case Study Method  
**First created:** 2026-07-31 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-31  
*A practical Polaris method for turning a live, networked political case into an evidence-controlled archive, analytical sequence and reusable mechanism without flattening participant meaning or pretending uncertainty has disappeared.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

A strong case study does more than collect material.

It must:

- establish what happened;
- preserve who said what;
- distinguish evidence from interpretation;
- track how meaning formed;
- map institutional machinery;
- record what changed;
- identify what remains unresolved;
- decide what can safely travel into another case.

The central transferable question is:

> How can Polaris build a case that is locally faithful, evidentially disciplined, analytically useful and reusable without turning one movement into a universal template?

The method uses ten stages:

```text
1. orientation
        ↓
2. factual spine
        ↓
3. cultural object and public interface
        ↓
4. grievance formation
        ↓
5. public capacity
        ↓
6. institutional machinery
        ↓
7. government response
        ↓
8. tolerance test
        ↓
9. reporting method
        ↓
10. reusable findings
```

The stages are sequential enough to prevent premature abstraction.

They are flexible enough to allow:

- new evidence;
- corrections;
- forks;
- loops;
- unresolved branches;
- later re-entry.

---

## 🧭 The Method In One Sentence  

Build the case from the inside out:

```text
what happened
        ↓
what participants meant
        ↓
how the public formed
        ↓
what machinery it confronted
        ↓
what changed
        ↓
what, if anything, can travel
```

Do not begin with the lesson.

Begin with the case.

## 1️⃣ Stage One: Orientation  

Orientation answers:

- What is this case?
- Why is it being built?
- What time period does it cover?
- Which institutions and publics are involved?
- What is known at the start?
- What remains uncertain?
- What is the initial evidentiary boundary?

The orientation node should include:

- a short case description;
- date range;
- central question;
- known actors;
- key source classes;
- author’s note;
- translation note where relevant;
- image-risk note where relevant;
- provisional folder map;
- explicit non-claims.

The purpose is not to resolve the case.

It is to prevent the project from beginning as an unbounded pile of material.

### Orientation Questions  

1. What is the smallest accurate description of the case?
2. What triggered the investigation?
3. Which terms come from participants?
4. Which terms come from institutions?
5. Which terms are analytical?
6. What evidence is already available?
7. What should not be published yet?
8. What would make the case too broad?

---

## 2️⃣ Stage Two: Build The Factual Spine  

The factual spine is the minimum chronology without which the case cannot be understood.

It should identify:

- dates;
- decisions;
- public statements;
- legal actions;
- institutional changes;
- protests;
- concessions;
- documented outcomes.

The factual spine should not yet contain every interpretation.

Keep separate:

```text
event
≠ claim
≠ source
≠ interpretation
≠ inference
≠ uncertainty
```

A factual-spine entry may use:

```text
DATE:
What happened.

SOURCE:
Where the claim comes from.

STATUS:
Confirmed / attributed / disputed / unresolved.

LIMIT:
What the source does not establish.
```

The chronology should preserve:

- original wording;
- correction history;
- source dependency;
- gaps;
- disputed counts;
- later changes.

### Do Not Let Chronology Become Narrative Closure  

A timeline is not proof of causation.

```text
event A
        before
event B
        ≠
event A caused event B
```

The chronology should make causal questions possible.

It should not answer them prematurely.

---

## 🧾 Claim–Source–Confidence–Limit  

Every important claim should be traceable.

Use:

```text
CLAIM:
Exact wording.

SOURCE:
Original or earliest defensible source.

SOURCE CLASS:
Participant / official / legal / document / local press /
regional press / national press / derivative.

CONFIDENCE:
High / medium / low / unresolved.

LIMIT:
What the source cannot establish.

DEPENDENCY:
Whether other reports rely on the same source.

ASSESSMENT DATE:
When this judgement was made.
```

This record allows later nodes to distinguish:

- established fact;
- attributed claim;
- participant interpretation;
- newsroom interpretation;
- analytical inference;
- comparative hypothesis.

## 3️⃣ Stage Three: Identify The Cultural Object And Public Interface  

Many live cases become legible through a public object.

This may be:

- a meme;
- slogan;
- song;
- image;
- gesture;
- mascot;
- performance;
- film reference;
- colour;
- altered quotation.

The task is not to celebrate the object.

It is to ask:

> What public function did it perform?

Possible functions include:

- recognition;
- compression;
- affiliation;
- emotional regulation;
- low-cost participation;
- distributed reproduction;
- memory;
- translation;
- return.

Document:

- earliest known use;
- participant meaning;
- original language;
- translation;
- variants;
- hostile interpretations;
- semantic drift;
- image risk;
- audience differences.

Keep separate:

```text
cultural object
≠ whole movement

participant meaning
≠ external interpretation

virality
≠ organisation
```

---

## 🗣️ Translation Note  

Where language matters, record:

- original script;
- transliteration;
- literal meaning;
- natural meaning;
- register;
- joke structure;
- cultural reference;
- translation uncertainty;
- who supplied the explanation.

Translation should not be hidden inside prose as if it were neutral.

It is part of the evidence.

```text
words translated
≠ meaning transferred
```

Where several translations are plausible, preserve them.

Do not silently choose the one that best fits the theory.

---

## 🖼️ Image-Risk Note  

For significant images, record:

- what the image establishes;
- what remains outside the frame;
- who is identifiable;
- location clues;
- professional or legal risk;
- whether public reposting changes exposure;
- whether cropping, blurring or withholding is needed;
- whether consent is known.

Keep separate:

```text
authentic image
≠ accurate caption

public appearance
≠ unrestricted future use

evidentiary value
≠ permission to expose
```

An image may belong in the archive without belonging in public view.

---

## 4️⃣ Stage Four: Trace Grievance Formation  

The case study should show how harm became shared.

Trace:

```text
private harm
        ↓
comparison
        ↓
shared language
        ↓
pattern recognition
        ↓
collective grievance
```

Relevant evidence may include:

- testimony;
- complaint records;
- family or student groups;
- participant posts;
- local reporting;
- legal framing;
- recurring institutional language.

Ask:

- What did people initially blame themselves for?
- When did they learn others were affected?
- What phrase or document helped name the pattern?
- What made speaking survivable?
- Who remained unable to speak?

Keep separate:

```text
shared exposure
≠ shared interpretation

shared interpretation
≠ public action
```

---

## 🚪 Participation Threshold Map  

Map how people entered.

Possible thresholds:

- recognition;
- private agreement;
- circulation;
- anonymous contribution;
- indirect support;
- visible participation;
- organisation;
- leadership;
- public pressure.

Use:

| Threshold | Example | Risk | Evidence | Missing Publics |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Identifies with grievance | Low to variable | Testimony, private circulation | People without language |
| Circulation | Shares phrase or document | Variable | Posts, forwards, adaptations | Offline publics |
| Indirect support | Transport, translation, legal help | Often hidden | Organiser records, interviews | Uncredited labour |
| Visible participation | Attendance, speech, performance | High | Images, reports, testimony | People unable to appear |
| Organisation | Repeated role or coordination | High | Logs, meeting records | Informal contributors |
| Pressure | Alters institutional calculation | Collective | Official response, timing, records | Rival causes |

This prevents the crowd from becoming the only recognised form of participation.

---

## 5️⃣ Stage Five: Map Public Capacity  

Public capacity is what allows visibility to become durable.

Map:

- communication;
- logistics;
- translation;
- legal support;
- first aid;
- transport;
- food and water;
- accessibility;
- documentation;
- verification;
- archiving;
- moderation;
- conflict management;
- morale;
- care;
- succession;
- redundancy.

Ask:

- What existed before the visible event?
- What looked spontaneous but depended on prior relationships?
- Which functions were centralised?
- Which were locally interoperable?
- Who carried invisible labour?
- Where were the single points of failure?
- What signs of burnout appeared?

Keep separate:

```text
meme fluency
≠ organisational durability

distributed action
≠ absence of structure

visibility
≠ healthy infrastructure
```

---

## 🛠️ Public-Capacity Map  

A capacity map may use:

| Function | People Or Group | Evidence | Dependency | Risk | Backup |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Translation | Regional volunteers | Published translations, interviews | One script specialist | Burnout | Second reviewer |
| Legal support | Lawyers or legal groups | Briefings, case records | Named contact | Retaliation | Referral list |
| Verification | Participant network | Corrections, source logs | Private groups | Impersonation | Double confirmation |
| Accessibility | Organisers and carers | Route plans, remote access | Informal labour | Exclusion | Design checklist |
| Archiving | Distributed record-keepers | Timelines, folders | Personal devices | Loss or seizure | Restricted duplicate |
| Media liaison | Spokespeople | Interviews, press lists | Visible individuals | Overrepresentation | Rotating contacts |

The map should preserve both:

- public capacity;
- participant exposure.

---

## 6️⃣ Stage Six: Map Institutional Machinery  

The case must identify what the public confronted.

Institutional machinery may include:

- ministries;
- regulators;
- examination bodies;
- universities;
- police;
- courts;
- contractors;
- subcontractors;
- software suppliers;
- platform systems;
- procurement relationships;
- data processors.

Map six questions:

```text
authority
≠ delivery
≠ ownership
≠ operational control
≠ liability
≠ political responsibility
```

Ask:

1. Who set the rule?
2. Who compelled use?
3. Who procured delivery?
4. Who performed each function?
5. Who could alter or suspend the system?
6. Who had the power to remedy harm?

The public should not be required to solve the institution’s internal fragmentation before receiving repair.

---

## 🏛️ Responsibility-Chain Map  

Use:

| Function | Actor | Power Held | Evidence | Failure | Repair Authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Policy | Ministry | Sets rule | Policy records | Harmful threshold | Minister or department |
| Procurement | Central body | Selects supplier | Tender and contract | Weak specification | Contracting authority |
| Technical delivery | Supplier | Builds or operates | Technical records | Defect or limitation | Supplier and authority |
| Local operation | Institution | Applies process | Manuals, logs | Human or procedural error | Operator and authority |
| Oversight | Regulator | Audits | Reports, notices | Missed warning | Regulator |
| Remedy | Public authority | Corrects harm | Appeals, orders | Delay or refusal | Non-delegable owner |

Do not confuse a vendor logo with proof of case-specific use.

---

## 👁️ Capability Map  

For technical, surveillance, platform or procurement claims, use:

```text
capability
≠ access
≠ implementation
≠ deployment
≠ demonstrated use
≠ attribution
≠ causation
```

Record the highest supported level.

| Level | Question | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Capability | Can the tool do this? | Technical records, testing |
| Access | Could the actor use it? | Contract, licence |
| Implementation | Was it integrated? | Architecture, training |
| Deployment | Was it operational? | Logs, instructions |
| Demonstrated use | Was it used in relevant operations? | Query logs, case notes |
| Attribution | Was it used in this case? | Identifiers, testimony |
| Causation | Did it affect the outcome? | Decision record, counterfactual |

Use the frame:

> Capability documented; case-specific use not established.

when that is the correct evidentiary level.

---

## 7️⃣ Stage Seven: Map Government Or Institutional Response  

Response should not be reduced to:

- victory;
- defeat.

Record:

- acknowledgement;
- resignation;
- withdrawal of coercion;
- compensation;
- procedural reform;
- technical fix;
- contractor blame;
- inquiry;
- consultation;
- managed transparency;
- redistribution of authority;
- independent oversight.

Keep separate:

```text
concession
≠ implementation
≠ repair
≠ trust
```

Ask:

- What changed immediately?
- What changed materially?
- What remained?
- Who benefited?
- Who remained exposed?
- Was the response enforceable?
- Did recurrence become less likely?

---

## 🧽 Concession Matrix  

Use:

| Original Harm | Concession | Implementation | Enforcement | Remaining Harm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Exclusion | Review announced | Cases pending | Internal only | Access not restored |
| Coercion | Withdrawal promised | Some orders issued | Mixed | Records remain |
| Financial loss | Compensation scheme | Limited payments | Claims process | Eligibility gaps |
| Technical failure | System reform | Pilot | Contractual | Authority unchanged |
| Public humiliation | Apology | Published | None | Individual record uncorrected |

The matrix should follow the harmed public, not only the institution’s preferred metric.

---

## 8️⃣ Stage Eight: Apply The Tolerance Test  

The tolerance test asks:

> Did the system become tolerable?

Possible outcomes:

### Tolerable  

Immediate harm stopped, meaningful repair occurred and recurrence is less likely.

### Provisionally Tolerable  

Some gains are real, but implementation or enforcement remains incomplete.

### Still Intolerable  

The visible crisis reduced, but the underlying harm or coercive structure remains.

The test should examine:

- implementation;
- enforceability;
- recurrence;
- retaliation;
- distribution;
- remaining cost;
- participant accounts;
- review dates;
- re-entry thresholds.

Keep separate:

```text
quiet
≠ satisfaction

pause
≠ trust

settlement
≠ completed repair
```

---

## ⏸️ Review Dates And Re-Entry Thresholds  

For unresolved commitments, record:

```text
COMMITMENT:
What was promised.

RESPONSIBLE BODY:
Who must act.

DEADLINE:
When.

EVIDENCE:
What proves completion.

REVIEW DATE:
When the case will be reassessed.

TRIGGER:
What counts as failure.

RE-ENTRY ACTION:
Legal, media, organisational or public response.
```

This converts indefinite monitoring into a bounded governance process.

## 9️⃣ Stage Nine: Build The Reporting Method  

The case should explain how it was reported.

Record:

- original sources;
- publication pathways;
- participant meaning;
- translation choices;
- source social position;
- image-risk decisions;
- search blind spots;
- regional press;
- diaspora knowledge;
- correction history;
- headline limits;
- implementation updates.

A reporting-method node should ask:

- What did the public produce?
- What did the newsroom add?
- Which claims remained unresolved?
- What publication increased risk?
- What was deliberately withheld?
- How were corrections propagated?

```text
reporting
        =
part of the information environment
```

The case study should not pretend the archive was created from nowhere.

---

## 📰 Live-Case Update Record  

Use:

```text
UPDATE DATE:
What changed.

NEW EVIDENCE:
Source and class.

CLAIM AFFECTED:
Which finding changed.

CONFIDENCE MOVEMENT:
Low → medium / medium → high / unresolved.

PUBLICATION CHANGES:
Body / headline / caption / metadata / social card.

REMAINING LIMIT:
What is still unknown.
```

A live case requires live evidentiary maintenance.

## 🔟 Stage Ten: Extract Reusable Findings  

Reusable findings come last.

Use the abstraction ladder:

```text
case-specific fact
        ↓
case-derived inference
        ↓
transferable mechanism
        ↓
comparative hypothesis
        ↓
general theory
```

Do not leap from:

> This happened here.

to:

> This is how movements work.

Ask:

1. What exact case material supports the finding?
2. Is it fact, inference or abstraction?
3. What mechanism is being identified?
4. What rival explanation remains?
5. What conditions made the mechanism possible?
6. Which parts are culturally specific?
7. What evidence would be needed elsewhere?
8. What would falsify the comparison?
9. Who might face different risks?
10. Does the finding remain useful after limits are stated?

Keep separate:

```text
memorable in one case
        ≠
causal in that case
        ≠
reusable elsewhere
        ≠
universal
```

---

## 🧪 Reusable Finding Template  

```text
TITLE:
A bounded mechanism statement.

CASE GROUNDING:
The exact evidence supporting it.

CLAIM LEVEL:
Fact / case-derived inference / transferable mechanism /
comparative hypothesis / general theory.

MECHANISM:
What process is being identified.

CONDITIONS:
What had to be present.

RIVAL EXPLANATIONS:
What else could explain the outcome.

TRANSFER LIMIT:
What does not travel cleanly.

EVIDENCE NEEDED ELSEWHERE:
What would support comparison.

FALSIFIER:
What would show the comparison is wrong.

COMPACT FINDING:
One precise paragraph.
```

A reusable node should remain useful after its limits are stated.

## 🗂️ Folder Pattern  

A durable case-study folder may use:

```text
README.md
notes.txt

🌑_The_System_Becomes_Intolerable/
🌒_The_Public_Comes_Outside/
🌕_What_The_Movement_Was_Up_Against/
🌘_Did_The_System_Become_Tolerable?/
🗞️_For_Readers_And_Newsrooms/
🧭_Reusable_Findings/
```

The phases serve different functions.

### 🌑 Intolerability  

- private harm;
- shared grievance;
- legitimacy rupture.

### 🌒 Public Formation  

- culture;
- circulation;
- participation;
- care;
- organisation.

### 🌕 Institutional Confrontation  

- authority;
- delivery;
- coercion;
- platforms;
- procurement;
- information narrowing.

### 🌘 Assessment  

- concession;
- repair;
- trust;
- monitoring;
- re-entry.

### 🗞️ Newsroom Method  

- sources;
- translation;
- images;
- headlines;
- corrections;
- regional and diaspora press.

### 🧭 Reusable Findings  

- abstraction;
- transfer limits;
- comparative mechanism.

The folder order is analytical.

It should not force the evidence into a sequence it does not support.

## 🗃️ README, Mega-Node Or Branch?  

Use a README when the file must:

- orient the reader;
- define the scope;
- map the folder;
- establish evidence boundaries;
- explain how nodes relate.

Use a mega-node when the subject has:

- one central analytical question;
- one evidence structure;
- enough material to be independently useful;
- internal sections that still belong together.

Create a branch when the material is:

- evidentially distinct;
- independently useful;
- too large;
- governed by different source classes;
- likely to require separate updating;
- carrying a different risk profile.

Do not pre-build branches simply because they might someday be useful.

```text
possible future branch
        ≠
necessary present file
```

---

## 🌿 No Branch Before Its Time  

A branch should earn its existence.

Fork when:

- the node has more than one central question;
- different claims require different evidence;
- one section can be cited independently;
- one section needs a distinct update cycle;
- the file becomes difficult to navigate;
- privacy or legal risk requires separation.

Do not fork merely to:

- make the tree look complete;
- duplicate headings;
- anticipate unconfirmed material;
- create aesthetic symmetry.

The case should grow from evidence.

Not from folder ambition.

---

## 🧭 Avoid Phase Duplication  

The same material may appear relevant to several phases.

Assign it by function.

Example:

- A meme’s origin belongs in the cultural-object node.
- Its effect on participation belongs in grievance or public formation.
- Its hostile amplification belongs in circulation.
- Its preservation as memory belongs in assessment and re-entry.
- Its general mechanism belongs in reusable findings.

Cross-reference rather than retell.

```text
one fact
        can support
several analytical functions

several analytical functions
        do not require
several duplicate narratives
```

## 🧪 Evidence And Confidence Labels  

Recommended labels:

### Confirmed  

Supported by high-quality primary or multiple independent sources.

### Attributed  

A named source makes the claim.

### Corroborated  

Several sources support the same point, with dependency checked.

### Disputed  

Credible sources conflict.

### Inferred  

The conclusion follows from evidence but is not directly stated.

### Plausible  

Consistent with available evidence but under-supported.

### Unresolved  

Evidence is insufficient.

### Deliberately Excluded  

Material exists but is withheld from the analysis for evidentiary, ethical, legal or scope reasons.

Do not use confidence labels as decoration.

They should change how the prose is written.

An actor map should distinguish:

- directly affected public;
- organisers;
- informal support;
- lawyers;
- journalists;
- contractors;
- public authorities;

- platforms;
- political opponents;

## 🚨 Public-Risk Assessment  

For each publication decision, assess:

| Material | Evidentiary Value | Identification Risk | Future Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Crowd image | Presence and scale | Faces visible | Professional or legal | Blur or crop |
| Group screenshot | Circulation | Names and numbers | Harassment | Recreate text |
| Testimony | Lived experience | Identity clues | Retaliation | Pseudonymise |
| Document | Institutional record | Metadata | Source exposure | Redact |
| Location data | Chronology | Movement mapping | Surveillance | Generalise |

The question is not only:

> Is this public?

It is:

> What does republication do?

## 🧪 Rival-Explanation Register  

For each major inference, record:

```text
FINDING:
What appears to have happened.

PRIMARY MECHANISM:
The current explanation.

RIVAL EXPLANATIONS:
Alternative causes.

EVIDENCE FOR EACH:
What supports or weakens them.

CURRENT ASSESSMENT:
Why one explanation is preferred.

FALSIFIER:
What would change the judgement.
```

Rival explanations prevent theory from becoming loyalty.

They also make the eventual finding stronger.

---

## 🕳️ Gap Analysis  

Record what the case still lacks.

Possible gaps include:

- participant interview;
- local-language source;
- contractor record;
- legal outcome;
- implementation evidence;
- regional comparison;
- casualty verification;
- translation review;
- image consent;
- data-retention detail;
- subgroup participation evidence.

Use:

| Gap | Why It Matters | How It Might Be Filled | Risk | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

A gap should remain visible.

Do not quietly fill it with general knowledge.

---

## 🗃️ Hold Uncertain Material Without Deleting It  

Uncertain material may still matter.

Store it in:

- notes;
- unresolved claims;
- restricted archive;
- evidence queue;
- source-check list.

Mark:

- provenance;
- confidence;
- publication status;
- reason for uncertainty;
- next verification step.

```text
not publishable yet
        ≠
worthless

uncertain
        ≠
false
```

Do not let speculative material leak into polished analytical prose.

But do not delete it merely because the evidence is incomplete today.

---

## 🚫 Record Deliberately Excluded Material  

Some material should be deliberately excluded.

Reasons may include:

- privacy;
- source protection;
- legal exposure;
- scope;
- duplication;
- cultural specificity;
- unsupported demographic inference;
- unverified product attribution;
- weak chronology;
- one retrospective account.

Record exclusion:

```text
MATERIAL:
What was considered.

REASON EXCLUDED:
Evidence / privacy / scope / duplication / risk.

WHERE RETAINED:
Restricted note or source record.

REVIEW CONDITION:
What would justify inclusion later.
```

Deliberate exclusion is part of methodological integrity.

It prevents later confusion about whether material was forgotten or rejected.

---

## 🧑‍💻 Working Notes Versus Published Nodes  

Working notes may contain:

- incomplete ideas;
- contradictory sources;
- rough language;
- possible links;
- questions;
- speculative connections.

Published nodes should contain:

- supported claims;
- labelled inference;
- clear evidence boundaries;
- transfer limits;
- readable structure;
- safe publication choices.

```text
research notebook
≠ publication record
```

Do not require working notes to sound polished.

Do require published nodes to remain traceable.

## 📝 Mandatory Node Elements  

A reusable Polaris node should normally contain:

- H1 title;
- first-created and last-updated dates;

## 🧷 Compact Finding As A Discipline  

Every major node should end with a compact finding.

The compact finding should state:

- what the mechanism is;
- what the case supports;
- what the claim does not exceed.

It should not be:

- a slogan;
- a universal law;
- a compressed list of everything in the node.

A strong compact finding remains accurate when detached from the full file.

## 🔗 Cross-References Instead Of Repetition  

Cross-references should help the reader move between:

- event;
- mechanism;
- institutional structure;
- reporting method;
- reusable finding.

Use them when:

- another node holds the chronology;
- another node owns the source table;
- another node develops the mechanism;
- another node contains the implementation record.

Do not cross-reference material that does not yet exist as if it were complete.

Mark future nodes honestly.

## 📅 Review Dates And Living Files  

A live case should identify:

- last updated;
- next review;
- claims awaiting evidence;
- deadlines;
- implementation dates;
- legal events;
- promised publications.

A review may be triggered by:

- judgment;
- inquiry report;
- contract disclosure;
- compensation deadline;
- case withdrawal;
- policy change;
- participant correction;
- new primary evidence.

Living files should update.

They should not silently rewrite history.

Preserve the correction trail.

## 🌿 When A Case Is Too Broad  

Fork the case when it contains several independently complex systems.

Signs include:

- more than one central harm;
- several jurisdictions;
- separate legal regimes;
- distinct publics;
- incompatible chronologies;
- several procurement stacks;
- different movement phases;
- too many source classes for one evidentiary spine.

Possible response:

```text
parent case
        ↓
shared orientation and chronology
        ↓
separate analytical clusters
```

Do not force everything into one mega-node because the cases are politically related.

Relation does not equal analytical unity.

---

## 🧱 When Not To Fork  

Do not fork when the difference is only:

- another example;
- one extra source;
- another quote;
- a minor regional variant;
- a future possibility;
- an aesthetic preference.

A proliferation of branches can hide:

- weak evidence;
- duplicated prose;
- unfinished thinking;
- false certainty.

The smallest useful structure is usually the strongest.

## 🧪 Rival Methods  

Other valid methods may organise cases by:

- chronology;
- actor;
- legal process;
- geography;
- issue;
- source class;
- institutional department.

Those methods may be better where:

- chronology is the central dispute;
- litigation controls the structure;
- geography determines evidence;
- several publics never form one movement.

The Polaris phase method is strongest where the central question concerns:

- how harm became public;
- how public capacity formed;
- what machinery was confronted;
- what response followed;
- what became reusable.

The method should not be used mechanically where the evidence demands another structure.

## 🌉 What Transfers And What Does Not  

### What may transfer  

- begin with orientation and factual spine;
- preserve claim–source–confidence–limit;
- treat translation and image risk as evidence work;
- map grievance formation and participation thresholds;
- map public capacity and invisible labour;
- separate authority, delivery, control and responsibility;
- use capability ladders;
- test concessions against implementation and repair;
- preserve review dates and re-entry thresholds;
- abstract only after the case is built;
- record gaps and deliberate exclusions.

### What may not transfer cleanly  

- exact folder emojis;
- exact lunar phases;
- exact Cockroach sequence;
- exact source mix;
- exact risk thresholds;
- exact branch structure;
- exact wording of compact findings.

The transferable object is the method.

The house remains responsive to the case.

## 🧪 Method Integrity Test  

Before treating a case as analytically complete, ask:

1. Is there a factual spine?
2. Can every major claim be traced?
3. Are participant meaning and analytical inference separate?
4. Is translation documented?
5. Is image exposure assessed?
6. Are missing publics visible?
7. Is invisible labour mapped?
8. Are authority and delivery separated?
9. Are capability and use separated?
10. Are concessions tested against implementation?
11. Are rival explanations recorded?
12. Are gaps preserved?
13. Is excluded material documented?
14. Are review dates active?
15. Do reusable findings state their transfer limits?

If several answers are no, the case may be publishable as a live archive.

It is not yet ready for confident abstraction.
