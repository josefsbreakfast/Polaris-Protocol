# 🏛️ Courts and Policing Powers
**First created:** 2026-10-08 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-08  
*🍊☘️ Seas an Fód — The difference between authorising a procession, judging the legality of that authorisation, policing the street and accounting for what happened afterwards.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

A lawful procession is not a police instruction to clear a road by any means necessary. A court refusing to overturn a regulator's decision is not the court taking command of a public-order operation. Nor does an operational decision not to force passage amount to the police cancelling the regulator's decision. **The institutions are answering different questions, at different times, using different powers and different information.**

That is the organising problem of the 2026 Drumcree/Garvaghy Road dispute. The Parades Commission issued a restricted determination; residents challenged it; the courts dealt with urgent applications; and the PSNI faced a crowd and a live risk assessment. The dispute therefore cannot be explained by asking only whether the march was permitted. We must also ask what the court actually ordered, what legal powers the police possessed, what information commanders had when they acted, and what alternatives were reasonably available. [S01][S08][S10][S12]

This node is the **courts and operational-policing sibling** of the [master legal and diplomatic chronology](./⚖️_legal_and_diplomatic_timeline.md). The [Commission node](./📜_parades_commission_and_the_law.md) owns the statutory determination and its internal approval machinery. Here the focus is judicial review, police powers, operational proportionality, information provenance, command continuity and accountability. The analysis does not equate public controversy with unlawfulness; nor does it treat a public body's assertion that it acted proportionately as a substitute for examining its evidence.

**Evidence cut-off: 8 October 2026.** Contemporary dates and reported case developments are drawn from the existing Polaris cluster's working chronology and source register. Some underlying judgments, original police submissions, operational records and appointment notices have **not** been inspected for this node. Accordingly, detailed reconstructions remain conditional. A source link is a route for verification, not a claim that every proposition attributed to it has been independently rechecked today.

**Evidence key:** **PRIMARY** — identified statute, judgment or official record; **REPORTED** — attributed public reporting; **OPEN** — original record outstanding; **DISPUTED** — competing claims not resolved here. Keep allegation, explanation and finding separate.

## 1. 🗺️ Drumcree: When the street becomes an institutional problem

### 1.1 The road is inhabited

The disputed return route from Drumcree Church towards Portadown passes along Garvaghy Road, a predominantly nationalist residential area. For the Orange Order, the traditional route is connected to religious and cultural practice. For residents, its history cannot be separated from experiences of sectarian intimidation, political exclusion and the unequal allocation of security. The law must consider the people who live on the road as well as the people who seek to travel along it. Neither group's experience becomes legally irrelevant because the other has a right to assemble. [S04]

### 1.2 The 1990s are not optional context

The disputes of 1995–98, including the 1996 reversal of an initial policing restriction amid severe disorder, showed the limits of making the police both practical enforcers and the public arbiters of contested routes. The deaths of the three Quinn brothers in the 1998 sectarian arson attack in Ballymoney belong to the wider period of violence and must be treated with care rather than reduced to a rhetorical flourish. Precise incident chronology and responsibility should be checked against contemporary records. [S04]

### 1.3 The institutional response

The North Review recommended an independent body to consider parades and facilitate dispute resolution. The Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998 supplied the statutory framework for the Parades Commission. That legislation predates the signing of the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement; the Agreement did not create the Commission. The institutional division was meant to stop every contested route decision from becoming an improvised police policy under threat of disorder. It did **not** remove the need for police to make independent, real-time decisions. [S01][S04][S05]

## 2. ⚖️ The architecture of authority

An event may involve multiple lawful decision-makers without any one of them being the overall commander of every consequence. This is not administrative fussiness. It is the difference between legal authorisation, review, implementation and oversight.

| Body | Central function | Principal source to consult | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parades Commission | Consider notifications; impose conditions; facilitate mediation | Public Processions (NI) Act 1998 | Does not command PSNI public-order tactics |
| PSNI / Chief Constable | Prevent and investigate crime; protect life and public order; deploy police resources | Police (NI) Acts 2000 and 2003; relevant criminal, public-order and human-rights law | Does not rewrite a Commission determination by operational fiat |
| High Court | Review legality; grant or refuse interim and final remedies | Judicial-review jurisdiction and applicable rules | Does not become Gold commander |
| Court of Appeal | Review lower-court rulings | Appellate law and procedure | Its actual order, not headlines, determines legal effect |
| Secretary of State | Exercise defined statutory functions and ministerial responsibility | Public Processions Act; wider constitutional law | Political concern is not unlimited police command |
| NI Department of Justice | Devolved justice and policing policy functions | Devolution and police legislation | Does not routinely direct individual operations |
| Northern Ireland Policing Board | Hold policing to account; scrutinise performance | Police (NI) Act 2000, as amended | Oversight is not live operational command |
| Police Ombudsman | Investigate complaints and specified police conduct matters | Police oversight framework | Not a substitute for a criminal trial or judicial review |
| Public Prosecution Service | Decide prosecutions under applicable tests | Prosecution framework | Not a substitute for police operational command |

**Practical diagnostic:** whenever a public figure says an institution *allowed*, *ordered*, *prevented* or *failed to enforce* something, ask which legal power is being described and obtain the actual instrument. 'The court allowed the march' may be shorthand for a much narrower ruling about an application for relief. [S01][S02][S03][S08]

## 3. 📜 A Commission determination is not an enforcement plan

### 3.1 Conditions and their legal effect

Under section 8 of the Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998, the Commission may impose conditions on public processions. The statutory criteria include risks of disorder and property damage, disruption to community life, effects on community relationships, previous compliance with the Code of Conduct, and the desirability of a customary route. The actual amended text and applicable guidelines must govern the legal analysis. A customary route is a factor to be weighed, not a right that defeats all others. [S01]

A determination may limit numbers, timing, route, bands, supporters or other conduct, depending on the powers exercised. Breaching a valid condition may carry criminal consequences. But the existence of an offence does not eliminate the police's responsibility to decide when arrest, dispersal or force would be lawful, necessary and proportionate. **The Commission can define conditions; it cannot pre-authorise every conceivable means of enforcing them.**

### 3.2 Three propositions to keep apart

1. **The procession is lawful subject to specified conditions.** This is a regulatory proposition.
2. **A person has committed an offence.** This requires applying the relevant criminal law to conduct and evidence.
3. **Police should use force now.** This requires a separate, contemporaneous justification.

One proposition does not automatically prove the next. The policing question is particularly sensitive where hundreds of people are present and intervention itself may cause injury or escalating disorder.

### 3.3 What the September record needs

The working record identifies the 25 September determination `PAR\111838` as permitting a tightly restricted procession: 35 participants, a morning window, no bands or accompanying supporters and limited banner provision. Those details must be checked against the complete original decision, including conditions, reasons and any corrections. The [Commission node](./📜_parades_commission_and_the_law.md) holds the paragraph-by-paragraph analysis. [S08]

## 4. 🧿 Human rights: Several rights occupy the same road

The Human Rights Act 1998 and the European Convention on Human Rights matter at every stage. Article 11 protects peaceful assembly; Article 10 expression; Article 9 religion and conscience; Article 8 private and family life; Article 2 life; Article 3 freedom from inhuman or degrading treatment; and Article 14 non-discrimination in the enjoyment of Convention rights. The precise legal engagement of each article must be established on the facts, not assumed because a party invokes it. [S06][S07]

### 4.1 Qualified rights and proportionality

Articles 10 and 11 protect conduct that may be unpopular or offensive. They are qualified rights: restrictions require a lawful basis, legitimate aim and necessity in a democratic society. Rights analysis therefore cannot consist of counting which side has more people, more history or more political allies. Nor does it allow the state to disregard credible threats of serious harm.

### 4.2 Positive obligations and hostile audiences

The state may have positive duties to take reasonable and appropriate measures to protect peaceful assemblies from hostile interference. But those duties do not mean police must use any level of force, irrespective of consequences. The question is what reasonable measures were available in the actual circumstances. Relevant Strasbourg jurisprudence includes *Plattform “Ärzte für das Leben” v Austria*; its facts and holdings must be read before using it as an answer to Drumcree. [S07]

### 4.3 Counterprotest is not an automatic veto

Residents and supporters may possess assembly and expression rights. A large counterprotest is not, by itself, a lawful mechanism for extinguishing a procession's rights. Conversely, a procession's legal authorisation does not extinguish the rights or physical safety of people protesting against it. Where obstruction, intimidation or violence is alleged, the precise conduct, available offences and individual evidence matter.

### 4.4 Do not confuse moral and legal questions

A reader may find a march provocative, a police decision frustrating, or a counterprotest justified. Those evaluations matter politically and socially. They do not answer the narrower questions of statutory authority, judicial remedy or the lawful use of force. The point is not to erase the history of Garvaghy Road. It is to avoid allowing one category of judgment to impersonate another.

## 5. 🏛️ Judicial review: What is actually under review?

Judicial review generally examines the legality of a public decision rather than substituting the judge's preferred policy outcome. Relevant grounds may include acting beyond powers, misdirecting oneself in law, procedural unfairness, irrationality, failure to consider mandatory matters, taking irrelevant matters into account, inadequate reasons and disproportionate interference with Convention rights. The applicable test depends on the pleaded ground and the legal context.

### 5.1 Permission and substantive determination

Permission is a gateway question. A refusal of permission may end that application, but it should not be described as a detailed endorsement of every assertion made by the regulator. Likewise, an interim injunction is not necessarily the court's final view of the merits. It may preserve a position while urgent arguments are heard.

### 5.2 Remedies matter

A **quashing order** sets aside a decision. A **declaration** states a legal position. A **mandatory order** requires specified action. An **interim injunction** may temporarily prohibit or require conduct. The operative wording, parties bound and duration of each order are crucial. It is possible to quash a determination for defective approval without deciding whether a procession would be substantively justified if reconsidered through a lawful process.

### 5.3 Read the order before the headline

For each 2026 court event, the node must retain: court, case number, judge or panel, parties, precise application, legal grounds, order made, reasons, appeal status and whether the ruling is interim or final. The original sealed order controls over a compressed media summary. **'Appeal allowed' does not tell us, by itself, who was ordered to do what.**

The 2 September parliamentary statement is a significant public account of the problem. The complete judgment, original determination and approval record remain necessary for a definitive explanation of the defect. [S09][S11]

**What follows from a procedural quashing?** The defective decision loses its legal effect in accordance with the court's order. It does not follow that the applicants have established an unconditional right to march or that opponents have won a permanent substantive prohibition. The regulator must act within the lawful framework when considering subsequent matters.

**Questions requiring the documents:** Which draft was approved? Which final text was issued? Who was required to approve it? Who actually did? Was the text materially changed? When was the discrepancy identified? What were the legal consequences specified in the judgment? Did the court address any additional grounds?

## 7. 🔄 Personnel turnover: The office, the officeholder and the handover

The cross-cluster personnel question is not 'why did everyone change jobs?' as a conclusion. It is **which relevant posts actually changed, on which dates, under whose appointment powers, and with what consequences for continuity?** An announcement date, legal appointment date and date of assuming operational responsibility can differ. Several announcements close together may reflect scheduled terms, reshuffles, retirements or entirely independent processes.

### 7.1 Police command

Identify the Chief Constable, Deputy Chief Constable, relevant assistant chief constables, the officers who prepared Commission advice, and Gold/Silver/Bronze command where publicly identifiable. Do not invent operational command names from press-conference appearances. A Chief Constable can answer to the Policing Board for an organisation without personally having made every tactical decision.

### 7.2 Information handovers

A meaningful handover may concern risk assessments, legal advice, intelligence summaries, community contact, operational contingencies, records of engagement with the Commission and known uncertainties. A change of personnel does not establish that any information was lost. Conversely, a formal organisational chart does not prove that a particular briefing occurred.

### 7.3 Judicial personnel are a separate category

Different judges dealing with successive urgent applications do not demonstrate unusual judicial turnover. Distinguish a **judicial appointment**, a **retirement**, a **transfer**, a **case assignment** and an **out-of-hours duty arrangement**. Court listings and published judgments may establish who heard a case, not why a particular judge was assigned.

## 8. 📅 September: A replacement determination under scrutiny

The working chronology identifies a replacement notification dated 3 September, Commission consideration around 23 September and determination issued on 25 September for `PAR\111838`. It permitted a restricted procession along the disputed route. That sequence occurred while a governance review was being established. A review being under way does not automatically suspend a statutory body's powers; the legal question is whether the particular decision complied with the law and applicable procedures. [S08][S19][S20]

The Commission's stated treatment of PSNI advice must be compared with the **actual advice**. Its treatment of residents' and organisers' representations must be compared with their **actual submissions**. A determination is a reasoned regulatory document, not a transcript of every representation and not an operational police risk log.

### 8.1 Two questions, two evidence sets

**Regulatory:** What conditions were justified by the evidence and statutory factors when the Commission decided?

**Operational:** What action was justified by the information available to police when people gathered on 27 September?

A change in circumstances between those dates may matter. So may a genuine disagreement about the original evidence. Neither should be assumed without the underlying documents.

## 10. 🚨 27 September: A permitted procession meets a live operation

The working record describes approximately 1,000 residents and supporters participating in a counterprotest and a PSNI decision not to force a route through the crowd. PSNI's public explanation was framed in terms of the risk and disproportionality of the force that would have been required. Numbers, precise timings, deployment details and the sequence of communications require verification against official statements and contemporaneous reporting. [S12]

### 10.1 Reconstruct the day minute by minute where evidence permits

- Planned procession assembly, route and permitted time window.
- Arrival and positioning of protesters and residents.
- Police deployments, cordons and contact with organisers.
- Negotiation or communication with each group.
- Changes in numbers, intelligence or immediate risk.
- Decisions made by operational command and their timestamps.
- Whether any alternative route, delay or other accommodation was proposed.
- Whether offences were recorded, arrests made or later investigations opened.
- When the operation was stood down and what was communicated publicly.

Do not fill gaps with cinematic inference from photographs. A photograph can show that a crowd existed; it cannot establish the content of a command briefing or the legal advice given to an officer.

### 10.2 The force question

Police may have powers to intervene against unlawful obstruction or other offences. They must still assess the lawful purpose, necessity, proportionality and risks of each contemplated intervention. An arrest power is not a standing instruction to arrest every suspect immediately. Nor is a route condition a general licence to injure people in order to demonstrate that the regulator's authority means something.

### 10.3 A decision not to clear is also a decision

Operational restraint is not automatically unlawful abdication; it is also not beyond scrutiny. Ask what reasonable alternatives existed, whether they were considered, what risks were identified, and whether the police had taken reasonable steps to protect the procession's rights. The evaluation must use information available **at the time**, not a convenient hindsight narrative.

## 11. 🧿 Information provenance: PSNI → Commission → courts → PSNI

This is the Polaris mechanism at the heart of the case. An intelligence assessment, a regulatory determination, a judicial bundle and a live operational command log are not the same information object. They can describe the same proposed procession while selecting different facts, applying different thresholds and serving different institutional purposes.

| Stage | Information object | Question asked | Missing primary record |
|---|---|---|---|
| PSNI advice | Written assessment and supporting material | What public-order risks are foreseeable? | Original submission and updates |
| Commission | Representations, police advice, statutory factors | What restrictions should be imposed? | Full decision and evidence bundle |
| High Court | Pleadings, affidavits, legal argument | Was the decision lawful / should interim relief issue? | Judgments, orders and bundles |
| Court of Appeal | Appellate record | Was the lower-court decision legally correct? | Order and reasons |
| Live PSNI operation | Updated intelligence, deployment and risk information | What action is necessary and proportionate now? | Command logs and debrief |
| Policing Board | Public explanations and requested documents | How should police account for their decisions? | Full hearing and responses |

### 11.1 Misrepresentation is a claim requiring comparison

If a public question asks whether the Commission misrepresented PSNI advice, the existence of the question does not prove misrepresentation. Compare the original words, the Commission's paraphrase, any clarifications and the context. It is possible for the Commission to make a different judgment from the police without inaccurately quoting the police. It is also possible for a summary to omit a material qualification. The documents determine which occurred. [S13][S14][S21][S22][S23][S24]

### 11.2 Personnel as an information variable

For each transfer, record sender, recipient, date, approval authority, version, attachments, qualifications and any follow-up. Then ask whether a personnel change crossed that transfer. Do not infer an information failure merely because a new person appears in the organisational chart.

## 13. 🏛️ Oversight: Several doors, different legal rooms

The Northern Ireland Policing Board scrutinises police performance and governance. The Police Ombudsman has independent powers concerning qualifying police complaints and conduct matters. The Public Prosecution Service considers prosecutions under its own legal tests. Courts may determine judicial-review, civil or criminal proceedings. PSNI may conduct internal operational reviews. Parliamentary and Assembly scrutiny address political and institutional responsibility. [S02][S03][S13][S25][S26]

These mechanisms overlap but are not interchangeable. A Board meeting does not itself establish criminal liability. An Ombudsman investigation is not proof that an officer committed misconduct. A prosecution decision is not an assessment of every institutional governance concern. A judicial-review ruling on the Commission does not settle every question about police tactics.

**For 2026:** identify which complaints were actually lodged, which investigations formally opened, what their terms are, and whether findings have been published. Mark an unverified investigation **OPEN**, not 'ongoing'.

## 14. ⚖️ Use of force: Lawful power is only the first test

A police officer considering force must have a lawful basis and must consider necessity, proportionality and reasonableness. Human-rights obligations, especially protection of life and bodily integrity, are not afterthoughts to be applied once the road has been cleared. The assessment is fact-sensitive and may change rapidly with crowd size, movement, intelligence and the risk of escalation. [S02][S06][S07]

### 14.1 Questions for any proposed intervention

- **Purpose:** What lawful objective would the action achieve?
- **Basis:** Which statutory or common-law power applies?
- **Necessity:** Could the objective reasonably be achieved with less intrusive measures?
- **Proportionality:** What injuries, escalation or wider interference might the action cause?
- **Individualisation:** Is action directed at identified conduct or indiscriminately at a crowd?
- **Alternatives:** Negotiation, separation, delay, individual arrest or other lawful measures?
- **Information:** What did commanders reasonably know at the moment of decision?
- **Review:** What was recorded and how was the choice reassessed as circumstances changed?

### 14.2 Avoid the two false absolutes

'An authorised march must be forced through' ignores the legal constraints on policing. 'A sufficiently large protest can always prevent an authorised march' risks turning threatened disorder into a practical veto. The actual law requires a more difficult inquiry into reasonable protective measures and the justification for any force used or withheld.

### 14.3 Crowd dynamics are evidence, not atmosphere

Numbers, density, access routes, available exits, vulnerable people, weather, duration, public-order resources and likely escalation may affect operational risk. These facts should be established from records, not embellished for dramatic effect. An operational commander must work with the street that exists, not the street described in a determination issued two days earlier.

## 15. 🌍 Four jurisdictions: Do not import the wrong police powers

Northern Ireland, England and Wales, Scotland, and the Republic of Ireland do not operate one interchangeable legal regime for public processions. The Convention rights framework may be relevant across jurisdictions, but statutory notification, regulation, prohibition, policing and judicial procedures differ.

| Jurisdiction | Main research starting point | Question for comparison |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Ireland | Public Processions (NI) Act 1998; police and public-order legislation | Why a specialist independent Commission? |
| England and Wales | Public Order Act 1986 as amended; police powers | Who may impose procession or assembly conditions? |
| Scotland | Scottish civic-government and procession framework | What notification and local-authority roles apply? |
| Republic of Ireland | Constitution, public-order law and Garda powers | How are assembly rights and policing balanced? |

Do not assume that a power available to police in England applies in precisely the same form to the PSNI. Equally, the existence of a specialist Commission in Northern Ireland does not establish that its procedures are inherently better or worse than another jurisdiction's. Compare the legal source, decision-maker, appeal route and accountability mechanism before drawing conclusions. [S01][S16][S27]

## 20. 🧬 Institutional information ecology: The same road, different questions

The Commission sees a notified procession and representations against statutory criteria. The courts see pleadings, evidence and questions of legality. Police commanders see a developing street-level situation, with changing risks and resources. Oversight bodies see decisions already taken and ask how those decisions can be justified. The same observation acquires different significance because the institutional questions differ.

```text
                     NOTIFIED PROCESSION
                             │
                             ▼
                     PARADES COMMISSION
                   conditions and reasons
                             │
              ┌──────────────┴──────────────┐
              ▼                             ▼
         JUDICIAL REVIEW              PSNI PLANNING
        legality and remedy        resources / intelligence
              │                             │
              ▼                             ▼
        COURT ORDER                    LIVE OPERATION
     (read actual terms)          necessity / proportionality
              │                             │
              └──────────────┬──────────────┘
                             ▼
                        PUBLIC OUTCOME
                             │
                             ▼
                   OVERSIGHT / ACCOUNTABILITY
```

At every arrow ask: **Who sent the information? Which version? Who received it? Under what authority? What was the next institution actually required to decide?** Then add the personnel question: **did responsibility change hands between those points, and what evidence exists of the handover?**

The working hypothesis is not that institutional disagreement proves failure. It is that **different institutional mandates can produce different decisions from overlapping evidence**, and that failures of procedure, transmission or governance must be demonstrated rather than guessed. A legal permission may coexist with a police decision that immediate force would be disproportionate. Whether the specific 2026 choices were lawful and reasonable is an evidential question, not a slogan.
