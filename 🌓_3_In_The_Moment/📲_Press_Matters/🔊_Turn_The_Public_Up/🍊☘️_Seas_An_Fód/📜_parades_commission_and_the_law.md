## 🛰️ Orientation

The Parades Commission exists because deciding whether a contested procession should take place is not the same task as deciding how police should respond to the crowd that appears on the day. Drumcree and Garvaghy Road made that distinction painfully difficult to avoid. A traditional Orange Order return route passes through a predominantly nationalist residential area whose inhabitants have objected to the procession in the context of historical intimidation, exclusion and violence. Procession participants invoke tradition, religious and cultural expression, and assembly rights. Neither set of claims makes the other disappear; the state has obligations to people on both sides of the road.

The Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998 created a specialised, independent decision-maker. It did not create a police command centre, an alternative court, or a body authorised to disregard its own procedures. **Regulatory independence is a division of authority, not immunity from law.** The Commission can impose conditions under statute. Police must independently assess lawful and proportionate operational responses. Courts can review legality. Ministers possess defined, not unlimited, powers. Mediation is a separate but related function, not a magical button labelled *everybody be reasonable now*.

The 2026 sequence makes those distinctions unusually visible: an earlier determination was quashed because of a procedural defect in approval of its final text; the Northern Ireland Office commissioned a governance review; a replacement determination allowed a tightly restricted procession; residents litigated; and the PSNI declined to force a passage through a large counter-protest. The fact that these events concern the same road does not mean that the same legal question was answered at every stage. [S01][S08][S11][S12][S15]

It does **not** claim to have inspected the original PSNI submission, the complete August judgment, the Commission's internal approval records, or the full O'Hara review evidence. Detailed claims about those records remain **OPEN**. Reported events are attributed as such; a reported allegation is not a judicial finding. [S11][S13][S14]

## 1. 🗺️ Before the regulator: the road, the march, the police

### 1.1 Why Garvaghy Road matters

Drumcree is not an abstract seminar in freedom of assembly. It is a dispute about people occupying and moving through an inhabited place. The annual Orange Order procession associated with Drumcree Church in Portadown became a focus of recurring conflict over the return route along Garvaghy Road. To participants, a customary route may express collective religious, cultural and historical continuity. To residents, the same movement through their neighbourhood may evoke sectarian power, exclusion and the experience of having their safety subordinated to other people's insistence on passage. The law has to assess the actual circumstances rather than presume either account is irrelevant.

### 1.2 The 1990s decision-making crisis

The confrontations of 1995–97, and particularly the 1996 sequence in which a police restriction was reversed amid widespread disorder, exposed the problem of leaving route decisions entangled with immediate policing pressure. Public disorder may make an operational situation dangerous. It cannot, by itself, supply a satisfactory public theory of whose rights count. The 1998 parading crisis and the murder of the three Quinn brothers in Ballymoney belong to this historical setting; the children's deaths must be recorded with gravity, not folded into a decorative history of marches. Consult the contemporary chronology before giving precise daily figures or attributing particular acts. [S04]

### 1.3 The institutional question

Who should decide whether conditions are imposed: police facing immediate threats, elected politicians under pressure, or an independent statutory body obliged to explain its reasoning? The North Review recommended an independent Commission with a role in both adjudication and mediation. Its design was not intended to make police irrelevant. It was intended to distinguish the **decision about lawful conditions** from the **decision about practical enforcement**. That is the thread running from the 1990s to September 2026.

## 3. ⚖️ The Public Processions (Northern Ireland) Act 1998

The Act received Royal Assent in February 1998, before the Belfast/Good Friday Agreement in April. The Commission is **not** a Strand One, Strand Two or Strand Three body created by that Agreement, although its work operates in the same wider political and human-rights environment. The operative statute, including amendments in force at the relevant date, must be used for legal propositions. [S01][S02]

### 3.1 Functions, notification and statutory instruments

Section 2 addresses the Commission's general functions. The Act also establishes the framework for procession notification, the Commission's guidelines and Code of Conduct, and determinations. Distinguish the statute from guidance made under it: statutory language is not interchangeable with a paraphrase in a Commission leaflet. Notification is the beginning of a regulatory process, not necessarily a guarantee that every proposed feature of a procession will be permitted. [S01]

### 3.2 Section 8: conditions on processions

Section 8 supplies the central power to impose conditions. The Commission must have regard to specified matters, including potential public disorder or damage, disruption to community life, effects on community relationships, previous failures to comply with the Code of Conduct, and the desirability of allowing a procession along a customary route. The precise statutory text and its current amendments should govern the final legal analysis. [S01]

A **customary route is a statutory consideration, not an unconditional trump card**. Nor is the possibility of hostile counter-protest a simple veto. The decision-maker must assess the relevant evidence and rights in context. The legal issue is how a lawful balance was reached, not which side can attach the largest moral adjective to its preferred route.

### 3.3 Section 9 and ministerial review

Section 9 provides a defined statutory mechanism concerning review of Commission determinations, involving the Chief Constable and Secretary of State. It must not be casually described as a general ministerial appeal whenever a decision becomes unpopular. Any claim that it was invoked in 2026 requires a specific application, date and official response. **OPEN:** obtain any section 9 correspondence or confirmation that none was made. [S01]

### 3.4 Section 10 and section 11

Read the current statutory text of section 10 for its specific procedural and substantive effect; do not infer a power from its position between two better-known sections. Section 11 provides the Secretary of State with powers concerning public processions, subject to statutory conditions. These are powers Parliament defined, not a general authority to instruct the Commission to reach a convenient answer. **OPEN:** establish whether any section 11 intervention was considered or exercised in the 2026 sequence. [S01]

### 3.5 Offences, enforcement and prosecution

Breach of an applicable procession condition may raise criminal-law questions, but criminal investigation, prosecutorial judgment and conviction are distinct stages. A regulator's determination does not itself order police to clear a road by any available means. Police must consider necessity, proportionality, safety and their own legal duties. A prosecution is not automatic simply because someone alleges that a condition was breached. [S01][S05]

## 4. 🧿 How the section 8 balance should be read

**Public disorder and damage:** What risk was predicted, by whom, on what evidence, and at what time? A prediction is not a fact merely because it appears in an official submission. The Commission may need to assess both likelihood and seriousness.

**Disruption to community life:** The experience of residents is not reduced to whether vehicles can pass. Access, safety, ordinary movement, fear and the social effects of a disputed route may matter. The statutory text, applicable guidelines and case law determine the legal relevance and weight of those factors.

**Impact on relationships within the community:** This consideration asks about the social consequences of the procession in context. It is not an invitation to assume that all unionists, nationalists, residents or Orange Order members hold identical views.

**Previous Code compliance:** The record must identify whose conduct, on which previous occasion, is legally relevant. Historical controversy does not automatically establish a specific breach.

**Customary route:** The route's historical use is relevant because Parliament made it relevant. It is not the only consideration. A regulator must explain why the route is permitted, restricted or prohibited in light of the full statutory framework.

**Human rights:** Freedom of peaceful assembly, religious expression, expression, residents' private and family life, and public safety may intersect. Some rights can be restricted under defined conditions. The Commission's decision should demonstrate a lawful and proportionate assessment rather than treat one right as an absolute permission slip. [S01][S18]

### A useful distinction

**Evidence** is what was submitted or reliably established. **Weight** is the Commission's assessment of its significance. **Reasons** are the explanation for the decision. **Lawfulness** is a question that courts may review. These four things must not be collapsed into one.

## 6. 👥 Membership, appointments and the people holding the pen

The Commission has an institutional identity, but its decisions are made through authorised officeholders. Appointment, resignation, expiry of terms, vacancies and administrative handovers therefore matter to the provenance of a determination. They do **not**, on their own, prove that a determination was defective or politically influenced.

The 2 September parliamentary statement is an important primary lead: it refers to a recent commissioner resignation and approaching expiry of remaining commissioners' terms. That provides a concrete reason to examine membership and continuity during 2026, rather than infer an extraordinary turnover pattern from a few coincident dates. [S11]

### 6.1 Three dates, not one

For each relevant appointment record: **announcement date → legal effective date → date responsibility was actually assumed**. Also capture departure announcement and effective departure, interim appointments, statutory term limits, reappointments and the appointing authority. A press release about an appointment is not proof that its subject signed off a decision that same day.

## 7. ⚖️ August 2026: a decision quashed for process, not a route awarded

The earlier determination was quashed on 25 August 2026. The Northern Ireland Office's subsequent parliamentary statement described a procedural defect concerning approval of the final determination text. That is the established public starting point for analysing the Commission's internal governance. **The complete August judgment remains outstanding in this node.** [S11][S17]

A quashing order removes the legal effect of the challenged decision according to its terms. It does **not**, merely by existing, amount to a judicial instruction that the Orange Order must be permitted to march unrestricted along Garvaghy Road. Nor does it establish that every factual assessment in the Commission's decision was wrong. The grounds, reasoning and remedy must be read separately.

The governance questions are exacting: What text did commissioners discuss? What text was issued? Who changed it, if anyone? Was the final wording circulated? Which approvals were required? Did a quorum or collective-consent rule apply? What contemporaneous records exist? Were other determinations audited after the defect came to light? These questions cannot be answered by repeating a ministerial summary as though it were the complete judgment.

There is a further information-ecology issue: a public institution may communicate a decision that appears to be its considered position even where the formal chain authorising that communication is incomplete. That is a problem of **decision provenance**. The document must be traceable back to the people empowered to approve it.

## 8. 🏛️ The O'Hara review: governance under examination

The Northern Ireland Secretary announced an independent judge-led review on 1 September; Sir John O'Hara KC was appointed on 23 September. The government described a review of Commission governance, procedures, fairness and related institutional arrangements. Its exact terms of reference and exclusions must be reproduced from the official document, rather than reconstructed from headlines. [S11][S19][S20]

A governance review is not the same as an appeal against a particular determination. It may examine how the regulator makes decisions, handles evidence, communicates, mediates and maintains lawful procedures. It should not be casually presented as a power to substitute the reviewer's preferred parade route for the Commission's statutory judgment.

### Questions the review could illuminate

- How is collective approval of final texts recorded?
- Are decision-making rules accessible, clear and consistently followed?
- How are commissioner vacancies, resignations and expiring terms managed?
- What training and legal support are available to commissioners?
- How does the Commission distinguish police intelligence from its own legal assessment?
- How are residents' and organisers' representations recorded?
- What happens when urgent litigation overlaps with a pending determination?
- How are mediation and formal adjudication separated or coordinated?
- What audit trail allows the public to understand the provenance of a published determination?

These are research questions, **not** assertions that the review has made findings on each. Record the review's announced timetable, recipient, publication commitments and any subsequent report when issued. The existence of a review does not automatically suspend the Commission's statutory functions.

## 11. 🚨 Police advice is not an operational order

The PSNI can provide the Commission with information about anticipated public disorder, public safety, community disruption and other matters relevant to a statutory determination. The Commission must consider that information under its own legal responsibilities. The PSNI later makes operational decisions under police law and human-rights obligations in light of the circumstances it faces. These stages are related but not identical. [S01][S05][S12]

Following the 27 September confrontation, the PSNI publicly explained that forcibly clearing a large counter-protest would have been disproportionate. That statement concerns the **operational use of force on the day**. It does not, without more, establish what the PSNI originally advised the Commission, or that the Commission's statutory determination was unlawful. [S12]

The Northern Ireland Policing Board's questions about the content of the submission, possible misrepresentation, level of detail and mutual-aid considerations are important evidence of scrutiny. A question appearing on the Board's website is not proof that the premise of the question is true. The original advice, supplementary exchanges, relevant operational assessments and complete answers are needed. [S13][S14][S21][S22][S23][S24]

### Possible explanations to test

1. The Commission and police applied different lawful tests to substantially the same information.
2. The Commission misunderstood or inaccurately summarised police evidence.
3. Intelligence or conditions changed between the submission and the operation.
4. Different personnel or channels handled different stages of communication.
5. Publicly available records omit important qualifications or later clarifications.
6. Several of the above occurred together.

None should be selected simply because it produces the most satisfying headline. **The documentary comparison is the test.**

## 12. ⚖️ Judicial review, urgent relief and what the courts decided

The replacement determination prompted urgent litigation. Contemporary reporting and the published *MacCionnaith* decisions identify an interim High Court intervention, an emergency appellate reversal and a later refusal of permission to seek judicial review. The precise sequence, grounds and remedies must be reconstructed from the orders and complete judgments, not solely from press descriptions. [S09][S10][S18]

Judicial review ordinarily concerns **lawfulness**, including whether a public authority acted within its powers, followed required procedures, took account of relevant matters and complied with applicable rights. It is not an invitation for a judge simply to become another commissioner and pick a preferred route. Interim relief may turn on urgency and procedural considerations distinct from final merits.

A refusal of judicial-review permission is not a finding that every possible use of police force would be proportionate. Likewise, a police decision not to clear a route is not a judicial reversal of the Commission's determination. **Courts decide legal questions; police decide lawful operations; the Commission makes statutory determinations.**

An appeal was heard on 5 October, with judgment reserved in the last contemporaneous reporting used for this draft. **OPEN:** check for judgment issued after that report and update the legal account, date and citation. Do not leave a reserved-judgment label in a living node after a later ruling has been published. [S15][S16]

## 13. 🕊️ Mediation: statutory function, not decorative aspiration

The Commission's remit includes a dispute-resolution dimension. That is not a side project. The historical justification for separating route regulation from immediate police command included the hope that engagement could reduce confrontation before the point at which force or litigation dominated the choices. The legal scope of the Commission's mediation functions should be drawn from the Act, guidelines and original North Review. [S01][S04]

The 2026 record requires a distinct mediation chronology: offers made before notification; engagement with residents and organisers; meetings held; written proposals; participation and refusals; any mediation after the replacement determination; and the relationship between Commission engagement and later NIO-facilitated talks. **OPEN:** obtain original records and participant statements, preserving conflicting accounts rather than harmonising them by editorial instinct.

The Northern Ireland Office's political negotiations, Commission mediation, independent facilitation and any proposed international mediation are different processes. A government minister may convene people without acquiring the Commission's statutory powers. An outside mediator may help people reach an agreement without becoming a judge or police commander. An agreement may still require a lawful regulatory or operational implementation step. The bureaucracy remains irritatingly real even when everybody has finally agreed to be civil.

### A mediation evidence ledger

For each attempt record **date, convenor, participants, authority, proposed terms, response, next step and source**. Distinguish “offered”, “accepted”, “began”, “ended without agreement” and “produced an implemented arrangement”. They are not synonyms.

## 14. 🏛️ Ministers, statutory review and independent decision-making

The Northern Ireland Secretary has responsibilities under the constitutional and statutory framework, including defined powers under the Public Processions Act. Ministerial oversight, appointment arrangements and the commissioning of a governance review are not the same as an unlimited power to dictate an individual Commission decision. Likewise, the Irish government may make diplomatic representations under the wider peace-process arrangements without acquiring domestic executive control of the Commission. [S01][S02][S03]

The questions to resolve are specific: Was a statutory review request made under section 9? Were section 11 powers considered? Did the NIO communicate with the Commission about procedure, governance or a particular outcome? Were any such contacts recorded? What safeguards separated political engagement from the Commission's statutory judgment? Which officeholder had authority on each date?

Independence does not mean nobody can ask questions. The proper tests are **lawful appointment, lawful procedure, reasoned decisions, judicial review and public accountability**. The review of governance should be assessed against those tests, not against a fantasy in which a public body is either entirely untouchable or subject to whatever the minister fancies on Tuesday.

## 15. 🔄 Personnel turnover and institutional memory: a bounded hypothesis

The sibling timeline records personnel changes across the NIO, PSNI, Commission, justice institutions and diplomatic posts. This node should only draw a causal line from those changes to Commission practice where there is evidence. A resignation and several terms approaching expiry are **documented leads**. A theory that they explain the August approval defect is **not established**. [S11]

### Five tests

**Timing:** Did a person actually hold the relevant office when the decision was considered, approved and published?

**Authority:** What power could they exercise personally, collectively or by delegation?

**Information:** What evidence, advice and previous decisions were available to them?

**Handover:** Was a transition documented, and were outstanding legal and procedural issues passed on?

**Effect:** Is there evidence that the transition changed a decision, caused a delay, weakened approval or altered a communication?

The comparison baseline matters. A normal term-expiry cycle should not be redescribed as an extraordinary institutional purge because it happened near a contested event. Conversely, a routine appointment process can still produce practical continuity problems. Both possibilities deserve examination.

**Minimum records:** Commission appointment notices, statutory terms, resignation announcement, membership lists by date, meeting attendance, delegated-authority policy, approval records and the O'Hara review's eventual findings. Keep personal speculation out of the record.

## 16. 🧬 Design, law and practice: an institutional audit

The North Review asks why a separate regulator was needed. The Act asks what powers Parliament conferred. The Commission's rules ask how those powers are exercised. The August judgment asks whether a particular exercise complied with the rules. The September determination asks how the regulator balanced evidence and statutory considerations. The PSNI operation asks what use of force was lawful and proportionate in the situation that developed. The O'Hara review asks whether the regulator's governance arrangements need reform. These are related questions, not interchangeable verdicts.

| Layer | Governing question | Evidence to inspect | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Historical design | Why independent regulation? | North Review | Lawfulness of a 2026 determination |
| Statute | What may the Commission do? | Current Act | What it actually did |
| Procedure | How must it decide? | Rules, minutes, approval logs | Merits of the route decision |
| Evidence | What was known and submitted? | PSNI and community representations | How the regulator weighed it |
| Determination | What conditions and reasons were issued? | Final authenticated decision | Operational feasibility on the day |
| Courts | Was a challenged act lawful? | Orders and judgments | A standing instruction to use force |
| Police | What action was necessary and proportionate? | Operational records | Whether Commission procedure was valid |
| Governance review | What institutional reforms are justified? | Terms, evidence, findings | A substitute appellate determination |

### Information is experienced and processed

A resident's account of intimidation, a parade organiser's description of tradition, a police prediction of disorder and a Commission's statutory balancing exercise can all refer to the same road without being the same kind of information. The institution's purpose changes the question it asks. The evidential danger arises when an answer to one question is transported as though it answered another: “the route was permitted” becomes “police must force a passage”; “police would not force a passage” becomes “the regulator lied”; “a procedural error occurred” becomes “the substantive claim was vindicated”. None of those transformations follows without further evidence.

This is why the approval trail matters. Public authority is not a free-floating institutional vibe. It must be traceable from evidence, through authorised consideration and collective sign-off, into a published decision capable of being challenged and understood.
