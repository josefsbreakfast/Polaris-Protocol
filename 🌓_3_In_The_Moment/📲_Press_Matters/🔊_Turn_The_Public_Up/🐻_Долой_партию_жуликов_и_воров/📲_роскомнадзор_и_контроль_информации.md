# 📲 Роскомнадзор и контроль информации
**First created:** 2026-10-10 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-10  
*Roskomnadzor and the Control of Information — Russian telecommunications regulation, legal restrictions, technical filtering, lived consequences and the cybernetics of a state attempting to manage what it can hear.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation — The Internet Has Been Referred to Administration

The internet made information easier to distribute. This was, administratively speaking, a complication. People published things. Other people read them. Sometimes the people publishing things were not approved, and sometimes the things being read were politically inconvenient. Fortunately, the Russian Federation developed an extensive framework of regulation, legal restrictions, network controls and enforcement institutions. Unfortunately for anyone hoping for one villain and one switch, that framework has several departments and a rather complicated wiring diagram.

**Роскомнадзор** (Roskomnadzor, or РКН) is a real federal regulator with responsibilities extending beyond censorship. It is also an important actor in restrictions on online information. The investigation must therefore answer *which legal power, which institution, which technical mechanism, which observed effect and whose lived consequence?* Those are separate questions. Conflating them produces excellent propaganda and dreadful systems analysis.

This node follows the development of Russia's information-control infrastructure from 2008 to **10 October 2026**, using established historical context and a clearly identified verification backlog for recent events. It is a **research-grounded analytical draft, not a claim of completed contemporary primary-source verification**. The governing argument is that information control intervenes in the feedback a political system receives from society. **A government can become better at restricting communications while becoming less certain what people actually believe.**

---
## 1. 🏛️ What Actually Is Roskomnadzor?

Roskomnadzor (Роскомнадзор; РКН) is the Federal Service for Supervision of Communications, Information Technology and Mass Media. Its remit encompasses communications and media supervision and aspects of personal-data regulation. Calling it *the Russian internet police* may make for a tidy headline, but it is a dreadful institutional map. The Russian government, legislature, courts, Prosecutor General's Office, security bodies, telecommunications operators and platforms all have distinct roles. Sometimes one actor issues a legally operative demand and another carries it out. Sometimes a platform removes material itself. Sometimes a network fails for reasons unrelated to censorship. These distinctions are the investigation, not tedious qualifications to be removed once the joke is written.

The agency was established in 2008 in the course of federal executive reorganisation. Its precise reporting arrangements, current leadership, powers and departmental subdivisions require dated official documents before publication. A regulator's formal mandate is one thing; a particular blocking order is another; its success on a particular network is a third. The name on the stationery cannot substitute for evidence of which office made which decision.

---

## 2. 📜 The Law Has Entered the Chat

The principal statutory trail begins with Federal Law **149-FZ** on information, information technologies and information protection; **126-FZ** on communications; **152-FZ** on personal data; and Law **2124-1** on mass media. Important later changes include **139-FZ (2012)** associated with the website-restriction register, **398-FZ (2013)** associated with expanded rapid restriction procedures, and **90-FZ (2019)** associated with the so-called sovereign internet. These are *research anchors*, not an assertion that the original text of each instrument remained unchanged in October 2026.

For every operative claim, retrieve the consolidated Russian-language provision as at the relevant date, the amendment's commencement date, the initiating authority, the implementing authority and the avenue for review. A law can establish a power without proving its exercise. An official announcement can establish an announced policy without demonstrating that the network behaves accordingly. Russian original: **ограничение доступа** — restriction of access; do not silently translate it as universal technical unavailability.

The constitution's protections of expression and access to information provide important legal context, but they do not automatically determine the outcome of any specific enforcement action. Statutes, subordinate instruments, procedural practice and judicial interpretation must be read together. One suspects the law has entered the chat and brought several further chats in which the public is not invited to participate.

---

## 3. 🗓️ From Regulator to Network Infrastructure

**2008–2011:** Establishment and early regulatory responsibilities. **2012–2013:** Expansion of statutory restriction mechanisms and register-based access controls. **2014–2016:** Greater pressure on platform compliance, data storage and publishing. **2017–2018:** VPN-related restrictions and the Telegram blocking confrontation. **2019–2021:** Sovereign-internet provisions and equipment deployment. **2022–2024:** Wartime restrictions on reporting and major services. **2025–10 October 2026:** A period requiring a fresh dated legal and measurement audit; this node does not fabricate new events to fill the calendar.

These periods overlap. Legal adoption, entry into force, procurement, equipment installation and observed interference should occupy separate rows in a chronology. Otherwise a legislative date becomes a fictional switch that supposedly changed every Russian connection at midnight. Internet engineers would be delighted if infrastructure were that obedient.

---

## 4. 🗂️ The Register of Things You Are Not Supposed to See

A restriction register is an administrative instrument linking legal decisions to action by intermediaries. Research its official Russian name, covered categories, the route by which entries are added, notification requirements, appeal mechanisms and removals. The register is not identical to every conceivable form of filtering, nor is it a public census of every inaccessible resource.

Ask who made the underlying decision, whether a court was involved, which resource was specified, whether an entire domain or narrower resource was affected, and whether a publisher had a realistic opportunity to challenge the measure. Distinguish an official listing from a test showing that a connection failed. A single URL can be unreachable because of a network error, platform policy, geoblocking, DNS failure or intentional state interference. Attribution requires evidence.

---

## 5. 🌐 How Does Blocking Actually Work?

**DNS interference** changes or prevents the translation of a domain name into a usable network address. **IP blocking** interferes with access to one or more network addresses and may catch unrelated services on shared infrastructure. **URL-level filtering** depends on what an intermediary can actually observe about a request. **TLS metadata** can reveal some connection characteristics without granting access to the encrypted message body. **Deep packet inspection (DPI)** describes techniques for classifying and inspecting traffic; it is not a magical universal decryption appliance. **Throttling** degrades performance rather than necessarily stopping access. **Platform removal** happens at the service layer and should not be mislabelled as ISP filtering.

```text
Reader → device/application → DNS and connection setup
                                ↓
                         access network / ISP
                                ├─ DNS interference
                                ├─ address restrictions
                                ├─ traffic classification
                                └─ deliberate degradation
                                ↓
                         remote platform/service
                                ├─ material available
                                ├─ platform-side removal
                                └─ service-side restrictions
```

These are possible intervention points, not a mandatory sequence through which every Russian packet passes. Tests should specify network, location, time, application and baseline. A screenshot reading *connection failed* is not, by itself, a forensic attribution.

---

## 6. 🧰 ТСПУ — The Boxes in the Network

**ТСПУ — технические средства противодействия угрозам** can be rendered as *technical means for countering threats*. In the sovereign-internet framework these systems are associated with network-level traffic-management and restriction capabilities. The investigation must separate the equipment's official stated purpose, its procurement and deployment, its control arrangements, and what independent measurements actually demonstrate.

Research who procures and maintains equipment, what obligations apply to operators, how instructions are distributed, and which network paths are affected. Do not mistake centralised instruction or filtering policy for proof that every byte of Russian traffic is routed through a single national exchange. Nor should we pretend that knowing the equipment's general purpose proves the precise implementation at a given ISP. The interesting technical questions live in those differences.

---

## 7. 🏝️ Суверенный Рунет — Sovereignty and Resilience

The policy label *sovereign Runet* combines claims about continuity of domestic communications, protection from external threats and greater domestic control over network operations. Security and censorship are not mutually exclusive technical uses: an instrument intended to manage disruptions may also permit state-directed restrictions. Conversely, a documented censorship application does not prove that every resilience function is fictitious.

The research needs official exercise notices, operator obligations, observed outages, DNS arrangements and independent measurements. What can the system do? What has it actually done? What collateral effects followed? Where does official explanation diverge from measurement? One can admire the administrative ambition while reserving judgement on whether the department has successfully preserved the internet by preventing people from using it.

## 8. 📱 Telegram, YouTube and the Platform Wars

The **2018 Telegram blocking campaign** is a particularly instructive historical case because attempted restrictions and collateral network disruption can be studied separately from whether the service remained usable for many people. Restrictions were subsequently lifted in 2020. Reconstruct the court and regulator sequence from original records before claiming a single causal explanation for the reversal.

For **YouTube**, distinguish reported throttling, regulator statements, service-side changes and independent measurements; do not treat an inaccessible video as proof of one central order. For **Facebook and Instagram**, document both access restrictions and relevant legal designations, identifying the different institutions involved. For **X/Twitter**, trace the changes in access and enforcement with dates. For messaging services in **2025–26**, leave developments unverified until original decisions or measurement records have been inspected.

## 9. 🪖 Wartime Information Boundaries

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 substantially changed the legal and political stakes of independent reporting. Restriction of access to news outlets, pressure on platforms, and criminal and administrative provisions concerning statements about the armed forces belong in the same wider information environment, but not in the same institutional box. Roskomnadzor can implement restrictions; investigators, prosecutors and courts exercise different powers.

The companion repression node examines administrative and criminal provisions including **20.3.3** of the Administrative Offences Code and **207.3** and **280.3** of the Criminal Code. The operative text and its amendments must be checked for the relevant date. A reporter's self-censorship, a platform removal, a website block and a criminal prosecution are different events even when they reinforce one another.

Most importantly, information availability is not belief. A person can encounter a claim and reject it, fail to encounter it, or encounter it through informal communication despite a blocked news site. We should not turn the existence of censorship into an invented public-opinion poll.

---

## 10. 📰 The Outlet Can Remain Online and Still Be Crushed

Information control also operates through registration, licensing, warnings, fines, advertising, financing, distribution and ownership pressure. A publisher may remain reachable but lose the resources needed to investigate, pay staff, retain sources or contest enforcement. These mechanisms affect **production** as well as **distribution** of information.

A serious case study records the outlet, action, competent authority, legal basis, date, response and subsequent operating status. Avoid treating every business failure as state suppression without evidence. Conversely, do not count only blocked domains and conclude that all other publishing is unaffected. The empty newsroom does not appear in the DNS log.

---

## 11. 🏷️ Labels, Foreign Agents and Undesirable Organisations

Foreign-agent designations and undesirable-organisation measures are distinct legal regimes. Their consequences can affect publication, collaboration, funding, reputational risk and the willingness of third parties to distribute material. Not every relevant designation is issued by Roskomnadzor. The institutional map must show the body empowered to designate, the body enforcing a particular obligation and any court involvement.

The information effect may begin before formal prohibition: potential sources, donors and collaborators can change behaviour when the legal costs of association rise. That is a plausible mechanism requiring evidence in individual cases. A label is not a neutral measurement of a publisher's accuracy, nor should it be treated as proof of wrongdoing independent of the state's classification.

---

## 12. 🔐 Personal Data Is Not One Giant Surveillance Database

Roskomnadzor's personal-data responsibilities should be distinguished from interception arrangements such as **SORM**, criminal investigations, security-service powers, data-localisation rules and platforms' disclosure obligations. These systems may interact, but they are not one organisation with one undifferentiated capability.

A legal requirement to store data domestically does not itself prove that any particular agency read a particular message. Network traffic metadata is not equivalent to decrypted content. Regulatory authority, technical possibility and documented use must be separate evidence fields. The popular image of a gigantic monitor showing every citizen's thoughts is cinematic; the actual architecture deserves the courtesy of investigation.

---

## 13. 🧠 The Cybernetics of Information Control

Information control is an intervention in the very system the state seeks to observe. People experience events, speak, publish, organise, consume information and alter their conduct in response to anticipated consequences. Filtering then changes what reaches audiences and what becomes visible to authorities. Those altered observations may inform later political decisions.

```text
Lived events → observation → testimony / journalism / research
                                  ↓
                      legal + economic + technical filters
                                  ↓
                      visible information environment
                                  ↓
                      public behaviour and state feedback
                                  ↺
                   anticipation changes future expression
```

**Selection** determines which signals circulate. **Delay** changes their timing. **Distortion** arises when speakers anticipate penalties. **Asymmetry** appears when officials or connected groups retain access unavailable to others. **Feedback contamination** occurs when decision-makers interpret the output of a filtered environment as an unfiltered description of society.

This is a mechanism to test, not an assertion that Russian officials receive no accurate information. Governments possess multiple channels, including private reporting and polling. The question is how reliable those channels remain and how they interact with public expression. An extremely expensive listening apparatus may be very good at recording who clicked and much worse at discovering why people stopped speaking.

---

## 14. 🫀 Information Is Experienced

A blocked connection is not only a technical event. It may be a journalist losing access to a source, a student unable to retrieve research, a family struggling to communicate, or a business unable to reach customers. The same restriction can be trivial to one person and life-altering to another. Disability, language, income, geography, network access and available alternatives change its effects.

This is **Embodied Information Ecology**: information systems are experienced by bodies and communities rather than by abstract users floating politely above their circumstances. Document human consequences through first-person testimony and corroborated reporting. Do not borrow people's suffering as decorative proof of a theory. Nor assume every restriction creates the same harm. The consequence is contextual, and the context is the evidence.

---

## 15. 🧱 Circumvention, Adaptation and Limits

VPNs, mirrors, alternative services, independent distribution and offline networks complicate any claim of comprehensive control. They also create unequal access: people differ in technical knowledge, money, devices and risk tolerance. The state's response may include further restrictions, which then generate further adaptation.

```text
Restriction → changed user/service behaviour → changed effectiveness
       ↑                                          ↓
       └──────────── policy and technical revision ─┘
```

This is a feedback loop, not a promise that censorship will fail or that circumvention is universally safe. For this investigation, measure adoption and effects where credible studies exist. Do not infer national prevalence from anecdotes, and do not turn a research section into an untested operational recipe.

---

## 16. 🧪 How Do We Know What Is Blocked?

A robust technical claim needs time, location, network or ASN, tested service, method, control measurement and alternative explanations. **OONI** measurements can help identify anomalies and compare vantage points. **RIPE Atlas** supports network observations. **Censored Planet** provides research methods and datasets. **Cloudflare Radar** and **Internet Society Pulse** can add context about connectivity and disruptions. These sources measure different things; they are not interchangeable votes in a referendum on censorship.

Classify findings as `official_restriction_documented`, `network_interference_measured`, `user_report_only`, `platform_side_action`, or `unattributed_failure`. The distinction between *observed failure* and *attributed censorship* is mandatory. A failed connection is an observation. A censorship decision is an attribution. Both may be important; they require different evidence.

---

## 17. 🏭 Who Builds and Maintains the Machinery?

Network controls require hardware, software, installation, operators, support contracts, budgets and engineering labour. Investigate public procurement, equipment suppliers, system integrators, telecom obligations and sanctions documents. For each company, identify the exact product or contract and its demonstrated role. Selling ordinary routers is not itself evidence of participation in political censorship.

The material infrastructure is a useful corrective to treating state information control as a purely rhetorical decision. A minister can announce a restriction; a working restriction requires implementation. Procurement records may reveal who pays, who maintains, and how fragile or expensive the machinery is. Where the public record stops, the node should stop too rather than invent an invisible supplier.

---

## 18. 💰 The Economics of Controlling Information

Separate direct government procurement, operator compliance costs, maintenance, litigation, platform adaptation, business disruption and broader opportunity costs. Some costs can be found in public contracts or company accounts; others require careful modelling. There is no defensible single number for *the cost of Russian internet censorship* without a clearly defined scope and data.

The wartime context matters because technical and administrative resources have competing uses. That does not prove that a particular filtering system displaced a particular public service. It supplies a research question: what is spent, by whom, for what measured result, and with what documented collateral effects? The finance department may prefer that the answer remain under *miscellaneous resilience*.

---

## 19. 🐻 Is the Kremlin Better Informed?

The state may gain the ability to restrict dissemination and observe some patterns of communication while losing representative feedback about political attitudes. These can occur together. Surveillance data are not a census of belief. Compliance is not agreement. Silence is not consent. Nor does the persistence of independent publishing prove that controls are harmless.

Test competing possibilities. Perhaps restrictions deter public criticism. Perhaps people move into private networks. Perhaps official polling or administrative channels capture some concerns. Perhaps officials receive warnings but cannot act on them. Each proposition requires evidence. The strongest conclusion is conditional: **when expression becomes costly, public expression becomes a more selective sample of private opinion**. Whether leaders understand that selection is a further question.

A slow connection is not a named regulator's order. The correct output of an investigation with missing evidence is an explicit blank field, not a satisfying story.

## 21. ⛓️‍💥 Roskomnadzor and the Wider Repression System

Information restrictions and political prosecutions may reinforce one another while remaining legally distinct. A publication may be removed, its author investigated, a prosecution pursued and other writers deterred. To document such a sequence, record each action's date, legal basis, authority and outcome. Do not turn temporal proximity into proof of coordination without documentary support.

This node complements `⛓️‍💥_репрессии_и_сопротивление.md`, which addresses broader legal repression and individual outcomes. The Roskomnadzor investigation focuses on the institutional and technical routes by which information becomes less available. The distinction preserves the mechanisms rather than attributing every Russian political action to the nearest recognisable acronym.

## 24. ⚖️ What We Can and Cannot Conclude

We can distinguish a statutory power, an official order, a measured network anomaly, a platform decision and a documented human consequence. We can investigate whether these form a causal sequence. We cannot infer from one that every other element necessarily occurred. Nor can we claim every Russian user experiences identical restrictions or that DPI automatically reveals encrypted message contents.

The historical framework in this draft is stronger than its contemporary incident register. **No live October 2026 audit of Russian regulatory orders, network measurements or court records was completed for this drafting pass.** Treat 2025–26 incidents as open questions until corroborated. The technical explanations are general mechanisms; the historical case descriptions are background requiring original-document linkage before formal publication. This is not a verified real-time monitoring report.

A state may control a communication channel without controlling how people interpret a message. A state may suppress a message without resolving the circumstances that produced it. Neither proposition predicts imminent regime collapse. The point is to make the information environment legible without turning uncertainty into theatre.

## 25. 🏁 The Internet Is Not a Filing Cabinet

Roskomnadzor is not the internet. It is not every court, security officer, telecom engineer or ministry. It is an institution operating within a larger legal and technical arrangement that can restrict access, change incentives to publish and alter the visible record of public disagreement.

But information is produced, interpreted, remembered and acted upon by people. Controlling a connection does not erase what somebody experienced. Restricting an article does not establish that its readers would have believed it. Silencing public criticism does not demonstrate that a problem has been solved.

**The state can control a communication channel without controlling the meaning of every message.** And the more extensively it filters the signals from society, the more carefully it must interpret what remains.

> 🦊: “We've successfully brought the information environment under control.”
>
> 🧠: “Excellent. What does the population think?”
>
> 🦊: “They have stopped telling us.”
>
> 🧠: “Ah.”

*Fictional satire.* The internet is not a filing cabinet, and the population is not an administrative error.
