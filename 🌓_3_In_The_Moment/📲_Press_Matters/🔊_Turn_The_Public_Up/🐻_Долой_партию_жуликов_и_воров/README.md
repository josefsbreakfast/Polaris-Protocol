# 🐻 Долой партию жуликов и воров!
**First created:** 2026-10-10 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-10**  
*Down with the Party of Crooks and Thieves! — A map of Russian political power, its quarrelling opponents, the war against Ukraine, the information environment, and the documents required before anybody declares a revolution.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation — Unfortunately, the Russians Are Revolting

The communists are revolting. Unfortunately, so is the opposition. In several directions. The government is attempting to maintain order. The economy has been referred to the war department. Roskomnadzor has developed a professional interest in what everybody is publishing. The anti-war movement has declined to cooperate with the official national consensus. And somewhere in the administrative machinery, somebody has been asked to explain why the presence of a red flag does not constitute a complete political intelligence report.

🐻 Welcome to Russia. **The country is considerably more complicated than its government would prefer.**

This folder follows that complication across nine connected investigations. Some Russians support the Kremlin; others oppose it. Some support Russia's war; others resist it. Some oppose mobilisation without opposing the invasion. Some communists support state policy while other communists reject it as imperialist. Some people object to economic consequences rather than the policy producing them. None of these positions is interchangeable. Neither the Russian government nor the most visible opposition personalities can stand in for an entire population.

The historical foundation is non-negotiable: **Russia's war against Ukraine began in 2014; the full-scale invasion began on 24 February 2022.** This is Russian state aggression against Ukraine. Ukrainian sovereignty and Ukrainian testimony are not an appendix to an account of Russian domestic political disagreement. The existence of Russian resistance does not absolve the state of responsibility, and the state's responsibility does not make individual Russians' differing actions imaginary.

This README is a **reading map**, not a new claim that nine drafts equal nine independent confirmations. The evidence trail runs through the dedicated [📚 Sources and Chronology](./📚_источники_и_хронология.md).

## 1. 🐻 Why This Name?

**Долой партию жуликов и воров!** means *Down with the Party of Crooks and Thieves!* The epithet **партия жуликов и воров** is strongly associated with Alexei Navalny's criticism of **Единая Россия (United Russia)** and the protest politics surrounding the 2011 State Duma election. It is a political slogan and a piece of oppositional culture, not a judicial finding against every person associated with that party. Nor does its presence on this folder mean every group discussed here subscribes to Navalny's politics.

The Russian matters. **Долой** is an imperative — down with it. **Жулики** carries the sense of swindlers or crooks; **воры** means thieves. This is not the sort of name a government commissions for its own public-information campaign. It is also a useful introduction to the problem of political labels: a memorable slogan can bring very different people into the same conversation without making them one organisation.

We retain the title because it belongs to the political history under investigation. We do not allow the title to do the investigation on our behalf.

## 2. 🧭 What Are We Actually Investigating?

Five questions organise the folder. **Who exercises power?** That means the presidency, parliamentary institutions, governing-party networks, security bodies and their relationships, rather than a story in which one man personally administers an entire federation. **Who disagrees?** Parliamentary parties, independent socialists, anti-war activists, journalists, families of mobilised personnel, emigrants and people whose politics do not fit any of those categories. **What is the disagreement about?** Elections, corruption, the war, mobilisation, wages, public services, censorship, policing, historical identity and Russia's relationship to Ukraine are not one issue. **What happens when people act?** This is the question of courts, repression, economic incentives, information channels and political organisation. **How do we know?** Dates, original statements, legal instruments, media provenance and contradictions must survive contact with the archive.

We are not announcing that Russia is on the verge of collapse. We are not assuming that economic pressure generates revolution, or that the visibility of dissent directly measures the distribution of private opinion. We are studying a political system whose institutions and population respond to one another through channels that are neither equal nor neutral.

## 4. 🪆 How a Republic Acquires the Habits of a Court

Start with [🪆 The Man Who Wanted to Be Tsar](./🪆_человек_который_хотел_стать_царём.md). The contemporary Russian Federation is formally a constitutional republic, not a restored monarchy. That is precisely why the comparison with a court is politically interesting: authority may become personalised without abolishing the ministries, courts, budgets, bureaucracies and informal relationships through which it is exercised.

This investigation traces institutional change and the use of historical legitimacy. It asks what powers moved, what arrangements made that movement possible and what the resulting system rewards. **Personalist authority is not a claim that one person personally carries out every decision.** A state needs people to implement its decisions, and those people have their own incentives and knowledge. The political question is how their activities become organised around the centre — and what happens when information reaching the centre is filtered by what subordinates think the centre wants to hear.

The reader who wants to understand the political system before examining its protests should begin here, then continue to [🧨 Opposition Within the Opposition](./🧨_оппозиция_внутри_оппозиции.md) and [⛓️‍💥 Repression and Resistance](./⛓️‍💥_репрессии_и_сопротивление.md).

## 5. 🚩 The Communists Are Revolting; Which Communists?

The entry point is [🚩 The Communists Are Revolting](./🚩_коммунисты_бунтуют.md). A red flag is a symbol, not a complete programme. Russia's parliamentary Communist Party, independent communists, socialists and anti-war leftists occupy different positions in relation to the state and to the invasion of Ukraine. Some employ Soviet imagery in defence of Russian state power; others invoke internationalism to oppose it. A photograph of a banner does not decide between them.

The companion [🧨 Opposition Within the Opposition](./🧨_оппозиция_внутри_оппозиции.md) examines why the category *opposition* becomes misleading when used as though it were a party with a single manifesto. Opposition to a tax, a minister, an election result or the war can coexist with support for other parts of government policy. An institutionally tolerated parliamentary opposition is not equivalent to an independent organisation whose members risk prosecution. Neither is automatically representative of every person who dislikes Putin.

> 🐻: “The opposition has arrived.”  
> 🏛️: “What does it want?”  
> 🐻: “Which opposition?”  
> 🏛️: “The opposition.”  
> 🐻: “They're currently opposing one another.”  
> 🏛️: “Can we put them in one category?”  
> 🐻: “They've objected to that as well.”

*Fictional satire.*

The documentary question remains stubbornly specific: **who organised this event, what did they say in Russian, what were their demands, and what happened next?**

## 6. 🍊 The War Economy Has Entered the Chat

[🍊 The Wartime Economic Investigation](./🍊_Лох_не_мамонт_не_вымрет.md) examines war expenditure, economic endurance, energy markets, sanctions and diplomatic bargaining. A budget is not a shopping list; an appropriation is not expenditure already incurred; a sanctions exemption is not automatically a completed purchase; a negotiation opportunity is not proof of leverage successfully exercised. The file asks whether available economic pressure could have been used to seek concessions and what, if anything, was actually requested.

**Open verification issue:** A reported October 2026 American arrangement concerning Russian diesel and sanctions remains **unverified** in the local documentary work. The precise instrument, legal scope, effective dates, transactions and any diplomatic quid pro quo require primary-source confirmation. The README does not turn that report into an accomplished deal by repeating it. The relevant record and research queue are in [📚 Sources and Chronology](./📚_источники_и_хронология.md).

Nor should we convert economic hardship into a prediction of revolution. Inflation, fiscal stress, recruitment incentives and household distress may alter political incentives; their relationship to collective mobilisation must be established rather than assumed. People can resent the cost of a war and continue supporting the war. Others can oppose the invasion without experiencing an immediate personal economic loss. **Different pathways; different evidence.**

## 7. 🕊️ The Russians Who Oppose the War

[🕊️ The Russian Anti-War Movement](./🕊️_российское_антивоенное_движение.md) maps people and organisations who have opposed the invasion through protest, journalism, feminist networks, socialist and liberal organising, political statements and other documented activity. The movement is neither one party nor a hidden national referendum. Its participants differ over tactics, political history, sanctions, Russia's future and the terms on which peace should be pursued.

Particular care is needed with the families of mobilised soldiers. A demand to bring a relative home, rotate troops or improve military conditions may reflect opposition to the war, but it need not. Equally, an anti-Putin statement does not automatically recognise Ukraine's territorial integrity. **Read the actual demand.** Preserve the original Russian. Ask whose territory is being discussed and whether Ukraine's people are permitted agency in determining their future.

The consequences of resisting also vary profoundly. A well-known figure speaking from exile, a person protesting inside Russia and someone supporting a prisoner face different risks and different opportunities. Their prominence in foreign-language media cannot be used as a measure of domestic representativeness. This node belongs beside [⛓️‍💥 Repression and Resistance](./⛓️‍💥_репрессии_и_сопротивление.md) and [📲 Roskomnadzor and Information Control](./📲_роскомнадзор_и_контроль_информации.md): the politics of resistance cannot be separated from the conditions under which people can communicate and organise.

## 8. ⛓️‍💥 The Government Would Prefer You Didn't

[⛓️‍💥 Repression and Resistance](./⛓️‍💥_репрессии_и_сопротивление.md) follows the legal and institutional consequences of dissent. Constitutional guarantees and the practical conditions of exercising rights must be examined together. The record needs statutes, dates, case numbers, legal stages, appeals and outcomes. **Arrest is not charge; charge is not conviction; conviction is not sentence; sentence is not time served.** A state allegation is evidence of what the state alleged, not automatic proof of the allegation's truth.

This is also where the cluster connects to the distinct Dagestan and Baymak legal research. Those cases must not be collapsed into a single protest movement or imported into the October Moscow investigation as though they were evidence of events in Moscow. They are important examples of the need for individualised legal records and carefully bounded claims.

Repression is not merely a headline about a government. It changes what a person can do, how their family lives, which messages can safely be sent and what other people observe. The gap between formal entitlement and lived capacity is part of the evidence, not an optional moral flourish.

## 9. 📲 Roskomnadzor Has Been Informed

[📲 Roskomnadzor and Information Control](./📲_роскомнадзор_и_контроль_информации.md) examines Russia's communications regulator and the wider architecture of information control. Blocking, throttling, platform restrictions, deep packet inspection and media regulation are related mechanisms, but they are not identical. Nor is Roskomnadzor the same institution as the police, courts or security services. Legal authority, technical capacity, actual use and measured effects must be separated.

A failed connection is a measured failure, not by itself proof of which institution caused it. A regulator's order is evidence of a decision, not automatic proof that every network complied in the same way. Encrypted communications do not become universally readable merely because the state possesses extensive surveillance powers.

The political problem is especially interesting. If public disagreement is discouraged or filtered, **what can officials still know about what people think?** The answer is not that the state becomes blind: intelligence services, administrative reporting, informants and polling continue to exist. It is that each information channel has its own incentives and distortions. Suppressing visible opposition can make an official portrait of consensus easier to produce without making that portrait more accurate.

## 10. 📣 What Happened in Moscow in October 2026?

[📣 The Moscow October 2026 Investigation](./📣_московские_протесты_октября_2026.md) is an **open event investigation**, not a declaration that a specific demonstration has been authenticated. The local draft does not independently establish the date, venue, organisers, slogans, attendance or detentions of a particular October 2026 Moscow protest. Images and political symbolism must therefore remain attached to provenance questions rather than promoted to a complete event narrative.

A red flag does not prove communist organisational membership. A crowd does not prove an anti-war position. An anti-government slogan does not prove that economic hardship caused a demonstration. A photograph's publication date is not necessarily its capture date. And a regional protest in another year is not a Moscow event because both involve dissent.

The investigation's questions are practical: **when, where, who, what demands, what response, and which original sources?** If the answers remain unknown, the fields remain `null`. The reader can follow the open verification trail in [📚 Sources and Chronology](./📚_источники_и_хронология.md). We can still ask interesting political questions without fabricating the event that would answer them.

## 11. 📚 The Archive Would Like the Original Documents

[📚 Sources and Chronology](./📚_источники_и_хронология.md) is the folder's shared documentary infrastructure. It holds the chronology, source taxonomy, stable identifiers for events and claims, translation rules, legal and technical evidence protocols, discrepancies and research priorities. Its first edition explicitly distinguishes local source-derived material from matters not independently checked through live research.

One event may appear in several investigations. A prosecution might matter to an anti-war organiser, a legal history, a study of censorship and a discussion of political feedback. It is still **one event**, and repeating it in four Markdown files does not create four independent sources. The archive exists so that the original record can be corrected without flattening four separate interpretations.

> 📚: “We've found the original document.”  
> 🐻: “Excellent.”  
> 📚: “It contradicts the summary.”  
> 🐻: “But the summary was very entertaining.”  
> 📚: “Then we'll write an entertaining correction.”  
> 🐻: “Research is becoming administratively demanding.”

*Fictional satire.*

The key distinctions are: **event versus report; source authenticity versus claim accuracy; publication date versus event date; legal allegation versus outcome; observation versus causal attribution; and evidence versus inference.** Official records are invaluable evidence of what an institution declared or did. They are not magic truth certificates.

## 13. 🧠 Cybernetics — What the Government Can See

The nine investigations can be read as parts of a feedback system:

```text
                 STATE INSTITUTIONS
                        │
            War, policy, regulation
                        ▼
              MATERIAL CONDITIONS
          Work, prices, safety, family
                        ▼
                   POPULATION
          Experience and interpretation
                        ▼
               POLITICAL RESPONSES
         Support · dissent · resistance
                        ▼
             INFORMATION CHANNELS
        Media · parties · private speech
                        │
            Censorship / repression
                        ▼
               OBSERVABLE ACTIVITY
                        ▼
              STATE INTERPRETATION
                        └──────► Further policy
```

The arrows are analytical, not a claim that the state has one neat central nervous system. Different institutions collect different information and sometimes disagree. Citizens, officials, journalists and researchers do not observe the same population under the same conditions. **What becomes visible is shaped by political preference, organisational capacity, risk, distribution infrastructure and the observer's method.**

A reduction in street protest could reflect fewer people wanting to protest, higher costs of protesting, emigration, new tactics, loss of organisational capacity, poorer reporting, or several of those at once. A poll can be useful without being a transparent census of private belief. An official narrative can be sincerely held by some people and still be misleading as a portrait of everyone. The point of cybernetics here is not to declare that repression guarantees political failure. It is to ask what kinds of feedback survive a system designed to discourage disagreement.

## 14. 🫀 Information Is Experienced

The state has a budget; the household has a shopping list. The regulator has a network-management decision; somebody has a relative they need to reach. The court has a case number; the defendant has a life. A mobilisation order is an administrative act and a reorganisation of relationships, work, care and risk. A blocked service is a technical observation and, sometimes, a broken route between people.

These descriptions refer to the same political environment from different positions within it. They should not be collapsed into a single measure of success, failure or stability. Nor should people's experiences be used merely as decoration for a geopolitical thesis. **Information is experienced**: its arrival, suppression, distortion and delay alter what people can know and do.

The anti-war movement, families of mobilised soldiers, independent journalists and people prosecuted for dissent may experience the same state policy differently. Their words should be preserved in their own context, not rewritten into a single opposition script. The invasion's consequences for Ukrainians are not reducible to Russian domestic political feedback. Ukraine is not an experimental variable in a study of Russian legitimacy.

## 15. 🔍 Established, Open, and Interpreted

**Historical foundation:** Russia's actions against Ukraine began in 2014 and escalated to full-scale invasion in February 2022. Russian political institutions, media regulation, repression and anti-war opposition have substantial historical and documentary records. The companion nodes investigate their specifics and their interactions.

**Open contemporary records:** The precise claimed October 2026 Moscow events and the reported October 2026 US diesel-sanctions arrangement have not been independently authenticated in the locally assembled drafts. They remain research questions, not established cornerstones. Some individual prosecutions, regulatory measures, photographs and recent organisational statements also require event-level verification. Check the source hub before repeating a detail.

**Analytical questions:** Does economic pressure alter elite incentives? Does censorship distort political feedback? Does opposition fragmentation make collective action harder? What can be inferred from the persistence or disappearance of visible dissent? These are arguments to test against evidence, not facts created by their plausibility.

An authentic political statement proves that a statement was made, not that its contents were correct. An authentic photograph proves what is visible in the image, not what everyone present believed. A government authorisation proves its legal scope, not that a shipment or diplomatic bargain occurred. **The archive does not owe our best paragraphs automatic corroboration.**

## 16. ⚖️ What This Folder Does Not Claim

It does **not** claim all Russians support the war; nor that most secretly oppose it. It does **not** equate opposition to Putin with opposition to invading Ukraine. It does **not** classify all communists as anti-war or all relatives of mobilised soldiers as peace activists. It does **not** predict regime collapse from a protest, a budget line or an internet outage. It does **not** treat official silence as proof of consent, or censorship as proof of a hidden majority. It does **not** use Russian political dissent to obscure Ukrainian sovereignty, the invasion or responsibility for it.

It also does not treat the presence of a claim in Polaris as verification. The files are living investigations, and their documentary limits are part of the work. Readers should be able to disagree with an interpretation, correct a date or produce a better source without having to accept an entire political narrative as a package deal.

## 18. 🧾 How to Read and Maintain the Evidence

The quickest path is simple: encounter a claim in one of the eight thematic investigations; follow its source or event reference to [📚 Sources and Chronology](./📚_источники_и_хронология.md); check the original record, its date, translation, provenance and qualifications; return to the analysis. Where a claim lacks a securely established source, that absence should remain visible. An unknown number is `null`, not a plausible-looking estimate with its shoes polished.

New material should be distinguished as **new evidence**, **correction**, **reinterpretation**, **expansion** or **unresolved question**. A new source can establish an event; a correction can change a date; a reinterpretation can change what an event seems to mean without changing the event itself. If an October Moscow photograph is geolocated and dated, update the event record and every node relying on it. If the American sanctions instrument cannot be located or turns out to have a narrower scope, revise the economic interpretation accordingly. Do not silently leave an old confident sentence stranded in another file.

Original Russian-language wording should be preserved alongside translations. Legal cases should retain their procedural stages. Internet measurements should record network and observation time. Economic figures should carry units, periods and definitions. Where the record conflicts, preserve the conflict and open a discrepancy entry. **The point of a shared archive is that a correction can propagate without forcing every essay to become the same essay.**

## 19. 🏁 Russia Is Not a Single Political Opinion

Russia is a state. It is also a society. These are related things; they are not interchangeable. The Russian government has pursued a war against Ukraine. Russian citizens have supported it, opposed it, argued about it, endured its consequences and made choices under different conditions of power and risk. Parties have defended state policy. Others have challenged it. Some people have protested; some have been prosecuted; some have emigrated; some have remained. Some have political positions that do not fit the boxes offered by the government or by its most visible critics.

The existence of disagreement does not tell us its size. The presence of repression does not tell us that a government is about to fall. The existence of an opposition politician does not tell us what that politician believes about Ukraine. But official declarations of unity do not make the population politically homogeneous either.

To understand the system, we need institutions, material conditions, dissent, enforcement, communications and the evidence connecting them. We also need the documents. Particularly when somebody announces that the entire country agrees with them.

**A government is not a population. A political party is not a country. And the visibility of dissent is not the same thing as its existence.**
