# 📚 Источники и хронология
**First created:** 2026-10-10 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-10  
*Sources and Chronology — the documentary spine of the Russian political investigation, preserving the difference between an event, a record of the event, and an argument about its meaning.*

## 🛰️ Orientation — The Investigation Has Entered Its Bibliography Era

The Kremlin has an opinion. The communists have several. The opposition has opinions about the opposition. Roskomnadzor has an administrative interest in how opinions travel. And somewhere in this festival of paperwork, events actually happened. 📚 Unfortunately, we are going to need the dates.

This is the **shared documentary hub** for eight companion Polaris investigations. It is not an additional essay asserting that all eight drafts are correct. It is where their claims are identified, dated, compared and—when necessary—sent back to the queue. Repetition across drafts is not independent corroboration. A primary source establishes that somebody said or recorded something; it does not necessarily establish that their description of the world was accurate.

**Audit boundary:** This first edition was assembled from the eight locally available Polaris Markdown nodes and three locally available Dagestan/Baymak research files, supplemented by stable historical and institutional background. No live web verification was performed for this edition. Thus the historical outline is an *orientation*, not a completed event-by-event primary-source audit; source URLs reproduced from existing research retain their existing verification qualifications. No October 2026 Moscow protest event or 9 October 2026 US diesel-sanctions decision is promoted to fact by this document.

**Cut-off:** 10 October 2026. **Rule:** unknown fields stay `null`; contested facts stay contested; fiction stays labelled fiction.


## 1. 🗂️ The Documentary Job

The eight narrative nodes ask different questions. This register supplies a common account of **which records support which claims**, and how firmly. A protest photograph may inform a discussion of communist symbolism, repression, media filtering and the legitimacy of the state. That does not create four photographs or four independent sources. The original image receives one source ID, one event ID if its context is established, and as many analytical cross-references as are useful.

The operating units are **events**, **claims**, **sources**, **legal proceedings**, **media artefacts** and **discrepancies**. Events are not interpretations; claims are not sources; sources are not automatically true; legal charges are not convictions. The design allows an interpretation to change without erasing the record of the underlying action. Corrections are part of the record, not evidence that the project has failed.

The same applies to official sources. A ministry statement is excellent evidence that the ministry issued a statement. Whether the described event occurred, and in precisely the manner claimed, may require other sources. The archive has accepted the paperwork. The archive would also like to know what the paperwork proves.

## 3. 🧾 Evidence Is Not One Column

A document can be authentic yet misleading; an original photograph can be genuine but misdated; a court card can show a conviction without exposing the full operative sentence. Each record therefore needs separate fields for **authenticity**, **provenance**, **event date**, **publication date**, **corroboration**, **scope**, **confidence** and **interpretation**.

Use the following controlled vocabulary, with combinations permitted: `primary_document_verified`, `official_legal_text_verified`, `court_record_verified`, `original_statement_verified`, `original_media_verified`, `geolocation_verified`, `technical_measurement_verified`, `corroborated_reporting`, `single_source_reporting`, `first_person_testimony`, `official_claim`, `organiser_claim`, `company_claim`, `historical_scholarship`, `analytical_inference`, `previously_documented_in_polaris`, `unverified`, `disputed`, `contradicted`, `superseded`, `fictional_satire`.

**A crucial prohibition:** `previously_documented_in_polaris` is a provenance label, never a verification upgrade. Nor does `official_claim` mean false: it means that the record of the assertion and the truth of the assertion must be assessed separately. Confidence is claim-specific. A single article may support the date of an announcement strongly and the organiser's attendance estimate only as an attributed estimate.


## 4. 🔍 A Verification Ladder That Can Be Reversed

1. **Locate:** obtain the original source or identify exactly which secondary account currently carries the claim.
2. **Authenticate:** confirm the document, issuer, publication date and version where possible.
3. **Extract:** preserve original-language wording and units; record quotation boundaries.
4. **Contextualise:** establish what event the source describes and when that event happened.
5. **Corroborate:** seek genuinely independent material rather than syndicated repetition.
6. **Challenge:** record contradictions, methodological weaknesses and alternative explanations.
7. **Conclude:** state only the proposition the available evidence supports.
8. **Revise:** retain correction history when new records change the conclusion.

This is not a ritual by which everything eventually becomes verified. Some claims remain unresolvable. Some photographs cannot be reliably geolocated. Some judicial decisions are not publicly accessible. The correct output is sometimes **unknown**. It is still useful information.

## 6. 🪆 The 1990s and the Vertical of Power

The 1993 constitutional settlement, late-1990s succession and subsequent executive consolidation require individual legal and electoral anchors. A political metaphor such as “vertical of power” describes an analytical pattern, not one dated statute. The companion historical node should point to exact reforms in federal governance, appointments, party competition, media ownership and constitutional revision. Record **who acquired which authority**, through what instrument, and when. Do not write the future backwards as though the outcome of every earlier decision was predetermined.


## 7. 🪧 2011–2013 — Protest Meets Regulation

The 2011 State Duma election, subsequent demonstrations and changing public-assembly rules form a consequential sequence. Build event entries for each gathering rather than a single omnibus “protests” row. Attendance counts require attributed methods and ranges; police detention counts are not crowd sizes. Legal amendments require dates of passage, signature, commencement and application. The emergence of internet-blocking infrastructure belongs on the same timeline but should not be asserted to have been caused by a particular demonstration without documentary support.


## 8. 🇺🇦 2014–2021 — War, Opposition and Network Control

Russia’s war against Ukraine began in 2014; the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. The chronology must include Ukrainian documents and reporting rather than making Ukraine an object in Russian domestic history. Trace Russian anti-war statements, the 2014 peace marches, the subsequent treatment of opposition, the 2018 Telegram dispute, the 2019 sovereign-internet law and the 2020 constitutional amendments as separate evidence tracks. An anti-war statement in 2014 is not automatically evidence of an unchanged position in 2022 or 2026.


## 9. 🪖 2022 — Full-Scale Invasion and Speech Restrictions

Build daily or weekly event rows for the invasion, first public anti-war actions, major legal changes, media blocking and mobilisation. For legislation, record the exact Criminal Code or Administrative Code provision and operative wording at the relevant time. For protest, preserve participant testimony, independent reporting, original footage and the relevant legal outcomes. A detention, charge, conviction, appeal and custodial status are different procedural facts. Equally, “against the war”, “for peace” and “for negotiations” must not be silently translated into one another.


## 10. ⛓️‍💥 2023–2024 — Case-Level Repression Records

The locally available Dagestan/Baymak audit is a model for disciplined separation of court records from reporting. One Dagestan court record identifies Kemran Nadirovich Agabekov’s case `1-275/2023`, conviction dated **1 February 2023**, and Criminal Code Article 318(1); the accessible court extract did not independently establish the fine reported by OVD-Info. For the Orsk group in the Baymak proceedings, the audit records a first-instance judgment on **26 February 2025** and an appeal on **6 June 2025**; appellate outcomes differed between defendants. These are findings **as documented in the supplied local audit**, not a fresh court-site inspection in this edition. Preserve names, case numbers, appeal history and the audit’s access limitations. Do not merge all defendants into one procedural fate.


## 11. 📲 2025–2026 — Internet Interference Is Not One Event

Roskomnadzor, courts, prosecutors, security bodies and network operators perform different roles. A regulatory decision is not a measurement; a measurement is not an attribution of responsibility. For every reported block, throttle or outage, capture the network, location, observation window, method, affected service, official rationale and competing explanations. The same service may behave differently across providers. A state can increase its ability to filter traffic without becoming omniscient about encrypted content or private political belief.


## 12. 🐻 2026 — Economy, Opposition and Wartime Incentives

Use dated Russian budget, central-bank and statistical publications for claims about inflation, military spending, public finance and household pressures. Specify whether figures are nominal or real, budgeted or spent, revised or preliminary. Economic strain does not prove that a government was about to change policy; opposition activity does not prove an economic cause. Treat links between sanctions, protest and negotiation as hypotheses to test against a timeline of what officials could have known at each decision point.


## 13. 📣 Moscow, October 2026 — The High-Priority Open Case

The companion Moscow node does **not** independently establish a verified demonstration chronology. The present record therefore has no justified precise protest dates, organisers, slogans, numbers arrested or confirmed photograph locations. Search first for original organiser announcements and contemporaneous reporting; then independent imagery and any police or court records. Determine whether there was one event or several. Red flags alone cannot prove party membership, anti-war demands or even the date and city of a photograph. Until this work is done, keep `RUS-EVT-2026-MOSCOW-OPEN` a **provisional investigation identifier**, not an event confirmed to have occurred.


## 14. 🛢️ October 2026 — The Diesel / Sanctions Claim

The companion economic node discusses a reported American sanctions authorisation dated 9 October 2026. This audit has not inspected a corresponding original US Treasury/OFAC legal instrument. Therefore **licence number, legal scope, dates, cargo volumes, completed purchases and negotiation effects are not established here**. First locate the instrument; then separate permission to transact from an actual purchase, shipping movement, payment and strategic benefit. Even a real authorisation would not, by itself, establish that it caused a Russian political or diplomatic response.


## 15. 🇷🇺 Original Language, Translation and Political Meaning

Store the Russian original before its translation. `Против войны` means “against the war”; `За мир` means “for peace”; `Переговоры` means “negotiations”; `Вывод войск` means “withdrawal of troops”. These are not interchangeable political programmes. `СВО` is an official Russian abbreviation for the government’s designation of its military campaign; translation should not uncritically adopt the state’s framing. Preserve speaker, date, context, punctuation and whether the text is a direct quotation or a paraphrase.


## 16. 🇺🇦 Ukrainian Evidence and Agency

Russian statements about Ukraine document Russian positions; they do not substitute for Ukrainian records. The master source register needs Ukrainian reporting, institutional documents, human-rights evidence and Ukrainian commentary on Russian anti-war politics. “Peace” cannot be evaluated solely as a dispute among Russian political factions. Ukraine’s territorial sovereignty, the experiences of affected people and the terms Ukraine itself articulates are indispensable to any responsible analysis.


## 17. ⚖️ Legal Procedure Is a Timeline, Not a Verdict-Shaped Blob

Each case should separately record incident, detention, administrative action, charge, trial, judgment, appeal, cassation and later status. A court case card can support a date and disposition without exposing every detail of a sentence. An indexed extract may show a court-published act without providing an authenticated scan. Preserve these differences explicitly. Never infer present incarceration from an old conviction, and never turn a reported fine into a court-verified amount if the accessible operative decision does not show it.


## 18. 🧪 Technical Measurements and Attribution

OONI measurements, RIPE Atlas probes, platform disclosures, operator notices and regulatory orders are different evidence classes. Record ASN, location, resolver configuration, test time, failure mode, expected control and alternative causes. An inaccessible site may be blocked, misconfigured, offline, restricted by the platform or unreachable for other reasons. Network interference can be well evidenced without the precise government instruction being publicly visible. The responsible statement is then “measured interference consistent with…”, not an invented named order.


## 19. 📊 Numbers Need Units and Denominators

For every quantitative claim preserve unit, geography, period, measurement method, source publication date and revision status. Protest crowd estimates are not detention totals. Poll support is not identical to private belief. A Russian federal budget allocation is not cash already spent. A sanctions licence is not a cargo manifest. A count of court cases is not a count of all people deterred from speaking. The easiest way to produce a convincing false narrative is to place several accurate numbers next to one another while quietly changing what they measure.


## 20. 🧷 Master Claim Register

A claim gets a stable ID even if it is false or unverified. Link to the source IDs that actually bear on it, plus contrary records and affected nodes. A single primary source can support several claims; several companion nodes citing that one source are still only one source. A correction should change the status of the claim, not erase the fact that an earlier draft contained it.


## 21. 🗓️ Master Event Register

An event is a dated occurrence, not a theory about its significance. Separate `claimed_date`, `verified_start`, `verified_end`, `location`, `actors`, `associated_claim_ids` and `source_ids`. Do not create a confirmed event from a photograph with an unverified caption. Where one demonstration produces several criminal proceedings, keep the protest event and the legal proceedings as related but distinct records.


## 22. 🔗 Master Source Register

A source record should retain original title, translation, issuer, date, type, URL, archival route, accessed date, language, version and limitations. A URL is a route to evidence, not its entire identity. A source may disappear, change its wording or be replaced; preserve enough metadata to identify the edition that was consulted. Mark original documents, indexed extracts, contemporary reporting and retrospective scholarship distinctly.


## 23. 📸 Images, Videos and the Problem of Captions

An image has a publisher, upload date, possible capture date, possible location, visible content and a claim made about it. Those are separate fields. Geolocation can be supported by landmarks; timing may require independent context; visible flags can establish symbolism without establishing the affiliations or intentions of every person in frame. The image is evidence. The caption is a claim. The photograph cannot establish what happened outside its frame.


## 24. 🧠 Chronology Is a Cybernetic Instrument

Political decisions occur under unequal and changing information. An event happens; a witness records it; publication may be delayed or restricted; institutions receive different versions; decisions generate further events. Retrospective investigators possess information actors did not have at the time. The chronology therefore needs an **information-availability date**, not merely an event date. This is essential when analysing sanctions, protests, wartime bargaining and state censorship. The archive must not lend the past knowledge it acquired later.


## 25. 🧨 Contradictions Are Research Tasks

Where sources disagree, record both accounts, the type of conflict and what could resolve it. Different protest estimates may reflect different counting windows. A court card and a journalist may describe different procedural stages. A translation may strengthen a demand beyond the Russian wording. An official statement may have been amended. A contradiction is not permission to choose the version that makes the funniest paragraph. The paragraph can wait; the evidence cannot.


## 26. 🚨 Priority Verification Queue

**Priority A:** October Moscow events and original media; the reported October US sanctions/diesel instrument; dated Russian statements about war termination and negotiations. **Priority B:** case-level Dagestan/Baymak judicial outcomes, anti-war prosecutions, party discipline and Roskomnadzor decisions. **Priority C:** historical electoral/protest anchors, organisational histories and longitudinal economic data. Order by downstream impact: a single weak claim underpinning four nodes deserves attention before a decorative detail with no analytical consequences.


## 27. 🧭 Citation Routing and Maintenance

Use stable IDs in this document and relative Markdown links from the eight companion nodes. Cross-references help readers navigate; they do not turn Polaris into an independent witness to itself. On correction, update the source/claim/event record first, identify all `appears_in_nodes` entries, then revise affected prose. Record the change date and what was altered. If the claim is downgraded, downgrade every dependent conclusion or explicitly explain why the conclusion still stands.


## 28. 📚 Bibliography as Infrastructure

The bibliography should be grouped into Russian official law and courts, Russian independent reporting, political organisations, Ukrainian sources, international instruments, technical measurement and historical scholarship. Use original publication titles and live, checked document links when available. Where only an institutional portal is presently identified, label it a **research entry point**, not a citation proving a specific incident. Never fabricate a deep link, page title, licence number or case outcome to make the bibliography look complete.

## 30. 🏁 Conclusion — The Dates Are Not Decorative

A government statement is not necessarily a fact. A photograph is not necessarily evidence of the event named in its caption. A protest is not necessarily a revolution. A sanctions authorisation is not a completed transaction. A charge is not a conviction. An opposition politician is not necessarily opposed to the war. These distinctions are administratively tiresome. They are also the difference between investigating political power and writing another story about it. **The dates are not decorative. The documents are not optional. And the people whose actions we describe deserve better than being rearranged to fit an argument.**
