# 🔊 Turn The Public Up  
**First created:** 2026-07-26 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-26  
*Public-facing studies of how people create, carry, contest and preserve political meaning under pressure.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

🔊 **Turn The Public Up** is a cluster within 📲 Press Matters examining the public as an active participant in the information environment rather than a passive audience waiting to be informed.

This folder tracks how people:

* witness events;
* produce and preserve evidence;
* report locally;
* translate between languages and political contexts;
* recognise patterns;
* circulate neglected stories;
* challenge imposed descriptions;
* use humour, music, slogans, images and public culture;
* keep information alive after institutions lose interest;
* turn isolated facts into shared political knowledge.

It also examines the systems that determine whose voices can be heard, trusted, circulated and remembered.

These may include:

* journalism and media ownership;
* policing and legal pressure;
* state and private advertising;
* licensing and accreditation;
* internet shutdowns;
* platform governance;
* algorithmic visibility;
* language hierarchies;
* source intimidation;
* political and communal mobilisation;
* narrative substitution;
* archive loss;
* the economic conditions under which reporting can survive.

The folder is not based upon the assumption that publics are always correct, unified or democratic.

People can circulate truth, testimony, correction and political imagination.

They can also circulate rumour, propaganda, hostility, manipulated material and organised confusion.

The relevant question is not whether the public is naturally virtuous.

It is:

**Who is able to participate in making public reality, and under what conditions can their contribution become audible?**

## 🪩 The Public Is Not Merely An Audience

A functioning public sphere requires more than the formal existence of newspapers, broadcasters or social-media accounts.

A fact may be technically published while remaining:

* difficult to find;
* unsafe to repeat;
* unavailable in the language people use;
* stripped of its political context;
* overwhelmed by competing material;
* treated as socially contaminating;
* economically impossible to investigate;
* detached from the people most affected;
* deleted or forgotten before it enters public memory.

People may give that fact political life by:

* filming it;
* naming it;
* translating it;
* connecting it to earlier events;
* correcting a misleading account;
* placing it in local context;
* preserving disappearing material;
* making it funny enough to travel;
* making it human enough to matter;
* refusing the official vocabulary supplied for it.

The public does not simply receive the record.

The public helps make the record possible.

## 🧭 Drafting Approach

Nodes in this cluster should distinguish between:

* documented conduct;
* available capability;
* structural dependence;
* political alignment;
* institutional convergence;
* reasonable inference;
* proven coordination;
* unresolved allegation.

They should identify specific actors rather than collapsing:

* a country into its government;
* a government into one leader;
* the press into its largest outlets;
* media ownership into automatic editorial command;
* public circulation into organised political coordination;
* criticism into proof of a fully open information environment;
* information control into one central censorship machine.

Country studies should remain country-facing for accessibility and actor-specific inside the analysis.

Case studies should follow the information pathway:

1. What happened?
2. Who witnessed or documented it?
3. Who attempted to report it?
4. What allowed or obstructed publication?
5. How did the story circulate?
6. Which descriptions competed to define it?
7. What happened to the people carrying it?
8. What entered the durable public record?
9. What remains disputed, missing or unproven?

The smallest supportable claim comes first.

The public can be turned up without the evidence being turned into noise.

## 🔭 Working Principle

Public knowledge is not created merely because a fact is true or because somebody has placed it online.

It is created through the people, institutions, relationships and infrastructures that allow the fact to be:

* discovered;
* described;
* verified;
* translated;
* published;
* circulated;
* understood;
* contested;
* preserved;
* remembered.

Power may control licences, cables, advertising, platforms, access or official briefings.

It does not automatically own what people hear in the noise.

Turn the public up.
