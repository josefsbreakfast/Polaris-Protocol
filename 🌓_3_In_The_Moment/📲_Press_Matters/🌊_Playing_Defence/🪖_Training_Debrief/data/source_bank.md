## 🧭 What This File Is

This is not a claim to exhaustive coverage.

It records:

- sources already used;
- sources identified for retrieval;
- primary documents underlying secondary accounts;
- contemporary reporting;
- parliamentary and audit material;
- academic and professional literature;
- historical defence-policy material;
- cultural sources used for interpretation rather than factual substantiation;
- machine-readable search instructions intended to support repeatable further research.

Sources should be distinguished by what they can actually establish.

A contemporary newspaper report, a parliamentary answer, an MOD publication, an NAO report, a retrospective interview, an academic study and a work of cultural interpretation are not interchangeable forms of evidence.

## 🤖 Open Admission: Machine-Assisted Research

Parts of the research supporting this cluster are being conducted using machine-assisted search, retrieval, classification and synthesis.

The machine-readable search text included below is deliberately designed to permit repeated searches by different capable systems.

This creates advantages:

- broader retrieval;
- repeatability;
- easier identification of missing source classes;
- easier comparison between searches conducted at different times;
- preservation of the questions that generated the research.

It also creates limitations.

Search results are shaped by:

- the wording of the query;
- search-engine and database indexing;
- ranking systems;
- publication accessibility;
- digitisation;
- language;
- date coverage;
- model/tool behaviour;
- the source ecosystem available to the searching system.

The resulting corpus must therefore not be treated as a neutral or exhaustive sample of everything written about British defence.

**Search is part of the methodology and therefore part of the evidence trail.**

Machine retrieval does not convert a secondary source into a primary source, establish the truth of a claim merely because several publications repeat it, or remove the need to distinguish evidence from interpretation.

Where practicable, important claims should be checked against the strongest available underlying source.

## 🧼 Source Hygiene

Before a source is promoted into the working evidence base, ask:

1. What exactly can this source establish?
2. Is it primary, secondary, retrospective or interpretive?
3. Is the wording contemporaneous with the event?
4. Does it rely on named evidence, anonymous sourcing or another publication?
5. Can the underlying source be retrieved?
6. Is the source independent of other items repeating the same claim?
7. What uncertainty or limitation should travel with it?
8. Which node or data file actually needs it?

The point is not to eliminate secondary sources.

It is to stop source quantity masquerading as evidential independence.
