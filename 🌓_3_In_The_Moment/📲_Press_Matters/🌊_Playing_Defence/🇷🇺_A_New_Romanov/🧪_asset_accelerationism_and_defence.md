# 🧪_asset_accelerationism_and_defence.md

- Purpose:
  - apply the existing Polaris asset-accelerationism concept to defence and national-security infrastructure;
  - study how genuine threats can accelerate ownership concentration and dependency.

- Core sequence:

  external threat
      ↓
  urgent strategic requirement
      ↓
  accelerated procurement / investment
      ↓
  private provider becomes increasingly essential
      ↓
  state dependency increases
      ↓
  provider itself requires protection
      ↓
  provider becomes increasingly integrated into state capability

- Important:
  - the system can accelerate because:
    - threat is real;
    - procurement is urgent;
    - existing large providers are easiest to use;
    - government cannot build alternatives quickly.

- No conspiracy required.

- Asset-accelerationism questions:
  - which firms gain strategic assets during crisis?
  - which actors acquire:
    - data;
    - infrastructure;
    - contracts;
    - influence;
    - market position?
  - does the emergency reduce competition?
  - does urgency favour incumbents?
  - do temporary acquisitions become permanent?
  - does public money increase private strategic power?

- Feedback loop:
  - state needs provider;
  - provider grows because state needs it;
  - provider becomes more important;
  - state becomes less able to lose provider;
  - provider receives more protection / access;
  - dependency compounds.

- Defence-specific examples to watch:
  - cloud;
  - AI;
  - cyber;
  - satellites;
  - logistics;
  - data centres;
  - defence manufacturing;
  - surveillance;
  - communications.

- Key distinction:
  - intentional exploitation
  versus
  - emergent path dependence.

- Avoid:
  - inferring intent from outcome.

- Link to Russia:
  - sustained confrontation itself may reshape Western economic and infrastructure power.

- Strong working line:
  > Threat can accelerate assets into strategic importance faster than governance frameworks can catch up.
