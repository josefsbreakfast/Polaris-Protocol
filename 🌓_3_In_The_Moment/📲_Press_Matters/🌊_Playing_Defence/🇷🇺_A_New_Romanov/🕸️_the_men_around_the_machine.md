# 🕸️_the_men_around_the_machine.md

- Purpose:
  - use selected influential men as probes into different surfaces of modern political, technological, security, and infrastructure power;
  - avoid turning them into a single network or single political project.

- Core principle:
  > The men are not "the machine". They are useful because their networks expose different parts of it.

- Sampling nodes:

### Ehud Barak
- useful surfaces:
  - political authority;
  - defence;
  - security;
  - intelligence-adjacent networks;
  - security technology;
  - venture capital;
  - international elite networks.

### Steve Bannon
- useful surfaces:
  - political mobilisation;
  - media;
  - populist movements;
  - ideological networks;
  - US–European political translation;
  - Russia-facing ideological relationships.

### Larry Ellison
- useful surfaces:
  - Oracle;
  - capital;
  - cloud;
  - data;
  - compute;
  - AI;
  - government systems;
  - defence infrastructure;
  - Tony Blair / TBI ecosystem.

### Tony Blair
- useful surfaces:
  - UK politics;
  - international policy;
  - TBI;
  - government access;
  - technology-driven state modernisation;
  - policy networks;
  - relationship with Ellison ecosystem.

- Important:
  - not equivalent actors;
  - not one organisation;
  - not necessarily coordinating;
  - not necessarily sharing a Russia policy;
  - not necessarily involved in wrongdoing.

- Why these men matter:
  - they sit at junctions between:
    - politics;
    - capital;
    - technology;
    - security;
    - media;
    - data;
    - infrastructure;
    - state policy.

- Network position → epistemic position:

  defence:
  - readiness;
  - force generation.

  intelligence:
  - intent;
  - access;
  - signalling channels.

  technology:
  - infrastructure;
  - compute;
  - data.

  capital:
  - investment;
  - exposure;
  - market opportunity.

  political strategy:
  - voters;
  - coalition formation;
  - public tolerance.

  diplomacy:
  - bargaining;
  - recognition.

  history / culture:
  - prestige;
  - status;
  - humiliation;
  - political mythology.

- Core problem:
  > Intelligent people can be locally correct and globally incomplete.

- Research question:
  - what does each network position make easier to see?
  - what does it make easier to miss?

- Russia relevance:
  - a competent state actor may map:
    - who owns capability;
    - who influences policy;
    - who can change political cost;
    - who controls information infrastructure;
    - who can move capital.

- Important:
  - none of that requires direct communication with Moscow.

- Strong working line:
  > Power networks matter not because everyone in them agrees, but because different actors control different levers of the same system.
