# 💸_emergency_is_a_market_condition.md

- Purpose:
  - examine how genuine crises alter commercial incentives, procurement, political access, and market concentration;
  - distinguish crisis exploitation from crisis manufacture.

- Central distinction:

  NOT:
  > These actors created the emergency to profit from it.

  POSSIBLE / OBSERVABLE:
  > Genuine emergencies alter demand and power, and actors may respond opportunistically.

- Emergencies can change:
  - demand;
  - procurement speed;
  - acceptable cost;
  - regulatory tolerance;
  - access to decision-makers;
  - strategic importance of existing assets;
  - government dependence on particular suppliers.

- Questions:
  - who already owns the capability suddenly required?
  - who becomes indispensable?
  - who gets accelerated procurement?
  - who gains government access?
  - who receives emergency exemptions?
  - which contracts persist?
  - which temporary arrangements become permanent?
  - which firms become "too important" to lose?

- Possible mechanisms:
  - explicit opportunism;
  - ordinary market behaviour;
  - genuine emergency need;
  - procurement inertia;
  - institutional panic;
  - path dependence;
  - combinations of all of them.

- Link to elite private communications:
  - private messages may sometimes reveal more openly opportunistic attitudes toward:
    - crises;
    - instability;
    - political access;
    - market opportunities.
  - use only where evidence supports the claim.

- Link to defence:
  - security emergencies can radically accelerate:
    - cloud spending;
    - defence tech;
    - cyber procurement;
    - AI infrastructure;
    - surveillance capability;
    - resilience contracts.

- Link to governance:
  - emergency procurement can produce durable structural dependencies.

- Important:
  - a company benefiting from crisis does not prove:
    - it caused the crisis;
    - it supports the crisis;
    - its product is unnecessary.

- Strong working line:
  > Emergency changes the market before anyone needs to invent a conspiracy.
