# ⚖️_emergency_power_and_governance.md

- Purpose:
  - separate legitimate national resilience from opportunistic, excessive, or path-dependent expansion of executive power;
  - examine what happens when strategic private infrastructure becomes subject to emergency state control.

- Core baseline:
  - genuine external threats can exist.
  - emergency powers can be necessary.
  - neither fact removes the need for governance.

- Current UK relevance:
  - stronger critical-infrastructure regulation;
  - national-security direction powers;
  - subsea-cable emergency measures;
  - state-threat legislation;
  - data-centre CNI status;
  - wider cyber-resilience powers.

- Central question:
  > When a real external threat intersects with a pre-existing state desire for greater control over strategic private infrastructure, how do we distinguish necessary resilience from durable expansion of power?

- Governance requirements:
  - necessity;
  - proportionality;
  - clear legal basis;
  - oversight;
  - accountability;
  - review;
  - transparency where possible;
  - expiry / sunset where appropriate;
  - protection against mission creep.

- Emergency-power problem:
  - temporary necessity can become permanent administrative normality.

- Possible sequence:

  emergency
      ↓
  extraordinary authority
      ↓
  new institutional relationship
      ↓
  operational dependence
      ↓
  normalisation
      ↓
  permanent governance change

- Questions:
  - who can direct whom?
  - what threshold activates power?
  - who reviews that threshold?
  - what information can remain secret?
  - what happens when the emergency ends?
  - can the power be repurposed?
  - does commercial secrecy reduce accountability?
  - does national-security secrecy reduce parliamentary visibility?

- Private-sector side:
  - firms may gain:
    - contracts;
    - strategic status;
    - protection;
    - government access.
  - they may also face:
    - state direction;
    - mandatory resilience duties;
    - constrained autonomy.

- Therefore:
  - emergency integration can increase both:
    - corporate power;
    AND
    - state power over corporations.

- Important:
  - do not assume expansion is malicious.
  - institutional incentives and path dependence can produce durable power shifts without deliberate abuse.

- Core Polaris question:
  > What powers would we accept during a real emergency that we would not want quietly becoming the normal architecture of government?

- Strong working line:
  > A real threat can justify emergency action without making every emergency power wise, proportionate, or temporary.
