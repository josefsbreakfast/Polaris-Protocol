# 🌐_when_private_infrastructure_becomes_state_power.md

- Purpose:
  - bridge surveillance-capitalism analysis directly into defence and foreign policy;
  - ask when private capability becomes functionally inseparable from national power.

- Core convergence:

  Western state:
  > This private infrastructure is essential to national security.

  potential adversary:
  > Then this infrastructure is relevant to national power.

- Possible infrastructure:
  - cloud;
  - data centres;
  - telecommunications;
  - subsea cables;
  - satellites;
  - ports;
  - logistics;
  - energy;
  - defence contractors;
  - AI infrastructure;
  - finance;
  - software.

- Main conceptual question:
  > What does Russia actually think "Britain" consists of?

- Not merely:
  - Downing Street;
  - MOD;
  - RAF bases;
  - Parliament.

- Potentially:
  - the entire capability ecosystem that permits Britain to:
    - communicate;
    - deploy;
    - calculate;
    - finance;
    - store;
    - coordinate;
    - defend;
    - attack.

- Private / public mismatch:
  - domestic governance may classify a company as commercial;
  - military planning may depend on it;
  - adversary planning may therefore classify it as strategically relevant.

- Questions:
  - when does a contractor become part of effective state capability?
  - when does a cloud environment become defence infrastructure?
  - when does a data centre become strategic terrain?
  - when does dependency convert a commercial asset into a security asset?

- Important:
  - strategic relevance does not automatically make civilian infrastructure a lawful military target.
  - legal targeting questions remain separate.

- Wider policy issue:
  - if governments intentionally integrate private infrastructure into core state capability,
  - governance needs to recognise the security consequences.

- Link to data-barons node:
  - informational concentration.

- Link to data-state node:
  - UK implementation.

- Link to emergency powers:
  - government may seek greater authority over infrastructure precisely because it has become strategically essential.

- Strong working line:
  > The public/private line can remain legally sharp while becoming operationally blurry.
