# 🪩_where_the_romanov_analogy_breaks.md

- Purpose:
  - stop the cluster becoming “Putin = Tsar” cosplay;
  - establish the limits and failure conditions of the Romanov frame.

- Core baseline:
  - Russia is not Imperial Russia.
  - Putin is not a Romanov.
  - Russians are not subjects of hereditary monarchy.
  - the institutional state is radically different.
  - capitalism, technology, mass media, nuclear weapons and contemporary bureaucracy change the operating environment completely.

- Major breaks:
  - Soviet political experience cannot be removed from modern Russia.
  - KGB / FSB institutional inheritance is not Romanov statecraft.
  - modern nationalism ≠ monarchism.
  - Orthodoxy ≠ restoration of autocracy.
  - imperial symbolism can be politically useful without constitutional restoration.
  - contemporary Russian public opinion places hard limits on how literal any imperial revival can become.

- Do not romanticise Romanov history:
  - autocracy;
  - inequality;
  - failed reform;
  - reaction;
  - court politics;
  - military defeat;
  - revolution;
  - Nicholas II;
  - dynastic collapse;
  - execution of the imperial family.

- Important research question:
  > Which Romanovs are foregrounded, which are rehabilitated, which are sanitised, and which are quietly left alone?

- Why selective memory matters:
  - selection tells us which political functions the history is serving.
  - glorifying Peter but not Nicholas II is not neutral.
  - celebrating empire while suppressing collapse is analytically meaningful.

- Test against overreach:
  - if a current behaviour is better explained by:
    - Soviet institutions;
    - contemporary nationalism;
    - security-service culture;
    - ordinary geopolitics;
    then do not force it into the Romanov frame.

- Strong working line:
  > The question is not whether the analogy is true. The question is where it illuminates, where it distorts, and where another Russian past is doing the actual work.
