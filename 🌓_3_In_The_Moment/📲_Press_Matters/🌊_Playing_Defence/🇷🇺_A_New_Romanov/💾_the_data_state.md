# 💾_the_data_state.md

- Purpose:
  - take the broad "data barons" problem and apply it specifically to the UK state;
  - examine how data, cloud, compute, health systems, defence systems, and critical infrastructure are being integrated into national-security thinking.

- Core areas:
  - data sovereignty;
  - sovereign cloud;
  - data centres;
  - MOD cloud;
  - NHS infrastructure;
  - government compute;
  - AI capacity;
  - communications;
  - critical national infrastructure;
  - emergency interoperability.

- Existing UK evidence:
  - data centres designated Critical National Infrastructure in 2024;
  - government explicitly links them to:
    - public services;
    - economic security;
    - national security;
    - AI infrastructure.

- Cyber Security and Resilience framework:
  - data centres increasingly treated as part of national-security architecture;
  - proposed ministerial direction powers relevant during imminent or live national-security threats.

- Key question:
  > At what point does "government using private infrastructure" become "private infrastructure functioning as part of the state"?

- MOD / cloud angle:
  - Oracle / MOD collaboration demonstrates:
    - commercial cloud;
    - defence data;
    - AI;
    - operational effectiveness;
    - national-security dependency
    becoming part of one infrastructure story.

- NHS / civilian data angle:
  - publicly stated separation between civilian and military systems does not by itself answer:
    - emergency interoperability;
    - emergency legal powers;
    - technical compatibility;
    - contingency arrangements.

- Important epistemic rule:
  - absence of a public prohibition ≠ evidence integration occurs.
  - absence of public detail ≠ evidence integration cannot occur.

- Research questions:
  - what is legally possible?
  - what is technically possible?
  - what has been exercised?
  - what requires ministerial authority?
  - what oversight exists?
  - what emergency powers apply?
  - what is publicly documented?

- Wider sovereignty question:
  - if government depends on:
    - foreign-owned cloud;
    - private compute;
    - commercial data infrastructure;
    - private AI capability
    for core state functions,
  - what does "sovereign" actually mean?

- Link to defence:
  - state capability may increasingly depend on systems the state does not own.

- Link to adversary analysis:
  - Russia may not care whether the capability is legally public or private.
  - it may care whether the capability helps Britain act.

- Strong working line:
  > The data state may be partly public, partly private, and strategically inseparable long before the law has caught up with the category.
