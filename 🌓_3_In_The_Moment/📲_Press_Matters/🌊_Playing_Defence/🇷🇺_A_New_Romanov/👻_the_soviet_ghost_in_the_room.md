# 👻_the_soviet_ghost_in_the_room.md

- Purpose:
  - prevent the Romanov thesis from swallowing the Soviet inheritance;
  - identify where current Russian messaging is more Soviet than imperial.

- Core proposition:
  - contemporary Russia may be using multiple historical repertoires at once.
  - Romanov memory and Soviet memory are not mutually exclusive.

- Soviet themes to track:
  - encirclement;
  - hostile external blocs;
  - strategic depth;
  - endurance;
  - sacrifice;
  - mobilisation;
  - industrial capacity;
  - security-state legitimacy;
  - intelligence culture;
  - propaganda;
  - information operations;
  - superpower status;
  - Great Patriotic War;
  - anti-fascist legitimacy;
  - strategic patience;
  - suspicion of Western intention.

- Sub-periods to distinguish:
  - revolutionary / early Soviet;
  - Stalinist;
  - post-Stalin;
  - Brezhnev-era;
  - late-Soviet;
  - intelligence-service institutional culture.

- Key questions:
  - where does current rhetoric look:
    - Romanov?
    - Soviet?
    - specifically late-Soviet?
  - when does “great-power recognition” sound imperial?
  - when does “hostile encirclement” sound Soviet?
  - when do both appear in the same message?

- KGB relevance:
  - intelligence-service professional culture should sit here as part of Soviet inheritance;
  - especially:
    - influence;
    - audience segmentation;
    - sustained communication;
    - perception management;
    - intermediaries;
    - behavioural effect.

- Important distinction:
  - “KGB = PR” is shorthand only.
  - serious theme:
    - influential communication as an operational tool.

- Strong working line:
  > If the Romanovs supply prestige and continuity, the Soviet inheritance may supply endurance, encirclement, security-state competence and the grammar of confrontation.
