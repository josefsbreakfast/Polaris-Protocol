# 🥣_the_weetabix_test.md

- Purpose:
  - create a deliberately mundane household-level diagnostic for national-security policy;
  - prevent grand strategy becoming detached from the civilian life it is supposed to secure.

- Core line:
  > I would like cheaper Weetabix.

- Meaning:
  - security should eventually produce:
    - lower risk;
    - fewer deaths;
    - functioning trade;
    - lower economic volatility;
    - greater fiscal room;
    - ordinary life becoming easier to sustain.

- Foreign policy eventually arrives as:
  - food prices;
  - energy bills;
  - freight;
  - insurance;
  - taxes;
  - public spending;
  - service availability.

- Desired security outputs:
  - fewer people dying;
  - lower probability of war;
  - stable borders;
  - resilient infrastructure;
  - functioning trade;
  - manageable energy costs;
  - manageable food costs;
  - lower fiscal pressure.

- Diagnostic:
  - if policy produces indefinitely:
    - more weapons;
    - more emergency powers;
    - more securitised infrastructure;
    - more defence spending;
    - more confrontation;
  - but cannot describe:
    - what peace looks like;
    - what de-escalation looks like;
    - what ordinary people gain,
  - then ask:
    > What is the system actually optimising for?

- Existing contrast:
  > This is not cheaper Weetabix.

- Use carefully:
  - not to trivialise war;
  - to force abstract security claims back into material consequence.

- Link to grain / Black Sea:
  - de-escalation can plausibly affect:
    - commodity expectations;
    - shipping;
    - insurance;
    - household food pressure.

- Wider use:
  - could also apply to:
    - energy;
    - defence taxation;
    - civilian service spending;
    - infrastructure resilience.

- Strong working line:
  > If security never becomes visible in ordinary life except as higher costs and greater emergency authority, the policy definition of "security" deserves scrutiny.
