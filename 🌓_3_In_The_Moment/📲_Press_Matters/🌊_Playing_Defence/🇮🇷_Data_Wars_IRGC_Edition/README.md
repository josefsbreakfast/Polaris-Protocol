# 🇮🇷 Data Wars: IRGC Edition
**First created:** 2026-08-01 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*Tracking essential-infrastructure cyber disruption across the widening Iran war without turning wartime proximity, actor branding, or political convenience into proof.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This pack tracks cyber incidents affecting the systems through which states and ordinary life continue to function during the Iran war.

That means more than defence networks.

It includes water, energy, health, education, government administration, policing and justice systems, transport, logistics, telecommunications, banking, payments, public-safety systems, and the contracted infrastructure that keeps those functions operating.

The war did not begin in cyberspace.

It does not remain neatly outside it either.

Iranian and Iran-linked cyber activity can be decentralised, deniable, and layered through state units, affiliated groups, proxies, contractors, criminal operators, access brokers, hacktivist branding, public amplifiers, and people who may not know who ultimately benefits from the task they have been given.

But the same wartime attack surface also contains ordinary ransomware, extortion, shared-vulnerability waves, copycats, unrelated hostile-state activity, opportunistic crime, and incidents whose operator may never become public.

The pack therefore refuses two opposite mistakes:

- attributing every badly timed breach to Iran;
- treating every unattributed breach as strategically meaningless.

Its governing discipline is:

```text
preserve the incident
+
preserve the effect
+
preserve the uncertainty
+
preserve the pattern
```

No single field is allowed to swallow the others.

## 🧭 What The Pack Contains

The pack has three layers.

### The Evidence Spine

The timeline records incidents, clusters, attribution developments, context events, operational effects, rival explanations, source provenance, review history, and correction status.

The CSV is the canonical portable dataset.

The XLSX reproduces the same 31 records and 40 fields, with a formula-driven summary, Pattern Watch, and Method & Scope sheets.

As at 20 August 2026, the register contains 16 Core records, 3 Adjacent records, and 12 Context records. Those categories describe evidentiary routing, not seriousness, authorship, or legal status.

### The Analytical Layer

The prose nodes explain how an event enters the pack, what different kinds of infrastructure mean, how operational technology changes the risk, how several threat ecosystems can coexist, and why identifying an operator does not automatically identify a customer or state director.

### The Public And Governance Layer

The reporting, legal, British-governance, and American-effects nodes explain what different audiences can responsibly say and what different publics, institutions, allies, sectors, and political coalitions may experience.

The result is not merely an incident list.

It is a method for keeping a live wartime evidence system from becoming either propaganda or amnesia.

## 🚦 Four Routing Outcomes

Not every relevant development belongs in the same evidentiary category.

### Core

An actual cyber incident affecting an essential function, system, or operational dependency.

### Adjacent

A strategically relevant event that does not yet clear the threshold for a core essential-infrastructure incident.

A warehouse disruption may belong here where shipment delays are established but port control, strategic cargo, or essential public-function effects are not.

### Context

An attribution, threat, policy, rhetoric, confidence, legal, or stakeholder development that changes how incidents are interpreted without being a new cyber incident itself.

The reported White House explanation that the Strait of Hormuz territorial remark was a joke belongs here. It weakens the proposition that a developed annexation policy had been announced. It does not erase the original rhetoric, establish a presidential retraction, or remove the wider coercive and sovereignty effects created by saying it publicly.

### Excluded

A duplicate, false claim, unsupported item, irrelevant event, or source chain that cannot sustain the proposed description.

```text
relevant enough to watch
≠
strong enough to count

strong enough to count
≠
strong enough to attribute
```

## 🧩 Five Linked Record Objects

The pack tracks five objects that may share a date without being forced into one row.

```text
INCIDENT
what happened to one system or organisation

CLUSTER
what recurs across several incidents

ATTRIBUTION DEVELOPMENT
how operator, affiliation, direction and confidence change

CONTEXT DEVELOPMENT
how threats, rhetoric, basing, policy or adversary response change

STAKEHOLDER CONSEQUENCE
who carries the service, economic, military, political or alliance effect
```

A responsibility claim can change the attribution history without producing a new operational effect.

A political statement can change allied confidence without changing the technical facts of an intrusion.

A cluster can become established while common authorship remains open.

## 🔎 Confidence Belongs To The Proposition

A cyber incident does not have one confidence level.

```text
INCIDENT:
🟢 ESTABLISHED

SERVICE DISRUPTION:
🟢 ESTABLISHED

TECHNICAL OPERATOR:
🟡 PROBABLE

COMMON CUSTOMER:
⚪ OPEN

FORMAL STATE DIRECTION:
⚪ NOT PUBLICLY ESTABLISHED
```

The traffic-light layer means:

```text
🟢 ESTABLISHED / CONFIRMED
🟡 PROBABLE
🟠 SUSPECTED / DEVELOPING
⚪ OPEN / UNATTRIBUTED
❌ EXCLUDED
```

`📣 ACTOR-CLAIMED` is a claim-status modifier, not a confidence category or formal attribution.

The pattern scale is also separate from attribution.

The pack keeps four related questions visible:

- Is there a recurring pattern?
- Is there a shared organising mechanism?
- Is there a common operator?
- Is there one strategic campaign or customer?

Those answers may move at different speeds.

## 🧬 One War Does Not Mean One Threat Ecosystem

The current record is becoming more differentiated rather than more unified.

### Water And Operational Technology

The Minnesota/core water wave remains the strongest Iran-relevant element.

The public evidence now combines established coordinated disruption across more than 30 Minnesota community water systems; reporting that United States intelligence agencies assessed Iran was likely responsible; an explicit responsibility claim from APT IRAN and CyberAv3ngers; and an established prior United States government description of CyberAv3ngers as affiliated with the IRGC Cyber-Electronic Command.

That materially strengthens the Iran-linked assessment for the Minnesota/core wave.

It still does not create formal FBI, CISA, NSA, or EPA attribution of the July–August campaign. It does not independently prove the actor claim. It does not establish that every incident in the wider multi-state wave shares the same operator, customer, or state direction.

On 19 August, federal officials still stopped short of formally attributing the recent local water attacks to Iran. The presidential rejection of Iranian responsibility issued on 31 July had therefore remained publicly unresolved for 19 days — nearly three weeks of executive-level uncertainty layered onto a live defensive problem.

A separate multi-agency warning on 19 August described an active threat to Siemens S7-series programmable logic controllers across water, energy, manufacturing, chemical, food and agriculture, and other critical sectors. The warning expands the defensive technology and sector perimeter. It does not identify a completed incident or attribute the active threat to Iran.

### Local Government

Suisun City and Darlington County strengthen the pattern of geographically distributed disruption to municipal and public-safety administration.

Suisun required cross-jurisdictional dispatch fallback and later disclosed a perpetrator demand, strengthening a criminal or extortion explanation. Darlington preserved emergency and 911 functions while ordinary county services were degraded.

The cluster matters. Common sponsorship is not established.

### Healthcare

AnMed strengthens the conventional ransomware and extortion line.

The Gentlemen claim became more credible when attacker-controlled ransom posts appeared on AnMed's Facebook page, but the group's data-theft claims remain unverified and no public Iran connection has been found.

Stryker and AnMed can belong to the same healthcare-effects watch without being treated as one actor ecosystem.

### Transport And Logistics

Los Angeles Metro and CEVA Logistics show how transport and contracted logistics can carry serious operational and data consequences.

CEVA remains adjacent rather than automatically becoming a port, state-infrastructure, strategic-cargo, or Iranian incident.

### Finance

The financial-sector vishing and extortion campaign is coherent as criminal activity inside a systemically important perimeter.

No public evidence currently connects it to Iran or a state customer.

That is not evidence of irrelevance. It is evidence that the threat environment contains more than one organising mechanism.

### Shared Software And Mass Extortion

The Cl0p campaign supplies a deliberately inconvenient comparator.

One shared enterprise-software opportunity can produce victims across energy, health technology, industry and finance without proving common strategic target selection. Campaign-level confidence can be high while individual victim claims, operational effects, and any state-customer proposition remain mixed or open.

### Public Administration Beyond The United States

France's DGFiP taxpayer-data breach adds a national public-administration incident with established confidentiality loss, approximately 700,000 affected taxpayers, and no established service disruption or Iran connection.

Its leading public explanation is criminal monetisation. Its inclusion is not a suggestion that Iran did it. It is proof of why a wartime register needs comparators capable of surviving the atmosphere around them.

Connecticut supplies the opposite kind of record: no reported new attack, but a documented preparedness response and a component-inventory gap across distributed water systems. A context row can matter because defenders cannot protect equipment they cannot reliably locate or assign.

### Historical Attribution Context

The 18 August Mabna Institute charging action adds a concrete Iran-specific example of alleged IRGC tasking moving through employees, contractors, affiliates, and hackers-for-hire.

The underlying conduct is historical and the allegations remain to be adjudicated. Mabna clarifies a possible commissioning structure. It does not attribute the current water, ransomware, logistics, French-tax, Cl0p, or Siemens developments.

## 🚰 The Control Layer Changes The Threshold

This pack distinguishes between the computers surrounding an essential service and the machinery through which that service is physically delivered.

```text
administrative IT
→ records
→ communications
→ scheduling
→ billing

operational technology
→ controllers
→ sensors
→ pumps
→ valves
→ treatment systems
→ physical processes
```

Both layers matter. The second creates a more direct path from intrusion to physical effect.

Reported consequences across the United States water wave have included loss of monitoring or control, credential and configuration changes, forced manual operation, pressure disruption, flooding, temporary shutdowns, and precautionary public-health measures within parts of the wider campaign.

Those effects were not uniform across every utility. No common contamination finding has been established.

Manual fallback is evidence of resilience. It can also be evidence that an intrusion reached far enough into the operational environment for fallback to become necessary.

The relevant question is not merely:

> Was the utility hacked?

It is also:

> What did the attacker reach, what could they change, what did operators lose, and what happened to the physical process?

## 🕸️ Attribution Is A Stack

Public discussion often collapses attribution into two settings:

```text
Iran did it
Iran did not do it
```

Real attribution is layered.

```text
initial-access actor
→ infrastructure or tooling
→ access broker or intermediary
→ technical operator
→ affiliate or contractor
→ target selector
→ customer or tasker
→ state affiliation
→ state direction
→ beneficiary
→ amplifier
```

Evidence at one layer does not automatically travel to the next.

Identifying a criminal operator proves neither the presence nor the absence of a state customer.

An established historical relationship between an actor and the IRGC does not independently establish state direction of a new incident.

A government may possess information it does not publish. Public silence cannot be converted into evidence that a hidden attribution exists.

From outside, these may look identical:

```text
we know more than we can disclose

and

we genuinely do not yet know
```

That uncertainty remains part of the record.

## 👾 Legal Significance Also Has Layers

Cyber operations do not sit outside international humanitarian law merely because the weapon is code.

Civilian infrastructure remains protected. Water, healthcare, education, public administration, finance, transport, and telecommunications do not become lawful military objectives merely because the state depends upon them.

```text
serious cyber incident
≠
Iranian operation
≠
operation attributable to the Iranian state
≠
international humanitarian law violation
≠
proved war crime
≠
individual criminal responsibility
```

The law does not become inapplicable because attribution is secret or incomplete.

The pack records protected-object relevance without rounding every serious incident upward into a war-crime claim.

## 🗺️ The Operational War Map Is Wider Than The Formal War

The United States and Israel are the central direct belligerents.

The wider perimeter includes states Iran may regard as participating where they host forces, provide basing, logistics, refuelling, intelligence, interception or air defence, support maritime or sanctions activity, or constrain Iran's ability to retaliate.

The pack distinguishes:

```text
Iranian perception of participation
≠
operational support
≠
political alignment
≠
legal belligerency
```

Hormuz rhetoric matters inside this map even when it does not become policy.

The reported joke explanation weakens the territorial-policy claim. It does not make the information effect disappear.

A public territorial remark can still narrow the space for compromise, alter allied expectations, intensify Iranian sovereignty rhetoric, and make a later navigation agreement easier to present as territorial surrender.

That is why context developments are preserved rather than dismissed as merely words.

## 🇺🇸 Why The American Effects Are Difficult To Contain Politically

The American consequences do not land inside one partisan geography or stakeholder group.

They can affect military personnel and traditional defence institutions; MAGA constituencies split between executive loyalty, anti-war politics, sovereignty rhetoric and hostility to Iran; governors, mayors, counties, utilities and emergency services; technology companies; healthcare systems; banks and insurers; households; diaspora communities; and allies deciding how much confidence to place in American executive judgment.

The mixture of red and blue states matters.

A campaign affecting politically mixed jurisdictions cannot be contained easily inside a narrative that one party, governor, region, or disfavoured population caused the problem.

Local failures can be blamed locally. Repeated effects across politically incompatible places are harder to narratively quarantine.

That makes executive confidence part of the infrastructure story.

So does allied confidence.

An ally does not need to agree that every incident is Iranian to notice that the United States is accepting wider civilian-infrastructure exposure while escalating the war and publicly contesting its own attribution signals.

## 🇬🇧 Britain And The Exploitable Seam

Britain may possess substantial cyber, defence, intelligence, regulatory and investigative capability while responsibility remains fragmented across departments, contractors, police forces, regulators and national-security refusals.

A state that protects systems but cannot provide an affected person or institution with a named lead authority, written account, lawful basis, risk assessment, protection pathway and route of challenge is advertising a seam.

That seam can be used by an adversary, contractor, partner, criminal group or internal institution without any one of them needing complete control of the system.

Fragmented responsibility is therefore part of national resilience.

## 📰 Reporting Rule

> Do not attribute beyond the evidence. Do not erase a developing strategic pattern merely because public attribution is incomplete.

And where international humanitarian law may become relevant:

> Do not call an incident a war crime merely because the target is civilian, the timing is wartime, or the suspected actor is Iranian. Do not erase the legal significance of an operation against protected civilian infrastructure merely because attribution remains unresolved.

Each record should preserve what is confirmed; what is claimed; what operationally changed; what reached the control layer; what fallback worked; who claimed responsibility; who has been technically identified; what relationship is established; whether formal attribution exists; what rival explanations remain viable; which stakeholders carry the effect; which source function supports each proposition; and how the assessment changed over time.

Where information is missing, distinguish:

```text
UNKNOWN
NOT PUBLIC
NO EVIDENCE FOUND
WITHHELD / NCND
NOT APPLICABLE
```

Repetition is not corroboration.

Ten articles may still trace back to one advisory, one unnamed official, one vendor report, or one Telegram claim.

The source chain matters.

## 🧪 How To Use The Pack

For a new incident:

1. Begin with [🧭 What This Pack Is Tracking](./🧭_what_this_pack_is_tracking.md) and choose Core, Adjacent, Context or Excluded.
2. Use [🔎 Confidence Labels And Source Rules](./🔎_confidence_labels_and_source_rules.md) to separate event, effect, relationship, operator, pattern and campaign confidence.
3. Use [🕸️ Attribution Is Not A Light Switch](./🕸️_attribution_is_not_a_light_switch.md) and [🧅 The Operator May Not Know The Customer](./🧅_the_operator_may_not_know_the_customer.md) before moving from operator to sponsor or state direction.
4. Use [🚰 When Cyber Reaches The Machinery](./🚰_when_cyber_reaches_the_machinery.md) where operational technology, monitoring, control, configuration, set-points or physical processes are involved.
5. Amend the CSV first, reproduce the same ordered record in the XLSX, confirm record-and-field parity, and preserve attribution and correction history.
6. Update the human-readable timeline where the event changes the public chronology.
7. Propagate material corrections into every node where the previous assessment performs analytical work.

For reporting:

1. Name who knows each fact.
2. Distinguish confirmed facts from claimed facts.
3. State what remains unknown or not public.
4. Preserve rival explanations.
5. Avoid inheriting attribution from one incident across a whole cluster.
6. Do not turn an official's interpretation into a presidential retraction, an actor claim into formal attribution, or repeated headlines into independent corroboration.
