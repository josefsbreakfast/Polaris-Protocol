PRIORITY B — NEW ANALYTICAL MODEL: CORPORATE LEGITIMACY AS ATTACK SURFACE

Working hypothesis:

Iran does not need Western publics to admire the Iranian state. It may benefit if target selection and narrative framing reduce the solidarity available to the victim.

Core formulation:

technical access
→ target selection
→ decision to conceal, exploit or publicise
→ narrative framing
→ differential public sympathy
→ political effect

Key concepts to add:

- “Denial of solidarity” rather than conversion to pro-Iranian sentiment.
- Corporate legitimacy is unevenly distributed.
- Fossil-fuel, defence, surveillance, monopoly, private-equity, tax, labour, healthcare-cost or Palestine-related reputational baggage may change the political cost of attacking or embarrassing an institution.
- This does not legitimise the attack. It changes the anticipated audience response.
- The strategically useful reaction may be neither support nor approval, but hesitation: “cyberattacks are bad, but…”.
- Iranian information operations already provide a mechanism comparator: cyber activity, hacktivist identities, amplification and narrative effects can be used together.
- Opportunistic access and strategic victimology can coexist: acquire access broadly, then decide which access to exploit, retain, sell, destroy or publicise.
- Publicity selection matters as much as intrusion selection. The actor may publicise socially unsympathetic victims while concealing access that would provoke immediate civilian hostility.

Testable dataset questions:

- How socially sympathetic is the affected institution to the relevant audience?
- Was the operation publicised, concealed or discovered by the victim?
- Who publicised it?
- What political narrative accompanied the disclosure or claim?
- Was an Israel/Palestine, anti-corporate, anti-war, anti-surveillance or anti-elite narrative available?
- Did the actor claim more damage than independent evidence established?
- Did the operation produce physical harm or obvious civilian suffering?
- Did the target combine high strategic value with low public sympathy?
- Which audience appears to have been addressed?
- Did target legitimacy affect official, media or public willingness to rally around the victim?

Important falsifiers / constraints:

- Water, hospitals, emergency communications and visible harm to children can destroy the solidarity-denial advantage and produce direct hostility toward the attacker.
- If Iran-linked targeting does not cluster by legitimacy, publicity choice or narrative compatibility, the hypothesis weakens.
- A socially unpopular victim does not establish strategic selection.
- Presence on a boycott list does not establish Iranian tasking.
- Genuine Western grievances can be exploited without being invented by Iran.
- Iran’s repression of Iranians and domestic internet shutdowns remain relevant context; accurate identification of Western grievances does not make the Iranian state benign.


PRIORITY C — COMMISSIONING CHAINS, MONEY AND RISK EXTERNALISATION

Principal teaching model:

someone wants an outcome
→ money, access or tasking changes hands
→ an intermediary chooses a method
→ an access broker or subcontractor may enter
→ an operator acts
→ effects travel beyond the original brief
→ responsibility becomes diffuse

Add the following distinctions:

- Commissioning does not imply operational control.
- Intended outcome is not the same as foreseeable ecosystem support.
- A legitimate-looking commission can finance wages, tooling, infrastructure, training, access inventories and relationships later used for other customers or crimes.
- A contractor can serve state, corporate, university, political and criminal demand without those categories forming a clean organisational membrane.
- Financial relationships may reveal who generated demand, who paid, what outcome was valued, when access was purchased, whether several customers existed, and who benefits from maintaining the capability.
- “Follow the money” is part of the control diagram, not a grubby appendix to the technical story.

Risk-governance formulation:

visible organisational risk may fall

while

operator autonomy rises
criminal capability rises
information leakage rises
secondary-market access rises
downstream harm rises
accountability distance rises

Name this as risk externalisation or risk displacement through delegation.

Add the selective-impunity feedback loop:

use questionable capability
→ capability creates wider harm
→ investigation threatens to expose the original purchaser
→ incentives favour under-enforcement, compartmentalisation or minimisation
→ ecosystem gains relative impunity
→ capability becomes easier to buy
→ more customers use it

Working principle:

Selective impunity changes the market price of misconduct.

Comparative material:

- Mabna as the Iran-specific public-evidence anchor.
- Medusa / ransomware affiliate markets as the necessary non-state comparator.
- I-Soon as a comparative contractor-market case, clearly labelled Chinese and non-Iranian.
- “Move fast and break things” as a cultural comparator: when migrated from one’s own software into intelligence, policing, political conflict, cybersecurity or reputation management, the thing broken is somebody else’s infrastructure, rights, safety or life.

Do not overstate:

- A payment does not automatically prove state direction.
- Use of an intermediary does not automatically prove concealment.
- A commissioner may genuinely not have intended downstream offences.
- Lack of intent does not automatically eliminate foreseeability, ecosystem support or governance responsibility.

- Trump publicly said he would declare the strait US territory.
- Subsequent reporting attributed a “joke” explanation to an unnamed White House official.
- The reported joke explanation weakens the claim that a developed annexation policy existed.

- “Rhetorical corner-building”: public sovereignty claims can turn a navigation/security negotiation into a contest over ownership, humiliation and surrender.
- The language may narrow compromise space while revealing the form of “victory” the US president wants to claim.
- Iranian negotiators may distinguish non-negotiable Iranian sovereignty from negotiable international navigation or security guarantees, creating a face-saving exit without conceding territory.
- The rhetoric supplies Iran with an anti-colonial narrative whether or not it becomes policy.

Water-risk governance addition:

- Keep two explicit conditionals: if the water incidents are ultimately established as Iranian retaliation, and if the administration knowingly accepted increased domestic infrastructure risk while escalating over Hormuz.
- Do not compress those conditions into “Trump knowingly chose attacks on American water systems.”
- Ask instead: what civilian consequences were foreseeable, what mitigations were available, and how much additional domestic risk was accepted in pursuit of war aims?
- Contrast symbolic sovereignty at the top with pumps, pressure, drinking water and local operators at the bottom.
- This governance question remains relevant even if the final operator attribution changes.

WORKING EDITORIAL PRINCIPLE

The next update should make the pack more capable of asking:

Who generated the demand?
Who acquired the access?
Who operated?
Who paid?
Who selected the target?
Who decided to publicise it?
Which audience was being addressed?
Who absorbed the downstream cost?
Did delegation reduce risk — or merely move it somewhere the commissioner could no longer see?

Those questions deepen the analysis without turning every wartime incident into an Iranian operation.
