There is a credible new development in the U.S. local-government cluster, plus one transport/logistics incident worth adding as an adjacent infrastructure signal. Neither materially strengthens Iranian attribution.

🇺🇸 Darlington County, South Carolina — government administration. On 12 August, the county disclosed a cybersecurity incident affecting computer systems and limiting some county services. Officials proactively took systems offline and brought in outside cybersecurity specialists and law-enforcement agencies. 911 and emergency dispatch remained operational, but some ordinary county functions and phone lines were disrupted. Attribution remains ⚪ OPEN / UNATTRIBUTED: the county has not identified ransomware, a threat actor, initial access, or data theft.  

The significance is the clustering rather than this incident in isolation. Darlington follows Coweta, Oklahoma; Suisun City, California; Washburn County, Wisconsin; and other recent U.S. municipal disruptions in a compressed period. The strongest competing explanation is still ordinary criminal and opportunistic activity against unevenly defended local-government networks. There is no public evidence connecting Darlington to Iran or to the water-utility campaign. But the frequency of degraded municipal functions means the government-administration layer now deserves its own pattern marker rather than being treated as incidental background noise.

There is also a 🇪🇺 European transport/logistics incident that is worth placing in the wider watch with a strong caveat. CEVA Logistics, part of French shipping group CMA CGM, suffered a cyberattack beginning around 29 July that affected eight European warehouses and produced shipment delays. Subsequent disclosures show that some customer delivery data was also exposed. CEVA provides freight forwarding, warehousing and transportation across major industrial and commercial supply chains.  

Attribution: ⚪ OPEN. Public technical detail is still sparse; ransomware or destructive malware has been suggested, but not established. There is no evidence of Iranian direction, OT compromise, port-control compromise, or deliberate military-logistics targeting. Because CEVA is a private logistics operator rather than a state service, I would classify it as adjacent essential logistics infrastructure, not automatically as essential-state infrastructure. Its relevance rises if later evidence shows affected warehouses supported defence, fuel, food, medical or other strategic supply chains.
