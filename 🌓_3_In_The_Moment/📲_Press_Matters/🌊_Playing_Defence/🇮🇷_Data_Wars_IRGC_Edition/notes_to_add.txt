That fits Iranian wartime incentives unusually well. Against a conventionally stronger adversary, a scalable ability to generate numerous small infrastructure risks can impose defensive cost, force asset inventories and remediation across whole sectors, generate uncertainty about where the next effect will occur, and learn which systems and organisations react poorly. But the same economics make the capability attractive to ordinary criminals and other states, so this development should raise concern about volume, not mechanically raise attribution confidence for every new incident.
