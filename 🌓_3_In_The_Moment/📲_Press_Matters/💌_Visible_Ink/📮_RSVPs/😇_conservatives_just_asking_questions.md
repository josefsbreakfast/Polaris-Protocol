# 😇 Conservatives Just Asking Questions
**First created:** 2026-09-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-17  
*Parliamentary questions, institutional collisions, machine-assisted scrutiny, and the ancient constitutional art of making somebody check the records.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

There is a version of *just asking questions* which means wrapping an allegation in a question mark.

This is not that.

Mike Wood MP's written parliamentary questions provide a useful worked example of a more disciplined scrutiny technology:

> **Find the join. Ask the question. Make the institution check its records.**

The question does not prove the suspected problem.

That is rather the point.

A public record can contain two facts which do not obviously join. A disclosure can leave an important category undefined. An answer can identify one institution while leaving another unexplained. A published methodology can say that records were searched without making it obvious what counted as a record, whose records were included, or where responsibility sat.

The responsible response is not necessarily to fill the gap with inference.

Parliament has another option.

It can ask.

The House of Commons' own procedural guidance says parliamentary questions are intended to obtain information or press for action on matters for which government is responsible. Questions must be based on fact rather than speculation, use neutral language, and concern ministerial responsibility. The Table Office edits questions under the Speaker's authority to make them conform to the rules; the Speaker is the final authority on admissibility.

That does not make an admitted question true.

It makes it a particularly interesting **container for uncertainty**.

## ✨ Key Features

The reusable method is:

```text
PUBLIC RECORD A
        +
PUBLIC RECORD B
        ↓
POSSIBLE COLLISION
        ↓
HUMAN TRIAGE
        ↓
bounded factual question
        ↓
PARLIAMENTARY SCRUTINY
        ↓
government checks records
        ↓
MINISTERIAL ANSWER
        ↓
UPDATED PUBLIC RECORD
        ↺
```

Keep the evidential ladder intact:

```text
question
≠ allegation proved

admissibility
≠ premise certified

collision
≠ anomaly

anomaly
≠ wrongdoing

relationship
≠ coordination

access
≠ use

recommendation
≠ decision

machine match
≠ human conclusion
```

The interesting space is between those two sides of the `≠` sign.

That is where the next question lives.

## 😇 Just Asking

Wood's recent questions range widely. The methodological interest here is not to pretend that every question belongs to one theory.

It is the opposite.

Across the corpus are repeated attempts to establish bounded facts about:

- Project Cannon and the institutional route between the NCSC, Cabinet Office and Joint Intelligence Organisation;
- contacts involving Lord Mandelson, OpenAI, Global Counsel and Palantir;
- the scope and handling of vetting advice;
- Developed Vetting decisions;
- classified access;
- off-boarding and information retained on non-corporate devices or accounts;
- the search scope used for the Humble Address disclosures;
- the No.10 Political Office and party-political material;
- the Prime Minister's General Counsel and the form of any report following fact-finding;
- departmental correspondence and meeting organisation;
- government use of communications platforms;
- public appointments, political activity and conflicts;
- government advertising, public relations and influencer expenditure;
- machinery-of-government changes;
- defence and NATO expenditure;
- transparency data;
- and which institution actually owns a responsibility.

The individual questions differ.

The recurring technique is narrower:

> **Where the public record leaves a join unresolved, ask for the missing join rather than manufacturing it.**

## 🧩 Find The Collision

A collision is not necessarily evidence that anybody has done anything wrong.

It can be as mundane as two institutional descriptions using different categories.

It can also be consequential.

Useful collision types include:

```text
published policy
        ↕
observed implementation

declared responsibility
        ↕
actual record holder

vetting recommendation
        ↕
appointment / clearance decision

formal clearance
        ↕
practical access

declared interest
        ↕
meeting / decision process

disclosure methodology
        ↕
possible record locations

official communications system
        ↕
non-corporate device / account

departmental answer
        ↕
cross-government responsibility
```

The collision tells the analyst where to look.

It does not tell the analyst what they will find.

## 🪜 Ask One Join At A Time

Large allegations are difficult to test because they contain too many propositions.

A bounded parliamentary question can separate them.

Instead of:

```text
X happened because Y secretly influenced Z
```

ask:

```text
Was X recorded?

Who held responsibility for X?

Was Y consulted?

Was correspondence exchanged?

On what date?

Was a recommendation made?

Was that recommendation followed?

Which body organised the meeting?

Which records were searched?

Which categories of material were in scope?

Which institution holds the information?
```

The answer may support the concern.

It may weaken it.

It may reveal that the original model used the wrong institution entirely.

That is a feature.

## 📬 Make Government Check Its Own Records

The outside observer often cannot establish the missing fact responsibly.

The department may be able to.

```text
PUBLIC / ANALYST
identifies missing join
        ↓
MP
asks bounded question
        ↓
DEPARTMENT
checks relevant records
        ↓
MINISTERIAL ANSWER
enters parliamentary record
        ↓
PUBLIC / ANALYST
updates model
```

This is not the same thing as asking government to investigate an allegation on the researcher's behalf.

The question can be much smaller:

> Do you hold this record?

> Did this communication occur?

> Which organisation made this decision?

> What category was searched?

> What was the date?

> Was advice given?

Sometimes the answer is that the department does not hold the information.

That is information too.

It can map custody, responsibility, record-keeping and the boundary between institutions.

## 🧭 Project Cannon And Following Responsibility

One Wood question asks which division of the Cabinet Office received the Labour Together/APCO dossier from the National Cyber Security Centre, and whether the Joint Intelligence Organisation received it.

The analytical value is not an assumption about what the dossier means.

It is the route.

```text
NCSC
  ↓
CABINET OFFICE
  ↓ ?
which division?
  ↓ ?
JIO?
```

An information object can move between institutions while the public description of the episode remains attached to the organisation first associated with it.

The scrutiny question therefore follows **responsibility and custody**, not merely the headline.

This is pure Visible Ink:

> **Follow the route by which responsibility moves as carefully as the route by which information moves.**

## 👀 Access Is More Than Clearance

The vetting questions expose another common collapse.

A person's vetting status is not a complete map of their information environment.

```text
vetting / clearance status
        ≠
technical access
        ≠
physical access
        ≠
practical access
        ≠
information encountered
        ≠
significance recognised
        ≠
information retained
        ≠
information disclosed
```

Wood has asked, among other things, how often Developed Vetting was granted contrary to a UK Security Vetting recommendation of refusal, and about the character of risks identified in an individual vetting process.

Those are different questions from asking what information a person actually encountered or retained.

A good scrutiny model keeps them different.

The same applies in reverse: evidence that somebody could access something does not establish that they did.

## 📦 The Humble Address And The Search-Scope Problem

A particularly useful cluster concerns the government's search for material responsive to the Humble Address relating to Lord Mandelson.

The government described search parameters and instructed relevant individuals to retain and provide information falling within scope.

Wood then asked questions including whether communications held by No.10 Political Office staff were in scope and, later, whether party-political material held by the No.10 Political Office was deemed within the referenced search parameters.

That illustrates a general information-governance problem:

```text
"WE SEARCHED THE RECORDS"
            ↓
Which records?

"WE SEARCHED RELEVANT MATERIAL"
            ↓
Relevant under which definition?

"ALL RELEVANT INDIVIDUALS"
            ↓
Who counted as relevant?

"IN SCOPE"
            ↓
What established the boundary?
```

A disclosure can be complete **within its search methodology** while the adequacy of that methodology remains a separate question.

That is not evidence that omitted material exists.

It is a reason to make the scope legible.

> **Completeness claims inherit the boundaries of the search that produced them.**

## 📱 Off-Boarding, Devices And The Official Record

Another cluster asks about non-corporate communications and information retained after Crown service.

Again, the useful distinction is architectural:

```text
government information
        ↓
official system?
non-corporate device?
personal account?
messaging platform?
local copy?
        ↓
record captured?
        ↓
record transferred?
        ↓
record retained?
        ↓
off-boarding complete?
```

The question is not automatically whether somebody acted maliciously.

It is whether the information architecture can account for Crown information when people, devices and roles change.

A person leaving the building does not itself reconcile the record.

## 🧾 An Answer Is A Record, Not Revelation

A ministerial answer has special value because it is an authoritative government response placed on the parliamentary record.

That does **not** make every answer incontrovertible truth.

Answers can be:

- complete;
- partial;
- framed by a category the questioner did not anticipate;
- referred to an earlier answer;
- corrected;
- superseded by later information;
- or followed by another question.

The correct model is:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
GOVERNMENT RESPONSE
   ↓
new public evidence
   ↓
UPDATED MODEL
   ↓
follow-up if warranted
```

Not:

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
FINAL TRUTH FOREVER
```

This matters particularly when the answer itself exposes a semantic or factual problem.

## 🧮 Seven Thousand Things That Were Not Seven Thousand Open Consultations

A useful example is UIN 27254.

Wood asked the Cabinet Office how many statutory consultation duties had been identified by an AI tool. The corrected answer, published on 14 September 2026, states:

> approximately **7,000 statutory duties to consult**

That formulation matters.

It does **not** say:

> 7,000 open consultations.

Nor does it say:

> 7,000 consultations currently being conducted.

The transformation looks tiny:

```text
statutory duties to consult
        ↓
"consultations"
        ↓
"open consultations"
```

The semantic change is substantial.

This is a useful warning for machine-assisted analysis, political communication and journalism alike.

Source terminology is data.

Correction history is data.

The exact unit being counted is data.

Do not tidy those inconveniences away.

## 🤖 Machine Finds The Join; Human Asks The Question

The Wood corpus suggests a potentially powerful workflow for machine-assisted public scrutiny.

```text
PUBLIC RECORDS
+ parliamentary records
+ contracts
+ registers
+ departmental publications
+ audit findings
+ released correspondence
+ transparency returns
+ machine-assisted comparison
        ↓
POSSIBLE COLLISIONS
        ↓
HUMAN TRIAGE
        ↓
context
materiality
alternative explanations
        ↓
PARLIAMENTARY QUESTION
        ↓
authoritative government response
        ↓
UPDATED PUBLIC RECORD
```

Machines are exceptionally useful for finding places where the institutional story may not join cleanly.

Humans remain responsible for deciding:

- what the collision means;
- whether it matters;
- whether the apparent discrepancy is merely categorical;
- what alternative explanations exist;
- whether the evidence supports asking anything at all;
- and what question should be asked next.

> **Machine discovery is not machine judgement.**

A model that spots two names beside the same company has found a possible relationship to inspect.

It has not discovered coordination.

A system that finds two inconsistent dates has found a discrepancy.

It has not discovered deception.

The human job begins before the allegation does.

## 🧱 Do Data Analysis Before Asking Machines To Analyse Data

There is an unfashionable prerequisite to large-scale AI scrutiny:

**know what the corpus is.**

Before clustering thousands of parliamentary questions, establish:

```text
authoritative identifier
member
date tabled
department
question text
answer status
answer date
answer text
correction status
linked / pursuant questions
grouped questions
source URL
retrieval completeness
```

Then analyse it.

Otherwise an LLM can produce a beautifully confident analysis of a dataset whose missingness nobody has measured.

That is not advanced intelligence.

That is a spreadsheet problem wearing a transformer.

## 🕳️ The Corpus Problem

Attempts to reconstruct Wood's question corpus through different information tools produced unstable enumeration: tiny result sets in some retrievals, larger but still apparently incomplete sets in others, and changing totals.

The authoritative UK Parliament member page currently reports **3,769 written-question results** for Mike Wood.

That does not mean every external tool can reliably reconstruct all 3,769 records.

The governance question is:

> **Can a nominally public parliamentary record be reliably reconstructed through the information tools people increasingly use to interrogate public records?**

The pipeline matters:

```text
AUTHORITATIVE SOURCE RECORD
        ↓
retrieval interface
        ↓
search / extraction layer
        ↓
tool representation
        ↓
LLM context
        ↓
USER-VISIBLE CORPUS
```

Failure can occur at every join.

A particularly dangerous failure mode is not an error message.

It is a plausible-looking partial corpus with no visible indication that it is partial.

> **117 records from an unknown corpus labelled complete is manufactured completeness.**

Do not confuse retrieval termination with corpus completion.

## 🔁 Questions Generate Better Questions

A good parliamentary answer does not have to end the inquiry.

It can:

- resolve the question;
- narrow the possible explanations;
- identify the correct institution;
- reveal that records are not held;
- expose a category distinction;
- establish a date;
- identify the decision-maker;
- disclose a methodological boundary;
- contradict the researcher's initial model;
- or generate the next bounded question.

```text
QUESTION
   ↓
ANSWER
   ↓
UPDATED MODEL
   ↓
BETTER QUESTION
```

This is iterative institutional debugging.

The researcher is allowed to be wrong.

The opposition MP is allowed to be wrong.

The useful requirement is that the method contains a route by which being wrong becomes discoverable.

## 🏛️ The Question Has Passed Through A Door

A published parliamentary question is not simply an online allegation with nicer stationery.

Commons procedure imposes constraints.

The House's guidance states that questions must seek information or press for action, concern something for which a minister is responsible, be based on fact rather than speculation, use neutral language, avoid unnecessary information, avoid asking for readily available information, and comply with the sub judice rule.

The Table Office acts under the Speaker's authority to ensure conformity with the rules. The Speaker is the final authority on admissibility.

So:

> **Admission gives the question procedural status, not evidential victory.**

It means the question has passed through an institutional gate designed for parliamentary scrutiny.

It does not mean the Speaker has certified the suspected concern as true.

That distinction is what makes the mechanism useful.

## ⚖️ Privilege Protects The Capacity To Ask

Article IX of the Bill of Rights 1689 protects freedom of speech and debates or proceedings in Parliament from being impeached or questioned outside Parliament.

The architecture is two-sided:

```text
                 PARLIAMENT
                     │
        ┌────────────┴────────────┐
        ↓                         ↓
EXTERNAL PROTECTION         INTERNAL GOVERNANCE

courts / executive          Speaker / Chair
cannot ordinarily           Table Office
turn proceedings            admissibility
into ordinary               order
external liability          procedure
```

Privilege is therefore not a licence to make every kind of statement everywhere.

Parliament protects its proceedings externally while regulating them internally.

> **Privilege protects the capacity to ask. Procedure governs how that capacity is exercised.**

The constraints are part of the constitutional technology.

## 🪞 Why Not Just Say They Lied?

Because an evidential discrepancy is not yet a finding about knowledge or intention.

```text
STATEMENT A
        ≠
STATEMENT B
        ↓
discrepancy

does not automatically establish

knowledge
        ↓
intention
        ↓
deliberate deception
```

A stronger question can therefore be:

> On one date the Minister told the House X. The later departmental record says Y. How are those statements reconciled?

That formulation keeps attention on the evidential collision.

It also makes the institution do useful work.

The prohibition on casually converting every discrepancy into an accusation of deliberate dishonesty can be annoying.

It can also improve the question.

## 📺 Parliament Now Has Two Audiences

There is, however, a modern interface problem.

Parliamentary language developed inside an institution whose participants understood its procedural meanings.

Parliament is now continuously broadcast, clipped, searched and redistributed.

```text
                 PARLIAMENTARY SPEECH
                         │
              ┌──────────┴──────────┐
              ↓                     ↓
      INSTITUTIONAL AUDIENCE     PUBLIC AUDIENCE

      knows procedure            ordinary English
      knows thresholds           short clips
      follows context            social platforms
      knows Chair's role         limited procedural
                                 context
```

A procedural intervention understood internally as:

> *you have crossed from challenging accuracy into alleging deliberate dishonesty, which requires a different route*

can be heard externally as:

> *Parliament will not let MPs say politicians lie.*

That is an interface problem worth addressing without discarding the underlying constitutional function.

Comprehensibility is not cosmetic.

It is part of institutional efficiency.

## 🧠 Opposition As Error-Correction Architecture

Opposition is not useful only because another party may eventually form a government.

Institutionalised disagreement can improve the state's information environment.

```text
GOVERNMENT
acts / explains
        ↓
OPPOSITION
tests / challenges / requests records
        ↓
GOVERNMENT
answers
        ↓
PUBLIC RECORD
expands
        ↓
further scrutiny
```

A politically opposed questioner may notice weak joins precisely because they do **not** grant the government's premises.

That does not make the opposition correct.

It makes adversarial scrutiny useful.

The healthiest version is not:

> *I already know you are guilty; please provide the missing footnote.*

It is:

> *These things do not currently join. What does the record say?*

> **Opposition can be part of the error-correction architecture of power rather than merely competition for possession of power.**

## 🐙 CRAKEN

The **Calcaneus Reflexion Anti-Kraken Ecological Navigation System** keeps a scrutiny question from quietly growing tentacles into an allegation.

### Hold separately

```text
question
≠ finding

procedural admission
≠ factual certification

public relationship
≠ coordination

formal access
≠ practical access

practical access
≠ use

recommendation
≠ final decision

search completed
≠ universe searched

disclosure complete
≠ every conceivable record located

ministerial answer
≠ infallibility

AI match
≠ analytical conclusion

result count
≠ corpus completeness
```

### Ask instead

- What is actually established?
- What is the smallest unresolved join?
- Which institution should possess the missing fact?
- Is the question within ministerial responsibility?
- What record would answer it?
- What alternative explanation would produce the same observable pattern?
- What answer would falsify the researcher's preferred interpretation?
- Has the question preserved that possibility?
- Does the answer change the model?
- What should be asked next?

> **Do not answer uncertainty by manufacturing certainty. Use the uncertainty to formulate the next question.**
