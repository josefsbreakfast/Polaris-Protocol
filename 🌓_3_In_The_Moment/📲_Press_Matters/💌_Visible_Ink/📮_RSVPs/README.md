# 📮 RSVPs

**First created:** 2026-09-17  
**Last updated:** 2026-09-17

*Multiple responses, missing joins, contradictory accounts, partial views, and the information that becomes visible when we stop asking which single account is “the story”.*

## 🛰️ Orientation

An information event happens.

A document leaks. A report is published. A minister makes a statement. A parliamentary answer appears. A judgment is handed down. A department releases records. A journalist publishes an investigation. An institution announces a decision.

Then the replies start arriving.

```text
                   EVENT
                     │
          ┌──────────┼──────────┐
          ↓          ↓          ↓
       DOCUMENT     MEDIA     GOVERNMENT
          │          │          │
          ├──────────┼──────────┤
          ↓          ↓          ↓
      OPPOSITION   EXPERTS   INSTITUTIONS
          │          │          │
          ├──────────┼──────────┤
          ↓          ↓          ↓
      THINK TANKS   PUBLIC   PARLIAMENT
```

Everybody has sent an RSVP.

But none of the RSVPs is automatically the event itself.

Each response comes from a particular position in the information environment. It may contain direct knowledge, records, interpretation, inference, political argument, institutional self-description, specialist expertise, journalistic synthesis, rhetoric, or several of those things at once.

The methodological mistake is demanding that one RSVP become the master narrative too early.

> **The interesting information is often in the gaps between the responses: what one account includes that another omits; where two descriptions use different categories; where an inference appears without its provenance; where responsibility changes hands; and where nobody has yet answered the question created by putting the accounts beside one another.**

The gap does not automatically contain the answer.

What comparison can do is make the **missing question legible**.

That is what this folder is for.

## ✨ Key Features

`📮 RSVPs` is a comparative information method.

It asks us to:

- preserve multiple responses before prematurely reconciling them;
- distinguish an event from representations of the event;
- identify what each information object can actually establish;
- compare scope, definitions, timing, provenance and evidential roots;
- distinguish difference from contradiction;
- distinguish contradiction from deception;
- trace information lineage rather than counting echoes as independent evidence;
- preserve unresolved joins rather than filling them with inference;
- turn genuine gaps into bounded questions;
- route those questions towards institutions capable of answering them;
- treat each new answer as another RSVP rather than automatic closure.

The core loop is:

```text
INFORMATION EVENT
        ↓
MULTIPLE RESPONSES
        ↓
COMPARISON
        ↓
GAPS / COLLISIONS
        ↓
BOUNDED QUESTIONS
        ↓
NEW INFORMATION
        ↓
NEW RSVP
        ↺
```

The method is deliberately non-teleological. There is no guarantee that enough responses will eventually produce one perfectly coherent story.

Sometimes the responsible result is a narrower uncertainty.

That is still progress.

## 📬 What Counts As An RSVP?

An RSVP is a **response or representation generated in relation to an information event**.

It can include:

- source documents;
- subsequent documents;
- official statements;
- ministerial answers;
- parliamentary questions;
- departmental correspondence;
- corrections;
- press reporting;
- headlines;
- broadcast discussion;
- political reactions;
- opposition responses;
- former officials' commentary;
- expert analysis;
- think-tank publications;
- institutional explanations;
- later policy decisions;
- implementation data;
- audit findings;
- court or tribunal material;
- subsequent disclosures.

The category is relational.

Something does not have to say *in response to X* in order to function as an RSVP. A later spending decision can become an RSVP to an earlier policy proposal when the two are placed beside one another. A source document can become an RSVP to a later public description of what it supposedly contained. A correction can become an RSVP to the original answer.

The important thing is to preserve what each object actually is.

## 🧩 The Information In The Gap

Suppose one response says:

```text
"We searched
relevant records."
```

Another asks:

```text
"Were records held
in this location included?"
```

The missing information is not automatically:

```text
"They excluded them."
```

The comparison instead reveals a question:

```text
What constituted
"relevant records"?
```

Or take a policy document:

```text
SOURCE                  PRESENTATION

"could explore"         "PLAN TO..."

        ← THE GAP →

What happened during
compression?
```

Or a policy timeline:

```text
APRIL POLICY             LATER POLICY

proposal X               announcement Y

        ← THE GAP →

same policy?
same objective?
shared provenance?
independent development?
```

Neither object necessarily contains the answer.

Putting them together makes the missing join visible.

> **Comparison can create information about what we do not yet know.**

That is not the same thing as filling the unknown.

It is considerably more useful.

## 🕳️ A Gap Is Not A Smoking Gun

This is load-bearing.

```text
gap
≠ concealment

difference
≠ contradiction

contradiction
≠ deception

omission
≠ deliberate omission

relationship
≠ coordination

sequence
≠ causation

similarity
≠ copying

benefit
≠ knowledge

anomaly
≠ attribution
```

There are many ordinary reasons why accounts fail to join cleanly.

Different actors may possess different records. They may be answering different questions. Their remits may differ. Their definitions may differ. They may be describing different time periods. One may know something the other does not. One may be legally unable to disclose information available to another. One may be compressing a complex source for a general audience. One may simply be wrong.

Good.

Boring explanations are allowed.

> **The gap is an investigative location, not an accusation.**

The task is to characterise it before explaining it.

## 🧭 Are They Even Answering The Same Question?

Before treating two accounts as contradictory, establish whether they are comparable.

| Dimension | RSVP A | RSVP B |
|---|---|---|
| Question being answered | ? | ? |
| Time period | ? | ? |
| Population / scope | ? | ? |
| Institution | ? | ? |
| Definition | ? | ? |
| Evidence available | ? | ? |
| Evidential threshold | ? | ? |
| Publication purpose | ? | ? |
| Certainty | ? | ? |
| Legal / procedural constraints | ? | ? |

Different answers may indicate contradiction.

They may instead indicate:

```text
DIFFERENT QUESTIONS
DIFFERENT DEFINITIONS
DIFFERENT PERIODS
DIFFERENT SCOPES
DIFFERENT KNOWLEDGE
DIFFERENT CONTAINERS
```

Do not manufacture a collision by joining unlike fields.

Before declaring a contradiction, check the bloody join.

## 🧱 Different Containers Do Different Things

A leaked slide, ministerial answer, newspaper article, Select Committee report, political speech, departmental submission and social-media post are not interchangeable containers.

They have different:

- purposes;
- authors;
- audiences;
- evidential standards;
- space constraints;
- disclosure constraints;
- procedural controls;
- incentives;
- temporal relationships to events.

Do not ask why a headline does not look like a policy submission before noticing that one is a headline and the other is a policy submission.

But do ask:

> **What changed when information crossed between them?**

That is the inheritance from `💌 Visible Ink`.

## 🪞 Representation Is An Event Too

Information changes as it moves.

```text
SOURCE
  ↓
selection
  ↓
compression
  ↓
categorisation
  ↓
layout
  ↓
headline
  ↓
public representation
```

At each stage, dimensions can disappear or become newly prominent.

Conditionality can disappear. Uncertainty can disappear. Alternatives can disappear. Chronology can disappear. Provenance can disappear. Visual grouping can create apparent relationships. A long submission can become a policy card. Several policy cards can become a numerical category. The numerical category can become a political claim.

That does not mean every transformation is misleading.

Transformation is unavoidable.

The important thing is to preserve enough lineage that the transformation remains inspectable.

> **The representation is evidence about the representation as well as evidence about the thing represented.**

## 🖼️ Words Are Not The Only RSVP

An information object contains more than prose.

Potentially relevant evidence includes:

- placement;
- sequence;
- grouping;
- headings;
- visual hierarchy;
- classification markings;
- annotations;
- redactions;
- version information;
- distribution labels;
- metadata;
- correction history.

```text
CONTENT
+
CONTAINER
+
STRUCTURE
+
ROUTE
+
TIMING
=
INFORMATION OBJECT
```

A transcript may preserve every word and still destroy evidence contained in the original structure.

This is why screenshots, document versions and presentation layouts can matter even where the text has been extracted correctly.

## 🏷️ Label The RSVP Before Interpreting It

Before interpretation, characterise the object.

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| RSVP | What is it? |
| Actor | Who produced it? |
| Date | When? |
| Container | Article, PQ, document, judgment, statement? |
| Trigger | What is it responding to? |
| Direct knowledge | What could this actor directly know? |
| Sources | What evidence does it cite? |
| Claims | What does it actually assert? |
| Inferences | What interpretation does it add? |
| Unknowns | What does it leave unanswered? |
| Constraints | What limits the response? |
| Provenance | Where did the underlying information originate? |
| Confidence | What can safely be concluded? |

This prevents **somebody said** from becoming an undifferentiated evidence category.

## 🧅 Observation, Inference And Attribution

An RSVP may contain several analytical layers simultaneously.

```text
OBSERVATION
"The document contains X."
        ↓
INFERENCE
"X suggests continuity."
        ↓
ATTRIBUTION
"Actor Y caused the continuity."
        ↓
MOTIVE
"Y did so because Z."
```

Every downward step requires additional evidence.

The problem is not inference. Analysis would be impossible without it.

The problem is allowing an inference to become grammatically indistinguishable from an observation, and then using the upgraded sentence as evidence for the next inference.

Keep the layers visible.

## 📏 Preserve The Evidential Distance

A useful shorthand is:

```text
observed
   ↓
reported
   ↓
corroborated
   ↓
inferred
   ↓
attributed
   ↓
established
```

Reality does not always move neatly down that ladder, but the ladder forces a useful question:

> **How far is this sentence from the thing we actually observed?**

Repeated reporting does not necessarily shorten that distance.

Six articles derived from one original article may still have one evidential root.

## 🌳 Count Roots, Not Echoes

Consider:

```text
SOURCE DOCUMENT
      ↓
NEWS REPORT
    ↙ ↓ ↘
PUNDIT  MP  THINK TANK
    ↘ ↓ ↙
"multiple sources say..."
```

There may be several responses.

There may still be one underlying source.

> **Multiplicity of responses is not multiplicity of independent evidence.**

Track lineage.

Ask whether an account independently corroborates the underlying fact or merely reproduces another account's description of it.

This is where provenance becomes more important than volume.

## 🔍 What Does One Know That The Other Doesn't?

When two responses differ, ask:

- Does one cite a source the other lacks?
- Is one temporally closer to the event?
- Does one have institutional access?
- Does one possess later information?
- Is one interpreting rather than observing?
- Does one use a narrower definition?
- Has one compressed the underlying material?
- Has one corrected an earlier statement?
- Are they actually talking about different objects?

Then classify the relationship.

Do not begin with *which one is lying?*

Begin with:

> **What kind of difference is this?**

## 🧮 Types Of Gap

The RSVP method benefits from a reusable gap taxonomy.

### Evidential Gap

Missing evidence between proposition and conclusion.

### Provenance Gap

Unknown origin of evidence, analysis or idea.

### Temporal Gap

Different accounts describe different moments.

### Scope Gap

Different populations, records, institutions or periods.

### Semantic Gap

The same or similar words mean different things.

### Custody Gap

It is unclear who held information or responsibility.

### Procedural Gap

A step between advice and decision is missing.

### Implementation Gap

A policy was announced but its delivery or experienced outcome is unclear.

### Attribution Gap

An outcome is known but the responsible actor or mechanism is not.

### Representation Gap

The source and its public representation differ materially.

### Classification Gap

Information is marked sensitive but the public record does not make the category of harm legible.

### Retrieval Gap

Public information nominally exists but cannot be reliably reconstructed through an interface or information tool.

Different gaps require different evidence.

Calling all of them **suspicious** tells us almost nothing.

## 🧵 Contradiction Is One Kind Of Gap

A direct contradiction looks something like:

```text
A says X
B says not-X
```

But:

```text
A says X
B says Y
```

may be:

- complementary;
- different in scope;
- temporally distinct;
- based on different definitions;
- unresolved;
- or contradictory only if another premise is established first.

> **Do not upgrade difference into contradiction merely because juxtaposition makes it exciting.**

Contradiction is a useful classification precisely because it should mean something narrower than *these things are not identical*.

## 🗺️ Map Before Explaining

Before deciding why actors differ, map what is actually present.

```text
who
 ↓
said what
 ↓
when
 ↓
based on what
 ↓
to whom
 ↓
through which container
 ↓
under which constraints
```

The same restraint applies to networks.

```text
ownership
≠ control

affiliation
≠ instruction

contact
≠ transfer

transfer
≠ coordination

beneficiary
≠ source
```

> **Map the topology before inventing the agency.**

An edge on the map means only what the evidence establishing that edge says it means.

## ❓ Turn The Gap Into A Question

A successful RSVP comparison often ends not with an answer but with a better question.

```text
RSVP A
+
RSVP B
   ↓
precise unresolved join
   ↓
QUESTION
```

For example:

> What records were included in the search?

> What evidence supported this policy proposition?

> Which institution supplied the analysis?

> Was the recommendation accepted?

> When did the classification rationale cease to apply?

> Are these two policy announcements materially the same?

> Which body held the record on the relevant date?

The objective is **question generation**, not story completion.

## 🧪 Ask A Question That Can Hurt Your Theory

A useful question permits an inconvenient answer.

If the working theory is that two policies were copied, the research design must permit the finding that both independently inherited longstanding departmental work.

If the theory is that information was deliberately leaked, it must permit an accidental, technical or mixed pathway.

If the theory is that a record was omitted, it must permit the finding that no such record exists.

If the theory is that two accounts contradict one another, it must permit the discovery that they were describing different populations.

> **An investigation which cannot produce an answer the investigator dislikes is not doing much investigating.**

Preserve the falsifier.

## 🏛️ Route The Question To The Right Institution

Not every gap is answerable through the same mechanism.

```text
GAP
 ↓
WHO COULD ACTUALLY KNOW?
 ↓
APPROPRIATE ROUTE
```

Possible routes include:

- parliamentary question;
- Freedom of Information request;
- Subject Access Request where appropriate;
- Select Committee inquiry;
- audit;
- departmental publication;
- regulator;
- court record;
- procurement record;
- Companies House;
- ministerial correspondence;
- journalistic inquiry;
- academic research;
- subsequent implementation data.

Do not ask a commentator to resolve a custody question which the institutional records can answer.

Do not ask a machine to infer a decision which the decision record can establish.

## 📮 The Answer Is Another RSVP

The method loops.

```text
GAP
 ↓
QUESTION
 ↓
ANSWER
 ↓
NEW RSVP
 ↓
compare again
 ↓
new gap?
 ↺
```

An answer can close the gap.

It can narrow it.

It can change its shape.

It can expose another institution.

It can contradict the working theory.

It can introduce a new definition.

It can establish that information is not held.

It can create another discrepancy.

There is no requirement that inquiry ends neatly.

Non-closure does not mean refusing conclusions.

It means refusing to close the system before the evidence warrants it.

## 🕸️ Nobody Has The God's-Eye View

Distributed information environments rarely contain one omniscient narrator.

Government may know things journalists do not.

Journalists may possess leaked material government has not publicly acknowledged.

Different departments may hold different records.

Opposition MPs may identify inconsistencies.

Researchers may connect public datasets.

Affected people may possess experiential evidence institutions do not.

Auditors may arrive later with broader access.

Courts may answer a narrower legal question.

```text
        [A]
      /     \
    [B]     [C]
      \     /
        [?]
      /     \
    [D]     [E]
```

The absence of a single actor with the complete view is not necessarily system failure.

It is a normal property of distributed information environments.

The governance question is whether the joins are **sufficiently legible and interrogable**.

## 🐣 Whose Feathers Are These?

An RSVP may contain information whose provenance predates the visible speaker.

It may contain:

- reused analysis;
- inherited policy;
- syndicated reporting;
- departmental evidence;
- think-tank language;
- consultation responses;
- previous journalism;
- machine-generated synthesis;
- ideas adapted from elsewhere.

So ask:

> **Whose information are we actually hearing?**

That is not necessarily the same question as:

> Who is speaking?

The visible messenger may be the originator.

They may instead be a carrier, editor, selector, synthesiser or final public interface.

Appearance is not provenance.

Yes yes. Very interesting.

Whose feathers are these?

## 🎠 Whose Calliopes?

The same principle applies to analytical production.

> **What information, institutions and analytical processes are sounding through this response?**

The actor delivering the RSVP may not be the origin of its:

- evidence;
- framing;
- policy idea;
- dataset;
- terminology;
- causal model.

This is why provenance travels through the entire folder.

We are not merely asking who said the sentence.

We are asking how the sentence came to exist.

## 🚨 Failure Modes

### The Single-Story Failure

```text
first coherent account
        ↓
accepted as master narrative
        ↓
later evidence interpreted
through it
```

**Countermeasure:** preserve multiple accounts before reconciliation.

### The Suspicious-Gap Failure

```text
missing information
        ↓
"why are they hiding it?"
```

**Countermeasure:** generate alternative explanations and seek discriminating evidence.

### The Echo-Chamber Failure

Ten responses derived from one source become "ten sources".

**Countermeasure:** trace evidential roots.

### The Compression Failure

A headline, card or summary loses conditionality and is later treated as verbatim source material.

**Countermeasure:** compare the representation with the underlying artefact.

### The Bad-Join Failure

Two records share a name, organisation, date or term and are treated as causally connected.

**Countermeasure:** establish the relationship independently.

### The Machine-Closure Failure

A retrieval system returns a plausible corpus and the analyst assumes it is complete.

**Countermeasure:** measure the retrieved corpus against the authoritative source.

### The Attribution Failure

An outcome becomes evidence of actor and motive.

**Countermeasure:**

```text
OUTCOME
 ↓
POSSIBLE MECHANISMS
 ↓
EVIDENCE
 ↓
ATTRIBUTION
```

The purpose of these controls is not to make investigation timid.

It is to make it harder to knock over.

## 🐙 CRAKEN

**Calcaneus Reflexion Anti-Kraken Ecological Navigation System**

RSVP analysis is particularly vulnerable to the Kraken because multiple responses generate lots of tempting edges.

Keep them separate.

```text
response
≠ event

representation
≠ source

difference
≠ contradiction

contradiction
≠ deception

gap
≠ concealment

omission
≠ deliberate omission

relationship
≠ coordination

ownership
≠ control

contact
≠ transfer

benefit
≠ knowledge

sequence
≠ causation

similarity
≠ copying

anomaly
≠ attribution

question
≠ finding

answer
≠ closure

multiple responses
≠ independent sources

retrieval result
≠ complete corpus
```

Then ask:

1. **What happened?**
2. **What are the information objects?**
3. **Who produced each one?**
4. **What question is each object actually answering?**
5. **What could that actor know?**
6. **What evidence does each rely upon?**
7. **Are apparently different answers actually comparable?**
8. **Where do they genuinely fail to join?**
9. **What alternative explanations exist?**
10. **What evidence would distinguish those explanations?**
11. **Who should possess it?**
12. **What bounded question can retrieve it?**
13. **What answer would make us revise our own model?**
14. **What does the new RSVP change?**

Do not answer uncertainty by manufacturing certainty.

Use the uncertainty to formulate the next question.

## 🏮 Sometimes The Answer Is Between Them

An RSVP tells us something.

Sometimes it tells us what happened. Sometimes it tells us what an institution believes happened, what a journalist could establish, what a politician wants examined, what a department's records contain, or what somebody thinks the available evidence means.

Put those responses beside one another before forcing them into a single story.

Some will corroborate. Some will contradict. Some will turn out to have been answering completely different questions.

And sometimes neither account contains the most useful information.

> **Sometimes the interesting information is in the gap between them.**

Do not fill the gap because it is uncomfortable.

Make it legible.

Ask what belongs there.

Then send another RSVP. 📮
