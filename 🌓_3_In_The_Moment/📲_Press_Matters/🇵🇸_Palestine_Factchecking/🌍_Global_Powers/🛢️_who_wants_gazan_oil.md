# 🛢️ Who Wants Gazan Oil?  
**First created:** 2026-09-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-20  
*An interest map for asking who benefits from the destruction of Gaza, where energy enters the decision-making environment, and what the press could reasonably interrogate without turning material interests into a single-cause conspiracy.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

**Well. First, it is mostly gas. Second, please take a number.**

There is a bad version of this question:

> *Was the war in Gaza secretly all about stealing the oil?*

That formulation is attractive because it promises one hidden cause underneath a conflict carrying more than a century of nationalism, imperial history, dispossession, occupation, security policy, religion, regional rivalry, Palestinian political division, Israeli politics, military strategy and international alliance.

It is also much too easy to knock down.

There is a second bad version:

> *The war plainly has other causes, therefore oil and gas are irrelevant.*

No.

Oil does not need to be **the reason** somebody acts in order to sway the environment in which decisions are made. Gas does not need to be **the cause** of a war in order to alter the value of territory, infrastructure, alliances, shipping routes, reconstruction plans or preferred political outcomes.

The useful journalistic question is therefore not:

> **Is this secretly about oil?**

It is:

> **Given the military action actually undertaken in Gaza, who has materially benefited, who has lost capability, whose leverage has increased, whose leverage has collapsed, and where --- if anywhere --- did energy security enter the decisions to undertake, enable, arm, finance, defend, restrict or continue that action?**

For this node, **support counts**.

The object of study is not limited to the people physically operating Israeli weapons inside Gaza. Decisions by other states to provide weapons, components, military assistance, intelligence or surveillance cooperation where documented, diplomatic protection, logistical support, finance, or other consequential forms of support are also decisions. They have their own policy environments. They can be investigated.

That does **not** mean every supporting state had the same role, motive or legal responsibility.

It means the journalist gets to ask:

> **What interests were in the room?**

And then has to do the annoying bit.

Find out.

## 1. 🧯 Before We Touch The Corkboard

This node uses a deliberately strict causal ladder.

``` text
A RESOURCE EXISTS
        ↓
AN ACTOR KNOWS IT EXISTS
        ↓
THE ACTOR HAS A MATERIAL INTEREST
        ↓
THE ACTOR HAS CAPABILITY TO AFFECT THE OUTCOME
        ↓
THE ACTOR TAKES AN ACTION RELEVANT TO THE RESOURCE
        ↓
THE RESOURCE MATERIALLY INFLUENCES THE DECISION
        ↓
THE RESOURCE IS A SIGNIFICANT CAUSAL FACTOR
```

Every arrow requires evidence.

We do not teleport from:

> **Chevron has an interest in Israeli gas**

to:

> **therefore Chevron caused Western policy on Gaza.**

We do not teleport from:

> **the European Union wants diversified Eastern Mediterranean gas**

to:

> **therefore European support for Israel was motivated by Gaza Marine.**

We do not teleport from:

> **an actor benefits from an outcome**

to:

> **therefore the actor sought or caused that outcome.**

But we also do not throw away the first five rungs because the seventh has not been proved.

**Benefit is evidence of benefit.\ Interest is evidence of interest.\ Capability is evidence of capability.\ Action is evidence of action.\ Intent requires evidence of intent.\ Causation requires evidence of causation.**

🧶 Corkboard privileges remain conditional.

## 2. 🛢️ Nothing Is "Just Oil"

Oil and gas are unusually good at becoming entangled with things that were already politically important.

They touch:

-   military capability;
-   electricity and industrial production;
-   transport;
-   government revenue;
-   foreign exchange;
-   corporate profit;
-   shipping;
-   ports;
-   pipelines;
-   refineries;
-   LNG terminals;
-   reconstruction;
-   alliance politics;
-   sanctions;
-   strategic reserves;
-   energy prices;
-   inflation;
-   domestic electoral politics;
-   climate and industrial-transition policy.

A territory can matter because of people, history, religion, borders, security and sovereignty **before anybody discovers a commercially useful hydrocarbon underneath or beside it**.

The discovery does not erase the earlier meanings.

It changes the incentive structure.

That gives us the rule for the entire node:

> **Oil does not have to explain a decision in order to change the choices available, the costs of those choices, the allies interested in them, or the preferred outcome.**

The question is not whether everything was *really* oil all along.

The question is:

> **When did energy enter the room, how much weight did it have, and what did it change?**

## 3. 🗺️ First Locate The Bloody Gas

Before geopolitics: geography.

A great deal of internet argument about "Gaza's gas" begins by putting several different gas fields into one conceptual bucket.

They are not the same field.

### 🇵🇸 Gaza Marine

Gaza Marine lies roughly 35--36 kilometres offshore Gaza.

The Palestinian National Authority granted the offshore licence in 1999 to British Gas and CC Oil & Gas. British Gas was operator. Gaza Marine-1 was drilled in 2000 and found commercial gas; Gaza Marine-2 appraised the discovery. CC Energy currently describes reserves as exceeding **1 trillion cubic feet**.

Following Shell's acquisition of BG and subsequent exit from the asset, the Palestinian Investment Fund and CC Oil & Gas/CCC became the principal Palestinian-side commercial interests. Public descriptions of the exact development structure have changed over time, so the final node should retain a dated ownership ledger rather than pretending one historical percentage is timeless.

**This is the field at the centre of this node.**

### 🇮🇱 Leviathan

Leviathan is a much larger Israeli offshore gas field farther north.

It is **not Gaza Marine**.

It is not made Palestinian by drawing a large blue circle around the Eastern Mediterranean on social media.

Its relevance is that it belongs to the same developing regional gas economy and shares corporate and infrastructure relationships with other Eastern Mediterranean projects.

### 🇮🇱 Tamar

Another major Israeli offshore gas field.

Again: not Gaza Marine.

### 🇨🇾 Aphrodite

Cypriot offshore gas.

Important because the corporate network crosses borders here: companies participating in Israeli fields can also participate in Cypriot development, while proposed export arrangements can converge on Egyptian infrastructure.

### 🇪🇬 Egypt

Egypt is not merely another country with gas.

Egypt has something every stranded gas field eventually develops strong feelings about:

**infrastructure.**

Pipelines.

Processing.

LNG terminals.

Export capacity.

A field under the sea is valuable.

A field connected to a buyer is more valuable.

## 4. 🫙 Having Gas Is Not The Same As Being Able To Use It

This distinction needs to sit near the top because it is where the power analysis begins.

``` text
GEOLOGICAL RESOURCE
        ↓
LEGAL / POLITICAL RIGHT
        ↓
LICENCE
        ↓
PHYSICAL ACCESS
        ↓
FINANCE
        ↓
OPERATOR
        ↓
EXTRACTION
        ↓
PIPELINE / PROCESSING
        ↓
BUYER
        ↓
MARKET
        ↓
REVENUE
```

Different actors can control different arrows.

A population can possess a recognised interest in a resource without possessing the military or infrastructural capacity to develop it.

A company can possess contractual rights without possessing sovereignty.

A state can possess military control without thereby acquiring lawful title.

A neighbouring state can make money from processing and transit without owning the upstream reservoir.

A distant market can influence what gets financed without ever owning the field.

That is why **"who owns the gas?" is only the first question**.

The more revealing questions are:

> **Who can stop it?**

> **Who can permit it?**

> **Who can finance it?**

> **Who can move it?**

> **Who can buy it?**

> **Who receives the money?**

## 5. 🇵🇸 Gaza Marine: The Palestinian Interest Comes First

One of the stranger effects of "great powers competing for Gaza gas" discourse is that Palestinians themselves can disappear from the story.

Gaza Marine has long been discussed as a potential Palestinian economic and energy asset.

UN Trade and Development has argued that restrictions on Palestinian development of oil and gas resources have imposed substantial opportunity costs and denied Palestinians potential revenue and energy benefits.

After the catastrophic destruction of Gaza's productive economy, UNCTAD has gone further: it has explicitly identified development of the offshore natural-gas fields as one possible means of helping finance reconstruction.

That matters.

Because the post-war question is not simply:

> **Who might profit from gas?**

It is also:

> **Could Palestinian control and development of Palestinian gas reduce Palestinian dependence on outside energy, aid and reconstruction finance?**

And if the answer is yes, another question follows:

> **Who gains or loses leverage when that autonomy becomes more or less possible?**

This is not a claim about why military action occurred.

It is an observable question about the distribution of capability after military action has destroyed much of Gaza's economy and infrastructure.

## 6. 🏗️ Gaza Marine: A Field That Has Spent Twenty-Five Years Not Becoming A Gas Field

The chronology matters because "Israel has just discovered Gaza's gas" is wrong, while "nobody was interested in Gaza Marine until the current war" is also wrong.

### 1999

The Palestinian National Authority grants the offshore exploration licence.

### 2000

Gaza Marine-1 discovers gas in commercial quantities.

Gaza Marine-2 appraises the discovery.

### 2000s

Plans repeatedly encounter political, commercial and security obstacles.

### 2007 onwards

The Hamas takeover of Gaza changes the political and security environment.

Israeli concerns include the destination of revenues and the possibility that money could reach Hamas.

BG's Israeli office closes in 2008.

### 2016

Shell acquires BG Group.

### 2018

Shell exits Gaza Marine.

Palestinian Investment Fund and CCC-related interests remain central to the asset and future development structure.

### 2022--23

Negotiations involving Palestinian, Egyptian and Israeli actors revive the possibility of development.

### June 2023

Israel announces approval in principle for development, subject to security and coordination arrangements.

This is important because it destroys a lazy chronology in which Gaza Marine suddenly becomes geopolitically interesting **after** 7 October 2023.

It was already being negotiated.

### October 2023 onwards

War transforms the entire material and political environment.

The question for the final evidence pass is:

> **What, precisely, happened to the 2023 development pathway after the war began?**

Not what people assume happened.

What happened.

## 7. 💥 Start With The Military Action: What Changed?

This is the journalistic centre of the node.

Do not begin by guessing motives.

Begin with consequences.

UNCTAD's 2026 assessment describes an extraordinary destruction of Palestinian productive capacity: Gaza's GDP in 2024 fell to around **13% of its 2022 level**, with economic activity showing no revival in 2025; infrastructure was widely destroyed and most businesses ceased operating.

So ask:

### What capability did Palestinians lose?

-   homes;
-   businesses;
-   productive infrastructure;
-   electricity and water systems;
-   transport;
-   administrative capacity;
-   fiscal capacity;
-   physical security;
-   bargaining power;
-   capacity to plan long-term development;
-   ability to finance reconstruction independently.

### What became more dependent on outside permission or finance?

-   reconstruction materials;
-   energy;
-   border access;
-   heavy equipment;
-   infrastructure;
-   external finance;
-   security arrangements;
-   governance arrangements;
-   maritime access;
-   long-term investment.

### What became more valuable?

Potentially:

-   reconstruction contracts;
-   energy supply;
-   infrastructure concessions;
-   border and logistics access;
-   security control;
-   external financing;
-   land;
-   ports and maritime arrangements;
-   offshore-resource development;
-   political authority over the reconstruction process.

The journalist's question is therefore:

> **Who gains leverage from the new dependencies created by destruction?**

And then:

> **Did any actor seek those dependencies?**

Those are separate questions.

## 8. 👀 Who Benefits?

"Who benefits?" is not an accusation.

It is an investigative starting point.

For every actor:

``` text
WHAT CHANGED?
      ↓
WHAT DID THE ACTOR GAIN OR LOSE?
      ↓
IS THE BENEFIT MATERIAL OR SPECULATIVE?
      ↓
DID THE ACTOR HAVE CAPABILITY TO SHAPE THE OUTCOME?
      ↓
DID THE ACTOR TAKE RELEVANT ACTION?
      ↓
IS THERE EVIDENCE THE BENEFIT INFLUENCED THAT ACTION?
```

The node should use a simple evidence register:

-   **🟢 Documented** --- contract, policy, licence, public statement,
transaction, official record.
-   **🟡 Analytical consequence** --- follows reasonably from documented
arrangements but is not itself evidence of motive.
-   **🟠 Contested interpretation** --- credible disagreement exists
over meaning or legal consequence.
-   **🔴 Unsupported causal claim** --- the evidence supplied does not
establish the proposed motive or causal chain.
-   **⚪ Open question** --- worth investigating; evidence not yet
located.

The point is not to make the diagram look exciting.

The point is to make every line earn its existence.

## 9. 🇮🇱 Israel: Several Interests, Not One

Israel has substantial energy interests that exist independently of Gaza Marine.

Those include:

-   domestic gas supply;
-   Leviathan;
-   Tamar;
-   exports to regional buyers;
-   relationships with Egypt and Jordan;
-   security of offshore infrastructure;
-   regional energy diplomacy.

Israel also has a direct political and security interest in what happens offshore Gaza because Gaza Marine cannot be developed in a vacuum separated from Israeli-Palestinian relations, maritime access and security arrangements.

But the node must distinguish:

> **Israel has an interest in the conditions under which Gaza Marine is developed**

from:

> **Israel seeks ownership of Gaza Marine**

and again from:

> **Israel undertook military action in order to acquire Gaza Marine.**

Those are three different claims.

The first is easy to document.

The second requires evidence.

The third requires substantially more.

The useful journalistic work sits in the space between them.

## 10. 🇪🇬 Egypt: Sometimes The Route Is The Asset

Egypt deserves a very large box.

Its potential interests include:

-   domestic gas supply;
-   regional imports;
-   pipeline throughput;
-   gas processing;
-   LNG utilisation;
-   export revenue;
-   regional energy-hub status;
-   relations with Israel;
-   relations with Palestinian authorities;
-   European energy demand.

The European Commission's 2022 EU--Egypt--Israel gas memorandum explicitly envisaged natural gas from Israel, Egypt **and other Eastern Mediterranean sources** reaching Europe through Egyptian LNG infrastructure.

That is not evidence that the EU planned to acquire Gaza Marine.

It is evidence that the physical and commercial architecture into which an eventual Gaza Marine development might fit was already being built.

Egypt demonstrates the node's central point beautifully:

> **You do not need to own the reservoir to acquire strategic importance from the route.**

## 11. 🇪🇺 Europe: Please Select Which European Interest You Mean

"The EU wants gas" is true at one level and useless at another.

The EU's post-2022 energy strategy has explicitly sought diversification away from Russian gas.

The Eastern Mediterranean became one potential source.

The EU--Egypt--Israel memorandum describes gas security and stable deliveries to Europe while simultaneously stating that European natural-gas use should decline after 2030 in line with climate-neutrality commitments.

So the EU can simultaneously possess:

### An energy-security interest

More diversified supply.

### A geopolitical interest

Less dependence on Russia.

### A Mediterranean infrastructure interest

Reliable routes and regional cooperation.

### A climate interest

Less fossil-fuel consumption over time.

### A Palestinian-policy interest

Economic development, humanitarian policy and a viable Palestinian political future.

Those interests can align.

They can also collide.

**Competing policy objectives are not evidence of a conspiracy.**

They are, however, excellent material for journalists.

## 12. 🇩🇪 Germany Is Not Merely A Small EU Box

Germany needs to be separated from the aggregate EU position.

Germany has:

-   its own industrial energy demand;
-   its own post-Russian-gas vulnerability;
-   bilateral relationships with Israel;
-   relationships with Egypt and Gulf suppliers;
-   LNG interests;
-   hydrogen and transition policy;
-   its own arms-export decisions;
-   its own political relationship with Israel and Palestine.

And Germany materially supported Israel through arms-export approvals after 7 October 2023.

The Bundestag recorded approvals worth approximately **€485 million** between 7 October 2023 and 13 May 2025 across a wide range of military categories.

That does not tell us why any particular export was approved.

It tells us there is a decision to investigate.

The question is:

> **When Germany weighed continued material support for Israel, what strategic interests were included in the assessment?**

Energy security may have been irrelevant.

It may have been background.

It may have affected broader regional strategy without affecting arms decisions.

It may appear explicitly in internal documents.

**Find out.**

## 13. 🇺🇸 America: Support Counts

The United States is not an observer to this story.

US support for Israel after 7 October 2023 included major military assistance and arms transfers. Congressional Research Service material records emergency arms-sale procedures used by the Biden administration in December 2023, including tank ammunition and artillery shells.

For this node, that means US decision-making is a legitimate object of investigation.

The question is not:

> **Did American troops conduct the IDF campaign?**

The question is:

> **What considerations shaped American decisions to arm, fund, diplomatically support, constrain, or continue supporting Israel during the campaign?**

Those considerations plainly include many non-energy factors.

But energy belongs on the list of things worth checking because the same period sits inside a wider transformation of American and global energy politics.

## 14. 🇺🇸🛢️ Drill Baby Drill Has Entered The Decision Environment

By 2025 the Trump administration had made **"energy dominance"** an explicit organising concept of US policy.

The White House's National Energy Dominance Council order connected expanded oil, gas, coal, uranium and other energy production to economic prosperity, national security, manufacturing, artificial intelligence and American geopolitical leverage.

By 2026 the administration was openly describing record LNG exports and expanded fossil-fuel development as achievements of its "Drill Baby Drill" agenda.

That does **not** retroactively explain Biden-era support for Israel.

It does show that the wider American political environment during the Gaza war and its aftermath contains a very visible struggle over:

-   fossil-fuel production;
-   LNG exports;
-   renewables;
-   climate regulation;
-   industrial policy;
-   strategic energy independence;
-   use of energy as geopolitical leverage.

That context belongs in the node because we are asking what energy assumptions Western governments were carrying while making decisions about the region.

## 15. 🌊 The Ocean Observatories Detour Is Not Entirely A Detour

The US National Science Foundation's Ocean Observatories Initiative became a revealing little side story in 2026.

NSF began removing or "descoping" parts of a large ocean-observation system relied upon by researchers and other users.

Following substantial opposition, NSF announced on 18 June 2026 that it would **stop further removal or descoping**, continue operations and planned maintenance, and develop plans to redeploy the Endurance Array equipment that had already been removed.

This is not evidence about Gaza.

It is useful contextual evidence for a wider political environment in which environmental monitoring, climate infrastructure and public scientific capacity could be treated as expendable while fossil-fuel "energy dominance" was being elevated.

Do not make the OOI prove more than it proves.

It is atmosphere.

Not causation.

## 16. 🚢 Hormuz: Sometimes The Energy Question Is Not Subtle

The Strait of Hormuz is the opposite of a speculative energy connection.

It is one of the world's major oil and LNG chokepoints.

US Energy Information Administration data for the first half of 2025 put flows through Hormuz at about **20.9 million barrels per day**, roughly **20% of global petroleum-liquids consumption**, alongside more than **20% of global LNG trade**.

This is the larger regional material environment.

Iran.

Gulf producers.

Qatar LNG.

Asian demand.

European vulnerability.

Shipping.

War risk.

Insurance.

Strategic reserves.

A conflict that destabilises the Gulf does not merely create a foreign-policy problem.

It can create an energy-security problem almost immediately.

So when Western governments make Middle Eastern strategic decisions, it is entirely reasonable for journalists to ask:

> **What assumptions about oil, LNG, shipping and chokepoint risk appear in the briefing papers?**

## 17. 🇨🇳 China Came With Snacks

China is useful here because strategic preparation can be measured.

EIA estimates that China added an average of roughly **1.1 million barrels per day** to strategic oil inventories during 2025, reaching nearly **1.4 billion barrels** by December 2025, and continued building inventories into early 2026.

EIA cautions that Chinese inventory data are not fully transparent and its estimate includes categories treated differently from some other national reserves.

Still:

China entered the 2026 Hormuz crisis with very substantial estimated stocks.

The United States also maintained a Strategic Petroleum Reserve, and IEA states maintain their own emergency arrangements.

So the question is not:

> **China planned everything because it bought oil.**

Please.

The question is:

> **How prepared were different powers for disruption, and did differing levels of energy vulnerability affect their appetite for regional risk?**

That is an ordinary strategic question.

## 19. 🌹 Rosebank And 🐦‍⬛ Jackdaw: Britain Has Its Own Hydrocarbon Argument

Rosebank and Jackdaw should not be inserted into the node as if a North Sea field secretly explains Gaza.

-   domestic hydrocarbon production;
-   climate obligations;
-   energy security;
-   imports;
-   industrial policy;
-   jobs;
-   taxation;
-   emissions assessment.

Both projects remained live regulatory objects in 2026.

Government pages for Rosebank and Jackdaw show continuing environmental-assessment and public-notice processes during 2026 following the earlier legal disruption to their approvals.

The comparison therefore asks:

> **What principles does Britain invoke when deciding whether hydrocarbons should be developed at home, and what principles does it invoke when discussing resource rights, occupation, sovereignty and development elsewhere?**

Not because the legal regimes are the same.

## 20. 🇫🇰🇦🇷 Oh Good, Another Sea

The Falklands/Malvinas--Sea Lion dispute is not Palestine.

The legal histories are different.

The sovereignty claims are different.

The populations and political contexts are different.

**Good.**

That makes it a comparator rather than an analogy pretending to be identity.

In September 2026 Argentina filed a criminal complaint against Israeli energy company Navitas Petroleum and executives over operations around the Falkland Islands/Malvinas under licences Argentina rejects.

Sea Lion is operated by Navitas, with Rockhopper holding the remaining major stake.

Argentina frames the issue through sovereignty and unauthorised exploitation.

The Falkland Islands authorities and UK reject Argentina's claim to authority over the licences.

So ask:

> **What principle does each government say determines who may grant rights to offshore hydrocarbons in disputed or contested political geography?**

Then:

> **Does the same government use the same principle elsewhere?**

If not:

> **What legal or factual distinction does it rely upon?**

That is a factchecking question.

Not a "gotcha".

## 26. 💥 And Then People Attack The Pipeline

During the 1936--39 Arab Revolt, pipeline infrastructure became a target.

British authorities had to treat energy infrastructure as part of the security environment.

This demonstrates an important direction of causality:

> **Oil does not have to cause the political conflict to change how the conflict is fought.**

Once infrastructure becomes strategically important:

-   it requires protection;
-   its disruption creates costs;
-   communities around it become security terrain;
-   military and policing priorities can change;
-   routes become political objects.

Energy can enter a conflict **after the conflict already exists**.

That is precisely why "was it all about oil?" is such a bad question.

## 27. 🇵🇸 Zionism, Britain And Oil: Keep The Chronology Straight

The node should explicitly reject:

> **Britain pushed Zionism into Palestine because it wanted the Kirkuk--Haifa pipeline.**

The chronology does not support that.

Political Zionism's focus on Palestine predates the pipeline by decades.

The Balfour Declaration was issued in 1917.

The famous Kirkuk--Haifa pipeline belongs to the Mandate-era petroleum system developed later.

But another proposition is worth investigating:

> **An existing Zionist project centred on Palestine intersected with a British imperial state that had its own strategic reasons for wanting influence in Palestine and that increasingly valued Middle Eastern petroleum and routes to the Mediterranean.**

Those interests need not have had the same origin to become mutually relevant.

The historical questions are therefore:

-   What did British policymakers say Palestine was for in 1917?
-   What weight did they give Suez?
-   Egypt?
-   France?
-   India?
-   wartime diplomacy?
-   Zionism?
-   future imperial communications?
-   Mesopotamia?
-   petroleum?
-   and when does oil become explicit rather than inferred?

**Do not back-propagate the pipeline.**

Write that on the wall.

## 28. 🧱 1948 Breaks The Energy Map Too

Political change alters infrastructure.

After the creation of Israel and the first Arab--Israeli war, the Kirkuk--Haifa route ceased normal operation.

A 1953 US diplomatic record describes the Haifa pipeline as not operating because Iraq embargoed oil shipments to Israel.

Earlier US records show that Washington regarded reopening the Haifa refinery and restoring Iraqi crude flows as economically desirable and diplomatically significant.

British officials likewise worried about the economic cost of the refinery's non-operation.

So here the direction reverses:

> **Politics changes energy infrastructure.**

The relationship runs both ways.

Energy changes incentives.

Political conflict changes energy routes.

Then the new routes change later incentives.

No singular master cause required.

## 36. 🧨 Claims To Put Through The Machine

### "The war is about stealing Gaza's oil."

**Problem:** singular causal claim; usually wrong resource; requires evidence of motive and causation.

**Investigate instead:** who benefits materially, what energy interests existed, and whether decision records connect those interests to support for military action.

### "There is no energy angle."

**Problem:** demonstrably too strong. Gaza Marine exists; East Mediterranean gas strategy exists; Palestinian resource-development interests exist; European diversification interests exist.

**But:** existence of an energy angle does not establish that it drove military decisions.

### "Leviathan is Gaza's gas."

No.

Different field.

### "Western governments support Israel because they want Gaza Marine."

Possible claim about motive requiring evidence.

Do not infer it merely from European gas strategy.

Ask for documents.

### "Gaza Marine could finance reconstruction."

Potentially relevant and explicitly proposed by UNCTAD, but scale, development cost, timing, governance, market arrangements and revenue distribution need to be quantified.

### "Britain backed Zionism because it wanted the Kirkuk--Haifa pipeline."

Chronologically defective if stated that simply.

Zionism's Palestine orientation and Balfour predate the later pipeline.

Investigate the broader intersection of British imperial strategy, Suez, Mesopotamia and increasingly important petroleum interests instead.

### "Argentina proves Britain is hypocritical."

No.

Argentina supplies a comparator.

The legal situations differ.

The job is to identify which principles Britain says distinguish them.

### "The fossil-fuel resurgence proves Gaza was an oil war."

No.

It establishes a decision-making environment worth interrogating.

The causal bridge still needs evidence.

## 38. 🦑 What The Kraken Actually Cares About

The Kraken is not here to announce that oil secretly controls history.

The Kraken wants the chain.

``` text
ACTOR
  ↓
RELATIONSHIP
  ↓
RESOURCE
  ↓
AUTHORITY / CAPABILITY
  ↓
PATHWAY
  ↓
OBSERVABLE CONSEQUENCE
```

Then, if somebody wants to make a causal claim:

``` text
OBSERVABLE BENEFIT
        ↓
EVIDENCE OF PREFERENCE
        ↓
EVIDENCE OF ACTION
        ↓
EVIDENCE ACTION WAS TAKEN FOR THAT PURPOSE
        ↓
CAUSAL CLAIM
```

This is why **support counts** but support does not automatically establish motive.

This is why **oil sways people** but does not automatically explain them.

This is why **who benefits?** is worth asking even when the answer does not tell us who caused the event.

And this is why a press corps can interrogate material interests without becoming Infowars with a pipeline map.

## 39. 🌍 The Larger Question

The deeper investigation is not:

> **Who wants Gazan oil?**

It is:

> **Who wants which energy outcome?**

Palestinians can want a Palestinian resource developed for Palestinian revenue and energy autonomy.

Israel can want secure domestic production, exports and control over security conditions around Gaza.

Egypt can want gas, processing and throughput.

The EU can want diversified supply while also wanting eventual decarbonisation.

Germany can have its own industrial, energy and Israel-policy interests inside the wider EU position.

Britain can have North Sea interests, historical Gaza Marine connections, regional security interests and its own resource-sovereignty arguments elsewhere.

The United States can support Israel while simultaneously pursuing LNG exports, fossil-fuel production, Gulf security and wider geopolitical strategy.

Argentina can challenge an Israeli company and a British-backed offshore project in an entirely different sovereignty dispute.

Companies can want profitable fields.

Reconstruction firms can want contracts.

Donors can want governance conditions.

Governments can want stability.

Populations can want electricity.

These interests can align in one theatre and collide in another.

**That is not evidence of one coordinated plan.**

It is evidence that the world contains interests.

Journalism is supposed to map them.

## 40. 🪶 The Sentence To Keep

If the whole node has to collapse into one paragraph, keep this:

> **Nothing here requires the war in Gaza to be "just about oil". Gaza already carried immense political, territorial, national, military, religious and humanitarian significance. But oil and gas do not need to be the sole cause of an action to influence the decision-making environment around it. Gaza Marine is a real Palestinian gas resource; neighbouring Israeli and Cypriot fields feed a real Eastern Mediterranean energy system; Egypt provides real export infrastructure; Europe has explicitly sought diversified gas from the region; Western states made real decisions to provide or restrict material support to Israel; and the destruction of Gaza has created real new dependencies around reconstruction, energy, finance and governance. The journalistic task is therefore not to declare an oil conspiracy. It is to ask who benefits from the resulting arrangements, what interests were present when support decisions were made, and whether the documentary record connects those interests to those decisions.**

That is enough.

Everything else is evidence.
