# 🇵🇸 Palestine Factchecking
**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*Evidence-first routing for recurring questions about Palestine and Israel across history, current events, law, statistics, identity, global power, ethics and the information environment.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder exists because questions about Palestine and Israel arrive carrying several different problems at once.

A single prompt can contain:

- a historical claim;
- a breaking-news claim;
- a legal proposition;
- a casualty statistic;
- an identity category;
- a translation dispute;
- a claim about American, British or regional involvement;
- a moral judgement;
- and an assumption about why particular information is visible in the first place.

Those questions cannot be handled well by throwing everything into one argument.

So this pack separates them.

The aim is not to manufacture one authorised narrative.

It is to make claims easier to inspect.

That means asking:

> **What exactly is being claimed?**

> **What kind of question is it?**

> **What evidence would answer it?**

> **What does the evidence actually establish?**

> **What remains uncertain, contested, interpretive or moral?**

And, increasingly:

> **What information environment is shaping the question before we even begin answering it?**

## 🧭 The Routing Principle

Before answering a question, identify its **primary claim type**.

```text
What happened before?
        ↓
      HISTORY

What is happening now?
        ↓
   CURRENT EVENTS

What does the law say?
        ↓
        LAW

What do the numbers establish?
        ↓
    STATISTICS

What does this word / identity mean?
        ↓
IDENTITY & LANGUAGE

What are outside powers doing?
        ↓
  GLOBAL POWERS

What should we think is right or wrong?
        ↓
      ETHICS

Why is this information appearing,
disappearing, repeating or changing behaviour?
        ↓
EMBODYING THE INFORMATION ECOLOGY
```

Many questions will need more than one route.

That is fine.

The point is to stop one category silently doing the work of another.

   ↓
revised
   ↓

The fuller explanation lives in:

- advocacy;
- public diplomacy;
- lobbying;
- commercial interests;
- platform governance;
- search optimisation;
- multilingual reporting;
- legal pressure;
- and increasingly explicit attempts to shape AI-facing information environments.

Israel is not unique in using public diplomacy.

Nor are Israel-related organisations unique in lobbying, PR, search optimisation or strategic communications.

Businesses, banks, governments, political organisations and campaign groups routinely try to influence how they are perceived.

The methodological consequence is not:

> **therefore everything is propaganda.**

It is:

> **therefore provenance, incentives, source ancestry and independent corroboration matter.**

## ⚖️ Core Factchecking Principles

The detailed rules live in:

**[⚖️ Factchecking Principles](./🌾_Start_Here/⚖️_factchecking_principles.md)**

At root, the pack should follow a few durable principles.

### 1. Define the claim

Do not factcheck a mood.

Write down the proposition.

### 2. Match evidence to the claim

A source can be credible and still fail to answer the question being asked.

### 3. Prefer provenance over repetition

Find the upstream evidence.

### 4. Preserve dates

Especially for breaking events, law, casualty figures and policy.

### 5. Preserve verbs

Reported, alleged, verified, estimated, identified, ruled, argued and confirmed are not synonyms.

### 6. Separate fact from inference

Say when a conclusion is analytical rather than directly evidenced.

### 7. Preserve uncertainty

“Unknown” is a legitimate result.

### 8. Correct visibly

Revision is part of evidence work.

### 9. Apply standards across identity

Do not lower or raise the evidential threshold because the claim is politically congenial.

### 10. Do not outsource judgement to AI

AI can assist with retrieval, comparison, translation and structure.

It is not provenance.

## 📚 Source Architecture

The developing source infrastructure lives in:

**[📚 Sources & Evidence Register](./🌾_Start_Here/📚_sources_and_evidence_register.md)**

The register should eventually help distinguish:

- primary documents;
- official records;
- court material;
- legislation;
- international institutions;
- datasets;
- academic literature;
- investigations;
- journalism;
- specialist NGOs;
- advocacy organisations;
- think tanks;
- eyewitness material;
- social media;
- corporate material;
- lobbying disclosures;
- PR material;
- and AI-generated synthesis.

The question is not merely:

> **Is this a good source?**

It is:

> **A good source for what proposition?**

A military may be the primary source for what it says it did.

That does not make its account independent verification.

An advocacy organisation may possess excellent specialist documentation.

That does not make its institutional perspective irrelevant.

A newspaper may be highly reputable while relying on the same upstream source as five other outlets.

Source class and evidential function both matter.

## 🦎 Limitations Of The Author

The fuller limitations note lives in:

**[🦎 Limitations Of The Author](./🌾_Start_Here/🦎_limitations_of_the_author.md)**

This pack should not pretend the author is:

- omniscient;
- institutionally neutral;
- a substitute for a lawyer;
- a native speaker of every relevant language;
- physically present at every event;
- or able to independently verify every classified, inaccessible or destroyed record.

Authorial position should be disclosed where it materially affects interpretation.

But “bias” should not be used as a magical solvent that dissolves evidence.

The appropriate response to perspective is method:

- show sources;
- show reasoning;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- preserve uncertainty;
- and make correction possible.

## 🧬 The Claim Stack

A useful way to diagnose difficult questions is to break them into layers.

For example:

> **“Was X genocide?”**

may contain:

```text
What happened?
      ↓
CURRENT EVENTS / HISTORY

How many people were affected?
      ↓
STATISTICS

What evidence exists about intent?
      ↓
CURRENT EVENTS / PRIMARY SOURCES

What is the legal test?
      ↓
LAW

Has a competent institution ruled?
      ↓
LAW

How is the term being used politically?
      ↓
IDENTITY & LANGUAGE / INFORMATION ECOLOGY

What moral judgement is the speaker making?
      ↓
ETHICS
```

Likewise:

> **“Why does ChatGPT say X about Israel?”**

may require:

```text
What answer was actually produced?
      ↓
DOCUMENT THE OUTPUT

What sources or retrieval routes are visible?
      ↓
INFORMATION ECOLOGY

Is the underlying factual claim correct?
      ↓
RELEVANT DOMAIN BRANCH

Are actors trying to influence AI-facing information?
      ↓
GLOBAL POWERS / INFORMATION ECOLOGY

Is there evidence that a specific campaign caused this answer?
      ↓
MECHANISM + EFFECT TEST
```

This is the basic architecture of the pack.

Complex questions become tractable when the layers stop pretending to be one question.

## 🧯 What This Pack Is Not

This folder is not intended to become:

- a live-fire argument archive;
- a collection of screenshots proving that strangers are stupid;
- a replacement for legal advice;
- a replacement for specialist historical scholarship;
- a replacement for Arabic or Hebrew expertise;
- a casualty database maintained without methodology;
- a list of approved political opinions;
- a repository where every allegation is preserved as fact;
- or a machine for turning uncertainty into certainty because certainty feels safer.

It is also not designed to create artificial “balance” by placing a weak claim beside a strong one and calling the symmetry fairness.

The standard is evidence.

Not team allocation.

## 🚧 Recurring Failure Modes

Across the whole pack, watch for:

- collapsing history into present politics;
- collapsing identity into ideology;
- collapsing law into morality;
- collapsing morality into law;
- treating allegations as findings;
- treating adult men as combatants by default;
- treating civilians as morally irrelevant because of political views;
- treating statistics without denominators as self-explanatory;
- treating revised figures as automatic proof of fraud;
- treating government statements as independent verification;
- treating activist statements as automatically false;
- treating ten repetitions as ten sources;
- mistranslating slogans;
- reading modern definitions backwards into history;
- treating influence as control;
- treating funding as proof of falsehood;
- treating lack of search visibility as proof of suppression;
- treating visible PR activity as proof that every downstream answer was caused by it;
- treating AI synthesis as source material;
- and deciding what must have happened before checking what can actually be shown.

## 🧭 A Quick Route For Readers

If you arrive with a question, use this sequence:

1. **Write the claim in one sentence.**
2. **Identify the primary branch.**
3. **Check whether the claim depends on another branch.**
4. **Find the earliest or most primary available evidence.**
5. **Check date, definition and procedural status.**
6. **Look for genuinely independent corroboration.**
7. **Separate what is established from what is inferred.**
8. **Record uncertainty.**
9. **Only then move to legal, political or moral conclusions.**
10. **If the information environment itself is part of the question, map the mechanism rather than guessing it.**

That is slower than reposting.

Good.

## 🛠️ Build Order From Here

The sensible next stage is not to create hundreds of empty nodes.

It is to:

- collect recurring questions;
- deduplicate them;
- route each question to a primary branch;
- identify cross-branch dependencies;
- build source cards for recurring evidence;
- and draft substantive nodes where multiple questions share the same factual spine.

That allows the architecture to emerge from use.

It also reduces the risk of building an enormous taxonomy that nobody actually needs.

## 🪞 Epistemic Position

This archive begins from a simple proposition:

> **People affected by war deserve better than information systems that require them to choose between propaganda and incuriosity.**

That does not mean every dispute has a clean answer.

Some claims will remain:

- contested;
- partly evidenced;
- inaccessible;
- dependent on classified information;
- legally unresolved;
- statistically uncertain;
- linguistically ambiguous;
- or morally irreducible.

The archive should be able to say that.

Precision is not weakness.

Uncertainty is not surrender.

And refusing to invent certainty is not the same thing as refusing to judge anything at all.
