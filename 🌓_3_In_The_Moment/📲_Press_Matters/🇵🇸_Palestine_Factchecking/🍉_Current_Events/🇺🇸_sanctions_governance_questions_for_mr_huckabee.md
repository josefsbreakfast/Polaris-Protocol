## 🛰️ Orientation

On 8 September 2026, the UK joined a wider group of states announcing or supporting restrictions on trade in goods with Israeli settlements in the occupied West Bank. The UK, France and Canada said they would bring forward national measures to ban trade in settlement goods. The policy is deliberately narrower than an embargo on Israel: it distinguishes economic activity in settlements regarded by the UK and most of the international community as illegal under international law from ordinary trade with Israel.

US Ambassador to Israel Mike Huckabee responded with unusually expansive economic warnings.

Speaking to the BBC, he asked why Britain was not imposing comparable sanctions on North Korea, China and Russia; warned that there would be American repercussions; and suggested that Florida law could mean British businesses would no longer be able to do business in the state.

There are several different propositions bundled together there.

They should be unbundled.

This node does not begin from the proposition that Huckabee is lying, nor from the proposition that there can be no economic consequences. It asks the more useful question:

> **If there is a dependency, show us.**

Which British measure activates which American law? Which British businesses become exposed? Which American industries depend upon settlement commerce? Which banks, investment funds, public bodies or private fortunes carry material exposure? What is the transmission
mechanism from a targeted settlement restriction to the "huge economic impact" being warned about?

That is an economic question before it is a rhetorical one.

## 🧮 What Britain actually announced

The joint statement of 8 September 2026 was signed by the foreign  ministers of Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Iceland, Ireland, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Spain, Sweden and the UK.

It said that participating states would introduce national restrictions, support European restrictions, or actively consider measures restricting trade in goods with settlements regarded as illegal under international law. The UK, France and Canada committed to national measures banning trade in settlement goods.

This matters because the object of the policy is not:

**Israel.**

It is:

**economic activity associated with settlements in occupied territory.**

That distinction is not incidental. It is the policy.

Any criticism that treats a settlement-goods restriction as automatically equivalent to an embargo on Israeli goods generally needs to explain why the legal and geographic distinction should be disregarded.

## ❓ "Where are the sanctions against North Korea, China, Russia?"

This is the easiest part to check.

### Russia

Britain has an extensive Russia sanctions regime and was still adding designations in September 2026. On 16 June 2026 alone, the UK announced 70 new Russia-related sanctions and said it had sanctioned almost 500 individuals, entities and ships under the Russia regime during 2026 alone.

So the answer to "where are the sanctions against Russia?" is:

**They are already there. In enormous quantity.**

### North Korea

The UK also has a broad Democratic People's Republic of Korea sanctions regime implementing multiple UN Security Council resolutions and additional UK purposes.

The regime includes financial sanctions, asset freezes, restrictions concerning DPRK financial institutions, trade restrictions, transport restrictions, controls on ships and aircraft, and prohibitions across substantial categories of goods, technology and services.

So the answer to "where are the sanctions against North Korea?" is also:

**They are already there.**

### China

China is the genuinely different comparison.

The UK maintains a very large trading relationship with China --- total UK-China trade was approximately £104.8 billion in 2025 --- while also imposing targeted sanctions against particular Chinese officials, companies and activities.

Examples include:

-   sanctions against four Chinese officials and a Xinjiang security body in 2021 over gross human-rights violations;
-   sanctions against a China state-affiliated company and two individuals in 2024 over malicious cyber activity;
-   sanctions against two China-based technology companies in December 2025 over cyberattacks.

That is not contradictory. It demonstrates the ordinary logic of targeted sanctions:

> **A government can maintain a substantial economic relationship with a country while sanctioning particular people, companies, conduct or networks associated with it.**

That is much closer to the structure of the British settlement policy than either a Russian or North Korean comparison.

## 🧿 The Florida claim needs reading, not vibes

Huckabee's most concrete warning concerned Florida.

He told the BBC that if Britain took the proposed action, British businesses would not be able to do business "with any company or business in Florida."

Florida does have unusually broad anti-boycott legislation.

But that sentence is not an adequate description of what the statute says.

### Florida's 2026 statutory structure

Florida Statute §215.4725 defines a "boycott of Israel" broadly enough to include actions limiting commercial relations with Israel or with persons or entities doing business in Israel **or Israeli-controlled territories**, where the action is considered discriminatory.

The 2026 version is important because its definition of an "other entity" expressly includes a **foreign government**, alongside public investment funds, sovereign wealth funds, educational institutions and nonprofit organisations.

That means the British policy cannot simply be waved away as legally irrelevant to Florida. There is a genuine question about how Florida's State Board of Administration will classify the UK government's action.

But the consequences still need to be described accurately.

The statute creates a **Scrutinized Companies or Other Entities that Boycott Israel List** and provides for public-fund investment restrictions and divestment.

Separately, Florida Statute §287.135 restricts **public contracting** by Florida agencies and local governmental entities with companies or other entities on the list or engaged in a covered boycott.

That is materially different from:

> every private company in Florida is legally prohibited from doing business with every British company.

The statute may create real consequences for:

-   British companies bidding for Florida public contracts;
-   entities appearing on Florida's scrutinised list;
-   Florida public investment exposure;
-   foreign-government debt or public investment vehicles where statutory conditions are met.

Those consequences deserve proper modelling.

But **state procurement and public-investment restrictions are not the same thing as a general private-sector trade embargo on Britain**.

### The useful Florida questions

Before repeating "huge economic impact," ask:

1.  Will Florida formally determine that the UK government's settlement policy constitutes a covered boycott under §215.4725?
2.  Which UK entities would actually be listed?
3.  Does classification of the British government automatically classify unrelated British private companies?
4.  Which existing Florida public contracts with British companies would become vulnerable?
5.  What is their total annual value?
6.  Which Florida public funds currently hold affected British sovereign, public-fund or corporate assets?
7.  What private British-Florida commerce remains completely untouched?
8.  What exemptions, review mechanisms or constitutional/federal constraints apply?

Those are answerable questions.

## 💰 Treasury problem, central-bank problem, or political problem?

These are not interchangeable.

### Treasury as sanctions administrator

Treasury can care enormously about a transaction without that transaction being macroeconomically important.

OFAC sanctions affect:

-   blocked property;
-   dollar transactions;
-   correspondent relationships;
-   compliance systems;
-   designated counterparties;
-   financial institutions' screening obligations.

That is a **sanctions-enforcement problem**.

It is not automatically a **US-economy problem**.

### The United States has already run West Bank sanctions

This is an especially useful control.

In February 2024, the Biden administration created Executive Order 14115 and imposed sanctions concerning people undermining peace, security and stability in the West Bank.

OFAC subsequently designated individuals and entities including settlement-linked organisations and businesses.

On 20 January 2025, President Trump revoked that executive order. OFAC then removed the West Bank sanctions programme and unblocked property previously designated under it.

Whatever one's political view of either decision, the history demonstrates something analytically important:

> **The US Treasury system itself has already administered targeted sanctions against settlement-linked actors.**

That makes it difficult to argue that the *concept* of targeted West Bank sanctions is inherently incompatible with the functioning of the American financial system.

### Federal Reserve / systemic-risk question

A genuinely systemic American exposure would normally leave evidence somewhere in:

-   material bank exposures;
-   payment-system dependence;
-   dollar-liquidity transmission;
-   important commodity or technology supply;
-   inflation/output modelling;
-   financial-stability reporting;
-   strategically significant corporate dependencies.

At present, this node has not identified evidence that settlement-linked commerce reaches that threshold.

That is not a declaration that no such exposure exists.

It is an invitation to produce it.

## 🔬 The United States itself identified financial-crime red flags

This is where the conversation becomes much more interesting.

In February 2024, the US Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network --- FinCEN --- issued an alert specifically concerning the **financing of Israeli extremist settler violence against Palestinians in the West Bank**.

It told US financial institutions to look for suspicious activity and supplied red flags.

Those included:

-   payments to organisations, including nonprofits, linked to violent extremist groups;
-   transaction references indicating support for violent extremist groups or campaigns;
-   rapid movement of funds with no apparent lawful or economic purpose involving nonprofits supporting violent extremist settlers;
-   purchases of tactical military equipment destined for non-government Israeli end-users linked to violent extremist groups.

In July 2024, FinCEN issued a supplemental alert adding further indicators including:

-   payments involving addresses or accounts linked to West Bank outposts;
-   payments to accounts held by violent extremist settler groups or implicated individuals;
-   fundraising for sanctioned or convicted violent extremists;
-   shared merchant representatives, contact details or addresses with designated entities;
-   crowdfunding associated with outposts, herding farms or violent campaigns.

FinCEN explicitly requested a dedicated SAR identifier:

**FIN-2024-WBEXTREMISM**

The alerts were rescinded on 29 January 2025 after the Trump administration revoked the underlying West Bank sanctions programme.

That chronology matters.

It means that American financial-crime authorities did not merely regard settlement-linked financing as a political disagreement.

For a period, they regarded specific patterns within it as sufficiently concerning to warrant **dedicated suspicious-activity reporting guidance to American financial institutions**.

That does **not** establish that the settlement economy generally is a money-laundering system.

It does establish that AML/CFT and suspicious-finance questions are legitimate questions to ask.

## 🧮 The British AML and charity-governance question

The British side now contains its own regulatory signals.

On 5 August 2026, the Charity Commission opened a statutory class inquiry into charities working in illegal Israeli settlements.

The Commission said it would investigate:

-   whether charitable funds were used in illegal settlements;
-   what those funds were used for;
-   whether expenditure furthered the charities' stated purposes;
-   whether charity assets or beneficiaries were at risk;
-   whether regulatory action was required.

Most importantly for financial-crime analysis, the Commission stated that it had already disclosed **specific concerns to the police and HMRC**.

Again:

That is not a criminal finding.

It is not permission to describe every charity operating in or around settlements as corrupt.

It is evidence that the financial routing question has reached the point of formal British regulatory inquiry.

In June 2026, the Foreign Secretary had also strengthened UK Business Risk Guidance to state that British citizens and businesses **should not conduct any economic and financial activities in illegal Israeli settlements**.

So the compliance environment was already moving before the September trade announcement.

## 🕸️ Why conduits matter

When economic activity becomes legally, reputationally or commercially difficult to conduct directly, incentives can arise to:

-   interpose charities or fiscal sponsors;
-   route money through legal vehicles;
-   fragment transactions;
-   alter geographic descriptions;
-   obscure ultimate beneficiaries;
-   use crowdfunding;
-   move through multiple jurisdictions;
-   rely on counterparties accustomed to complex compliance environments.

None of those things is automatically money laundering.

Many complicated transactions are lawful.

But AML is largely about asking whether **complexity has a legitimate explanation** and whether source, destination, beneficial ownership and economic purpose remain intelligible.

That is why the correct question is not:

> "Are settlements a money-laundering network?"

It is:

> **"What controls exist to prevent settlement-linked financial routes from being used for sanctions evasion, fraud, laundering, terrorist or extremist financing, false origin declarations, or other financial crime?"**

Those controls should be boringly inspectable.

## 🧿 Private wealth: ask, don't assume

The national trade numbers do not currently explain the intensity of the warning.

That makes private exposure a legitimate research question.

It does **not** make private exposure a fact.

For administration officials and politically connected families, the useful test is:

> **Does the person own, control, finance, advise, receive money from, or hold a material economic interest in an entity whose value could be affected by settlement sanctions?**

That requires tracing actual assets and counterparties.

Relevant categories include:

-   direct holdings in settlement businesses;
-   stakes in Israeli banks, insurers or asset managers financing settlement activity;
-   private-equity holdings;
-   real-estate exposure;
-   sovereign or corporate debt;
-   charitable and donor networks;
-   paid relationships;
-   family-controlled companies;
-   public pension or state investment exposure.

A political relationship with Israel is not a financial conflict.

A donation to an Israeli charity is not automatically settlement finance.

An investment in a major Israeli financial institution is not automatically an investment in settlements.

There is now a concrete corporate chain worth testing against the

**Rosebank** is a major UK Continental Shelf oil and gas development north-west of Shetland.  

Ithaca Energy currently l holds a **20% non-operated interest** in the field; the remaining 80% is held by operator Adura.  

Ithaca's 2025 annual report states that **Delek Group**, through wholly owned DKL Energy Limited, owns **50.5% of Ithaca Energy** and is treated as a controlling shareholder under the UK Listing Rules.  

Delek Group is also not an arbitrary Israeli company selected because it

### 🧮 What this does --- and does not --- establish

This is **not yet evidence that the British settlement measures affect Rosebank**.

It is evidence that there is a plausible and unusually concrete transmission route worth checking.

The arithmetic is useful only as orientation.  

Delek's 50.5% shareholding in Ithaca multiplied by Ithaca's 20% Rosebank interest gives an approximate **10.1% look-through economic exposure**.  

That is not the same thing as saying Delek directly owns 10.1% of Rosebank: the legal asset belongs within the corporate structure, Ithaca has governance arrangements intended to preserve its operational independence, and the economic consequences of any restriction would depend on the exact measure applied.

> **A company identified in the UN settlement-business database is the
