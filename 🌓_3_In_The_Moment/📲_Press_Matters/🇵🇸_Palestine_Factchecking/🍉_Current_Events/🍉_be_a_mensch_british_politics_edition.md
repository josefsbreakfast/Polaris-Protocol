# 🍉 Be A Mensch: British Politics Edition

**First created:** 2026-09-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-20 *Stand taller. You both look better with your heads a little higher.*

*But I am desperate to see you both step into it further; the work is not yet done.*

---

## ✨ Introduction

*I am writing this before I rest, in quite a lot of pain and not feeling particularly well. I am writing it now because I think it matters to say it now.*

Since Rosh Hashanah, I have been spending rather a lot of time listening to Irish and Irish-language music.

There is a reason for that.

Like many British Jews, I am not a purely white, Ashkenazi, institutionally convenient Jew whose entire identity can be cut down into something easily digestible by British Jewish communal life.

There are other parts of me.

They collide sometimes.

They rub against one another. They produce friction. They complicate the neat boxes into which institutions would sometimes prefer to place us.

I have not really felt able to access synagogue life comfortably since October 2023. Some of that is location. Some of it is events which have happened since.

Over the same period, I have experienced harassment and what has felt to me like an intimidation-like campaign, alongside extraordinary and sometimes difficult-to-explain problems across ordinary administrative life: healthcare, legal access, banking, post, digital systems and work.

I need to be extremely precise here.

I have never been formally told that any of this is connected to Palestine. I do not know that it is. There may be lawful or entirely unrelated explanations for things I have experienced.

The problem is that I have spent years without an adequate explanation.

Eventually, because enough things appeared sufficiently unusual to me, I submitted material to the Investigatory Powers Tribunal. That is an attempt to establish facts, not evidence that I already know what those facts are.

And the consequences of the wider period are much easier to establish.

I am disabled. My health has substantially worsened. I am now mostly housebound and sometimes confined to bed for many hours a day.

For context: I was a medical student. I was training to become a doctor. I had nearly qualified.

I know something about what prolonged uncertainty and exhaustion can do to a person.

So perhaps that is why I am not especially interested in standing at a safe distance and sneering at Jewish men who appear to be trying to work through extraordinarily difficult questions in public.

I understand fear.

I understand why you might be frightened of losing Jewish community.

I was frightened of that too.

Then I realised I had already lost parts of it, because remaining comfortably inside the box required me to make myself smaller.

## 🕯️ Jewish Life Cannot Require Us To Become Smaller

This is one of the things I want Zack Polanski to understand when he talks about protecting Jewish members who might, for example, attend a synagogue which identifies itself as Zionist.

I understand the concern.

A Jewish person might attend a synagogue because their parents went there. Because their children go there. Because they like the rabbi. Because it is the nearest synagogue of their denomination. Because their friends are there. Because Jewish communal life is not a political-party membership card.

Nobody should be forced to surrender ordinary Jewish communal belonging merely because an institution with which they are affiliated uses the word Zionist.

But there is another Jew standing in this problem.

Where does the anti-Zionist Jew go?

What happens when the assumptions operating inside mainstream Jewish political or communal structures themselves make another kind of Jew feel unwelcome?

Safeguarding Jewish belonging cannot mean safeguarding only the form of Jewishness which already possesses most of the institutions.

I am not asking Zionist Jews to become me.

I am asking whether there is enough room in British Jewish life for me to remain entirely myself.

And perhaps that is why, watching Jewish men in British politics begin to move through these questions, I feel less inclined to shout at them than some people apparently do.

I want them to keep moving.

## 🦁 Stand Taller

There is something happening here which I do not remember seeing before in quite this form.

I am watching Jewish men in relatively mainstream British political life explore, however cautiously and imperfectly, a Jewish identity which does not require recourse to Israeli state violence as the guarantor of Jewish safety.

I do not know either man's family circumstances.

I do not know their private social pressures.

I do not know what security advice they receive, what legal advice they receive, what conversations happen behind closed doors or what it costs personally to have every sentence about Palestine pulled apart in public.

I have not experienced their media exposure.

But I know what it feels like when life seems determined to exhaust you.

And I know what I am seeing from my side of the screen.

I am seeing Jewish men carrying their heads a little higher.

I want them to stand taller still.

Not because they need to perform masculinity for me.

Not because Jewish men need to become warriors.

Not because there is only one politically acceptable kind of Jew.

Because Jewish existence should never have been conditional upon somebody else's punishment.

Jewish safety should never require Palestinians to surrender theirs.

Jewish belonging should never require unquestioning allegiance to the coercive power of any state.

And there are so many forms of Jewish courage available to us.

The tradition did not exactly run short of brave men.

So perhaps the question is not whether you are strong enough.

Perhaps it is:

**What kind of strength do you want to practise?**

## ☘️ What I Found Somewhere Else

This is partly why I have been listening to so much Irish music.

The Irish question is not the Jewish question.

Irish history is not Palestinian history.

Irish diaspora identity is not Jewish diaspora identity.

Flattening any of these things together would teach us very little.

But recently I saw another model of masculinity emerging through an identity which also exists uneasily inside Britishness: a masculinity which looked strongest not when it moved towards institutional power, but when it stood beside people who possessed less of it.

That affected me.

There is something immensely powerful about watching somebody decide that strength means standing shoulder to shoulder with people who are being crushed.

Palestinians do not need our pity.

They need people prepared to stand beside them.

And perhaps there is something for Jewish men to recover there too.

## ⚰️ More Shoulders Under The Weight

There are powerful interests around this conflict.

There are international political networks. There are states. There are media organisations. There are ideological movements, including transnational far-right movements. There are institutions with their own incentives and relationships.

Anyone moving through this landscape publicly may encounter pressure from several directions.

The answer cannot be to demand that one person simply become strong enough to withstand everything.

The answer is to put more shoulders underneath the weight.

It is like being a pallbearer.

The more people carrying, the less impossible the weight becomes.

That might seem like an extraordinarily morbid metaphor.

But this is an extraordinarily morbid subject.

There are Palestinian families who have had to carry their dead. There are families still waiting for bodies to be recovered or identified. There are children whose imaginative play has incorporated the rituals and objects of death around them.

So perhaps we should not sanitise the metaphor.

**The solution to carrying an unbearable weight is not always becoming stronger. Sometimes it is finding more people to carry it with you.**

That is what solidarity is for.

## 🧱 Safety Can Come From Solidarity

This is one of the lessons British political history keeps trying to teach us.

People imagine safety as something delivered downwards by powerful institutions.

Sometimes it is.

Sometimes institutions are enormously important.

But safety can also travel sideways.

Community to community.

Worker to worker.

Neighbour to neighbour.

Cable Street sits in British memory partly because people who were not themselves Jewish stood beside Jewish East Enders against fascism.

The miners' strike generated its own extraordinary networks of solidarity across communities which, on paper, might have seemed to have very little to do with one another.

Lesbians and Gays Support the Miners became one of the best remembered examples because the relationship did not end with one group rescuing another. Solidarity changed both communities.

Other communities fed families when wages disappeared and political power was being used against organised labour.

Those things matter.

I come from working-class Britain. I come from a part of the country where mining history has not disappeared merely because the pits have.

People remember these stories.

They inherit them.

They tell them.

There is a deep political memory in Britain of what it means to put bread on somebody else's table when powerful people have decided that hunger will teach them obedience.

And there is a lesson in that for frightened people now:

**Safety does not always come from whiteness.**

**Safety does not always come from institutional power.**

**Safety can come from community and solidarity.**

It is frightening to rely on other people.

It can also be the safest you have ever been.

## 🍉 Palestinians Cannot Become Scenery In Our Jewish Argument

None of this matters if Palestine becomes merely the setting for Jewish self-discovery.

Palestinians are not a metaphor through which British Jews get to understand ourselves better.

They are people.

They have political agency.

They have families, histories, rights, disagreements, aspirations and futures of their own.

Whatever British Jews eventually decide Zionism means to us, Palestinians cannot perpetually be asked to wait outside while we finish discussing ourselves.

So political courage eventually has to become material.

I want a full British arms embargo on Israel.

I want serious scrutiny of F-35 supply arrangements rather than treating existing multinational arrangements as the end of the conversation.

I want pressure against illegal settlements.

I want procurement decisions examined.

I want divestment examined.

I want businesses and suppliers involved in settlement expansion or other unlawful activity examined.

I want politicians to keep asking what practical levers actually exist rather than merely repeating that present arrangements are complicated.

Some of that is already changing.

The British government has suspended licences for equipment which might be used by the IDF in Gaza, while retaining specific provisions for the multinational F-35 programme. In September 2026, the government also announced that it would refuse licence applications for arms and other exports that materially contribute to the occupation, alongside an intended import ban on goods from illegal settlements and further measures against people and companies facilitating settlement expansion.

Those measures are not everything I want.

But they demonstrate something important:

**Policy is movable.**

## 🏉 You Already Have Possession

And Britain itself is moving.

We should be precise about this rather than romanticising it.

A YouGov poll conducted for Medical Aid for Palestinians in April 2026 found **62% of British adults opposed Israel's ongoing military actions in Gaza and the West Bank**, while 13% supported them. The same polling found **54% supported ending all British arms exports to Israel, including F-35 components**, against 22% opposed.

That does not establish that everybody agrees about Zionism.

It does not establish that antisemitism has disappeared.

It certainly does not mean British people have reached some perfect consensus about Palestine.

But it does mean politicians should stop assuming that substantial action against particular Israeli state policies is necessarily politically marginal.

And this is where I apparently have to resort to rugby.

You already have possession of the ball.

It is inside your team.

Neither of you particularly strikes me as somebody we should place on the wing and hope for a glorious eighty-metre sprint, but that is fine. We have other positions available.

You have already crossed part of the ground.

Why on earth would you break formation now?

The frightening part is often precisely the point where forward movement has become real enough that retreat begins to feel tempting.

Keep going.

## 🌍 The World Is Complicated And You Can Have Lots Of Feelings

You can mourn the Israelis murdered on 7 October.

You can be frightened by antisemitism.

You can worry about Jewish institutions and Jewish children.

You can oppose illegal settlements.

You can be horrified by Palestinian children being killed.

You can oppose Israeli military actions.

You can want Palestinians to be free.

You can be uncertain about Zionism.

You can change your mind.

You can even say:

**I spoke to somebody. I read the actual motion. I understand it differently now.**

That is allowed.

Changing your position because you learned something is not weakness.

And none of those positions requires believing that Israel and Jewish people are interchangeable.

Antisemitism remains antisemitism.

Israeli state conduct remains Israeli state conduct.

Neither becomes the other because somebody finds the distinction politically inconvenient.

## 🪨 A Few Grains Of Sand

There may be legal constraints.

There may be international agreements.

There may be American pressure.

There may be security considerations that the public cannot see.

Fine.

Then tell us what can be told.

Let Parliament ask questions.

Let lawyers identify the boundaries.

Let campaigners identify lawful alternatives.

Let civil society find pressure points.

Let journalists investigate.

Let workers organise.

Let consumers decide what they wish to buy.

Let pension holders ask where their money goes.

Let everybody put a few grains of sand into machinery they believe should slow down.

One grain does very little.

Millions of grains become something else.

The point is not that everybody must become a hero.

The point is that nobody has to stop the machine alone.

## 📜 You Are Not Required To Finish The Work

*Pirkei Avot* gives us one of Judaism's most useful answers to impossible work:

> It is not your duty to finish the work, but neither are you free to
> desist from it.

There is another thing worth noticing.

It does not say you have to do the work alone.

Jewishness means many things to many Jews.

For me, increasingly, it means trusting that other people can come to your aid too.

That requires a different kind of courage.

It requires admitting that perhaps our safety does not depend upon possessing the strongest state, the strongest military or the most powerful friends.

Perhaps sometimes safety means building relationships strong enough that somebody else steps forward when you cannot.

One shoulder underneath the coffin.

One meal for somebody else's child.

One person standing beside another.

One grain of sand.

## 🤍 Help Them Make Jewish People Safer

And there is something I want to say particularly to non-Jewish people watching all of this.

I do not want to watch individual Jewish politicians being told endlessly that it is personally their responsibility to make every Jewish person in Britain feel safe before they are permitted to move further on Palestine.

If Jewish people are frightened, help make Jewish people safer.

Challenge antisemitism.

Protect Jewish neighbours.

Stand against threats to synagogues.

Make political meetings safe.

Do not tolerate the idea that Jewish people become legitimate targets because somebody is angry with Israel.

And then allow Jewish politicians to do their actual jobs.

Otherwise you are asking individual Jewish people simultaneously to carry a major change in British political attitudes towards Israel and Palestine **and** carry the entire historical burden of Jewish communal safety.

Put your shoulder underneath that too.

If we ask people to be brave, we acquire some responsibility for making bravery survivable.

## 🌅 The Head Is Already Visible

There is an old line about the old world dying, the new world struggling to be born, and the morbid symptoms which appear in the interregnum.

I am increasingly unsure that the new world is still struggling to be born.

**It is crowning.**

One can see its head.

The morbid symptoms are certainly still here.

There will be backlash.

There will be ugliness.

There will be fear.

But there are also hands everywhere.

Hands ready to hold people up.

Hands ready to carry some of the weight.

The British public does not consume information through one newspaper, one television bulletin or one political party anymore. Arguments escape the institutions trying to contain them.

Britain's own policy is moving.

Its public opinion is moving in measurable ways on arms exports and Israeli military action.

The conversation is moving.

And Jewish people are moving within it too.

## 🦁 Be A Mensch

So yes.

You can be frightened.

You can be uncertain.

You can be absolutely shitting yourself.

Courage has never required the absence of fear.

I am not asking either of you to become the Jew I am.

I am not asking you to agree with every political conclusion I have reached.

I am not asking you to solve Palestine.

I am not asking you to solve antisemitism.

I am asking you to recognise the extraordinary historical position in which you find yourselves and decide what you can do with it.

You are Jewish men.

You are British men.

If you needed stories from which to construct courage, neither inheritance exactly leaves you short of material.

There are lions in this tradition.

But perhaps being a Lion of Judah in this century does not mean demonstrating that you can dominate somebody else.

Perhaps it means being secure enough in Jewish life to say that another people's freedom does not diminish ours.

Perhaps it means discovering that Jewish safety can survive Palestinian freedom.

Perhaps it means standing shoulder to shoulder with people who need you, and trusting that when the weight becomes too much, other people will put their shoulders underneath it too.

The work is not finished.

You are not required to finish it.

But you have already started carrying it.

So:

**Be a mensch.**

**Stand taller.**

You both look better with your heads a little higher.

And take the next step.

**We'll have to carry some of it too.**
