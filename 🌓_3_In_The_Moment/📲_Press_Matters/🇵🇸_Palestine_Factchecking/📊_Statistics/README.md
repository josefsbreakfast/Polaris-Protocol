# 📊 Statistics

**Status:** scaffold / awaiting dataset and methodology review  
**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*Statistical factchecking routes for Palestine: counts, estimates, denominators, uncertainty, revisions, data provenance and the difference between a number that is available and a number that is meaningful.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder is the quantitative branch of **🇵🇸 Palestine Factchecking**.

It exists because numbers often appear to be the least political part of a dispute.

They are not.

A number can be:

- carefully measured;
- approximately estimated;
- administratively recorded;
- incompletely reported;
- politically selected;
- methodologically incompatible with another figure;
- outdated;
- revised;
- double-counted;
- undercounted;
- or perfectly accurate while answering a different question from the one the reader thinks it answers.

That does not make statistics useless.

It makes methodology part of the factcheck.

## 🧮 The First Question Is: What Is Being Counted?

Before comparing numbers, define the unit.

Examples:

> **deaths**

may mean:

- reported deaths;
- identified deaths;
- deaths recorded in a registry;
- directly violent deaths;
- conflict-attributable deaths;
- excess mortality;
- confirmed civilian deaths;
- all-cause deaths during a period.

Likewise:

> **aid**

may mean:

- trucks authorised;
- trucks inspected;
- trucks crossing a border;
- tonnage entered;
- consignments collected;
- aid distributed;
- aid reaching a final recipient.

If two sources count different things, disagreement in the headline number may not be an error.

It may be a denominator or definition problem.

---

## 🧭 Preserve The Verb

A number should carry the operation that produced it.

Prefer:

- **reported**;
- **recorded**;
- **identified**;
- **verified**;
- **registered**;
- **estimated**;
- **modelled**;
- **surveyed**;
- **sampled**;
- **projected**;
- **counted**;
- **audited**;
- **revised**.

Those verbs tell the reader what kind of knowledge the number represents.

Do not silently upgrade:

> **estimated**

to:

> **known**

or:

> **reported**

to:

> **verified**.

---

## 📚 Source Provenance Matters

For every important number, trace where possible:

```text
Original collector
      ↓
Administrative database
      ↓
Aggregating organisation
      ↓
Report
      ↓
News article
      ↓
Political speech
      ↓
Social post
      ↓
AI summary
```

Ask:

- Who collected the original data?
- Was it direct observation, registration, modelling or aggregation?
- What information did the collector have access to?
- Was the dataset complete?
- Who cleaned or transformed it?
- Did later publications use the same source?

Ten outlets quoting the same dataset are not ten independent datasets.

---

## 🔬 Methodology Before Argument

A statistical disagreement should be examined through method before rhetoric.

Useful questions include:

- What population is covered?
- What geography?
- What dates?
- What inclusion criteria?
- What exclusions?
- How are duplicates handled?
- How are missing records handled?
- How are unidentified people handled?
- Are retrospective corrections possible?
- Is the method stable over time?
- Are several data sources merged?
- Is the uncertainty published?
- Has the methodology been peer reviewed or independently assessed?
- Can the result be reproduced?

A source can be politically controversial and methodologically strong.

A politically congenial source can use weak methods.

Read the method.

---

## ⏳ Statistics Have Dates

A number without a date is often useless.

Live statistics can change because:

- new incidents occur;
- bodies are recovered;
- identities are confirmed;
- duplicate records are removed;
- missing people are located;
- registries are reconciled;
- boundaries change;
- survey waves are updated;
- or an estimate is revised.

Prefer:

> **As of 20 August 2026, Source X reported...**

to:

> **There are...**

where the figure can change materially.

---

## 🔄 Revision Is Normal

A revised number is not automatically evidence that the earlier number was fraudulent.

Revisions can reflect:

- better identification;
- deduplication;
- new access;
- data cleaning;
- updated population denominators;
- later forensic evidence;
- revised modelling assumptions;
- or corrected clerical errors.

The right question is:

> **Why did the number change?**

Record the revision path where it matters.

---

## 🚨 But Revision Can Also Reveal Problems

Revision should not be romanticised either.

A changing dataset can reveal:

- poor methodology;
- political interference;
- inconsistent inclusion criteria;
- unacknowledged changes;
- duplicate records;
- missing documentation;
- or uncontrolled estimation.

If the methodology changes, say so.

If historical comparisons become invalid, say so.

If a source silently changes figures without explanation, document that too.

---

## 📊 Counts, Rates And Denominators

Raw counts can mislead without denominators.

Examples:

> **1,000 incidents**

means something different in populations of:

- 50,000;
- 500,000;
- 5 million;
- 50 million.

Likewise:

> **20%**

is not meaningful without knowing:

- 20% of what;
- measured among whom;
- with what sampling frame;
- and over what period.

Where appropriate, distinguish:

- counts;
- rates;
- proportions;
- percentages;
- per-capita figures;
- cumulative totals;
- rolling averages;
- and incidence over time.

---

## 🧮 The Denominator Can Be Political

Choosing a denominator can change the apparent conclusion.

Examples might include:

- civilians as a share of all reported deaths;
- civilian men as a share of adult male deaths;
- aid per person;
- arrests per population;
- settlers as a share of a territorial population;
- military spending as a share of GDP;
- support for a policy among all adults versus likely voters.

The denominator should match the proposition.

Do not choose the denominator after deciding what emotional effect the number should have.

---

## 🧍 Population Estimates Need Care

Population figures may differ because of:

- census date;
- migration;
- displacement;
- births and deaths;
- territorial boundaries;
- household estimation;
- unregistered populations;
- refugee status;
- methodology;
- and wartime disruption.

Historical population figures may be even more fragile.

Where a population denominator materially affects a result, record:

- source;
- reference date;
- geographic boundary;
- estimation method;
- and uncertainty.

## ☠️ Casualty Statistics

This branch will likely need several dedicated nodes.

Potential distinctions include:

- reported deaths;
- identified deaths;
- recovered bodies;
- missing people presumed dead;
- civilians;
- combatants;
- children;
- women;
- men;
- aid workers;
- journalists;
- medical workers;
- security personnel;
- direct violent deaths;
- indirect deaths;
- and excess mortality.

Those categories are not always mutually exclusive.

They are not always collected by the same institution.

And they may answer different questions.

---

## 🩺 Direct Deaths, Indirect Deaths And Excess Mortality

These are different concepts.

### Direct violent deaths

Deaths resulting directly from violence.

### Indirect conflict-attributable deaths

Deaths arising from conflict-related breakdown such as:

- disease;
- malnutrition;
- lack of medical treatment;
- destruction of infrastructure;
- or displacement.

### Excess mortality

An estimate comparing observed or modelled deaths against an expected baseline.

These should not be merged casually.

A study estimating excess mortality is not necessarily claiming that all excess deaths were directly killed by weapons.

Read the methodology.

---

## 👶 Age And Sex Categories

Breakdowns by age and sex can be politically salient and methodologically difficult.

Questions include:

- What age threshold defines a child?
- Are age records complete?
- How are unidentified bodies treated?
- Does “women and children” combine two analytically different groups?
- Are male civilians being implicitly classified as combatants?
- Are combatant status claims independently verifiable?
- Are categories mutually exclusive?
- Has identification improved over time?

Preserve the classification rule.

---

## ⚔️ Civilian And Combatant Counts

This area requires unusual caution.

Combatant status may be:

- self-reported;
- claimed by a military;
- inferred from age and sex;
- identified by an armed group;
- established after investigation;
- or contested.

Do not convert:

> **adult male**

into:

> **combatant**

without evidence.

Likewise, do not automatically assume every person omitted from a combatant list was necessarily civilian.

The classification method matters.

---

## 🧱 Destruction And Damage

Claims about destroyed homes, buildings or infrastructure may rely on:

- satellite imagery;
- municipal records;
- engineering assessments;
- remote sensing;
- damage classification;
- field surveys;
- or modelled estimates.

Important distinctions include:

- destroyed;
- severely damaged;
- damaged;
- rendered unusable;
- partially damaged;
- affected;
- or within a damaged area.

A “damaged building” is not necessarily a “destroyed building”.

Check the threshold.

---

## 🚚 Aid Statistics

Aid figures are particularly vulnerable to category drift.

Possible measures include:

- trucks authorised;
- trucks inspected;
- trucks entered;
- trucks collected;
- tonnage;
- calories;
- fuel;
- medical supplies;
- commercial goods;
- humanitarian goods;
- distribution to warehouses;
- final delivery to communities.

Do not treat one stage as equivalent to another.

A truck crossing a border does not establish that its contents reached the intended recipient.

Likewise, distribution failure does not mean nothing entered.

Track the pipeline.

---

## 🍞 Food Security And Famine Metrics

Food-security analysis may involve specialist frameworks.

Potential measures include:

- household food consumption;
- acute malnutrition;
- mortality;
- market access;
- food availability;
- coping strategies;
- population coverage;
- geographic classification;
- and forecast versus observed conditions.

Do not turn:

> **risk of famine**

into:

> **famine has been formally classified**

or vice versa.

Where specialist classification systems are used, explain what threshold was actually met.

---

## 🏥 Health-System Statistics

Potential measures include:

- hospitals operational;
- hospitals partially operational;
- bed capacity;
- emergency capacity;
- surgeries;
- medication stocks;
- attacks on health facilities;
- health-worker deaths;
- infectious disease;
- maternal mortality;
- disability;
- rehabilitation access.

A facility described as **operational** may still be functioning at radically reduced capacity.

Definition matters.

---

## 🏠 Displacement Statistics

Displacement can be counted as:

- people displaced at least once;
- people currently displaced;
- displacement events;
- households displaced;
- people displaced multiple times;
- shelter registrations;
- estimates based on area movement.

A person displaced five times is still one person but five displacement events.

Do not confuse the two.

---

## 🔐 Hostages, Prisoners And Detainees

Potential measures include:

- people taken hostage;
- people currently held;
- people released;
- people confirmed dead;
- bodies recovered;
- administrative detainees;
- criminal prisoners;
- security detainees;
- people arrested during operations;
- and people whose legal status is disputed.

A total can change because people move between categories.

Record the classification framework.

---

## 🏘️ Settlements And Territorial Statistics

Potential questions include:

- number of settlements;
- number of outposts;
- settler population;
- geographic distribution;
- land area;
- housing approvals;
- construction starts;
- demolitions;
- displacement;
- permit data.

Different organisations may define:

- settlement;
- outpost;
- neighbourhood;
- municipal boundary;
- built-up area;
- or jurisdictional area

differently.

Do not compare incompatible counts without explaining the definitions.

---

## 🗳️ Polling And Public Opinion

Polling can be especially vulnerable to overinterpretation during war.

Check:

- sample size;
- sampling frame;
- geographic coverage;
- date;
- mode of interview;
- weighting;
- question wording;
- response options;
- nonresponse;
- displacement effects;
- safety concerns;
- and whether respondents could answer freely.

Do not generalise from:

> **respondents to one survey**

to:

> **all Palestinians**

or:

> **all Israelis**

without justification.

---

## 💰 Financial And Budget Statistics

This branch may also include:

- state budgets;
- military spending;
- public-diplomacy spending;
- foreign aid;
- arms transfers;
- investment;
- divestment;
- trade;
- procurement;
- donor flows;
- reconstruction estimates;
- and corporate exposure.

Distinguish:

- announced;
- allocated;
- authorised;
- contracted;
- obligated;
- spent;
- disbursed;
- and received.

A budget line is not necessarily expenditure.

A contract value is not necessarily money already paid.

---

## 🧵 Category Definitions Can Change Mid-Series

Longitudinal comparisons require stable definitions.

Watch for:

- changed territorial boundaries;
- changed age thresholds;
- changed casualty categories;
- changed polling questions;
- changed reporting partners;
- changed verification rules;
- changed definitions of “operational”;
- changed aid categories;
- or changed treatment of missing data.

If the series breaks, mark the break.

Do not draw a smooth line through a methodological discontinuity.

---

## 📈 Visualisations Can Mislead

Charts can create certainty that the underlying data does not possess.

For any important visualisation, check:

- axis scale;
- truncation;
- cumulative versus daily values;
- missing periods;
- smoothing;
- normalisation;
- denominator;
- category definitions;
- annotations;
- and whether uncertainty is visible.

A visually dramatic chart can be mathematically accurate and still misleading.

---

## 🧪 Statistical Claims Need Adversarial Checks

Before accepting a politically important number, ask:

> **What would make this number too high?**

> **What would make it too low?**

> **What records are missing?**

> **Who benefits if this method is accepted?**

> **Would I accept this methodology if it produced a politically inconvenient result?**

This is not cynicism.

It is method.

## 🧪 Questions For The Dataset Review

To return to later:

- Which casualty datasets should be treated as canonical for which questions?
- Which numbers are administrative counts and which are estimates?
- Which datasets preserve individual-level records?
- Which publish revision histories?
- Which humanitarian dashboards distinguish entry from distribution?
- Which food-security metrics require specialist interpretation?
- Which damage assessments are based on satellite methodology?
- Which population denominators are current enough for rate calculations?
- Which polling organisations publish full questionnaires and sampling methods?
- Which detainee datasets distinguish legal categories clearly?
- Which settlement datasets use compatible definitions?
- Which arms-transfer and aid databases distinguish authorised from delivered?

- Which statistics need local archival snapshots because dashboards overwrite history?

- Which recurring headline numbers are actually incomparable?
