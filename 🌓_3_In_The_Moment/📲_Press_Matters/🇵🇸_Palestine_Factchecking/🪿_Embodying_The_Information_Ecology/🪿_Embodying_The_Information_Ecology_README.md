# 🪿 Embodying The Information Ecology
**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*The framework layer for understanding Israel–Palestine information not only as claims to verify, but as an environment produced by bodies, institutions, platforms, incentives, repetition, power and lived consequence.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Most of this factchecking pack asks a relatively familiar question:

> **Is this claim true?**

This branch asks the question immediately around it:

> **What kind of information environment made this claim visible, believable, repeatable, frightening, suppressible, profitable, actionable or difficult to verify in the first place?**

That distinction is the point.

A person encountering information about Palestine is not standing outside the system calmly inspecting isolated propositions.

They are inside it.

So are:

- journalists;
- editors;
- governments;
- militaries;
- Palestinians;
- Israelis;
- Jewish and Palestinian diasporas;
- campaigners;
- lawyers;
- researchers;
- aid organisations;
- intelligence services;
- advertisers;
- PR firms;
- banks;
- technology companies;
- moderators;
- search engines;
- recommender systems;
- generative AI systems;
- and the person trying, at 02:13, to work out whether the photograph in front of them is actually from the event everyone says it is.

Information is produced by bodies.

It passes through machinery.

It changes behaviour.

That changed behaviour produces new information.

That is an information ecology.

---

## 🪿 Why “Embodying”?

Polaris uses **Embodied Information Ecology** because information does not remain abstract.

It lands somewhere.

A casualty statistic can become:

- grief;
- disbelief;
- political mobilisation;
- numbness;
- a policy argument;
- a moderation decision;
- a threat assessment;
- a donation;
- an investment decision;
- or a reason somebody stops speaking.

A slogan can become:

- solidarity to one listener;
- threat to another;
- evidence in a disciplinary process;
- a search query;
- a police report;
- an advertising category;
- or training material for a model.

A government press release can become a headline.

The headline becomes indexed text.

Indexed text becomes retrieval material.

Retrieval material becomes an AI answer.

The AI answer becomes somebody else's starting assumption.

The loop has acquired a body.

## ♻️ The Core Framework

A useful minimal model is:

```text
EVENT / CONDITION
        ↓
OBSERVATION
        ↓
RECORD
        ↓
CLASSIFICATION
        ↓
DISTRIBUTION
        ↓
INTERPRETATION
        ↓
ACTION
        ↓
NEW CONDITIONS
        ↺
```

Every arrow can introduce:

- error;
- delay;
- selection;
- power;
- incentives;
- translation;
- compression;
- omission;
- amplification;
- or deliberate intervention.

The framework therefore does not ask only:

> **Is the final statement accurate?**

It also asks:

> **What happened to the information on the way here?**

## 🧬 The Ecology Has Layers

For this pack, a useful working stack is:

```text
BODY
  ↓
EVENT
  ↓
WITNESS / SENSOR
  ↓
RECORD
  ↓
INSTITUTION
  ↓
CLASSIFICATION
  ↓
DATABASE / ARCHIVE
  ↓
JOURNALISM / ADVOCACY / OFFICIAL COMMUNICATION
  ↓
PLATFORM / SEARCH / ADVERTISING / PR
  ↓
AI / SYNTHESIS / RETRIEVAL
  ↓
READER
  ↓
BEHAVIOUR
  ↓
INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE
  ↺
```

The layers are not always sequential.

They can bypass one another.

They can conflict.

A witness may publish directly to a platform.

A government may buy advertising that changes what appears around a search.

A journalist may work from a database whose categories were defined by a ministry.

An AI system may synthesize a hundred webpages that ultimately derive from one press release.

The diagram is therefore a map of **functions**, not a claim that information always travels neatly.

It very much does not.

## 🫀 Bodies Are Part Of The Evidence System

Bodies:

- witness;
- remember;
- misremember;
- bleed;
- panic;
- translate;
- photograph;
- identify;
- bury;
- grieve;
- testify;
- dissociate;
- survive;
- and sometimes cannot produce the tidy documentation demanded of them.

Embodied evidence can be incomplete without being worthless.

Trauma can affect recall without making a witness disposable.

Fear can affect testimony without proving fabrication.

Political commitment can affect interpretation without making observation imaginary.

The same applies to institutional observers.

There is no magical category of source that has escaped embodiment and become pure objectivity.

The task is to understand the source's position, access, constraints and method.

---

## 🛰️ Sensors Extend Bodies

Modern conflict is also observed through:

- satellites;
- drones;
- CCTV;
- smartphones;
- acoustic sensors;
- radar;
- seismic data;
- ship and aircraft tracking;
- remote sensing;
- geolocation;
- metadata;
- and network telemetry.

These can provide extraordinary evidence.

They also have:

- owners;
- access rules;
- resolutions;
- blind spots;
- collection schedules;
- processing pipelines;
- and interpretation layers.

A satellite image is not “the view from nowhere”.

It is a measurement produced by a system.

That does not weaken it.

It tells us how to read it properly.

---

## 🗂️ Records Are Constructed Objects

Before information becomes a statistic, it usually becomes a record.

Someone decides:

- what fields exist;
- what counts as an incident;
- how a name is spelled;
- whether a death is verified;
- whether a person is civilian or combatant;
- whether a building is damaged or destroyed;
- whether a truck has “entered”;
- whether an event belongs in the database at all.

Those choices can be excellent.

They can also be constrained, inherited, political or simply designed for a different purpose.

A database does not remove judgement.

It **stores judgement in fields**.

That is why the Statistics branch needs methodology and the Law branch needs procedural posture.

---

## 🧵 Classification Changes Reality

Labels do more than describe.

They can determine:

- who is protected;
- who is counted;
- whose death enters a dataset;
- which speech is moderated;
- which organisation can receive money;
- which person can travel;
- which story is searchable;
- which event becomes “terrorism”;
- which population becomes “civilian”;
- which building becomes a “military objective” in public argument;
- and which content a model learns to associate with danger.

Classification can therefore become intervention.

This is one of the central Polaris propositions.

Observation is not always passive once institutions act on what they observe.

---

## 🏛️ Institutions Have Different Ways Of Knowing

A military, hospital, newsroom, court, aid agency, polling organisation and social platform do not produce the same kind of knowledge.

They ask different questions.

They operate under different constraints.

They have different incentives.

They may also possess different access.

A hospital may know names before an international investigator can verify them.

A military may possess intelligence it cannot publicly disclose.

A court may deliberately decide only the narrow question properly before it.

A newsroom may publish before an archive is complete.

A platform may need to make millions of moderation decisions at speed.

Do not treat disagreement between institutions as automatically proving that one is lying.

First ask whether they are even performing the same epistemic job.

## 📣 Strategic Communication Is Part Of The Environment

States communicate strategically.

So do:

- armed groups;
- NGOs;
- campaign organisations;
- political parties;
- corporations;
- lawyers;
- lobbyists;
- public-relations firms;
- and activists.

Strategic communication can include:

- accurate information;
- selective information;
- framing;
- repetition;
- emotional emphasis;
- omission;
- advertising;
- spokesperson deployment;
- briefing;
- lobbying;
- search optimisation;
- influencer work;
- and direct attempts to affect AI-facing information environments.

The existence of strategic intent does not establish that the underlying claim is false.

It changes the evidential question from:

> **Is this propaganda?**

to the more useful:

> **What is being claimed, what supports it, who is distributing it, and what are they trying to make happen?**

## 💰 Money Is An Information Variable

Information environments have budgets.

Money can buy:

- staff;
- researchers;
- lawyers;
- advertisements;
- press offices;
- polling;
- monitoring;
- content;
- translation;
- social distribution;
- lobbying;
- SEO;
- events;
- access;
- expert commentary;
- and time.

It can also buy the capacity to answer a claim quickly while somebody with fewer resources is still collecting documents.

This does not mean:

> **well-funded = false**

or:

> **poorly funded = true.**

It means resource asymmetry affects **production, persistence and discoverability**.

The source register should therefore care about funding and material interests where they are relevant to understanding how evidence enters circulation.

## 🏦 Material Interests Do Not Stop At Governments

The wider ecology includes organisations whose primary identity is not “Israel/Palestine advocacy” but which may have material interests touching the conflict.

These can include:

- banks;
- investors;
- defence firms;
- technology providers;
- insurers;
- universities;
- pension funds;
- consultancies;
- trade bodies;
- media owners;
- and corporate clients of public-affairs firms.

Their interests may concern:

- contracts;
- market access;
- regulation;
- reputational risk;
- investment exposure;
- procurement;
- litigation;
- sanctions;
- or government relationships.

That does not make every institutional statement corrupt.

It means “stakeholder” is an evidentially relevant category.

## 📰 Journalism Is A Network, Not A Pile Of Articles

Ten headlines do not necessarily mean ten independent investigations.

A story may propagate through:

```text
primary statement
      ↓
wire service
      ↓
national newspaper
      ↓
broadcast
      ↓
aggregator
      ↓
social account
      ↓
AI answer
```

Or an investigation may genuinely combine:

```text
witnesses
 + satellite imagery
 + documents
 + geolocation
 + expert analysis
      ↓
independent reconstruction
```

Those are radically different evidential structures.

This is why source ancestry matters.

The ecology cares about **independence of evidence**, not the number of tabs open.

---

## 🔎 Search Is An Interface To The Ecology

Search results are not a neutral inventory of everything that exists.

Visibility can be affected by:

- indexing;
- crawlability;
- language;
- geography;
- freshness;
- authority signals;
- backlinks;
- technical structure;
- moderation;
- legal removals;
- user behaviour;
- optimisation;
- advertising;
- and ranking systems.

Therefore:

> **I searched and couldn't find it**

is evidence about retrieval.

It is not automatically evidence that the underlying thing does not exist.

Conversely:

> **it is the first result**

does not make it authoritative.

Search is part of the evidence environment.

It is not the evidence environment entire.

---

## 🤖 Generative AI Sits Downstream — And Then Feeds Back Upstream

Generative AI complicates the ecology because it can both consume and produce information.

A simplified loop looks like:

```text
documents / reporting / websites
            ↓
     searchable corpus
            ↓
      model / retrieval
            ↓
        AI answer
            ↓
      user publication
            ↓
     new searchable text
            ↺
```

This creates several risks:

- source laundering;
- citation loss;
- repetition becoming apparent consensus;
- confident synthesis of incompatible categories;
- disappearance of minority-language nuance;
- and feedback loops in which AI-produced wording becomes future source material.

It also creates opportunities:

- cross-source comparison;
- translation assistance;
- chronology reconstruction;
- question generation;
- contradiction detection;
- and rapid routing to primary material.

The answer is not “never use AI”.

It is:

> **Do not mistake synthesis for provenance.**

---

## 🧠 Retrieval Can Be Influenced Without Controlling A Model

This distinction matters particularly for this pack.

Actors may attempt to influence:

- search rankings;
- available webpages;
- FAQ ecosystems;
- media volume;
- terminology;
- expert visibility;
- advertising;
- and other material likely to be retrieved or encountered by AI systems and users.

That does **not** mean they can simply reach inside a model and dictate its answer.

The more defensible chain is:

```text
produce / optimise / promote information
                ↓
change parts of the accessible information environment
                ↓
potentially affect retrieval or exposure
                ↓
potentially affect downstream synthesis
```

Each arrow requires evidence.

Intent is not effect.

Spending is not reach.

Reach is not persuasion.

Retrievability is not model control.

This folder exists partly to keep those distinctions intact.

---

## 🕸️ Repetition Can Become Apparent Consensus

Repeated claims gain familiarity.

Familiarity can be mistaken for corroboration.

This is especially dangerous when many repetitions share one upstream source.

The ecological question is therefore:

> **How many genuinely independent evidential routes reach this conclusion?**

not:

> **How many times can I find the sentence?**

This applies to:

- atrocity claims;
- casualty numbers;
- historical assertions;
- legal summaries;
- translations;
- political slogans;
- and claims about public opinion.

---

## 🧿 Absence Can Also Be Produced

Information environments are shaped not only by what circulates, but by what does not.

Absence may result from:

- lack of access;
- danger to witnesses;
- destroyed records;
- language barriers;
- poor indexing;
- editorial decisions;
- legal restrictions;
- platform moderation;
- commercial incentives;
- classification;
- source protection;
- or simple lack of interest.

Sometimes absence is meaningful.

Sometimes it is just absence.

Do not automatically convert:

> **I cannot see it**

into:

> **someone suppressed it.**

But do not assume visibility is naturally distributed either.

Investigate the mechanism.

---

## 🧯 Moderation Is Governance At Scale

Platforms must decide what to do with:

- graphic violence;
- praise or support for designated organisations;
- hate speech;
- dehumanisation;
- misinformation;
- documentation of atrocities;
- journalistic material;
- historical archives;
- satire;
- and political slogans.

Those decisions can protect users.

They can also remove evidence, flatten language and disproportionately affect communities whose political vocabulary intersects with automated risk categories.

The important questions are:

- What rule applied?
- How was it operationalised?
- Which language?
- Which classifier or human process?
- What appeal route existed?
- What false positives and false negatives are documented?
- What happened downstream?

“Moderated” is not an explanation.

It is the beginning of one.

## 🌍 Language Produces Different Ecologies

Arabic, Hebrew and English information environments are not mirrors of one another.

They can contain different:

- primary reporting;
- political assumptions;
- slogans;
- eyewitness material;
- institutional sources;
- search rankings;
- moderation risks;
- and translation pathways.

An English-only review can therefore miss not just sources but **whole routes through which meaning is produced**.

Translation can bridge ecologies.

It can also flatten them.

This is why the Identity & Language branch is structural rather than decorative.

## 🫀 Repeated Exposure Changes The Reader

People do not process unlimited atrocity imagery, casualty counts, hostage testimony, racist rhetoric, propaganda and argument without consequence.

Repeated exposure can produce:

- hypervigilance;
- numbness;
- anger;
- compulsive checking;
- avoidance;
- certainty-seeking;
- despair;
- identity threat;
- moral injury;
- and reduced ability to tolerate ambiguity.

These responses can affect subsequent information behaviour.

For example:

```text
fear
 ↓
more checking
 ↓
more exposure
 ↓
less sleep
 ↓
lower ambiguity tolerance
 ↓
more urgent checking
 ↺
```

That is a feedback loop.

The body is now changing the epistemic environment it encounters.

## 🧠 Cognitive Load Is Not Stupidity

Breaking events often require readers to track simultaneously:

- geography;
- history;
- law;
- military terminology;
- casualty methodology;
- Arabic and Hebrew;
- political factions;
- international institutions;
- and rapidly changing chronology.

That is a heavy cognitive task.

Simplified narratives can become attractive because they reduce load.

The answer is not to sneer at people for needing compression.

It is to build **better compression**:

- clear routing;
- short answers;
- visible uncertainty;
- definitions;
- timelines;
- and links to deeper evidence.

That is one reason this pack exists.

---

## 🧭 Identity Threat Changes Information Processing

When information is perceived as threatening:

- Jewish safety;
- Palestinian existence;
- Israeli security;
- family history;
- religious identity;
- national identity;
- or a person's moral self-conception,

the claim may not be processed as an abstract proposition.

It may be processed as threat.

That can affect:

- attention;
- memory;
- willingness to revise;
- interpretation of ambiguity;
- and trust.

Recognising this is not a reason to abandon factchecking.

It is a reason to avoid designing factchecking as humiliation.

The aim is to make correction cognitively possible.

---

## 🪞 Moral Sorting Can Replace Evidence Sorting

Information ecosystems often encourage a shortcut:

> **good people believe X; bad people believe Y.**

Once that happens, changing one's mind becomes socially expensive.

Evidence is no longer only evidence.

It becomes a loyalty signal.

This can happen in any political camp.

The archive should resist it by allowing propositions to be separately evaluated.

A source can be wrong about one thing and right about another.

A person can condemn an atrocity without joining an entire political programme.

A correction is not defection.

---

## 📈 Metrics Can Become Targets

Once institutions know they are being judged by a metric, behaviour can reorganise around the metric.

Examples might include:

- casualty categories;
- aid-truck counts;
- content-removal rates;
- engagement;
- sentiment;
- polling;
- compliance statistics;
- or “misinformation” volumes.

This does not mean metrics are fake.

It means measurement can alter the system being measured.

That is basic feedback.

A good factcheck asks whether the metric is still measuring what readers think it measures after institutions begin optimising around it.

---

## ♻️ Observation Can Become Intervention

This is the wider Polaris seam.

An information system may begin by observing.

Then its outputs are used to decide:

- who receives scrutiny;
- what gets removed;
- what gets funded;
- what gets investigated;
- which content is promoted;
- which account is trusted;
- which person is classified as risky;
- or which narrative receives institutional attention.

The system is no longer merely describing reality.

It is participating in the production of the next state of reality.

That is why data governance, moderation, PR, surveillance, search and AI belong in the same conceptual ecology even though they are not the same technology.

## 🧬 Feedback Loops To Watch

### The repetition loop

```text
claim
 ↓
repetition
 ↓
familiarity
 ↓
perceived credibility
 ↓
more repetition
 ↺
```

### The outrage loop

```text
provocative content
 ↓
engagement
 ↓
algorithmic visibility
 ↓
more exposure
 ↓
more outrage
 ↺
```

### The correction failure loop

```text
false claim
 ↓
viral spread
 ↓
later correction
 ↓
lower reach
 ↓
original claim remains familiar
```

### The institutional citation loop

```text
organisation A cites B
 ↓
B cites reporting based on A
 ↓
later synthesis sees two sources
 ↓
apparent independent corroboration
```

### The AI laundering loop

```text
weak claim online
 ↓
AI synthesis
 ↓
confident prose
 ↓
republication
 ↓
future retrieval sees more copies
 ↺
```

### The silence loop

```text
anticipated punishment / harassment
 ↓
self-censorship
 ↓
less visible disagreement
 ↓
appearance of consensus
 ↓
greater perceived cost of dissent
 ↺
```

These are analytical patterns.

Their presence in a specific case must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

## 🧿 Information Operations Need Evidential Discipline

The existence of propaganda, lobbying, public diplomacy, psychological operations, bot activity or coordinated influence campaigns does not license free-form suspicion.

For a specific claim, ask:

1. **Actor** — who is alleged to be acting?
2. **Capability** — what can they actually do?
3. **Mechanism** — through which platform, contract, network or process?
4. **Artefact** — what observable output exists?
5. **Distribution** — who encountered it?
6. **Effect** — what changed?
7. **Alternative explanation** — what else could produce the observation?

The further the claim moves from **artefact** to **effect**, the more evidence it needs.

This is particularly important in an environment where people already have good reason to expect strategic communication.

We do not improve the ecology by becoming sloppy about the ecology.

---

## 🏷️ Provenance Should Travel With Claims

Where practical, preserve:

- original source;
- date;
- language;
- archive link or copy;
- author;
- institutional affiliation;
- funding or material interest where relevant;
- whether the source is primary or derivative;
- and what proposition it actually supports.

A useful factchecking pack should make it harder for a claim to become detached from its ancestry.

Provenance is anti-laundering infrastructure.

---

## 🗃️ Versioning Is Epistemic Infrastructure

When a source changes, preserve the change where it matters.

When the archive changes its assessment, say why.

When a page disappears, record that it existed if lawful and appropriate.

When a translation is corrected, retain the correction trail.

When a casualty figure is revised, preserve the earlier figure and its date rather than pretending the final dataset arrived fully formed.

Version history protects against two opposite failures:

- treating early uncertainty as permanent;
- and rewriting earlier uncertainty out of existence.

It combines:

- live warfare;

- international law;
- mass casualty reporting;
- intelligence and military secrecy;
- enormous diplomatic interest;
- organised advocacy;
- lobbying;
- public diplomacy;

## 🧪 A Compact Diagnostic

When something in the information environment feels strange, ask:

> **What exactly am I observing?**

Then:

> **Where did it come from?**

> **Who classified it?**

> **What machinery distributed it?**

> **Who had an incentive to amplify or suppress it?**

> **What evidence shows that intervention actually occurred?**

> **What would the boring explanation be?**

> **What changed downstream?**

That sequence is deliberately less exciting than jumping straight to motive.

It is also much more useful.

## 🚧 Common Failure Modes

Watch for:

- treating every information problem as deliberate manipulation;
- treating manipulation as impossible because a system is automated;
- assuming official sources are neutral;
- assuming activist sources are unreliable by definition;
- counting repetitions as independent corroboration;
- treating search ranking as truth ranking;
- treating non-discovery as proof of nonexistence;
- treating visibility changes as proof of suppression without mechanism evidence;
- treating PR spending as proof of persuasion;
- treating AI-facing content campaigns as direct model control;
- treating moderation as either pure censorship or pure safety;
- forgetting that language environments differ;
- ignoring material interests;
- over-attributing private actors to governments;
- under-attributing institutional incentives because nobody issued an explicit instruction;
- treating trauma as evidence of unreliability;
- treating emotion as evidence of truth;
- and forgetting that the researcher is also inside the ecology.

## 🌱 The Practical Aim

The goal is not to make readers suspicious of everything.

That produces paralysis.

The goal is to make them **better at locating the level of the problem**.

Sometimes the problem is:

- a false claim.

Sometimes it is:

- a true claim with a misleading caption.

Sometimes:

- a good statistic answering the wrong question.

Sometimes:

- a legal opinion reported as a judgment.

Sometimes:

- twenty articles with one upstream source.

Sometimes:

- a translation failure.

Sometimes:

- a platform rule.

Sometimes:

- a well-funded communications campaign.

Sometimes:

- ordinary breaking-news uncertainty.

Sometimes:

- grief.

And occasionally the boring explanation really is the boring explanation.

A robust information ecology is one in which we can tell those things apart.
