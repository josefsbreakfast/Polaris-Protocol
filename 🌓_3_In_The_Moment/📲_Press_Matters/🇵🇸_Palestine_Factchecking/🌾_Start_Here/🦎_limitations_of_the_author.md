# 🦎 Limitations Of The Author

**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*What this archive can reasonably claim to know, what it cannot independently verify, and why disclosed standpoint is part of the method rather than an apology for having one.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This archive has an author.

That sounds obvious.

It is worth stating anyway.

Factchecking can acquire an odd institutional voice in which conclusions appear to have been generated by a neutral machine floating somewhere above politics, language, geography, history and human experience.

That is not what is happening here.

The author selects questions.

The author searches for sources.

The author decides which distinctions matter.

The author interprets evidence.

The author can misunderstand things.

The author can miss things.

The author can be wrong.

This node exists so those limitations do not have to be rediscovered by the reader through guesswork.

It is not a ritual confession of bias.

It is part of the audit trail.

---

## 🦎 The Core Limitation

The author is not omniscient.

Neither is anybody else.

The practical question is therefore not:

> **Is the author perfectly neutral?**

It is:

> **Can the reader see what the author relied upon, what the author inferred, what the author could not verify, and where another competent reader might reasonably disagree?**

That is a much more useful standard.

---

## 🪞 Standpoint Exists

This is a survivor-authored archive with an identifiable ethical and political standpoint.

Polaris does not pretend that questions are selected from nowhere.

The author has views about:

- human rights;
- state power;
- racism;
- antisemitism;
- colonial and military violence;
- civilian protection;
- surveillance;
- institutional accountability;
- information power;
- and the treatment of Palestinians.

Those views affect what questions appear important enough to investigate.

They may also affect which explanations initially seem plausible.

That is precisely why the archive needs:

- visible sources;
- explicit claim classification;
- contrary evidence;
- correction history;
- and an evidential standard that survives politically inconvenient findings.

A standpoint can shape inquiry without being allowed to dictate the answer.

---

## 🧿 Political Commitment Does Not Confer Factual Immunity

Supporting Palestinian rights does not make a Palestine-supporting claim true.

Opposing Israeli government policy does not make an allegation about the Israeli government true.

Recognising antisemitism does not require accepting every allegation of antisemitism.

Recognising anti-Palestinian racism does not require accepting every allegation made in response to it.

Being morally horrified by an event does not establish who caused it.

Being sceptical of an institution does not establish that the institution is lying.

This archive should therefore be willing to correct misinformation that appears politically useful to positions the author broadly supports.

Otherwise the methodology is decorative.

---

## 📍 The Author Is Not Reporting From The Ground

Unless a particular node explicitly says otherwise, the author is **not functioning as an on-the-ground correspondent in Gaza, Israel or the West Bank**.

The archive therefore depends heavily on material produced by others, including:

- witnesses;
- journalists;
- official records;
- humanitarian organisations;
- researchers;
- courts;
- investigators;
- imagery;
- datasets;
- archives;
- and open-source material.

That creates unavoidable distance between the author and many events being described.

A well-sourced remote analysis can still be useful.

It should not pretend to be direct observation.

---

## 👁️ Access Is Unequal

Evidence from a conflict zone is not produced under laboratory conditions.

Access can be affected by:

- military restrictions;
- border controls;
- communications outages;
- physical danger;
- displacement;
- destruction of infrastructure;
- death or detention of witnesses;
- institutional collapse;
- classification;
- censorship;
- source protection;
- platform removal;
- and simple inability to reach a place.

This means evidential gaps are not evenly distributed.

Some actors possess surveillance, intelligence, forensic, satellite or administrative capabilities unavailable to ordinary people.

Some communities may possess direct lived knowledge while lacking the infrastructure required to preserve or internationally distribute it.

The archive must therefore avoid treating **documentation capacity** as if it were identical to **truth capacity**.

The best-documented actor is not automatically the most truthful actor.

The least-documented event is not automatically imaginary.

---

## 🌍 Geography Creates Distance

The archive is produced from outside the immediate conflict zone.

That affects:

- access to local reporting;
- physical verification;
- cultural context;
- informal knowledge;
- language exposure;
- speed of verification;
- and the ability to distinguish ordinary local knowledge from claims that appear novel only to outsiders.

Remote research should therefore remain modest about what proximity can add.

It should also avoid romanticising proximity.

Being physically present does not make a witness omniscient either.

Different forms of access produce different kinds of knowledge.

---

## 🧵 Language Is A Material Limitation

Israel and Palestine cannot responsibly be studied through English alone.

Important material exists in:

- Arabic;
- Hebrew;
- and other languages relevant to international diplomacy, archives and diaspora communities.

Translation can alter:

- political terminology;
- legal meaning;
- religious language;
- tone;
- idiom;
- humour;
- threats;
- ambiguity;
- and register.

Where the author cannot independently evaluate the original language, that limitation should be stated rather than hidden behind a confident English translation.

Where wording is materially important, the archive should seek:

- the original text;
- more than one translation where necessary;
- qualified human linguistic expertise for high-stakes disputes;
- and clear separation between literal and interpretive translation.

Machine translation is a tool.

It is not a magic linguistic witness.

---

## 🎓 Expertise Has Boundaries

This archive crosses disciplines.

That is useful.

It is also dangerous.

Questions about Palestine can require specialist knowledge in:

- history;
- international humanitarian law;
- international criminal law;
- constitutional law;
- military operations;
- weapons analysis;
- medicine;
- epidemiology;
- demography;
- statistics;
- economics;
- archaeology;
- theology;
- linguistics;
- intelligence;
- cybersecurity;
- geospatial analysis;
- media studies;
- and political science.

No individual author is professionally expert in all of those fields.

A source can therefore be accurately quoted while its specialist significance is misunderstood.

Where specialist interpretation materially affects a conclusion, the archive should seek specialist sources and make the competence boundary visible.

---

## ⚖️ This Archive Is Not A Law Firm

The author is not acting as the reader's lawyer.

Legal sections can:

- identify legal texts;
- distinguish procedural stages;
- compare legal arguments;
- summarise judgments;
- trace litigation;
- and document what courts or legal bodies have actually said.

They do not replace jurisdiction-specific legal advice.

This matters particularly where a reader intends to:

- publish allegations;
- respond to legal threats;
- make regulatory complaints;
- litigate;
- disclose confidential material;
- or rely on a legal interpretation in a professional setting.

Go and get a lawyer.

Preferably before everything is on fire.

---

## 🩺 Medical And Public-Health Claims Need Their Own Discipline

Conflict reporting frequently contains medical claims.

These can concern:

- deaths;
- injuries;
- disability;
- starvation;
- infectious disease;
- maternal health;
- trauma;
- hospital capacity;
- medication;
- rehabilitation;
- and mortality estimates.

Medical plausibility is not the same thing as factual verification of an individual event.

Likewise, clinical knowledge does not automatically confer expertise in epidemiological modelling, forensic pathology or conflict mortality estimation.

Medical and public-health claims should therefore be checked against the appropriate form of evidence rather than granted authority because they sound medically technical.

---

## 📊 Statistical Literacy Is Not Dataset Omniscience

Numbers can look unusually solid.

They are often where the assumptions are hiding.

The author may be able to interrogate:

- denominators;
- rates;
- sampling;
- missing data;
- uncertainty;
- definitions;
- revisions;
- and obvious methodological problems.

That does not mean every dataset can be independently reproduced.

Some rely on inaccessible administrative systems.

Some require specialist statistical expertise.

Some contain proprietary or unpublished components.

Some are produced under wartime conditions in which normal verification is impossible.

Where independent replication is not possible, say so.

---

## 🛰️ OSINT Has Competence Boundaries

Open-source intelligence can be extremely powerful.

It can also encourage people with an internet connection to believe they have become weapons engineers before lunch.

Geolocation, chronolocation, satellite analysis, blast analysis, weapons identification and military reconstruction require different skills.

A convincing thread is not automatically a forensic report.

Where the archive relies on specialist OSINT:

- identify the analyst;
- inspect methodology where possible;
- distinguish observation from inference;
- look for independent replication;
- and avoid pretending the author personally verified technical conclusions that require expertise the author does not possess.

---

## 🖼️ Visual Evidence Has Limits

Images feel immediate.

That can make them unusually persuasive.

The author may be able to inspect:

- visible content;
- metadata where available;
- obvious editing;
- reverse-image results;
- location clues;
- sequence;
- and contextual inconsistencies.

That does not mean every image or video can be forensically authenticated.

Deepfakes are not the only problem.

Real media can be attached to:

- the wrong date;
- the wrong place;
- the wrong event;
- the wrong actor;
- or a conclusion extending beyond what is visible.

Where authentication matters and cannot be independently established, preserve that uncertainty.

---

## 📰 Media Access Is Imperfect

Not every publication is equally accessible.

Sources may be:

- paywalled;
- geoblocked;
- removed;
- inaccessible to automated tools;
- archived incompletely;
- available only through snippets;
- or republished without their original context.

The archive should not pretend to have read material it has not read.

If only a headline, abstract or secondary quotation is available, say so.

Where an important proposition depends on inaccessible material, seek another route to the underlying evidence.

---

## 🤖 AI Assistance Is A Limitation As Well As A Tool

Generative AI may be used in building this archive.

That creates useful capabilities and additional failure modes.

AI can assist with:

- source discovery;
- comparison;
- summarisation;
- organisation;
- drafting;
- terminology;
- and identifying questions worth checking.

It can also:

- invent citations;
- misread sources;
- merge events;
- omit qualifiers;
- reproduce dominant framings;
- flatten minority evidence;
- overstate legal conclusions;
- and make uncertain material sound settled.

This is particularly relevant because organised communications campaigns increasingly attempt to influence the searchable and retrievable information environment encountered by AI systems.

AI assistance therefore does not remove the need for provenance.

It increases it.

---

## 🕸️ Search Results Are Not A Neutral Sample Of Reality

What the author can find affects what the author can check.

Search visibility can be shaped by:

- language;
- SEO;
- public-relations campaigns;
- platform ranking;
- media repetition;
- link structures;
- institutional authority;
- publication volume;
- moderation;
- geography;
- personalisation;
- and the availability of machine-readable material.

A claim that is easy to search is not necessarily better evidenced.

A source that is difficult to find is not necessarily more authentic.

Search is part of the information environment being studied.

---

## 💰 Funding And Material Interests Are Easy To Overread

The archive examines:

- public diplomacy;
- lobbying;
- PR;
- corporate relationships;
- financial interests;
- procurement;
- investment;
- and institutional incentives.

This creates a particular analytical risk.

It is easy to move from:

> **Actor A has a material interest in outcome B**

to:

> **therefore Actor A caused information event C.**

That leap requires evidence.

Funding can establish funding.

A contract can establish a contractual relationship.

A lobbying filing can establish declared lobbying activity.

An investment can establish financial exposure.

None automatically establishes covert coordination, dishonesty or causation.

The archive should document incentives without allowing them to become a universal explanation.

---

## 🧬 Pattern Recognition Can Become Pattern Imposition

Polaris is structurally interested in systems, feedback, institutional incentives and information environments.

That orientation is useful.

It creates its own danger.

Once a researcher becomes good at seeing systems, everything can start looking like a system.

Coincidences can become networks.

Shared incentives can become coordination.

Repeated language can become evidence of common authorship.

Temporal proximity can become causation.

The corrective is not to stop looking for patterns.

It is to ask:

> **What evidence would distinguish this pattern from coincidence, shared incentives, common sourcing or ordinary institutional behaviour?**

Patterns are hypotheses until the evidence carries them further.

---

## 🧠 Cognitive Load Matters

This subject is emotionally and cognitively demanding.

Repeated exposure to:

- death;
- injury;
- starvation;
- hostage testimony;
- sexual violence allegations;
- destroyed homes;
- grieving families;
- racism;
- antisemitism;
- dehumanising language;
- and graphic imagery

can affect attention, judgement and tolerance for uncertainty.

Fatigue matters too.

So does anger.

So does grief.

So does the desire for one more source to finally make the picture simple.

When the author is overloaded, the correct response may be to stop.

A tired factcheck is not made more rigorous by suffering heroically through another forty tabs.

---

## 🪿 Embodiment Is Not Noise

The factchecker has a body.

So does the witness.

So does the reader.

People encounter evidence while:

- frightened;
- grieving;
- angry;
- exhausted;
- traumatised;
- politically threatened;
- socially isolated;
- or saturated by repeated images of violence.

That does not make their knowledge worthless.

It means information is experienced rather than merely downloaded.

The archive should neither romanticise emotion nor pretend cognition becomes pure when emotion is removed.

This is one reason **🪿 Embodying The Information Ecology** exists as its own branch.

---

## 🧿 Selection Is A Limitation

There are more claims about Palestine than this archive can ever check.

Choosing one question means not choosing another.

That creates selection effects.

A folder containing fifty debunked false claims from one political constituency can create the impression that the other constituency rarely produces misinformation even if the archive simply did not investigate it.

Therefore the archive should remain aware of:

- which questions it selects;
- why;
- where those questions came from;
- which categories are overrepresented;
- and which absences might distort the apparent landscape.

An archive is shaped by what it does not contain.

---

## 🧭 Evidence Can Change

A conclusion can be reasonable today and wrong six months later because better evidence emerges.

This is especially likely with:

- casualty identification;
- missing persons;
- battlefield attribution;
- hostage information;
- forensic investigations;
- intelligence disclosures;
- court proceedings;
- archival releases;
- mortality studies;
- and investigations conducted after access becomes possible.

The archive should therefore distinguish:

> **wrong when written**

from:

> **superseded by later evidence.**

Those are not the same failure.

---

## 🪫 Some Questions Will Remain Unresolved

The archive will sometimes reach the end of the available evidence and still not know.

That is not an invitation to choose the answer that feels most plausible.

It is a finding.

> **Unresolved.**

Sometimes:

> **probably, but not established.**

Sometimes:

> **the available evidence supports several possibilities.**

Sometimes:

> **we may never know.**

The ability to stop there is part of the methodology.

---

## 🔄 Corrections Are Expected

This archive will contain mistakes.

The goal is not to achieve a mythical state in which correction is unnecessary.

The goal is to make correction possible.

When an error is identified:

- preserve the evidential history;
- correct the current text;
- explain material changes;
- update downstream claims;
- and learn from the failure mode.

A project that cannot visibly change its mind is not demonstrating confidence.

It is demonstrating fragility.

---

## 🌾 What The Reader Should Expect

The reader should expect this archive to try to:

- show its sources;
- distinguish primary evidence from downstream reporting;
- preserve uncertainty;
- separate fact from inference;
- distinguish legal findings from legal arguments;
- treat statistics as methodological objects;
- disclose relevant interests;
- avoid inferring coordination without evidence;
- acknowledge inaccessible evidence;
- correct mistakes;
- and say **I don't know** when that is the strongest answer available.

The reader should **not** expect:

- omniscience;
- perfect neutrality;
- comprehensive coverage;
- instant verification of breaking events;
- professional expertise in every discipline;
- independent authentication of every image or dataset;
- legal advice;
- or an archive magically untouched by the information environment it is attempting to study.

---

## 🪞 The Offer

The author cannot offer a view from nowhere.

The archive can offer something else:

> **Here is the claim.**

> **Here is where it came from.**

> **Here is the evidence I could find.**

> **Here is what I think it establishes.**

> **Here is what I inferred.**

> **Here is what I could not verify.**

> **Here is what might change the conclusion.**

> **Here is where I may be wrong.**

That is the bargain.
