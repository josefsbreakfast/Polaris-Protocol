# 📚 Sources & Evidence Register

**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*Working register for source provenance, evidential use, limitations and future source review across Palestine Factchecking.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node is deliberately a **shell**.

It is not yet a finished source hierarchy, approved bibliography or declaration that particular organisations are inherently reliable or unreliable.

Its purpose is to provide somewhere structured to think.

The Palestine Factchecking archive will draw on sources with very different relationships to the events and propositions being checked:

- primary documents;
- courts and legal materials;
- governments;
- militaries;
- Palestinian institutions;
- Israeli institutions;
- international organisations;
- humanitarian organisations;
- journalists;
- civil-society organisations;
- academic research;
- historical archives;
- datasets;
- polling;
- OSINT;
- satellite and geospatial evidence;
- photographs, video and audio;
- eyewitness testimony;
- advocacy organisations;
- think tanks;
- public-relations material;
- corporate communications;
- social media;
- search engines;
- Wikipedia and other reference works;
- and generative AI.

These sources do different jobs.

The central question is therefore not:

> **Is this a good source?**

It is:

> **A good source for what?**

---

## 🧭 Working Principle

A source should be evaluated in relation to the proposition it is being used to establish.

An official statement may be the best available primary source for what an institution says its policy is.

That does not automatically make it the best source for whether the policy was implemented as described.

An eyewitness may be uniquely authoritative about what they personally experienced.

That does not give them omniscient knowledge of an entire event.

A newspaper may perform excellent independent investigation.

Another article from the same publication may simply reproduce a wire report or official statement.

A dataset may be methodologically strong for one question and unusable for another.

A partisan or interested source can contain important primary evidence.

A prestigious source can still be wrong.

The register should preserve those distinctions.

## 📚 What This Register Should Eventually Contain

For significant recurring sources, record where useful:

| Field | What to record |
|---|---|
| **Source / organisation** | Name of source |
| **Source class** | Court, government, NGO, media, dataset, academic, archive, etc. |
| **Jurisdiction / location** | Where relevant |
| **Primary or secondary** | Relationship to underlying evidence |
| **Useful for** | What this source can particularly help establish |
| **Does not establish by itself** | Important evidential limits |
| **Methodology** | How information is collected or produced |
| **Funding / governance** | Materially relevant institutional context |
| **Known interests** | Political, financial, strategic or advocacy interests where relevant |
| **Access limitations** | Paywall, language, geographic access, archive limitations, etc. |
| **Correction policy** | Where known |
| **Archive status** | Whether copies or stable links should be preserved |
| **Notes** | Context, cautions and cross-references |
| **Last reviewed** | Date the source assessment was last checked |

Not every source needs every field.

This is an evidence register, not an administrative punishment.

### 📰 Journalism

This category will need substantial work.

Rather than assigning permanent labels such as **reliable** or **unreliable**, consider:

- original reporting capacity;
- reporters physically present;
- access to primary documents;
- investigative record;
- correction practice;
- ownership;
- editorial positioning;
- dependence on wire services;
- use of anonymous officials;
- headline/body divergence;
- language editions;
- and whether multiple reports share the same underlying source.

Possible subgroups to consider later:

- Palestinian media;
- Israeli media;
- regional media;
- British media;
- US media;
- European media;
- international wire services;
- specialist investigative outlets;
- financial press;
- technology press.

### 📊 Statistics & Datasets

To consider:

- casualty datasets;
- demographic datasets;
- displacement figures;
- detention data;
- aid flows;
- destruction assessments;
- economic data;
- polling;
- military datasets;
- trade and investment data;
- arms-transfer data.

Every important dataset should eventually answer:

> **Who counted what, how, where, and when?**

Also record:

- denominator;
- inclusion criteria;
- exclusions;
- duplicates;
- missingness;
- revisions;
- uncertainty;
- verification threshold;
- and whether the dataset is being used outside its intended purpose.

### 👁️ Eyewitness, Testimony & Survivor Accounts

First-person evidence matters.

It also needs to be handled according to what a witness could reasonably know.

Record where appropriate:

- proximity;
- date;
- location;
- contemporaneous or retrospective nature;
- translation;
- corroboration;
- trauma and memory considerations;
- whether testimony has changed;
- and whether the witness is describing direct perception or repeating information received from others.

Do not reduce testimony to “anecdote”.

Do not inflate testimony into omniscience.

Questions for later:

- What is the organisation's mission?
- What evidence does it collect directly?
- What methodology does it publish?

### 🧵 Social Media

Social media can contain:

- primary eyewitness material;
- official statements;
- propaganda;
- breaking news;
- archival material;
- recycled misinformation;
- deleted evidence;
- jokes detached from context;
- mistranslations;
- coordinated campaigns;
- ordinary people being wrong on the internet.

For significant posts, consider preserving:

- original account;
- URL;
- timestamp;
- media;
- surrounding thread;
- edits;
- archive copy where lawful;
- and whether the account is actually who it claims to be.

Virality is not corroboration.

---

### 📖 Wikipedia & Reference Works

Useful for:

- orientation;
- terminology;
- finding dates;
- identifying disputes;
- discovering references;
- locating legislation, cases, books and reports.

Usually not the end of the evidential chain for contentious propositions.

Where the underlying citation is available, follow it.

The fact that Wikipedia is editable does not make everything on it false.

The fact that something has a citation does not mean the citation supports the sentence as written.

Read the source.

---

### 🤖 Generative AI

Useful for:

- generating research questions;
- identifying terminology;
- locating possible sources;
- comparing formulations;
- surfacing contradictions;
- organising large source sets;
- and testing how information is mediated.

Not a primary source for the underlying events.

Where an AI system provides a factual proposition:

> **follow the citation or find the underlying evidence.**

Where an AI answer itself is the object of study, preserve:

- model/system;
- date;
- prompt;
- response;
- citations;
- relevant settings where known;
- and reproducibility limitations.

## 🔗 Source Independence

A later version of this register should track **source ancestry**.

Examples:

```text
Government statement
        ↓
Wire service
        ↓
Five newspapers
        ↓
Wikipedia
        ↓
AI answer
```

or:

```text
Primary dataset
      ↓
NGO report
      ↓
News article
      ↓
Political speech
      ↓
Social post
```

Five downstream sources do not necessarily equal five independent evidential routes.

Where possible, identify the trunk.

## 🪞 Interests & Funding

This section is intentionally unfinished.

Funding and institutional interests can matter.

They should not become a lazy reliability score.

For recurring sources, later review may consider:

- government funding;
- philanthropic funding;
- corporate funding;
- membership structures;
- political affiliations;
- statutory mandate;
- commercial relationships;
- litigation interests;
- advocacy objectives;
- donor transparency;
- and governance.

The relevant question is:

> **Does this interest materially affect how we should interpret this source for this proposition?**

Not:

> **Can we find an interest and therefore throw the source away?**

## 🗄️ Preservation & Archiving

To develop.

Potential practices:

- stable URLs;
- archived webpages;
- downloaded public PDFs;
- repository copies where lawful;
- publication dates;
- access dates;
- hashes for important files;
