# 🪬 Why This Folder Exists

**First created:** 2026-08-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-20  
*Why Palestine requires not only factchecking of individual claims, but scrutiny of the information environment in which those claims are produced, financed, regulated, distributed, retrieved and believed.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder exists because people trying to understand Palestine are not encountering an untouched field of information.

They are encountering history, testimony, journalism, military statements, government communications, legal proceedings, statistics, advocacy, scholarship, lobbying, public relations, advertising, social media, search engines, corporate communications and generative AI.

They are also encountering institutions operating inside legal, contractual, financial and reputational environments that can affect what those institutions are willing to fund, publish, procure, endorse, boycott, divest from, host, describe or challenge.

Some of the resulting material is excellent.

Some is wrong.

Some is incomplete.

Some is produced by parties with direct interests in how events are understood.

Some is produced by people trying, sincerely and imperfectly, to establish what happened.

Often several of those descriptions apply at once.

The problem is therefore larger than:

> **Is this claim true or false?**

We also need to ask:

> **Where did this claim come from?**

> **What evidence supports it?**

> **What kind of proposition is it?**

> **Who is repeating it?**

> **What interests surround its production or distribution?**

> **What incentives surround the institutions handling it?**

> **Has its wording changed as it travelled?**

> **What can the available evidence actually establish?**

And increasingly:

> **What happens when a machine synthesises this information and gives the resulting answer back to a human being as a conversation?**

That is why this folder exists.

## 🪬 Palestine Is Not Unknowable

The first principle is important.

The existence of propaganda does not mean that facts cease to exist.

The existence of competing narratives does not mean that every account is equally well supported.

The existence of uncertainty does not mean that nothing can be established.

And discovering that an interested actor has promoted a claim does not, by itself, prove the claim false.

Epistemic nihilism is not factchecking.

Neither is credulity.

The work is slower and less glamorous:

**find the proposition, find the evidence, find the provenance, establish the limits, and preserve the uncertainty that remains.**

---

## 🕸️ The Information Environment Is Not Naturally Occurring

Governments attempt to influence public opinion.

So do political organisations, campaign groups, corporations, investors, charities, lobbyists, public-relations firms, think tanks, universities, law firms, militaries, NGOs and individual activists.

This is not peculiar to Israel.

It is a normal feature of modern political and commercial life.

But normal does not mean irrelevant.

Israel and Palestine sit inside an unusually dense international information environment in which governments, companies and institutions can have substantial political, strategic, reputational or financial interests.

Some interests are ideological.

Some are electoral.

Some concern national security.

Some concern diplomatic relationships.

Some are commercial.

Some concern investment, contracts, procurement, technology, defence, banking, philanthropy, universities or institutional reputation.

Sometimes several overlap.

Material interest does not establish dishonesty.

It establishes **context worth documenting**.

## 📣 Public Diplomacy Is Infrastructure

Israel's investment in public diplomacy provides a particularly clear example of why the information environment itself needs to be studied.

In December 2024, *The Times of Israel* reported that the Israeli Foreign Ministry was to receive an additional **$150 million** for public diplomacy in the 2025 budget — more than twenty times what such activity had typically received previously.

The reported plans included influencing sentiment in foreign press and social media, with particular attention to American university campuses and cooperation with advocacy organisations.

By December 2025, *The Jerusalem Post* reported agreement on **NIS 2.35 billion** for Israel advocacy campaigns in the proposed 2026 budget. The plans included social-media campaigns, cooperation with civil-society organisations, international delegations and influencer activity, alongside the creation of a dedicated public-diplomacy unit within the Foreign Ministry.

By April 2026, *The Times of Israel* reported that the approved 2026 national budget contained roughly **$730 million for public diplomacy**, nearly five times the previous year's allocation.

Its reporting described expenditure including:

- international social-media advertising;
- foreign delegations involving politicians, religious figures, influencers and university leaders;
- a media monitoring operation tracking hundreds of outlets and thousands of Israel-related items;
- paid influencer activity;
- campaigns directed at particular constituencies;
- and contracts involving digital and AI-related communications work.

The point is not that Israel possesses a public-diplomacy apparatus.

Many states do.

The important fact for this archive is that **substantial resources are deliberately being invested in the information environment through which international audiences encounter Israel and Palestine**.

That environment should therefore be studied as infrastructure rather than treated as naturally occurring background.

## 🤖 The Audience Is No Longer Only Human

There is another development.

Public relations traditionally tried to influence:

**the person reading the information.**

Search optimisation added another target:

**the system deciding which information the person sees.**

Generative AI introduces another:

**the system synthesising information into an answer.**

Reporting during 2025 and 2026 describes Israeli government-funded communications work involving the US firm Clock Tower X.

Ynet reported on US Foreign Agents Registration Act filings describing work to build:

> “websites and content to provide GPT framing results in GPT conversations.”

The same programme has subsequently been described very differently by different publications.

*The New Arab* headlined the activity as an effort to “train ChatGPT to be pro-Israel.”

*International Business Times UK* described an attempt to shape what ChatGPT says about Gaza.

Ynet described an effort to influence generative-AI systems as part of a much larger public-diplomacy campaign.

*Politico Influence* has also reported on the Israeli PR effort in the context of answering users' ChatGPT questions.

Those descriptions should not simply be treated as interchangeable.

That discrepancy is itself useful.

## 🔬 What The Evidence Does — And Does Not — Establish

There is a meaningful difference between:

1. creating material intended to influence the information environment available to AI systems;
2. getting that material indexed or retrieved;
3. having that material cited by an AI system;
4. influencing a particular generated answer;
5. changing model training;
6. altering model weights;
7. controlling what a chatbot says.

Those are different technical and evidential propositions.

Reporting and disclosed contractual language support the existence of deliberate work aimed at influencing the digital information environment, including material explicitly described in terms of “GPT framing.”

Later reporting has also described campaign-linked websites appearing in some AI-generated results.

That does **not**, by itself, establish:

- a contract with OpenAI;
- privileged access to ChatGPT;
- direct modification of OpenAI's models;
- permanent alteration of model weights;
- control over ChatGPT's answers;
- or that any particular ChatGPT answer was caused by this campaign.

This distinction matters.

A documented information campaign does not become imaginary because an overstated headline describes its mechanism badly.

And an alarming headline does not become technically accurate merely because the underlying information campaign is real.

Both propositions can be true:

> **An organised attempt to influence AI-facing information can be documented.**

and:

> **“They are training ChatGPT” may inaccurately describe how that influence is being attempted.**

This archive needs to be capable of holding both thoughts at once.

## 🧿 Factcheck The Factcheck

There is an obvious trap here.

Once people learn that governments, companies or political organisations attempt to influence information systems, every unwanted result can begin to look like evidence of manipulation.

That is not a reliable method either.

If a government funds material intended to influence AI answers, that fact can be documented.

If an AI system retrieves that material, that can potentially be tested.

If someone claims that a particular answer was caused by the campaign, **that additional causal claim requires additional evidence**.

Factchecking the information environment includes factchecking claims about manipulation of the information environment.

Otherwise scepticism simply becomes another route to credulity.

## 🌍 The State Is Only One Actor

State public diplomacy is unusually visible because budgets, contracts, parliamentary proceedings and foreign-agent registrations can leave documentary traces.

The wider influence economy is harder to map.

Israel and Palestine are surrounded by an international ecosystem containing:

- public-relations agencies;
- public-affairs consultancies;
- lobbying organisations;
- banks and investment institutions;
- defence and technology companies;
- institutional investors;
- universities;
- charities and foundations;
- law firms;
- religious organisations;
- advocacy networks;
- media organisations;
- think tanks;
- governments and diplomatic missions;
- platform companies;
- search providers;
- and AI companies.

Many have legitimate reasons to communicate publicly.

Some also have material interests in Israel, Palestine, regional security, defence, technology, investment or the political relationships surrounding them.

Their communications are not automatically false because those interests exist.

But the interests should not become invisible merely because professional communications have successfully made the resulting message look frictionless.

## ⚖️ The Information Environment Is Also Regulatory

Information environments are made not only from messages.

They are made from **incentives, rules, contracts, liabilities and institutional risk**.

Anti-BDS laws provide one useful example.

Measures described collectively as anti-BDS laws vary substantially between jurisdictions. In the United States, many state-level measures have taken one of two broad forms: restrictions connected to public contracting, including requirements concerning participation in boycotts of Israel; and investment rules directing public funds away from entities participating in such boycotts.

These measures have also generated constitutional litigation and sustained disagreement about political boycotts, discrimination, public procurement and First Amendment protections.

The wider international picture is not uniform. Different countries have taken different legal and policy approaches to boycotts, public procurement, public investment and political expression relating to Israel.

That complexity matters.

The existence of an anti-BDS law does **not** establish that a particular bank, university, contractor, publisher, platform or public body made a particular decision because of that law.

A relevant legal or commercial incentive is not proof of a causal pathway.

But neither should the incentive disappear from the analysis.

Institutions make decisions inside environments containing:

- procurement requirements;
- contractual obligations;
- investment rules;
- regulatory duties;
- litigation risk;
- donor and investor relationships;
- reputational concerns;
- employment obligations;
- and political pressure.

Those conditions can affect organisational behaviour without producing a memo saying:

> **We made this decision because of Palestine.**

The methodological rule is therefore:

> **Do not infer a causal pathway merely because a relevant incentive exists — but do not erase the incentive from the analysis either.**

This is especially important when examining institutions with material relationships to Israel, Israeli companies, occupied territory, defence procurement, technology infrastructure, investment portfolios or public contracts.

The question is not:

> **Can we find an incentive and therefore declare motive proven?**

It is:

> **What institutional environment was this actor operating inside, and what evidence exists about how that environment affected the decision?**

That is a factcheckable question.

## 🧬 Coordination Is Not Required

One of the easiest mistakes in information-environment analysis is assuming that visible alignment requires a single hidden command structure.

It does not.

A government can pursue public diplomacy.

A company can protect a commercial relationship.

A bank can manage investor exposure.

A university can protect institutional partnerships.

A lobbying organisation can pursue policy objectives.

A PR company can serve its client.

A public body can respond to procurement rules.

A lawyer can advise against litigation risk.

A journalist can follow editorial incentives.

A platform can optimise engagement.

An AI retrieval system can preferentially surface material that has been successfully optimised for discoverability.

Those actors do not need to sit in a room together.

They do not even need to agree with one another.

Their incentives can nevertheless produce an information environment in which particular narratives become unusually visible, professionally packaged, repeatedly encountered or difficult to trace back to their origins.

> **An information environment can be heavily managed without every participant in it being coordinated.**

That distinction matters because otherwise structural analysis degenerates into conspiracy theory precisely where ordinary institutional incentives already explain a great deal.

## 🧭 Provenance, Not Teams

The response to this environment cannot be:

**never trust Israeli sources.**

Nor can it be:

**never trust Palestinian sources.**

Both would make serious factchecking impossible.

Israeli government documents may be primary evidence about Israeli government policy.

Palestinian records may be primary evidence about Palestinian institutions or lived conditions.

An IDF statement is primary evidence of what the IDF stated.

A Hamas statement is primary evidence of what Hamas stated.

Neither automatically proves the underlying factual proposition.

Humanitarian organisations, journalists, courts, researchers, governments, witnesses, satellite analysts and civil-society organisations likewise have different access, methodologies, incentives and limitations.

The useful questions are:

**What is the source?**

**How close is it to the proposition?**

**What does it actually establish?**

**Can it be independently corroborated?**

**What are its limitations?**

**Has the claim changed downstream?**

The archive follows provenance rather than teams.

## 🧵 Headlines Are Claims Too

The reporting around AI influence demonstrates another reason for maintaining this folder.

Consider the difference between:

> **creating web content intended to influence GPT framing**

and:

> **training ChatGPT to be pro-Israel**

and:

> **shaping what ChatGPT says about Gaza.**

Those phrases describe overlapping concerns.

They do not make identical technical claims.

A reader encountering only headlines can therefore acquire a stronger causal proposition than the underlying documentation necessarily supports.

That does not require deliberate deception.

Compression does this.

Headlines do this.

Social media does this.

Political argument does this.

Human memory does this.

AI summaries can do it too.

Factchecking therefore includes examining **what happened to a claim while it travelled**.

## 🪿 Why This Matters For Generative AI

A conventional search engine usually exposes at least some of its source environment.

The user sees links.

A conversational AI can make the mediation less visible.

Several sources become a paragraph.

Contradictions become synthesis.

Uncertainty can become fluent prose.

A source with a material interest can lose its institutional context as its information is incorporated into a wider answer.

And the resulting text can sound remarkably like knowledge simply because it is written clearly.

This creates a distinctive information-ecology problem.

The user is not merely reading sources.

The user is **experiencing a machine-mediated interpretation of an information environment**.

That is why:

> **“ChatGPT said it” is not a citation.**

Neither is:

> **“Google says.”**

Nor:

> **“I saw a screenshot.”**

These may be useful starting points.

They are not the end of the evidential chain.

## 🌾 Why Build This Now

There is already an enormous amount of information about Palestine.

The problem is not scarcity.

The problem is navigating abundance without allowing provenance, chronology, uncertainty and category distinctions to disappear.

This archive therefore exists to make it easier to ask ordinary questions carefully.

What happened?

When?

According to whom?

How many?

Measured how?

What does the law actually say?

What has a court actually decided?

What is historical fact and what is historical interpretation?

What does this word mean in this context?

Who funded this?

What interest might they have?

What legal or commercial environment were they operating inside?

Does that establish motive, or merely identify a possible incentive?

Does that interest affect the evidence?

What remains disputed?

What remains unknown?

And what has happened to the information between the original source and the thing now appearing on somebody's screen?

That is not an attempt to construct a perfectly neutral archive.

It is an attempt to construct an **auditable one**.

## 🧿 The Working Principle

The reason for building this folder can ultimately be reduced to something fairly simple:

> **If powerful actors are investing money, law, institutional capacity or professional expertise in shaping the information environment, preserving provenance becomes more important, not less.**

That principle applies whether the actor is Israeli, Palestinian, British, American, Iranian, corporate, charitable, political, military or commercial.

It also applies to this archive.

Claims should remain inspectable.

Sources should remain visible.

Corrections should remain possible.

Uncertainty should survive editing.

Incentives should be documented without being mistaken for proof of motive.

And no source — including this one — gets to become authoritative merely by sounding certain.
