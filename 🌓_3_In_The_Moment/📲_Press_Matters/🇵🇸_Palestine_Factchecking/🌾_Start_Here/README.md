## 🛰️ Orientation

This is the methodological front door to **🇵🇸 Palestine Factchecking**.

Israel and Palestine are not difficult to understand because facts do not exist.

They are difficult to understand because historical claims, breaking news, law, statistics, identity, ethics, propaganda, testimony, state communications, journalism, social media and personal experience are routinely presented in the same information environment — often without making clear **what kind of claim is actually being made**.

A casualty estimate is not a legal finding.

A legal allegation is not a judicial determination.

An official statement is evidence that an institution made that statement; it is not automatically proof that the underlying event happened as described.

An eyewitness can provide important evidence without possessing a complete view of an event.

A photograph can be genuine while the caption attached to it is false.

A historical fact can be real while the political conclusion someone draws from it remains contestable.

And an ethical judgement does not become an empirical fact merely because the person making it feels very strongly about it.

This folder establishes the working methods used to keep those things apart long enough to examine them properly.

### 🪬 Why This Folder Exists

Why maintain a dedicated Palestine factchecking archive at all?

This node explains the purpose, scope and boundaries of the project: what it is trying to establish, what it is not claiming to establish, and why preserving an inspectable evidential chain matters in an information environment this contested.

### ⚖️ Factchecking Principles

The methodological rules.

This covers distinctions between:

- established fact,
- attributed claim,
- allegation,
- estimate,
- inference,
- interpretation,
- legal argument,
- legal finding,
- historical interpretation,
- and ethical judgement.

It also establishes how uncertainty, conflicting evidence, corrections and changing information should be handled.

### 📚 Sources & Evidence Register

Where information comes from matters.

This node explains how the archive approaches primary documents, courts, governments, militaries, international organisations, humanitarian agencies, journalists, researchers, civil-society organisations, eyewitnesses, datasets, archives, OSINT, photographs, video and social media.

The basic rule is simple:

> **Using a source does not require adopting the source's worldview.**

Israeli sources belong in an archive about Israel and Palestine.

Palestinian sources belong in an archive about Israel and Palestine.

So do international, governmental, humanitarian, academic, journalistic and independent sources.

The question is not whether a source is politically comfortable.

The question is **what that source can actually establish**.

### 🦎 Limitations Of The Author

Factchecking does not become more reliable by pretending the person doing it has disappeared.

This node documents relevant limits on knowledge, access, expertise, language, verification and perspective.

The purpose is not ritual self-disqualification.

It is to let the reader distinguish between:

> **I can establish this.**

> **I have good reason to think this.**

> **I cannot independently verify this.**

> **I do not know.**

Those are different statements.

A useful archive should be able to make all four.

### 🗂️ Documentation, Complaints & Legal Pressure

Factchecking itself creates records.

Claims may be challenged. Sources may change. Pages disappear. Statistics are revised. Errors are corrected. Legal complaints may be made. Platforms may remove material. Institutions may dispute characterisations. Sometimes critics will be right.

This node establishes how challenges, corrections, amendments, takedowns, complaints and legal pressure should be documented without quietly rewriting the history of the archive.

A correction should make the archive **more trustworthy**, not make the original error mysteriously cease to have existed.

## 🧭 What This Folder Is For

`🌾_Start_Here/` contains the methodological infrastructure.

The substantive Palestine Factchecking archive is organised separately:

- **🫒 History** — historical events, chronology, competing narratives and historical claims.
- **🍉 Current Events** — developing events and claims requiring time-sensitive verification.
- **⚖️ Law** — international law, domestic law, courts, treaties, legal arguments and legal findings.
- **📊 Statistics** — casualties, injuries, displacement, detention, hostages, aid, demographics, destruction, polling and the methodologies behind numbers.
- **🧵 Identity & Language** — terminology, identity, translation, political labels and contested language.
- **🌍 Global Powers** — foreign governments, alliances, regional powers, international institutions, military support, diplomacy and external influence.
- **🧿 Ethics** — moral arguments and questions that cannot honestly be resolved merely by locating another statistic.
- **🪿 Embodying The Information Ecology** — what happens when human beings have to perceive, interpret, communicate and make decisions inside the information environment surrounding Israel and Palestine.

These categories will overlap.

That is expected.

The purpose of the structure is not to pretend the subjects can be perfectly separated. It is to make the crossings visible.

## 🔬 A Factcheck Is Not A Vibe Check

This archive does not use **factchecking** to mean determining whether a claim agrees with the author's politics.

Nor does disagreement with the author make a proposition false.

Where a proposition can be tested against evidence, the evidence should be shown.

Where sources disagree, the disagreement should be visible.

Where a number is an estimate, it should remain an estimate.

Where evidence is incomplete, the gap should remain a gap.

Where something changes, the date matters.

Where a legal question remains unresolved, the archive should not quietly promote an argument into a judgement.

And where the question is fundamentally ethical, political or philosophical, it should be treated as such rather than dressed up as a factual dispute.

## 🪞 Standpoint Is Not The Same Thing As Method

No archive is produced from nowhere.

Selection matters.

Language matters.

Sources matter.

The questions considered worth asking matter.

This project therefore does not promise a fictional view from nowhere.

It promises something more useful:

> **The reader should be able to inspect how a conclusion was reached.**

That means preserving sources, distinguishing evidence from interpretation, documenting uncertainty, correcting mistakes, identifying limitations and resisting the temptation to quietly lower an evidential standard because a claim happens to support a preferred conclusion.

A disclosed standpoint can be interrogated.

An undisclosed methodology cannot.

## 📊 Numbers Need Verbs

Statistics deserve particular care because numbers often acquire an authority their underlying methodology does not justify.

People were **reported** killed.

Researchers **estimated** mortality.

Authorities **identified** bodies.

An organisation **recorded** incidents.

Investigators **verified** cases.

A survey **sampled** respondents.

A database **included** records.

Those verbs matter.

So do denominators, definitions, collection methods, geographic boundaries, reporting delays, missing data, revisions and dates.

`📊_Statistics/` therefore treats methodology as part of the statistic rather than inconvenient small print beneath it.

---

## ⚖️ Law Needs Procedural Precision

Legal language presents a similar problem.

Courts consider applications, jurisdiction, evidence, provisional measures, warrants, charges, findings and judgements at different procedural stages.

These are not interchangeable.

Neither:

> **"A court has not finally ruled that X occurred."**

nor:

> **"Someone has alleged that X occurred."**

answers the underlying question by itself.

The archive should record what was alleged, what evidence was presented, what legal test applies, what the relevant body actually decided, what it did **not** decide, and where proceedings currently stand.

---

## 🕸️ Information Is Also An Environment

The archive does not stop at asking whether individual claims are true.

People encounter Palestine through feeds, headlines, photographs, notifications, search engines, television, demonstrations, government statements, group chats, recommendation systems, disappearing posts, factchecks, maps, casualty counters — and increasingly through generative AI.

That environment affects:

- what becomes visible,
- what appears important,
- what gets repeated,
- what disappears,
- what becomes emotionally saturated,
- what feels credible,
- what becomes exhausting to verify,
- and what a person eventually believes they know.

This is why **🪿 Embodying The Information Ecology** sits beside History, Law and Statistics rather than underneath them.

The information environment is not merely the container in which knowledge travels.

It can alter the conditions under which knowledge is produced, encountered and believed.

---

## 🧿 Principles Before Conclusions

The archive should be capable of reaching conclusions.

It should also be capable of saying:

**not established.**

**insufficient evidence.**

**contested.**

**outdated.**

**misleadingly framed.**

**partly correct.**

**we do not yet know.**

Those are not failures of factchecking.

Sometimes they are the most accurate findings available.

The standard should remain recognisable when the conclusion is inconvenient.

Otherwise we have not built a factchecking system.

We have built a machine for manufacturing receipts for things we already wanted to believe.
