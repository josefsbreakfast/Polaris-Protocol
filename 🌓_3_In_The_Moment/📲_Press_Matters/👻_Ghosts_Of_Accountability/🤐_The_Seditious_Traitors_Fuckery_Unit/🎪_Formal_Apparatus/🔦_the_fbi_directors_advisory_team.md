# 🔦 The FBI Director’s Advisory Team  
**First created:** 2026-07-12 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-12  
*An advisory title, an investigative function, and the question of who chose where to point the light.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The FBI Director’s Advisory Team sits at the most concrete and most evidentially delicate point in the Seditious Traitors Fuckery Unit framework.

Its official title sounds consultative.

Reporting has described something more operational:

- a small team positioned close to FBI Director Kash Patel;
- work on politically sensitive investigations;
- attention to perceived enemies of Donald Trump and the administration;
- development or discussion of possible cases against former senior officials;
- investigative theories connected to alleged prior “weaponisation” of government.

NOTUS reported that people familiar with the team referred to it internally as the **“payback squad.”**

That phrase is a reported nickname.

It is not the official institutional title.

The distinction matters.

> **The official name describes proximity to the director. The reported nickname describes how some insiders understood the political function.**

## 🏷️ What Is Publicly Established

Public reporting identifies an internal body called the:

**Director’s Advisory Team.**

The available public record does not yet provide a complete official account of:

- its charter;
- membership;
- case-selection criteria;
- reporting lines;

- inspection arrangements;

NOTUS reported in May 2026 that the team included former FBI agent John Eckenrode and was involved in politically sensitive work, including discussion of the Brennan investigation with federal prosecutors.

Later congressional scrutiny also referred to Patel’s advisory team while questioning reported bonus payments to members of his close circle.

These reports strengthen the evidence that an identifiable advisory structure exists.

They do not, by themselves, establish every allegation concerning its purpose or conduct.

## 🎭 Why The Official Name Matters

“Advisory team” sounds:

- consultative;
- strategic;
- managerial;
- non-operational;
- institutionally ordinary.

Reporting describes activity involving:

- investigations;
- possible criminal cases;
- contact with prosecutors;
- politically sensitive targets.

The gap between title and reported function is analytically important.

Bland naming can make an unusual structure appear administratively routine.

The issue is not that advisory structures are inherently improper.

FBI directors have historically used advisory boards and senior advisers for:

- strategy;
- management;
- science and technology;
- national-security consultation.

The question is whether this team:

- advises on investigations;
- directs investigations;
- develops cases;
- selects targets;
- redirects existing work;
- operates outside ordinary programme and field-office controls.

## 🔦 Advisory Or Investigative

The central institutional question is:

> **Is the team advising the director about cases, or acting as a mechanism through which cases are selected and built?**

Possible functions exist on a spectrum.

### Strategic advice

The team reviews broad bureau priorities and reports findings to the director.

### Case consultation

The team advises on unusually sensitive investigations already opened through ordinary channels.

### Special-project coordination

The team connects investigators, field offices and prosecutors around selected matters.

### Investigative development

The team identifies theories, gathers records or helps build cases.

### Political case selection

The team begins with a politically designated person and searches for an offence or investigative route.

The public record does not yet resolve where the team sits on this spectrum.

## ⚖️ Position Relative To DOJ Prosecutors

FBI agents investigate.

Federal prosecutors provide legal supervision, use grand-jury powers and decide whether charges should be pursued.

The advisory team therefore cannot be understood without asking:

- Which US attorney’s offices receive its work?
- Which Main Justice components supervise?
- At what stage are prosecutors involved?
- Do prosecutors receive evidence or political target lists?
- Are career prosecutors included in early review?
- What happens when prosecutors decline?

NOTUS reported that members of the team discussed the Brennan investigation with federal prosecutors.

That indicates at least some prosecutorial interface.

It does not establish the full chain of authority.

## 🔍 Predicate Or Person

The central operational test is whether the team begins with:

- predicating evidence;
- an identified offence;
- a politically selected person;
- a request from senior leadership.

These starting points are not equivalent.

### Evidence first

A factual predicate generates an investigation.

### Offence first

A possible statutory violation generates suspect identification.

### Person first

A named enemy generates a search for records and legal theories.

### Leadership request first

Senior officials direct the bureau to revisit or develop a matter.

This is where the team intersects with:

- person-first investigation;
- target routing;
- entrepreneurial repression;
- crown and regime protection.

## 🧪 Building A Comparable Case

NOTUS reported an expectation that agents build cases resembling prosecutions pursued against other former officials.

That formulation is important.

A legitimate analogy begins with:

- similar conduct;
- similar evidence;
- similar legal elements.

A politically driven analogy may begin with:

- a desired defendant;
- a prior prosecution used as a template;
- a demand for symmetrical punishment.

The question becomes:

> **Are investigators comparing conduct, or being asked to reproduce an outcome?**

“Find a case like X against person Y” is structurally different from “investigate evidence that person Y committed offence X.”

## 🧯 FBI Independence

The FBI director possesses substantial managerial authority.

That authority exists inside a constitutional and professional system intended to protect investigations from improper political influence.

The FBI’s own historical descriptions of the director’s role have emphasised responsibility for protecting bureau independence.

The issue is therefore not whether a director may create advisory structures.

It is whether the structure preserves:

- factual predication;
- legal consistency;
- professional supervision;
- institutional independence;
- equal treatment.
