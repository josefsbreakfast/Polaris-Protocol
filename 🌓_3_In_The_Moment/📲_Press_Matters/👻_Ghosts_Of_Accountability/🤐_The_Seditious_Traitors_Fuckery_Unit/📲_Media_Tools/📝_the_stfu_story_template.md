# 📝 The STFU Story Template  
**First created:** 2026-07-14 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
*Begin with the concrete consequence, prove the route, map the wider pattern, and return to what remains altered.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node provides a reusable structure for reporting individual cases within the **Seditious Traitors Fuckery Unit** apparatus.

The template is designed to prevent four common failures:

- personality-only framing;
- scandal chronology without mechanism;
- unsupported claims of central coordination;
- treating formal legality as the end of analysis.

The story should answer:

> **Who was targeted, who designated them, which vulnerability was activated, how the pressure travelled, what was unusual about the process, what else followed, and what changed afterward?**

The target must remain a person.

They should not disappear into the framework built to explain what happened to them.

---

## 🧭 The Core Story Sequence

Use this order:

1. concrete consequence;
2. target;
3. designation;
4. route;
5. process;
6. cumulative pressure;
7. wider apparatus;
8. government and institutional response;
9. legal and factual limits;
10. output;
11. residual consequence.

This sequence keeps the story:

- human;
- procedural;
- evidential;
- structural.

---

## 📰 Working Headline

The headline should identify:

- target;
- pressure route;
- political context.

Possible structures:

> **How [Institution] Used [Route] Against [Target] After [Political Designation]**

> **[Target] Faced [Concrete Consequence] After [Official Or Political Action]**

> **Inside The [Route] Used Against [Target Category]**

Avoid headlines that rely only on:

- outrage;
- personality;
- unsupported motive;
- historical analogy.

The mechanism should be visible.

---

## 🧷 Standfirst Or Dek

The standfirst should state:

- the practical consequence;
- the responsible actor;
- the evidence level connecting the action to political pressure;
- the wider relevance.

Example structure:

> After administration officials publicly targeted the firm, a federal action exposed its contracts and clients to new risk. Records show a formal referral, but do not establish that every later private decision was directed by the government.

This gives the reader both:

- the structural claim;
- the evidential boundary.

---

## 🚨 Opening — The Concrete Consequence First

Begin with what happened in practice.

Possible openings include:

- the target lost a job;
- a visa was revoked;
- a contract was frozen;
- a clearance was suspended;
- a law firm lost clients;
- a university changed policy;
- legal costs became unsustainable;
- threats forced security changes.

Do not begin with:

- abstract authoritarianism;
- a long constitutional theory;
- a historical analogy;
- a general description of Trump.

The first paragraph should show the cost.

---

## 🧍 Keep The Target Human

The opening should establish:

- who the person or institution is;
- what ordinary work they were doing;
- what changed;
- what the change cost;
- what remains uncertain.

Avoid making the target merely:

- “an example”;
- “a node”;
- “a case study.”

The system is being reported because it acted on real lives.

---

## 🎯 Section One — The Target

Answer:

- Who is affected?
- What role did they play?
- What made them politically salient?
- What vulnerability made pressure possible?
- What conduct is alleged?
- What is established, disputed or unknown?

Include relevant context without turning biography into moral qualification.

A target does not need to be flawless to deserve accurate process reporting.

---

## 🧠 Political Salience

Explain whether the target was:

- a critic;
- former official;
- lawyer;
- journalist;
- protest organiser;
- donor;
- university;
- nonprofit;
- contractor;
- non-citizen.

State why they became visible.

Do not assume political salience proves retaliation.

---

## 🪤 Vulnerability

Identify the practical dependency that made the route effective.

Examples include:

- employment;
- visa status;
- clearance;
- contract access;
- clients;
- grant funding;
- reputation;
- licensing;
- family safety.

The vulnerability is often the central mechanism.

---

## 📣 Section Two — The Designation

Answer:

- Who named or accused the target?
- On what date?
- What language was used?
- What target category appeared?
- What evidence was publicly offered?
- Was the accusation repeated?
- Did naming precede formal action?

The designation section should distinguish:

- political rhetoric;
- official allegation;
- legal finding;
- public evidence.

---

## 🗣️ Quote Sparingly And Precisely

Use only the language necessary to establish:

- category;
- allegation;
- demanded outcome;
- signalling function.

Do not repeat:

- defamatory claims;
- slurs;
- harassment;
- private information;

more than necessary.

Paraphrase where the exact wording adds no evidential value.

---

## 🧷 Evidence-Level Marker

At the end of the designation section, state the evidence level.

Examples:

> The remarks publicly signalled a preferred outcome, but no direct instruction has been made public.

> The allegation was formally referred to the department two days later.

> Records released in litigation show documented communication between the offices.

This prevents the reader from carrying an inflated connection into the rest of the story.

---

## 🔀 Section Three — The Route

Answer:

- Which agency, institution or private actor acted?
- What legal or commercial authority was used?
- What vulnerability made the route effective?
- What immediate consequence followed?
- Did the route remain narrow or multiply?

Possible routes include:

- criminal;
- civil;
- immigration;
- financial;
- procurement;
- clearance;
- employment;
- litigation;
- crowd harassment.

Name the route clearly.

---

## ⚖️ State The Authority

Identify:

- statute;
- executive order;
- regulation;
- contract clause;
- personnel standard;
- immigration power;
- civil cause of action;
- institutional policy.

Then explain:

- how the authority normally works;
- how it worked here;
- what is contested.

Formal legality should be reported.

It should not substitute for analysis of selection, severity or effect.

---

## ⚙️ Identify The Carrier

Be specific.

Use:

- named agency;
- named office;
- employer;
- law firm;
- university;
- client;
- funder;
- private litigant;
- influencer network.

Avoid “the government” where a narrower actor is known.

The selector and carrier may be different.

---

## 🧭 Section Four — The Process

Answer:

- Who initiated it?
- What predicating evidence existed?
- What ordinary procedure should apply?
- What was unusual?
- Were political appointees involved?
- Were career officials consulted or overruled?
- Was a special team created?
- Were ordinary deadlines accelerated?

This is where retaliation claims become testable.

---

## 🔍 Person First Or Offence First?

Establish the sequence.

Ask:

- Was the target named before a violation was identified?
- Did the legal theory emerge later?
- Was an old matter reopened?
- Was the allegation formally referred?
- Did the agency receive new evidence?

Use chronology rather than assertion.

---

## 📋 Ordinary Procedure

Explain the baseline.

What should normally happen?

Possible steps include:

- complaint;
- preliminary review;
- predication;
- notice;
- opportunity to respond;
- career-staff review;
- appeal.

Without a baseline, “unusual” is empty.

---

## 🚨 What Was Unusual?

Possible irregularities include:

- senior political involvement;
- bypassed intake;
- special team;
- accelerated deadline;
- novel authority;
- maximal severity;
- pre-adjudication publicity;
- target-first selection;
- shifting legal theory.

Each claim should be sourced.

---

## 🔗 Section Five — Cumulative Pressure

List other actions affecting the target.

Possible categories include:

- parallel investigations;
- employment consequences;
- legal costs;
- client or funding loss;
- security costs;
- threats;
- family impact;
- professional exclusion;
- reputational damage.

Do not blend them into one actor.

Preserve each institution separately.

---

## 🧾 Cumulative-Pressure Format

For each event, state:

- date;
- actor;
- route;
- stated basis;
- connection level;
- practical effect.

Possible connection levels:

- directly coordinated;
- formally referred;
- publicly signalled;
- temporally linked;
- independently aligned;
- connection unknown.

This is the evidential heart of the story.

---

## 💸 Quantify Where Possible

Report:

- legal fees;
- lost salary;
- contracts;
- client loss;
- security costs;
- duration;
- days of work;
- number of proceedings.

Distinguish:

- documented figure;
- estimate;
- range;
- unquantified burden.

Do not invent precision.

---

## 🏠 Family And Security Impact

Include only where:

- relevant;
- consented to;
- safely reportable.

Do not expose:

- addresses;
- routines;
- children;
- security measures;
- private records;

without a compelling reason.

The target’s vulnerability should not be reproduced by the story.

---

## 🕸️ Section Six — The Wider Apparatus

Answer:

- Are there similar targets?
- Has the same method been used before?
- Do the same personnel recur?
- Does the same language appear?
- Is there formal policy?
- Is there a known target category?
- Are private actors responding in similar ways?

This section should move from case to structure.

It should not turn resemblance into proof of coordination.

---

## 🗺️ Pattern, Doctrine And Coordination

State explicitly whether the case shows:

### Recurring Pattern

A similar route or output appeared in other cases.

### Shared Doctrine

The same language, target category or theory recurred.

### Documented Coordination

Records show communication, referral or joint action.

Do not collapse these.

---

## 🧬 Prior Template

Identify earlier cases that supplied:

- legal theory;
- routing method;
- target category;
- private-sector response;
- rhetorical framing.

Explain both:

- similarity;
- material difference.

---

## 🏛️ Formal Policy Connection

Where applicable, link the case to:

- executive order;
- agency memorandum;
- working group;
- advisory team;
- announced doctrine;
- statutory authority.

Do not imply policy connection where the evidence shows only ideological similarity.

---

## 🗣️ Section Seven — Government And Institutional Response

Include:

- stated justification;
- denial of retaliation;
- authority cited;
- evidence supplied;
- process described;
- safeguards claimed;
- unanswered questions.

Represent the response fully.

Then test it.

---

## 🛡️ Stated Justification

State the official rationale accurately.

Possible rationales include:

- national security;
- law enforcement;
- public safety;
- contract integrity;
- immigration compliance;
- personnel suitability;
- civil-rights enforcement.

Do not caricature the rationale.

Examine whether the record supports it.

---

## 🚫 Denial Of Retaliation

Report the denial clearly.

Then assess it against:

- chronology;
- comparator cases;
- public statements;
- process irregularity;
- route choice;
- multiplication;
- output.

A denial is part of the evidence.

It is not the final conclusion.

---

## 📂 Evidence Supplied

State whether the institution supplied:

- documents;
- legal authority;
- aggregate data;
- case facts;
- classified basis;
- no public evidence.

Use precise language.

---

## ❔ Unanswered Questions

List only material unanswered questions.

Examples:

- who initiated the review;
- whether a referral occurred;
- why the route differed;
- what comparators exist;
- whether political appointees intervened;
- what records were shared.

Repeated non-answers may become a separate finding.

---

## ⚖️ Section Eight — Legal And Factual Limits

State:

- what is proven;
- what is strongly indicated;
- what fits a pattern;
- what is only temporally associated;
- what remains unknown;
- what contrary evidence exists.

This section protects the whole story.

---

## 🔴 Proven

Use for:

- signed orders;
- formal referrals;
- admitted communications;
- court findings;
- documented actions.

---

## 🟠 Strongly Indicated

Use where records support a connection but do not establish full command or motive.

State which records.

---

## 🟡 Pattern Evidence

Use where the method, target category or output resembles earlier cases.

Name the comparison.

---

## ⚪ Temporal Only

State clearly:

> The action followed the public demand, but no direct connection has been established.

Chronology is still worth reporting.

---

## ❔ Unknown

Unknowns may include:

- classified evidence;
- confidential employment process;
- unavailable referral record;
- private communication;
- undisclosed motive.

Do not fill unknowns with certainty.

---

## 🧪 Contrary Evidence

Include:

- agency resistance;
- court relief;
- neutral comparator treatment;
- strong predication;
- independent private reasons;
- evidence of genuine misconduct;
- safeguards that worked.

Contrary evidence defines the limits of the apparatus claim.

---

## 🤫 Section Nine — The Output

Ask:

- Did behaviour change?
- Did the target stop speaking?
- Did associates withdraw?
- Did employers or clients change policy?
- Did representation become harder?
- Did others avoid similar activity?
- What deterrent message was sent?

The output may be wider than the formal result.

---

## 🧠 Direct Output

Direct outputs may include:

- firing;
- suspension;
- visa loss;
- clearance loss;
- contract exclusion;
- prosecution;
- settlement;
- injunction.

---

## 🌫️ Behavioural Output

Behavioural outputs may include:

- silence;
- withdrawal;
- reduced protest;
- declined representation;
- donor retreat;
- resignation;
- self-censorship;
- institutional concession.

These require evidence.

Do not assume internal fear without testimony or records.

---

## 🪤 Associate Output

Ask whether:

- lawyers declined;
- clients left;
- funders withdrew;
- family members changed routines;
- colleagues distanced themselves;
- institutions refused association.

This shows the pressure spreading beyond the named target.

---

## 🔚 Suggested Ending

Return to the practical consequence.

Show what remains altered even if:

- the action failed;
- a court intervened;
- the investigation closed;
- the order was withdrawn;
- the target was vindicated.

A strong ending may return to:

- empty office;
- lost client;
- delayed project;
- altered security routine;
- withdrawn public role;
- unresolved record.

The story should end with what the process changed.

## 🧱 Mandatory Elements

For an investigative or long-form story, the following should usually be mandatory:

- concrete consequence;
- target;
- designation;
- route;
- process;
- evidence level;
- official response;
- legal and factual limits;
- output;
- residual harm.

The wider-apparatus section may be shorter where evidence is limited.

---

## 📰 Short News Version

A short report should still include:

1. concrete consequence;
2. responsible actor;
3. authority;
4. key chronology;
5. evidence level;
6. official response;
7. immediate effect;
8. what remains unknown.

Avoid inserting the full apparatus thesis into a story that cannot prove it.

---

## 🔬 Investigation Version

A full investigation should include:

- target chronology;
- opening documents;
- comparator cases;
- personnel;
- referrals;
- route choice;
- cumulative pressure;
- associate effects;
- apparatus map;
- court record;
- residual harm.

The longer format earns the stronger structural claim.

---

## 🧑‍⚖️ Legal Review Box

Where useful, include:

- authority cited;
- legal challenge;
- court;
- procedural stage;
- ruling;
- appeal status;
- unresolved questions;
- practical effect before and after relief.

Do not report an interim order as a final universal conclusion.

## 🚫 Avoid

Avoid:

- personality-only framing;
- scandal chronology without mechanism;
- unsupported claims of central coordination;
- treating formal legality as the end of analysis;
- beginning with abstract authoritarianism;
- using “secret police” as an unsupported standalone label;
- turning investigation into conviction;
- treating the target as morally perfect or disposable;
- repeating harassment unnecessarily;
- publishing private information without necessity;
- erasing contrary evidence;
- treating legal victory as complete restoration.

---

## 🛡️ Harm-Minimising Reporting

Before publication, ask:

- Does this expose the target to new danger?
- Does it reproduce a defamatory claim unnecessarily?
- Does it identify associates who did not consent?
- Does it disclose security detail?
- Does it make private family information searchable?
- Does it turn trauma into spectacle?
- Does the structural value justify the detail?

The reporting should not become another pressure route.

---

## 🧷 Claim Audit

For every major sentence, ask:

- What is the source?
- What evidence level applies?
- Is the actor named correctly?
- Is the process term accurate?
- Does the verb overstate the record?
- Is a comparator meaningful?
- Is contrary evidence included?
- What would require revision?

The claim audit should happen before headline writing.

---

## 🧪 What Would Weaken The Story?

The structural claim should be narrowed if:

- initiation was ordinary;
- predication preceded political naming;
- comparator treatment was consistent;
- private actions had unrelated documented reasons;
- the alleged connection was only temporal;
- no meaningful cumulative effect occurred;
- legal relief fully restored the prior position;
- key chronology was wrong;
- contrary evidence outweighs the inference.

A story should be capable of becoming smaller.

## ⚠️ Evidential Boundaries

This node does not claim that:

- every story requires a full apparatus section;
- every target is innocent;
- every cumulative burden proves coordination;
- every legal action is punishment;
- every behavioural change was intended;
- official responses are necessarily pretextual;
- one template can replace editorial judgment.

It makes a narrower methodological claim:

> **A strong STFU story begins with concrete harm, identifies the target and designation, proves the route and process, reconstructs cumulative pressure, connects the case to the wider apparatus only at the evidence level supported, represents official responses fairly, states legal and factual limits, and ends with the practical output that remained.**
