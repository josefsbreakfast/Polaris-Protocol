# 📰 The STFU Reporting Test  
**First created:** 2026-07-14 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
*A rapid test for deciding whether an incident belongs inside the apparatus or remains an isolated controversy.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The **STFU Reporting Test** is a rapid screening tool.

Its purpose is to help reporters decide whether an incident should be examined as part of the wider **Seditious Traitors Fuckery Unit** apparatus rather than treated as a self-contained political outrage.

The core question is:

> **Does this incident convert political hostility into practical pressure through state, commercial or supporter-enabled mechanisms?**

The test does not prove motive.

It identifies whether the incident deserves:

- deeper routing analysis;
- comparison with earlier cases;
- inclusion in the target and method register;
- reporting as part of a cumulative apparatus.

---

## 🧭 The Five-Part Core Test

Ask five questions.

### 1. Public Designation

Was a person, institution or network publicly named, accused or classified?

### 2. Vulnerability Identification

Was a practical vulnerability made legible?

Examples include:

- immigration status;
- security clearance;
- government contracts;
- employment;
- funding;
- licensing;
- legal exposure;
- clients;
- family safety.

### 3. Vulnerability Activation

Did an official, agency or aligned private actor activate that vulnerability?

### 4. Associative Spread

Did pressure spread to:

- lawyers;
- employers;
- clients;
- funders;
- relatives;
- colleagues;
- institutions?

### 5. Wider Deterrence

Did the incident change the behaviour of people beyond the immediate target?

If the answer to several of these is yes, the incident may belong inside the apparatus.

---

## 📣 Test One — Public Designation

Ask:

- Who named the target?
- What language was used?
- Was the target described as an enemy, traitor, extremist, corrupt actor or hostile network?
- Was the accusation repeated?
- Was the target named before formal action?
- Was evidence publicly supplied?
- Did the designation appear in official or movement media?

Public designation can perform several functions:

- mark the target;
- identify the preferred allegation;
- mobilise supporters;
- signal institutions;
- prepare observers for action.

Public designation alone is not enough.

It becomes more significant when practical consequences follow.

---

## 🎯 Test Two — Vulnerability Identification

Ask:

- What made the target punishable in practice?
- Which dependency was exposed?
- Was the vulnerability named explicitly?
- Had the same vulnerability been used in earlier cases?

Common vulnerabilities include:

- visa or immigration status;
- security clearance;
- government employment;
- public contracts;
- grants;
- licences;
- professional regulation;
- client relationships;
- donor access;
- reputation;
- platform access;
- family safety.

The mechanism matters more than the insult.

---

## ⚙️ Test Three — Vulnerability Activation

Ask:

- Who acted?
- What authority did they use?
- Was the action state, commercial or supporter-enabled?
- What practical consequence followed?
- Was the action ordinary or unusual?

Possible actors include:

- White House officials;
- federal agencies;
- state institutions;
- contractors;
- employers;
- law firms;
- universities;
- clients;
- funders;
- media owners;
- influencers;
- supporter networks.

The incident becomes apparatus-relevant when hostility is translated into an actionable route.

---

## 🪤 Test Four — Associative Punishment

Ask whether pressure spread beyond the named target.

Possible affected associates include:

- lawyers;
- employers;
- clients;
- funders;
- relatives;
- colleagues;
- universities;
- contractors;
- professional bodies.

Associative punishment matters because it:

- isolates the target;
- raises the cost of support;
- weakens representation;
- expands deterrence.

A case becomes more structurally significant when others are punished for proximity.

---

## 🤫 Test Five — Wider Deterrence

Ask whether anyone stopped:

- speaking;
- representing;
- funding;
- employing;
- associating;
- protesting;
- publishing;
- remaining in public service.

The output may be:

- silence;
- withdrawal;
- resignation;
- settlement;
- client loss;
- cancelled events;
- reduced participation.

The apparatus is most efficient when one visible case changes many invisible decisions.

---

## 🧾 Evidence-Level Questions

The reporting test must distinguish different kinds of connection.

Ask:

- Is there a direct order?
- Is there a formal referral?
- Is there repeated official signalling?
- Is there documented anticipatory compliance?
- Is there only temporal proximity?
- Is there no current evidence of connection?

Do not collapse these levels.

---

## 🔴 Direct Order

Evidence may include:

- written instruction;
- recorded command;
- executive order;
- signed memorandum;
- documented oral direction;
- court finding.

Use language such as:

- ordered;
- directed;
- instructed.

This is the strongest coordination evidence.

---

## 🟠 Formal Referral

Evidence may include:

- referral letter;
- case transfer;
- agency notification;
- official request for review;
- documented interagency routing.

Use language such as:

- formally referred;
- transmitted to;
- requested review by.

A referral establishes routing.

It does not establish that the outcome was predetermined.

---

## 🟡 Repeated Official Signalling

Evidence may include:

- repeated public demands;
- target naming;
- statements making the preferred outcome legible;
- movement figures urging action;
- public threats.

Use language such as:

- officials repeatedly called for;
- the remarks made the preferred outcome legible;
- action followed repeated public demands.

Signalling is weaker than instruction.

It may still shape anticipatory obedience.

---

## 🔵 Documented Anticipatory Compliance

Evidence may include:

- internal messages;
- policy changes;
- client withdrawals;
- declined representation;
- pre-emptive suspensions;
- concessions before formal action.

Use language such as:

- the institution changed course after;
- records show concern about government exposure;
- the organisation acted before receiving a formal order.

This shows transmission through perceived risk.

---

## ⚪ Temporal Proximity Only

Evidence may show only that one event followed another.

Use language such as:

- followed;
- came after;
- coincided with;
- no direct connection has been established.

Temporal proximity is relevant chronology.

It is not proof of coordination.

---

## 🔀 The Routing Test

Identify the route through which pressure travelled.

Possible routes include:

- criminal;
- civil;
- immigration;
- financial;
- procurement;
- clearance;
- employment;
- litigation;
- crowd harassment.

A case may involve more than one route.

---

## 🚔 Criminal Route

Ask:

- Was an investigation opened?
- Was an old matter reopened?
- Was a novel offence theory used?
- Was enforcement unusually severe?
- Were comparable actors treated differently?

---

## ⚖️ Civil Route

Ask:

- Was civil enforcement used?
- Were penalties or injunctions sought?
- Was regulatory authority stretched?
- Were settlement demands disproportionate?

---

## 🛂 Immigration Route

Ask:

- Was visa, entry or status used?
- Did political speech feature in the decision?
- Were standards vague?
- Was the target a non-citizen with heightened vulnerability?

---

## 💰 Financial Route

Ask:

- Were accounts, donors or transactions scrutinised?
- Were assets restricted?
- Did financial institutions withdraw?
- Was access to payment or insurance affected?

---

## 🧾 Procurement Route

Ask:

- Were contracts reviewed, cancelled or threatened?
- Were suppliers or clients pressured?
- Did representation create government-business risk?

---

## 🪪 Clearance Route

Ask:

- Was access to classified or sensitive work affected?
- Was clearance review linked to public criticism?
- Were named enemies treated differently?

---

## 🏢 Employment Route

Ask:

- Was the target suspended, fired or sidelined?
- Did the employer cite government, client or security risk?
- Did action precede any adjudication?

---

## 🧷 Litigation Route

Ask:

- Was private litigation used to impose cost?
- Did the legal claim follow public designation?
- Was the objective correction, damages, silence or exhaustion?

---

## 📲 Crowd-Harassment Route

Ask:

- Did supporters or influencers amplify the target?
- Were complaints coordinated?
- Did threats or doxxing follow?
- Did institutions act because of supporter pressure?

---

## ⚖️ The Selectivity Test

The same law can be legitimate in one case and politically abusive in another.

Ask:

- Was equivalent conduct treated differently?
- Was enforcement unusually severe?
- Did political identity appear before the alleged offence?
- Was the target named publicly before formal action?
- Were political allies treated more leniently?
- Was ordinary process accelerated or bypassed?
- Was the legal theory novel or shifting?

Selective use is often where political purpose becomes visible.

---

## 🔍 Person First, Offence Second

A major warning sign is:

> **The person is identified before the offence.**

Ask:

- Was the target already on an enemy list?
- Did officials demand investigation before naming a specific offence?
- Was a legal theory found after the target was selected?
- Was an old matter reopened following political pressure?

This does not prove the target committed no misconduct.

It raises questions about initiation and purpose.

---

## 📣 Public Naming Before Formal Action

Ask:

- Was the target accused before investigators acted?
- Did officials publicly predict the outcome?
- Was punishment celebrated before adjudication?
- Did agencies act after repeated public demands?

Public naming can signal the preferred institutional response.

---

## 🪤 The Associative-Punishment Test

Ask whether pressure reached:

- lawyers;
- employers;
- clients;
- funders;
- family members;
- professional partners;
- institutions hosting or supporting the target.

Record:

- who was contacted;
- what risk was invoked;
- what action followed;
- whether the associate withdrew.

Associative spread is a strong apparatus indicator.

---

## 🤫 The Output Test

Ask what changed after the pressure.

Did someone stop:

- speaking;
- representing;
- funding;
- employing;
- associating;
- publishing;
- protesting;
- serving in office?

The output test moves reporting beyond formal action.

It records behavioural effect.

---

## 🕸️ The System Test

Ask whether the case connects to a known:

- person;
- agency;
- doctrine;
- target category;
- prior method;
- legal route;
- supporter network.

Possible connections include:

- same official;
- same working group;
- same allegation;
- same executive authority;
- same target class;
- same private-sector response;
- same deterrent output.

A repeated method may matter even without proof of one central command.

---

## 🗺️ Pattern, Doctrine And Coordination

Keep three categories separate.

### Recurring Pattern

Similar actions recur.

### Shared Doctrine

Actors use the same language, categories or theory.

### Documented Coordination

Records show referral, instruction, communication or joint action.

Pattern is not coordination.

Doctrine is not command.

The map should display each accurately.

---

## 📊 Suggested Scoring

A simple intake score may help prioritise cases.

### One Indicator — Monitor

The case may be politically salient but lacks enough structure for inclusion.

### Two Or Three Indicators — Possible Routing Event

The incident merits chronology, comparator research and mechanism review.

### Four Or More Indicators — Strong STFU Relevance

The case likely belongs in the apparatus map and target register.

### Direct Evidence Of Coordination — Apparatus Case

Documented orders, referrals or joint action justify a stronger classification.

---

## 🧮 What Counts As An Indicator?

Possible indicators include:

1. public designation;
2. named vulnerability;
3. official or private activation;
4. selective enforcement;
5. associative punishment;
6. multiple routing mechanisms;
7. repeated official signalling;
8. anticipatory compliance;
9. wider deterrent output;
10. connection to prior methods or target categories.

The score is a reporting aid.

It is not a mathematical proof of motive.

Cases may be tagged as:

- `MONITOR`;

- `STRONG_STFU_RELEVANCE`;
- `APPARATUS_CASE`.

Add a connection tag:

- `DIRECT_ORDER`;

- `PUBLIC_SIGNALLING`;
- `ANTICIPATORY_COMPLIANCE`;
- `INDEPENDENT_ALIGNMENT`;
- `TEMPORAL_ONLY`;
- `CONNECTION_UNKNOWN`.

This prevents evidence levels from disappearing inside the narrative.

---

## 🧪 Failed Or Abandoned Actions

Failed actions should still be recorded.

Capture:

- date opened;
- intended route;
- interim consequence;
- court response;
- abandonment or withdrawal;
- remaining harm;
- copied behaviour elsewhere.

A failed action may still produce:

- legal cost;
- deterrence;
- employer fear;
- client loss;
- reputational damage.

Legal defeat does not necessarily reset the target.

---

## 🧾 Minimum Reporting Record

For every screened incident, capture:

- target;
- public selector;
- allegation;
- vulnerability;
- routing mechanism;
- responsible actor;
- evidence level;
- associate impact;
- observed output;
- comparator cases;
- legal status;
- current classification.

This record should feed the target and method register.

---

## 📚 Sources To Prioritise

The strongest sources include:

- target chronology;
- formal documents;
- court records;
- official statements;
- referral records;
- comparative treatment;
- interviews with affected institutions;
- employment and contract notices;
- threat records;
- internal communications.

Do not rely on rhetoric alone where process records exist.

---

## ⚠️ What The Test Cannot Prove

The checklist cannot by itself prove:

- retaliatory motive;
- central coordination;
- legal invalidity;
- innocence of the target;
- shared command;
- secret-police activity.

It identifies cases that warrant deeper investigation.

---

## 🚫 Do Not Force Every Bad Act Into The Framework

Not every:

- insult;
- investigation;
- firing;
- lawsuit;
- visa action;
- protest dispute;
- regulatory decision;

belongs inside STFU.

The framework is weakened when it becomes indiscriminate.

A case should remain outside or provisional where:

- no practical vulnerability was activated;
- no meaningful selectivity appears;
- no associative spread occurred;
- no wider deterrent effect is visible;
- ordinary process adequately explains the action.

---

## 🧭 Rapid Desk Version

Under deadline, ask:

1. Who was publicly designated?
2. What vulnerability made them punishable?
3. Who activated it?
4. What route was used?
5. Was the action selective or unusually severe?
6. Did pressure reach associates?
7. What behaviour changed afterward?
8. What evidence connects the steps?
9. Does the method repeat an earlier case?
10. What remains unknown?

If the answers reveal:

> **designation → vulnerability → activation → associative spread → deterrence**

the incident has strong STFU relevance.

## ⚠️ Evidential Boundaries

This node does not claim that:

- a high score proves motive;
- temporal proximity proves coordination;
- every political target is innocent;
- every severe action is selective;
- every private decision was state-directed;
- every failed case was intended as punishment;
- every controversial event belongs inside STFU.

It makes a narrower methodological claim:

> **An incident becomes apparatus-relevant when political designation is translated through a practical vulnerability into state, commercial or supporter-enabled pressure, especially where the pressure spreads to associates and changes wider behaviour.**
