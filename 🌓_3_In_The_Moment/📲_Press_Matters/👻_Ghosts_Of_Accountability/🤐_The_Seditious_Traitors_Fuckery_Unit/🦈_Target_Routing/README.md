# 🦈 Target Routing  
**First created:** 2026-07-12 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-12  
*How a political enemy becomes an actionable target, moves through several pressure systems, and teaches the wider audience to stay quiet.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder maps the conversion pipeline from political hostility to behavioural silence.

The apparatus does not begin with one standard offence or one standard punishment.

It begins by identifying a person, institution or network that is politically useful to punish.

It then asks:

- How visible is the target?
- Which grievance makes the target legible?
- What vulnerability makes the target reachable?
- Which institution can impose cost?
- Which associates can be pressured?
- Which supporters can amplify the attack?
- What behavioural change will teach the wider audience?

The core sequence is:

> **Select the target → name the target → match the vulnerability → activate several pressure surfaces → make association costly → let the audience discipline itself.**

---

## 🧭 What This Folder Does

The seven nodes answer seven linked questions.

1. **Why does this person or institution become useful to punish?**
2. **How can public denunciation function as tasking without an explicit order?**
3. **How is the target matched to the strongest available coercive surface?**
4. **What happens when the person is selected before the offence theory exists?**
5. **How does pressure spread to lawyers, employers, funders and associates?**
6. **How do aligned media, influencers and supporters add informal enforcement?**
7. **What is the real output when conviction or formal punishment is unnecessary?**

Together, they show how fragmented actions can create one cumulative field of pressure.

## 🎯 How A Target Is Selected

[`🎯_how_a_target_is_selected.md`](./🎯_how_a_target_is_selected.md)

This node defines target selection as the point where a person, institution or network becomes politically useful to punish.

Targets may be selected because they:

- investigated Trump or his allies;
- obstructed a regime priority;
- contradicted a senior official;
- represented a designated enemy;
- funded or employed opposition;
- symbolised an institution marked for discipline;
- possessed an exploitable vulnerability.

It distinguishes:

- personal enemy selection;
- ideological enemy selection;
- institutional enemy selection;
- opportunistic selection.

Its central formulation is:

> **The strongest target is often visible enough to send a message, but isolated enough to be safely attacked.**

Hostility supplies motive.

Leverage supplies reach.

[`📣_public_naming_as_informal_tasking.md`](./📣_public_naming_as_informal_tasking.md)

Public naming may supply:

Different audiences hear different instructions.

Ambiguity is useful because it creates plausible deniability while broadening the response.

## 🔀 How The Target Is Routed

[`🔀_how_the_target_is_routed.md`](./🔀_how_the_target_is_routed.md)

This node defines routing as the movement from political designation to the institution, authority or network capable of imposing the greatest available cost.

Possible routes include:

- criminal investigation;
- civil enforcement;
- immigration;
- security-clearance review;
- employment action;
- tax and nonprofit scrutiny;
- procurement;
- licensing;
- grants;
- private litigation;
- public harassment.

The route is matched to the target’s vulnerability.

A student may be reachable through immigration.

A university through funding.

A former official through clearance or records.

A law firm through contracts, access and clients.

The node’s crucial warning is:

> **Cumulative coherence is not the same thing as proven coordination.**

## 🔍 Person First, Offence Second

[`🔍_person_first_offence_second.md`](./🔍_person_first_offence_second.md)

This node examines the investigative inversion in which a politically selected person becomes the starting point and officials search for an actionable offence, violation or process failure.

The central contrast is:

- offence first, suspect second;
- person first, offence second.

The working phrase is:

> **Find the person, then find the paperwork.**

Modern legal systems contain extensive records, complex rules and broad discretion.

The inquiry may uncover genuine misconduct.

The evidential question remains:

- Why was this person selected?
- Why this degree of scrutiny?
- What were the original predicating facts?
- Were comparable cases treated similarly?

The investigation itself may also create new process-offence risks.

## 🪤 Associative Punishment

[`🪤_associative_punishment.md`](./🪤_associative_punishment.md)

This node defines associative punishment as pressure imposed on people or institutions because they assist, employ, fund, represent or remain connected to a target.

Possible secondary targets include:

- lawyers;
- employers;
- colleagues;
- donors;
- clients;
- contractors;
- relatives;
- journalists;
- universities;
- NGOs.

Its central claim is:

> **Isolating the target is often more effective than punishing the target alone.**

The message expands from:

- do not oppose;

to:

- do not help the people who oppose.

The target loses the infrastructure required to resist.

The audience learns that support itself may become punishable.

## 🦈 The Target-Routing Model

The seven nodes assemble into one operational chain.

### 1. Political selection

A person or institution becomes useful to punish.

### 2. Public designation

The target, accusation and expected political position become legible.

### 3. Vulnerability matching

Legal status, employment, funding, contracts, clearance or reputation determine reach.

### 4. Route activation

An agency, institution, employer, court or crowd imposes cost.

### 5. Offence construction

Where necessary, records and rules are searched for an actionable theory.

### 6. Associative widening

Lawyers, funders, employers, relatives and institutions acquire secondary risk.

### 7. Informal amplification

Media, influencers and supporters add complaints, threats and security burden.

### 8. Behavioural output

The target, associate or observer withdraws, avoids, softens or stays silent.

## 🔁 The Feedback Loop

Target routing is not always linear.

It can loop.

1. Public naming creates attention.
2. Supporter research produces allegations.
3. Allegations create complaints.
4. Complaints trigger institutional review.
5. Review produces new records.
6. Records create new offence theories.
7. Formal action legitimises earlier rhetoric.
8. Associates withdraw.
9. The target becomes more isolated.
10. The wider audience adjusts before being targeted.

The pressure field can therefore generate its own future inputs.

## 🧱 Fragmented Authority, Cumulative Pressure

Each participant may describe only one action.

An agency used its statutory authority.

An employer made a personnel decision.

A firm reassessed commercial risk.

A university reviewed compliance.

A supporter exercised speech.

A platform enforced policy.

The target experiences:

- investigation;
- lost work;
- legal expense;
- status risk;
- harassment;
- isolation;
- exhaustion.

This folder exists to preserve both truths:

- the actions may be formally separate;
- their effects may be politically cumulative.

## ⚖️ Evidential Discipline

The folder does not establish that:

- every critic is a political target;
- every public attack is an operational order;
- every concurrent action shares one origin;
- every investigation is pretextual;
- every associate is coerced;
- every supporter act is coordinated;
- every behavioural change is caused by fear.

The analysis must distinguish:

- selection from ordinary prominence;
- signalling from direct instruction;
- routing from routine administration;
- comparative over-enforcement from legitimate scrutiny;
- association pressure from ordinary withdrawal;
- foreseeable crowd effects from coordinated action;
- persuasion from exhaustion, commercial pressure or fear.

The confidence level must match the evidence.
