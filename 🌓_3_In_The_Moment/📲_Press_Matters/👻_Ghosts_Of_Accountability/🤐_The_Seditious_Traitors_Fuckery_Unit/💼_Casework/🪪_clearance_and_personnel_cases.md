# 🪪 Clearance And Personnel Cases  
**First created:** 2026-07-14 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
*A specialist case file for security-clearance action, dismissal, reassignment and loyalty-linked personnel control, with strict separation between symbolic signalling and real employment consequence.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node is the specialist case file for:

- security-clearance action;
- dismissal;
- reassignment;
- facility-access restriction;
- contractor exclusion;
- loyalty-linked personnel control.

Its purpose is to compare cases involving:

- former officials;
- current civil servants;
- intelligence personnel;
- military officers;
- inspectors general;
- prosecutors;
- contractors;
- advisers.

The central question is:

> **How did political conflict, prior official conduct or public designation become clearance, access or employment pressure, through which authority and with what procedural safeguards?**

The core named examples include:

- Chris Krebs;
- Miles Taylor;
- John Bolton;
- former intelligence officials named in clearance memoranda;
- agents and prosecutors associated with Trump investigations;
- senior military officers removed or reassigned.

This node should remain a living case file rather than a fixed list.

---

## 🧭 The Core Analytical Sequence

For each case, record:

1. role and status;
2. political conflict or prior official act;
3. public designation;
4. initiating authority;
5. stated security or personnel basis;
6. clearance, access or employment action;
7. procedural protections;
8. effect on associates;
9. practical employment consequence;
10. resistance, reversal and residual harm.

The core chain is:

> **designation or conflict → personnel vulnerability → clearance or employment action → professional transmission → deterrent effect → legal or institutional resistance**

## ⚖️ Separate The Action Types

Every entry must distinguish:

### Symbolic Rescission Of Unused Access

Public cancellation of access no longer operationally required.

### Active Clearance Suspension

Temporary removal of active eligibility pending review.

### Formal Revocation

Completed administrative removal of eligibility.

### Employment Dismissal

Termination from a role.

### Reassignment

Movement to another role, duty or location.

### Facility-Access Restriction

Restriction on entering sites or systems.

### Contractor Exclusion

Loss of eligibility to perform or obtain government work.

These actions may overlap.

They should not be collapsed.

---

## 🟦 Symbolic Rescission

Record:

- whether the person still held active access;
- whether access was needed;
- whether employment changed;
- whether the announcement was primarily political;
- practical consequence.

A symbolic action can still produce reputational or deterrent effect.

It should not be represented as operationally equivalent to active revocation.

## 🪞 Comparator Questions

For each case, ask:

- Were similarly situated officials treated the same?
- Was the person actively using the clearance?
- Was ordinary procedure followed?
- Did allies accused of similar conduct retain access?
- Was the action proportionate to the stated risk?
- Did employment consequences exceed the security rationale?
- Were associates targeted without separate conduct?
- Did public rhetoric precede the formal process?

A meaningful comparator should involve:

- similar role;
- similar status;
- similar alleged conduct;
- similar authority;
- similar employment consequence.

---

## 🧪 Symbolic Versus Material Comparator

Do not compare:

- revocation of unused former-official access;

with:

- suspension of an active employee’s required clearance;

as if the consequences were equivalent.

The political message may be similar.

The operational harm is not.

---

## 🛡️ Institutional Resistance

Record where:

- career security officials resisted;
- agencies narrowed directives;
- inspectors general intervened;
- courts granted relief;
- Congress demanded records;
- military leaders objected;
- contractors retained affected staff;
- colleagues publicly supported targets.

Resistance is a core case field.

---

## 🧾 Contrary Evidence

Every case should record evidence showing:

- ordinary security concern;
- documented misconduct;
- proper procedure;
- active appeal;
- no real employment effect;
- legitimate reorganisation;
- consistent comparator treatment;
- full restoration;
- independent contractor decisions.

Do not hide disconfirming evidence.

## 🛡️ Important Cautions

Do not:

- treat symbolic rescission as active clearance loss;
- treat clearance action as dismissal unless employment changed;
- treat reassignment as removal without evidence;
- assume contractor consequences from rhetoric alone;
- collapse clearance, access and personnel routes;
- ignore broad presidential and agency authority;
- treat political rhetoric as a security finding;
- imply every named person faced operational harm;
- publish classified or personnel detail unnecessarily;
- treat restoration as complete remedy.

---

## 🧪 What Would Weaken The Loyalty-Control Analysis?

The analysis should be narrowed where evidence shows:

- active and credible security concerns;
- ordinary adjudication;
- proper notice and appeal;
- no political involvement;
- action predating public designation;
- consistent comparator treatment;
- no real employment consequence;
- legitimate reorganisation;
- full restoration;
- no effect on associates or wider service behaviour.

A credible case file must actively seek these facts.

- What remains after clearance restoration?
