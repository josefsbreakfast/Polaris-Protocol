📋 Target And Method Register

* Create the master case register for the entire STFU apparatus.
* Purpose:
    * prevent incidents from being lost as isolated scandals;
    * compare target classes;
    * compare methods;
    * identify repeated personnel, language and institutions;
    * track legal and practical outcomes.
* Each entry should record:
    * target name;
    * target type;
    * political relevance;
    * selector or first public designator;
    * allegation or enemy category;
    * responsible institution;
    * pressure route;
    * associated targets;
    * evidence level;
    * current status;
    * practical consequence.
* Suggested target-type tags:
    * politician;
    * prosecutor;
    * investigator;
    * civil servant;
    * military officer;
    * lawyer;
    * law firm;
    * university;
    * student;
    * journalist;
    * media organisation;
    * nonprofit;
    * donor;
    * contractor.
* Suggested method tags:
    * investigation;
    * prosecution;
    * clearance action;
    * personnel action;
    * visa action;
    * detention;
    * grant pressure;
    * contract review;
    * tax or financial scrutiny;
    * private litigation;
    * public naming;
    * supporter harassment;
    * associative punishment.
* Evidence-level fields:
    * direct written order;
    * formal referral;
    * acknowledged senior involvement;
    * documentary inference;
    * pattern evidence;
    * temporal association;
    * unverified allegation.
* Add initiation fields:
    * date of first public designation;
    * date formal action began;
    * known initiator;
    * predicating evidence if public;
    * whether an offence or a person came first.
* Add cumulative-pressure fields:
    * number of institutions involved;
    * employment effect;
    * financial effect;
    * legal cost;
    * security effect;
    * effect on associates;
    * observed chilling effect.
* Add legal-status fields:
    * active;
    * closed;
    * dismissed;
    * enjoined;
    * settled;
    * convicted;
    * pardoned;
    * unresolved.
* Add apparatus-mode fields:
    * crown protection;
    * movement protection;
    * regime protection;
    * mixed.
* Add coordination fields:
    * direct coordination documented;
    * formal interagency contact;
    * shared rhetoric;
    * independent alignment;
    * unknown.
* Include comparator cases:
    * similar conduct by allies;
    * ordinary treatment of comparable cases;
    * explanation for different treatment.
* Important caution:
    * one person can have several distinct case entries;
    * do not collapse separate legal matters into one allegation.
* Important caution:
    * record exculpatory facts, institutional resistance and contrary evidence.
* Important sources:
    * official documents;
    * court filings;
    * congressional records;
    * agency statements;
    * target interviews;
    * reputable investigative reporting;
    * procurement and grant records.
* Link to:
    * 📰_the_stfu_reporting_test.md;

    * 📣_public_designation_to_consequence_timelines.md;

    * all specialist casework nodes.
* Open questions:
    * should each target have one row or one row per action?
    * which fields can be reliably standardised?
    * what confidence threshold is required for public inclusion?
    * how should sealed, classified or disputed material be marked?

⚖️ Law-Firm Orders

* Build a case file for executive action, threatened action and negotiated settlements involving law firms.
* Core case set:
    * Perkins Coie;
    * Covington & Burling;
    * Paul Weiss;
    * Jenner & Block;
    * WilmerHale;
    * Susman Godfrey;
    * firms entering agreements without a formal published order.
* For each firm, record:
    * stated government grievance;
    * relevant client, lawyer or prior representation;
    * date of public designation;
    * text of executive action;
    * agencies instructed to act;
    * clearance consequences;
    * access consequences;
    * contract consequences;
    * effect on clients and associates.
* Track litigation:
    * complaint filed;
    * constitutional claims;
    * interim relief;
    * final judgment;
    * appeal;
    * rescission or settlement.
* Track practical effects before legal resolution:
    * client loss;
    * staff departure;
    * representation withdrawn;
    * diversity policy changes;
    * pro bono changes;
    * security-clearance impact.
* Track negotiated agreements:
    * amount or quantity of pledged legal work;
    * stated eligible causes;
    * diversity or hiring commitments;
    * whether written terms are public;
    * whether the government describes the agreement as payment or tribute.
* Examine repeated order language:
    * national-security risk;
    * access to government;
    * discriminatory employment practices;
    * weaponisation;
    * representation of political opponents.
* Compare order structure:
    * identical sections;
    * changed justifications;
    * escalation across firms;
    * references to clients or employees.
* Distinguish:
    * published order;
    * threatened order;
    * private negotiation;
    * anticipatory settlement.
* Record resistance:
    * firm litigation;
    * judicial findings;
    * bar-association response;
    * client support;
    * public refusal.
* Important caution:
    * later invalidation does not erase earlier commercial effects.
* Important caution:
    * do not assume every internal firm change resulted from government pressure.
* Important sources:
    * White House orders and fact sheets;
    * court complaints and judgments;
    * firm statements;
    * congressional correspondence;
    * client and employee reporting;
    * procurement records.

📣 Public Designation To Consequence Timelines

* Create compact timelines connecting public naming, institutional action and downstream consequence.
* Purpose:
    * establish sequence;
    * identify possible signalling;
    * compare response times;
    * separate documented connection from mere temporal proximity.
* Each timeline should include:
    * target;
    * first relevant public designation;
    * repeated or escalated designation;
    * formal referral if known;
    * first institutional action;
    * secondary institutional action;
    * private-sector response;
    * supporter response;
    * legal outcome;
    * continuing practical effect.
* Suggested event categories:
    * public statement;
    * social-media post;
    * executive order;
    * agency memorandum;
    * investigation;
    * subpoena;
    * detention;
    * contract action;
    * clearance action;
    * employer decision;
    * threat campaign;
    * court ruling.
* Add source and confidence fields to each event:
    * primary document;
    * court record;
    * official statement;
    * reputable report;
    * target account;
    * disputed claim.
* Add connection labels:
    * direct order;
    * formal referral;
    * acknowledged influence;
    * repeated shared language;
    * plausible signalling;
    * temporal sequence only.
* Build target-journey timelines for:
    * named law firms;
    * universities;
    * visa cases;
    * former officials;
    * prosecutors;
    * media organisations.
* Compare short and long latency:
    * action within hours or days;
    * action after repeated public campaigning;
    * action after personnel change;
    * action after a triggering event.
* Track escalation:
    * criticism;
    * accusation;
    * demand for investigation;

* Track de-escalation and resistance:

* Important caution:
