# 📣 Public Designation To Consequence Timelines  
**First created:** 2026-07-14 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-14  
*Compact target journeys for connecting public naming, institutional action and downstream consequence without confusing chronology with causation.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node contains compact timelines linking:

- public designation;
- repeated or escalated accusation;
- formal referral;
- institutional action;
- private-sector response;
- supporter response;
- legal resistance;
- continuing practical effect.

Its purpose is to:

- establish sequence;
- identify possible signalling;
- compare response times;
- identify escalation;
- preserve resistance and de-escalation;
- separate documented connection from temporal proximity.

The central rule is:

> **Chronology is necessary for causal analysis, but chronology alone does not prove causation.**

Each event therefore requires:

- date;
- actor;
- event type;
- source;
- confidence;
- connection label;
- practical effect.

---

## 🧭 What The Timeline Must Show

A complete target journey should answer:

- Who first named the target?
- What language was used?
- Was the designation repeated or escalated?
- Was there a formal referral?
- What institution acted first?
- What followed second?
- Did private actors respond?
- Did supporters mobilise?
- What legal resistance occurred?
- What consequence remains?

The timeline should make sequence legible without drawing unsupported arrows.

---

## 🧱 Unit Of Analysis

The unit of analysis is one dated event.

Examples include:

- one public statement;
- one social-media post;
- one executive order;
- one agency memorandum;
- one referral;
- one investigation;
- one subpoena;
- one detention;
- one contract action;
- one clearance action;
- one employer decision;
- one threat campaign;
- one court ruling;
- one settlement;
- one continuing practical effect.

Several events may occur on the same day.

Record them separately where the actor or mechanism differs.

## 🆔 Event ID

Suggested format:

```text
TL-[TARGETCODE]-[YYYYMMDD]-[SEQUENCE]
```

Example:

```text
TL-LAWFIRM-20260314-02
```

The ID should remain stable even if:

- the interpretation changes;
- a source is corrected;
- a later event weakens the connection.

## 🎯 Target Journey

Each target journey should include:

1. first relevant public designation;
2. repeated or escalated designation;
3. formal referral if known;
4. first institutional action;
5. secondary institutional action;
6. private-sector response;
7. supporter response;
8. legal outcome;
9. continuing practical effect.

Not every case will contain every stage.

Missing stages should be marked rather than invented.

---

## 📣 First Relevant Public Designation

Record:

- speaker;
- date;
- wording;
- medium;
- target category;
- allegation;
- demanded outcome;
- source.

The first relevant designation is not necessarily:

- the earliest criticism;
- the first media mention;
- the first allegation by anyone.

It is the first designation plausibly relevant to later institutional action.

---

## 🔊 Repeated Or Escalated Designation

Record whether rhetoric escalated from:

- criticism;
- accusation;
- demand for investigation;
- demand for punishment;
- naming associates;
- claiming national-security risk;
- demanding institutional exclusion.

Escalation should be shown through exact dates and sources.

---

## 📬 Formal Referral

Where known, record:

- sender;
- recipient;
- date;
- allegation;
- requested action;
- source;
- agency response.

A referral is a stronger connection than shared language or temporal sequence.

It still does not establish the final motive or outcome.

---

## 🏛️ First Institutional Action

Record the earliest formal action by:

- executive office;
- agency;
- court;
- employer;
- university;
- contractor;
- private litigant.

Examples include:

- opening review;
- suspending access;
- freezing funds;
- issuing notice;
- filing complaint;
- detaining target;
- cancelling contract.

Use the actual action date where known, not only the announcement date.

---

## 🔁 Secondary Institutional Action

Record later actions by distinct institutions.

For each action, preserve:

- actor;
- authority;
- route;
- connection to earlier events;
- practical effect.

Do not merge several institutions into one “government response.”

---

## 🏢 Private-Sector Response

Record decisions by:

- employers;
- clients;
- law firms;
- contractors;
- funders;
- universities;
- insurers;
- professional bodies.

Classify the response as:

- directly requested;
- formally notified;
- government-risk responsive;
- independently aligned;
- connection unknown.

---

## 📢 Supporter Response

Possible events include:

- harassment;
- doxxing;
- threats;
- coordinated complaints;
- boycott calls;
- reputational campaigns;
- protest at workplace or home.

Record only where:

- sufficiently sourced;
- materially connected;
- safely reportable.

Do not reproduce harmful private information.

---

## ⚖️ Legal Outcome

Record:

- injunction;
- stay;
- dismissal;
- settlement;
- conviction;
- acquittal;
- rescission;
- appeal;
- no-charge decision;
- release;
- restoration.

State whether the outcome is:

- interim;
- final;
- appealed;
- partial.

---

## 🌫️ Continuing Practical Effect

Record effects continuing after formal action ends.

Examples include:

- legal debt;
- lost employment;
- lost clients;
- research interruption;
- security changes;
- public stigma;
- family disruption;
- reduced speech;
- copied institutional policy.

This is the final event category in many timelines.

---

## 🎚️ Confidence Fields

Suggested confidence labels:

- `CONFIRMED`;
- `HIGH_CONFIDENCE`;
- `MODERATE_CONFIDENCE`;
- `LOW_CONFIDENCE`;
- `DISPUTED`;
- `UNKNOWN`.

Confidence should reflect:

- source quality;
- source agreement;
- documentary support;
- public accessibility;
- correction history.

Do not convert confidence into causation.

---

## 🔗 Connection Labels

Every event-to-event relationship should use one of:

- `DIRECT_ORDER`;
- `FORMAL_REFERRAL`;
- `ACKNOWLEDGED_INFLUENCE`;
- `REPEATED_SHARED_LANGUAGE`;
- `PLAUSIBLE_SIGNALLING`;
- `ANTICIPATORY_COMPLIANCE`;
- `INDEPENDENT_ALIGNMENT`;
- `TEMPORAL_SEQUENCE_ONLY`;
- `CONNECTION_UNKNOWN`.

These labels should appear in:

- tables;
- diagrams;
- prose;
- data exports.

---

## 🔴 Direct Order

Use where a record shows an instruction.

Example:

> The executive order directed specified agencies to review contracts and access.

---

## 🟠 Formal Referral

Use where an official matter was transmitted through a documented process.

Example:

> The committee referred the allegation to the department six days after the public demand.

---

## 🟡 Acknowledged Influence

Use where an institution admits that:

- public pressure;
- government action;
- contract exposure;
- official concern;

affected its decision.

---

## 🟢 Repeated Shared Language

Use where several actors repeat the same:

- allegation;
- target category;
- legal framing;
- demanded outcome.

This may indicate shared doctrine.

It does not establish contact.

---

## 🔵 Plausible Signalling

Use where:

- preferred outcome was made public;
- action followed;
- no direct order or referral is public;
- the institution had incentives to anticipate government preference.

State the evidential basis.

---

## 🟣 Anticipatory Compliance

Use where a private or public institution acts before formal instruction.

Record:

- perceived risk;
- decision;
- timing;
- internal evidence;
- absence or presence of direct contact.

---

## ⚪ Independent Alignment

Use where actors adopt similar positions or actions without evidence of contact.

---

## 🕰️ Temporal Sequence Only

Use where:

- one event followed another;
- connection remains unestablished.

This label should appear prominently where causal inference is weak.

---

## ❔ Connection Unknown

Use where:

- private contact may have occurred;
- records are unavailable;
- the relationship cannot be classified.

Do not fill the gap with speculation.

---

## ⏱️ Latency Analysis

Compare:

- action within hours;
- action within days;
- action after weeks of campaigning;
- action after personnel change;
- action after a triggering event;
- action after formal referral;
- action after new evidence.

Latency can help identify:

- rapid compliance;
- slow escalation;
- dormant policy;
- personnel-enabled action;
- ordinary investigative development.

---

## ⚡ Short Latency

Short latency may support:

- direct implementation;
- formal referral;
- anticipatory compliance;
- pre-existing planning.

It may also reflect:

- urgent facts;
- routine rapid response;
- simultaneous information.

Do not treat speed alone as proof.

---

## 🐢 Long Latency

Long latency may indicate:

- repeated campaigning;
- personnel turnover;
- new legal theory;
- new evidence;
- institutional resistance;
- delayed implementation.

The timeline should show what happened during the interval.

---

## 🧨 Triggering Event

Record whether action followed:

- new evidence;
- court decision;
- public controversy;
- election result;
- appointment;
- congressional hearing;
- media report;
- target statement.

A triggering event may explain the timing independently of prior political pressure.

---

## 📈 Escalation Ladder

Track movement through:

1. criticism;
2. accusation;
3. enemy classification;
4. demand for investigation;
5. formal referral;
6. official action;
7. associate pressure;
8. punishment or settlement;
9. residual harm.

Not every case follows the full ladder.

Do not force events into missing stages.

---

## 📉 De-Escalation And Resistance

Track:

- agency refusal;
- career-official resistance;
- judicial injunction;
- public backlash;
- client support;
- institutional refusal;
- withdrawal;
- settlement;
- rescission;
- restoration.

Resistance can interrupt the chain.

It should be placed directly on the timeline.

---

## 🛡️ Agency Refusal

Record:

- agency;
- official;
- request refused;
- date;
- reason;
- later consequence.

A refusal may disconfirm the existence of a unified command structure.

---

## ⚖️ Judicial Injunction

Record:

- court;
- date;
- action blocked;
- procedural stage;
- reasoning;
- appeal;
- practical effect.

---

## 📣 Public Backlash

Record only material responses, such as:

- professional-body action;
- client coalition;
- congressional pressure;
- mass resignation;
- donor response;
- sustained public campaign.

---

## 🔄 Withdrawal Or Rescission

Record:

- who withdrew;
- date;
- scope;
- whether implementation ceased;
- what effects remained.

---

## 🤝 Settlement

Record:

- date;
- parties;
- terms;
- restored benefits;
- concessions;
- monitoring;
- unresolved issues.

## 🗃️ Deleted Or Edited Posts

For deleted or edited public material, preserve:

- archived URL;
- timestamp;
- screenshot where lawful;
- archive source;
- original text;
- edit history;
- confirmation status.

Use labels:

- `ARCHIVED`;
- `DELETED`;
- `EDITED`;
- `SCREENSHOT_ONLY`;
- `UNVERIFIED_CAPTURE`.

Do not rely on unattributed screenshots where better evidence exists.

## 🔒 Unknown Private Contact

Timelines may need to represent possible but undisclosed contact.

Use:

```text
[Public designation]
        ↓
[Private contact: unknown]
        ↓
[Institutional action]
```

Label the middle stage clearly:

- no public record;
- records requested;
- institution declined to answer;
- connection unknown.

A blank is better than a fabricated arrow.

## 🧷 Narrative Timeline Language

Prefer:

> The agency opened the review three days after the public demand. No direct instruction has been made public.

> The formal referral followed repeated statements using the same allegation.

> The employer acted after the contract order but described its decision as independent.

> The court blocked the action, although the client loss had already occurred.

Avoid:

> The president’s post caused the firing.

unless the evidence establishes causation.

---

## 🧪 Events That Weaken The Suspected Connection

Every timeline should include events that may weaken the hypothesis.

Examples include:

- predicating evidence predating public naming;
- ordinary complaint intake;
- agency refusal;
- inconsistent shared language;
- delayed action caused by new facts;
- independent private rationale;
- comparator treatment;
- successful internal safeguards;
- full restoration.

Disconfirming events belong on the same timeline.

---

## 🛡️ Important Cautions

Do not:

- treat chronology as causation;
- draw arrows without evidence labels;
- omit events that weaken the connection;
- merge separate institutions;
- use announcement date where action date is known;
- treat deleted posts as verified without preservation;
- infer private contact from silence;
- erase resistance or de-escalation;
- treat legal relief as complete restoration;
- expose private or security-sensitive information unnecessarily.

---

## 🧪 What Would Weaken A Signalling Analysis?

The analysis should be narrowed where evidence shows:

- action began before public designation;
- strong independent predication;
- ordinary institutional process;
- no shared language;
- no formal contact;
- new triggering evidence;
- long latency explained by unrelated developments;
- consistent comparator treatment;
- private actors had documented independent reasons;
- no practical or chilling effect.
