# 🎙️ How To Cover And Interview  
**First created:** 2026-07-09 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-17  
*Practical coverage and interview discipline for reporting spectacle-driven political actors without becoming their stage.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

Spectacle politicians do not only answer questions.

They try to turn questions into theatre.

A documentary question becomes a grievance.

A declaration issue becomes persecution.

A finance question becomes “the establishment fears us.”

A legal process becomes a mandate play.

A missing document becomes a culture-war monologue.

The task is not to defeat the persona.

The task is to keep the question alive.

Do not argue with the weather.

Ask where the money came from.

---

## ✨ Key Features  

- Keeps the public-interest frame ahead of the grievance frame.
- Treats evasion as evidence without treating it as proof of the underlying allegation.
- Separates documents from drama.
- Refuses personality capture.
- Uses narrow, repeatable questions.
- Narrows the evidential gap rather than repeating the same sentence forever.
- Distinguishes evasion, refusal, inability, and lawful constraint.
- Makes process and role visible.
- Avoids clinical labelling.
- Protects readers, sources, complainants, survivors, and junior staff from risk transfer.
- Keeps the interview attached to evidence, money, liability, and democratic consequence.

---

## 🧭 Core Rule  

> Do not argue with the persona.  
> Ask narrow documentary questions until evasion becomes visible.

The aim is not to win the interview.

The aim is to make the unanswered question legible.

A successful interview may not produce confession.

It may produce a clean public record of:

- refusal
- contradiction
- evasion
- deflection
- inability
- procedural constraint
- missing paperwork

That is useful.

That is journalism.

---

## 🚪 Before Booking The Interview  

An interview is not automatically accountability.

Before booking, ask:

- What specific information could this interview obtain?
- Which unanswered questions are suitable for live questioning?
- Is the guest likely to possess the relevant documents or authority?
- Has the outlet already given repeated opportunities to answer?
- Will the format permit meaningful follow-up?
- Can false claims be corrected without turning the interview into spectacle?
- Is the appearance likely to generate evidence, or merely content?
- Could written questions produce a cleaner record?
- Would a document-led report serve the public better?
- Is the guest being booked because they are materially relevant, or because they are reliably dramatic?

Access is not automatically journalistic value.

An interview invitation can itself confer:

- status
- legitimacy
- visibility
- searchable prominence
- fundraising value
- grievance material

The decision to book is already an editorial decision about power.

---

## 🎭 Establish The Speaking Role  

At the beginning, establish whether the guest is speaking as:

- elected representative
- party officer
- candidate
- employee
- broadcaster
- shareholder
- donor
- campaigner
- private individual

Then establish:

- whether the role is paid
- whether the appearance is editorial or promotional
- whether the organisation benefits commercially
- whether relevant interests have been disclosed
- whether the guest is authorised to speak for the organisation they invoke

A person should not move between roles whenever accountability becomes inconvenient.

If they make a claim as a party leader, they should not retreat into “private citizen” status when asked for the party document.

If they speak as a broadcaster, the audience should know whether the appearance also advances a political or commercial interest.

---

## 🧾 Frame Before Quote  

Do not release the quote without the frame.

Bad pattern:

> Farage says he is the victim of an establishment stitch-up.

Better pattern:

> Farage, who faces unresolved questions about donor support, declarations, and financial provenance, described scrutiny as an establishment stitch-up. He has not published documents resolving who paid, who benefited, and what was declared.

The quote is not the story.

The quote is evidence of the strategy.

Frame it before circulation.

### Portable template

> [Actor], who faces unresolved questions about [documented public-interest issue], described the scrutiny as [grievance claim]. They have not yet provided [specific missing evidence].

This keeps the:

- public-interest issue
- evidential gap
- procedural status
- actor’s response

in the correct order.

---

## 🪤 Common Traps  

Spectacle actors often try to move the interview from evidence into identity.

Common moves:

- “This is a smear.”
- “The establishment is terrified.”
- “The people can decide.”
- “You are obsessed with me.”
- “Everyone does it.”
- “This is old news.”
- “I have answered that.”
- “You are not asking the real question.”
- “What about them?”
- “This is why people hate the media.”
- “You are trying to silence me.”
- “The public already knows the truth.”
- “I cannot comment for legal reasons.”

Some of these may contain fragments of legitimate public frustration.

That does not answer the question.

Return to the paperwork.

Where a legal or procedural constraint may be genuine, test the scope of the constraint rather than assuming evasion.

---

## 🪜 The Question Ladder  

Move through five levels.

### 1. Open question

> Who paid for the support?

### 2. Narrow question

> Was the payment made by an individual, company, trust, or intermediary?

### 3. Document question

> Which declaration, invoice, agreement, or bank record identifies the payer?

### 4. Status question

> Are you refusing to identify the payer?

### 5. Record statement

> As of this interview, you have not identified who paid or provided a document establishing it.

Do not repeat the same question forever.

Narrow the evidential gap until the public record is clear.

The ladder should move from:

- information
- category
- document
- status
- record

The interviewer is not trying to exhaust the guest.

The interviewer is trying to define what remains unanswered.

---

## 🎯 The Return Line  

Use a calm return line.

Examples:

> That does not answer who paid.

> That does not answer what was declared.

> That does not answer whether the support was assessed as a donation, loan, gift, or service in kind.

> That does not answer who performed due diligence.

> That does not answer whether you will publish the documents.

> That explains your view of the scrutiny. It does not answer the documentary question.

The return line is not dramatic.

That is the point.

The spectacle wants heat.

The paperwork wants sequence.

---

## 🧩 Types Of Non-Answer  

Not every failure to answer is the same.

### Evasion

The guest changes the subject or replaces evidence with grievance.

### Refusal

The guest clearly declines to answer.

### Inability

The guest says they do not know, do not remember, or do not hold the information.

### Legal or procedural constraint

The guest says they cannot answer because of:

- litigation
- confidentiality
- security
- employment obligations
- regulatory process
- privilege
- data protection
- an active investigation

These should be reported differently.

A lawful inability to answer is not automatically evasion.

The follow-up is:

- Who can answer?
- What process governs disclosure?
- When might the information become available?
- Can the underlying document be supplied?
- Can the guest explain the exact scope of the restriction?
- Is the restriction legal, contractual, procedural, or voluntary?

Do not call every non-answer a refusal.

Do not allow vague invocations of “legal reasons” to end the inquiry without clarification.

---

## 💸 Money Questions  

Ask:

- Who paid?
- Who benefited?
- What was paid for?
- What was the support worth?
- Was it cash, loan, gift, staffing, security, accommodation, travel, advice, digital work, media support, or service in kind?
- Was it declared?
- When was it declared?
- Who advised on declaration?
- Who checked permissibility?
- Who performed due diligence?
- What was the source of funds?
- What was the source of wealth?
- Were companies, trusts, offshore vehicles, crypto rails, gambling platforms, or intermediaries involved?
- Was any value supplied on discounted or non-commercial terms?
- Will you publish the relevant invoices, agreements, correspondence, declarations, and advice?

Do not let “I followed the rules” end the question.

Ask:

- which rules
- which jurisdiction
- which category
- who checked
- what document proves it

---

## 🧾 Declaration Questions  

Ask:

- Was this support declared?
- If yes, where?
- If no, why not?
- Was it assessed as personal support, political support, campaign support, parliamentary support, media support, or party support?
- Who made that assessment?
- Was independent advice taken?
- Was the advice written down?
- Will the written advice be published?
- Has the relevant authority been notified?
- Has the declaration been corrected?
- If correction is needed, when did you first become aware?
- Was the value independently assessed?
- Does the declaration identify the economic source or only the named provider?

Declaration questions work because they are boring.

Boring is where the trapdoor lives.

---

## 🪙 Crypto And Digital-Money Questions  

Ask:

- Do you, your party, your donors, your advisers, your employers, or relevant entities hold interests in crypto, stablecoins, exchanges, gambling platforms, payment rails, or digital-money infrastructure?
- Have any of those interests benefited from your public position?
- Have you raised digital-money policy with ministers, regulators, central bankers, officials, or parliamentary colleagues?
- Was that lobbying declared?
- Who was present?
- Was the meeting minuted?
- What policy change were you seeking?
- Who would benefit financially if that policy position prevailed?
- Were any donors or advisers informed before or after the meeting?
- Were any relevant token, exchange, or company interests declared?
- Will you publish the correspondence?

Do not let “I oppose central-bank digital currency” remain only an ideological claim.

Ask who benefits.

Preserve the distinction between:

- policy view
- financial interest
- lobbying
- donation
- ownership
- control

---

## ⚖️ Legal-Exposure Questions  

Ask:

- Are there filed civil claims connected to this relationship?
- Has any court entered judgment?
- Has any settlement been reached?
- Are any relevant proceedings ongoing?
- Has your insurer been notified?
- Is any indemnity relevant?
- Are any assets subject to a court order, freeze, charge, or security interest?
- Have lenders, banks, insurers, donors, counterparties, or political organisations been informed of relevant exposure?
- Are there documents that may become disclosable?
- Who is legally responsible if the factual position changes?
- Could one claim affect the ability to meet another?
- Is there a realistic collectability issue?

The question is not only scandal.

The question is risk transfer.

Do not ask a guest to speculate about unsupported allegations merely because the language sounds legally serious.

Anchor the question to:

- filed proceedings
- known judgments
- disclosed settlements
- documented obligations
- identified insurance or enforcement issues

---

## 📺 Media-Platform Questions  

Ask:

- Are you speaking as an elected politician, party leader, paid presenter, campaigner, private citizen, shareholder, or employee of a media organisation?
- Who benefits commercially from this appearance?
- Does your broadcaster, platform, sponsor, employer, or funder have interests connected to the story?
- Has any editorial-independence issue been assessed?
- Are there staff cuts, financial losses, ownership pressures, advertiser pressures, or political relationships relevant to coverage?
- Has the audience been told which role you are occupying?
- Is this interview accountability, campaigning, brand management, or content?
- Who selected the format?
- Who controls the clips and onward distribution?
- Is the appearance monetised?

Role confusion is useful to spectacle politics.

Make the role explicit.

---

## 🗳️ Democracy Questions  

Ask:

- Why should a by-election, vote, poll, or audience reaction replace a standards process?
- If the public is being asked to judge, will you publish the documents needed for judgment?
- What would count as evidence against your position?
- What process should apply to politicians you oppose in the same circumstances?
- Should all politicians disclose this kind of support?
- Should the rules be tightened?
- Would you support publication of the relevant records?
- Are you asking voters to decide before they can see the evidence?
- Should popularity alter the applicable legal or regulatory standard?
- Would you accept the same reasoning from an opponent?

“The people can decide” is not an answer if the people are denied the paperwork.

---

## 🧠 Evasion As Evidence  

Evasion does not prove the underlying allegation.

But it can prove that the question remains live.

Mark the status clearly:

- answered
- partially answered
- not answered
- refused
- unable to answer
- legally constrained
- contradicted
- unsupported
- awaiting documents
- referred to regulator
- subject to legal process
- political claim only

Do not overclaim.

Do not under-record.

A clean non-answer record is valuable.

---

## 🧪 Live Correction Discipline  

Correct immediately where the claim is:

- clearly false
- material
- likely to mislead
- safely verifiable during the interview

Do not improvise certainty where:

- documents are incomplete
- jurisdiction is unclear
- legal proceedings are active
- the factual dispute requires specialist review
- a number or quotation cannot be checked safely in real time

Use:

> We cannot verify that claim during this interview. We will check it against the record.

Or:

> The available record does not currently support that claim.

A delayed accurate correction is better than a theatrical but unreliable rebuttal.

Live fact-checking should not become a competition in who can assert certainty fastest.

---

## 🛡️ Protect The People Outside The Studio  

Do not allow the guest to answer scrutiny by:

- naming private individuals unnecessarily
- exposing complainants
- speculating about survivors
- directing harassment
- revealing protected personal data
- blaming junior staff without evidence
- disclosing confidential medical or legal information
- implying guilt through association

Interrupt where necessary.

The interview is not permission to transfer risk onto people with less power.

Where a guest begins naming or targeting an individual:

- ask whether the person is relevant
- establish whether the claim is evidenced
- stop the disclosure if necessary
- explain the editorial boundary
- remove unlawful or gratuitous material before publication

---

## 🧯 Anti-Platforming Discipline  

Covering an actor is not the same as platforming them.

But the line can collapse quickly.

Avoid:

- name-first headlines
- grievance-first framing
- empty “hits back” stories
- reaction loops
- slogan repetition
- theatrical walkouts as standalone content
- publishing clips that reward evasion without context
- treating delay as exoneration
- treating wealth as credibility
- treating popularity as procedural compliance
- using the most inflammatory clip as the promotional asset

Use:

- issue-first headlines
- document-first framing
- process status boxes
- timeline placement
- question trackers
- grouped reactions
- quote framing
- proportional updates
- visible corrections
- full-context transcripts where appropriate

Cover the risk.

Do not feed the spectacle.

---

## 🛠️ Interview Structure  

A useful structure:

1. **Establish the public-interest frame.**
2. **Establish the guest’s speaking role.**
3. **Ask the narrow documentary question.**
4. **Let the answer happen.**
5. **Identify whether it answered the question.**
6. **Move up the question ladder.**
7. **Ask for the document.**
8. **State what remains unresolved.**
9. **Set any promised follow-up date.**

Example:

> You have described this as a political attack. The unresolved question is who paid for the support and whether it was declared. Who paid?

If evaded:

> That does not answer who paid. Was the payment made by an individual, company, trust, or intermediary?

If evaded again:

> Which document identifies the payer?

Then:

> So as of this interview, you are not providing the name or document showing who paid. Is that correct?

This is not rudeness.

It is record construction.

---

## 🛑 Stopping Conditions  

End or redirect the interview where:

- the guest repeatedly refuses every documentary question
- the format has become a vehicle for targeted harassment
- falsehoods are arriving faster than they can be responsibly handled
- the guest uses the interview to reveal protected information
- the remaining exchange adds heat but no information
- the agreed public-interest subject has been abandoned
- the guest is using the appearance solely to distribute slogans or threats
- the interviewer cannot safely preserve evidential clarity

Ending an unproductive interview is not censorship.

It is editorial control.

The stopping decision should be based on:

- public-interest yield
- safety
- evidential integrity
- proportionality

not irritation.

---

## 📝 After The Interview  

Publish:

- the central unanswered questions
- the documentary status
- any claims requiring verification
- relevant corrections
- promised documents and deadlines
- the guest’s precise role
- what materially changed
- which answers were partial, refused, or constrained

Avoid:

- presenting aggression as effectiveness
- clipping only the theatrical exchange
- writing “refused to answer” where the guest gave a partial answer
- allowing a promised document to disappear from follow-up
- presenting a correction as a fresh controversy rather than repair
- allowing the guest’s grievance to become the headline

Maintain a question tracker.

An interview is not complete when the microphone is switched off.

## 🧰 Reporter Question Bank  

### Money

- Who paid?
- Who benefited?
- What was the support worth?
- Was it declared?
- What category was it declared under?
- Who checked the source of funds?
- Was any value supplied below market price?
- Was a loan, guarantee, or service involved?

### Paperwork

- What documents exist?
- Who holds them?
- Will they be published?
- Has any regulator seen them?
- Has any declaration been corrected?
- Has any advice been put in writing?
- What date should the public expect publication?

### Influence

- What policy positions overlap with donor interests?
- Were meetings held?
- Were officials lobbied?
- Was lobbying declared?
- Were donors or advisers briefed?
- What access followed the support?

### Liability

- Are there filed civil claims?
- Are there judgments?
- Are there settlements?
- Are there indemnities?
- Are there disclosure risks?
- Are there banking or insurance consequences?
- Are assets reachable if liability is established?

### Democracy

- Why should public mandate replace process?
- What evidence should voters see?
- Should the rules apply equally to opponents?
- What would count as a breach?
- What would count as exoneration?
- What process would you support if the roles were reversed?

## ⚖️ Evidence Discipline  

Do not diagnose personality.

Describe behaviour.

Use terms like:

- spectacle-driven
- grievance-led
- authoritarian
- evasive
- documentary avoidance
- role confusion
- attention capture
- mandate theatre
- procedural displacement

Preserve the distinctions:

- evasion is not proof of the allegation
- refusal is not inability
- inability is not bad faith
- legal constraint is not automatic evasion
- popularity is not compliance
- access is not accountability
- aggression is not effectiveness
- a live correction is not reliable merely because it is immediate
- a quote is not verification
- a promised document is not a published document
- an interview is not automatically public-interest journalism

The issue is not whether the actor is interesting.

The issue is whether the public question has been answered.

---

## 🧪 What Would Disconfirm The Concern?  

Evidence that the interview format is working may include:

- direct answers
- publication of documents
- corrections made during or after the interview
- clear role disclosure
- meaningful concessions
- verifiable new facts
- a narrowed evidential dispute
- a credible explanation of lawful constraints
- a defined route to future disclosure
- a record that improves later regulatory, legal, or public understanding

A spectacle-driven actor may answer clearly.

A long interview may produce important evidence.

A grievance claim may contain legitimate criticism.

The model should preserve those possibilities.
