Yes. I’d treat each immediate subfolder of 🤑_Hot_Money_Politics/ as a daughter cluster, and give each one a tight two-sentence remit before we touch individual nodes.

🧭 How To Read The System

The methodological doorway into the cluster. It teaches the reader how to distinguish connection from culpability, influence from corruption, and threat modelling from attribution, while keeping the central rule that mechanisms may transfer between cases but findings do not.

💷 Money, Access And Obligation

Looks beyond cash to the wider economy of value: hospitality, gifts in kind, introductions, prestige, friendship, information and access. Its central question is not merely what did somebody receive?, but what did receiving it make easier — and what did it make harder to refuse?

🏛️ Lobbying And Democratic Friction

Places modern lobbying inside Britain’s much longer history of organised interests attempting to shape political decisions. It asks how concentrated interests change the relative friction of political choices, including the important distinction between outright suppression and the softer dynamics of incorporation, access and institutionalisation.

🪧 Unions, Communities And Aggregated Power

Uses trade unions and other representative organisations to prevent the analysis becoming a one-directional critique of wealthy or corporate lobbying. It asks how dispersed people legitimately aggregate power, who gains authority to speak for the group, and what happens when the interests of representatives begin to diverge from those represented.

🌍 Diaspora Money And Transnational Life

Updates older British ideas about foreign allegiance for a post-imperial country in which transnational family, cultural, charitable and political relationships are ordinary parts of British life. It asks how genuine risks such as diversion, coercion or foreign direction can be investigated without treating transnational identity itself as evidence of disloyalty.

💣 Influence, State Threats And The Fifth Column

Examines the inherited security model of the hostile outsider, secret agent and organised fifth column — including why that model once made considerable strategic sense. It then asks where that model fails in an information environment containing autonomous actors, self-radicalisation, distributed networks, allied influence and behaviour without a headquarters.

🕸️ Information Ecosystems And Crowd Effects

Moves from individual actors to emergent behaviour: prestige, social proof, amplification, crowd dynamics, silence, mobilisation and feedback loops. It gives the cluster its cybernetic vocabulary while remaining careful that coordinated-looking outcomes do not themselves establish coordination or deliberate manipulation.

🧱 Dependency As Attack Surface

Connects Hot Money Politics directly to the Data Wars and Little Database work by treating ordinary dependencies as potential sources of leverage. Contracts, shared software, technical stacks, information access, suppliers and relationships can all become vulnerabilities because a vulnerability does not require a villain; a villain merely gives somebody an incentive to exploit it.

🏦 Dirty Money Without The Cartoon Villain

Uses lessons from anti-money-laundering and financial compliance to show why wrongdoing rarely presents itself in a form that announces what it is. It asks how apparently respectable transactions, professional relationships and fragmented structures can obscure their eventual function — including from people participating in them without fully understanding the system.

🏛️ Parliament, Compliance And Self-Regulation

Examines the peculiar governance position of elected politicians, who are neither ordinary employees nor civil servants but nevertheless encounter money, sensitive information, lobbyists, foreign interests and safeguarding risks. It asks what should properly remain with parties and individual judgement, and what might require a common parliamentary or independent baseline without compromising democratic representation.

🧿 Race, Prejudice And Evidentiary Discipline

Deals explicitly with the fact that a genuine problem and discriminatory treatment of that problem can coexist. Using antisemitism, anti-Palestinian racism and other forms of racialised suspicion, it develops ways to investigate money, influence and allegiance without collective attribution — while refusing to make prejudice a veto on legitimate scrutiny.

📺 Media Amplification And Purchased Capacity

Asks why some stories, claims and political positions acquire extraordinary persistence and visibility while others disappear. Rather than reducing this to “someone bought the media”, it follows what resources actually purchase: research, litigation, spokespeople, PR, expertise, introductions, legitimacy, repetition and ultimately capacity to occupy public attention.

🧨 Hard Cases

The stress-testing daughter: only after the reader has learned the analytical instrument do we apply it to subjects where sloppy network reasoning can cause serious harm. Sanctioned Russian wealth, Epstein-associated networks, atrocity-linked economic interests, exploitation, anti-trans political financing, migration carcerality and historically accumulated wealth can therefore be examined without implying that connection, benefit, facilitation, knowledge, control and culpability are interchangeable.

🧰 Reporting And Investigation Kit

Turns the conceptual work back into a practical Press Matters tool. It gives journalists, researchers and readers repeatable questions for examining power — what was received, what access changed, what became harder to refuse, what the evidence actually establishes, whether like is being compared with like, and how to investigate power without reproducing prejudice.

And I think those daughters now have a pretty coherent progression:

learn the instrument → understand value → understand organised power → test it on non-capital aggregation → deal with transnational Britain → update the security model → understand information behaviour → map dependencies → recognise disguised transactions → examine the political regulatory gap → introduce evidentiary safeguards → examine media amplification → attempt the dangerous cases → give the reader the toolkit.

That feels much more intentional than simply having thirteen buckets of related nodes.
