# 🛰️ Orientation  
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01  
*Conceptual foundations for tracing Palestine’s many routes through public life without allowing the model, its comparisons, or its domestic consequences to displace Palestine itself.*

---

## 🛰️ Purpose

This cluster establishes the conceptual foundations of **🍉 Palestine As Rhizome**.

The wider pack asks why Palestine remains a durable site of:

- protest;
- political identification;
- legal dispute;
- professional concern;
- institutional conflict;
- public grief;
- democratic scrutiny;
- and international solidarity.

It uses the rhizome as a model for tracing the political relationships, solidarities, obligations, institutional connections, disputes, and forms of participation that have developed around an unresolved Palestinian reality.

The rhizome is not Palestine itself.

It describes the wider field through which Palestine enters public life.

Palestinian people, material conditions, testimony, political agency, history, rights, and competing visions of political resolution remain the substantive centre.

A connected model requires discipline.

Without it, the work may:

- mistake decentralisation for incoherence;
- mistake connection for equivalence;
- turn Palestinians into symbols for other people’s politics;
- allow domestic controversies to replace Palestinian conditions;
- reproduce contested labels as neutral fact;
- treat conceptual resemblance as proof of a material relationship;
- or present every connected issue as though it were secretly the same issue.

This orientation cluster therefore establishes four principles:

1. the rhizome is useful because Palestinian solidarity has multiple roots, routes, and points of emergence;
2. Palestine and Palestinians remain central to every route being traced;
3. connection does not make distinct histories, harms, populations, or political situations equivalent;
4. and the whole pack requires shared editorial guardrails governing evidence, language, representation, comparison, and harm.

These principles should be read before the wider branches are used for reporting, analysis, or casework.

This cluster establishes the rules by which those wider questions are approached.

The substantive mapping of institutions, movement composition, reporting failures, and repression belongs to the relevant daughter clusters.

## 🌱 The Model

The rhizome model helps explain political persistence without searching for:

- one central command;
- one representative organisation;
- one fixed ideology;
- one typical marcher;
- one route into solidarity;
- or one visible structure that can be removed to end the movement.

Within this pack, a root may be:

- a grievance;
- a constituency;
- an identity or family relationship;
- an ethical or religious commitment;
- a professional obligation;
- a political experience;
- an institutional connection;
- an experience of repression;
- or a route into participation.

A route describes how that root becomes connected to Palestinian solidarity, public mobilisation, or institutional dispute.

People may arrive through:

- Palestinian identity and family;
- humanitarian concern;
- international law;
- anti-war politics;
- religious commitment;
- Jewish dissent;
- anti-racism;
- professional ethics;
- trade unionism;
- student politics;
- arms exports;
- public procurement;
- policing;
- protest rights;
- media credibility;
- or democratic consistency.

These routes intersect.

They do not become identical.

A visible expression may be suppressed while the unresolved issue continues through another institutional, legal, professional, political, or relational route.

This redistribution is not automatically growth.

Suppression may generate additional political routes while also:

- injuring participants;
- destroying practical capacity;
- excluding people from further participation;
- increasing fear;
- or weakening the organisations expected to sustain collective action.

The rhizome model provides a way to map persistence, movement, interruption, and re-emergence.

It does not suggest that everything is Palestine.

It asks where a material, legal, institutional, political, professional, historical, tactical, conceptual, or relational route to Palestine can actually be shown.

## 🇵🇸 The Centre

The routes are not the centre.

Palestine is.

Palestinian people are not an analytical device through which other societies examine:

- their own democratic health;
- institutional contradictions;
- communal identities;
- political morality;
- professional ethics;
- or media failures.

Those questions belong in this pack where they are materially connected to Palestinian conditions.

They must not displace:

- Palestinian life;
- land;
- history;
- testimony;
- material survival;
- political plurality;
- rights;
- and collective agency.

Humanitarian recognition is not sufficient if it turns Palestinians into passive recipients of concern while removing their political claims, histories, disagreements, and agency.

Domestic institutions may become important objects of scrutiny.

They must not become the main characters merely because they are more familiar to the presumed reader.

The rhizome describes how the issue travels.

It must not become a method for travelling away from Palestine.

## 🕸️ The Connections

Rhizomatic analysis requires connection.

The work may trace:

- institutional connections;
- financial connections;
- legal connections;
- political connections;
- professional connections;
- historical connections;
- tactical connections;
- conceptual connections;
- and relationships of solidarity.

Each type of connection supports a different claim.

A university investment may create a financial and institutional route.

A court decision may create a legal route.

A professional rule may create an ethical or regulatory route.

A shared campaign may create a relational route.

A historical comparison may reveal a transmitted institution, political technique, legal mechanism, or recurring pattern.

A conceptual resemblance may justify a question or comparison.

It does not, without further evidence, establish:

- a material relationship;
- a shared cause;
- common responsibility;
- institutional transmission;
- or political equivalence.

Connections therefore differ in evidential strength.

A route should be:

- identified;
- evidenced;
- classified;
- limited;
- and described with the differences preserved.

The existence of one type of connection should not be used to imply another.

A conceptual relationship should not be written as though it were financial.

A political affinity should not be written as though it established command.

A shared tactic should not be written as though it proved shared ideology.

A historical analogy should not be written as though it erased different histories.

Connection makes the relationship visible.

Equivalence erases the ground on either side.

## 🌀 The Discipline

The pack should remain capable of naming:

- mass harm;
- displacement;
- state violence;
- attacks on civilians;
- antisemitism;
- anti-Palestinian racism;
- Islamophobia;
- movement failure;
- political manipulation;
- institutional inconsistency;
- protest misconduct;
- and abusive uses of security language

without turning any population into a permanent suspect class.

This requires editorial discipline.

Across the pack:

- Palestinian people and material conditions should remain visible;
- claims, evidence, inference, and findings should be distinguished;
- time-sensitive facts should be dated;
- contested language should be attributed;
- protest should be represented proportionately;
- populations, movements, networks, organisations, institutions, and events should not be conflated;
- antisemitism and anti-Palestinian racism should be assessed seriously and specifically;
- comparison should preserve historical difference;
- security claims should be examined rather than assumed;
- images should not consume suffering;
- financial and institutional relationships should be described at the level the evidence supports;
- and institutional responses should be studied as possible causes of further harm.

The aim is not neutral distance from consequence.

It is sufficient accuracy to describe consequence without reproducing it.

## 🧭 Core Distinctions

The orientation cluster maintains several distinctions throughout the pack.

### Route Is Not Origin

An issue may provide a route into Palestinian solidarity without being the origin of the Palestinian struggle.

### Connection Is Not Equivalence

Two cases may share an institution, mechanism, law, technique, or political concern without becoming interchangeable.

### Material Connection Is Not Conceptual Resemblance

A conceptual comparison may help formulate a question.

It does not independently prove money, command, responsibility, institutional transmission, or common cause.

### Solidarity Is Not Ownership

Recognising a relationship does not confer the right to redefine another population’s history or political demands.

### Plurality Is Not Incoherence

A movement may contain serious disagreement while retaining a minimum shared centre.

That centre must be established through evidence such as:

- recurring demands;
- participant testimony;
- organiser statements;
- and observable collective action.

It should not be inferred from one slogan, speaker, incident, or social-media post.

### Decentralisation Is Not Absence Of Structure

Networks may operate without one command centre while still containing:

- leadership;
- influence;
- funding;
- hierarchy;
- coordination;
- and accountability.

### Humanitarian Recognition Is Not Political Resolution

Relief can be urgently necessary without settling questions of rights, land, accountability, representation, political status, or collective agency.

### Scrutiny Is Not Collective Liability

Specific conduct should receive specific scrutiny.

It should not become permanent suspicion directed at an entire population.

### Removal Is Not Resolution

A visible action may be suppressed while the underlying grievance, institutional connection, or political route remains active.

### Redistribution Is Not Automatic Growth

Repression may generate new routes while also harming participants, reducing capacity, and preventing further participation.

## ❓ Orientation Questions

Before entering the wider pack, ask:

- What Palestinian condition, experience, or political claim is at the centre?
- Does the rhizome describe Palestine, or the political field that has developed around it?
- Through which route did this wider issue become connected?
- Is that route material, institutional, financial, legal, political, professional, historical, tactical, conceptual, or relational?
- What evidence establishes the connection?
- What level of claim does that evidence support?
- Is a conceptual resemblance being presented as though it proved a material relationship?
- Which differences must remain visible?
- Are Palestinians present as people and political actors?
- Has a domestic institution become the main character?
- Are movement, organisation, network, institution, population, and event being distinguished?
- Is a contested label being presented as neutral fact?
- Is one incident being made representative of a whole movement?
- Has analogy replaced evidence?
- Is any population being treated as a permanent suspect class?
- Is the movement’s minimum shared centre evidenced or merely assumed?
- What has the institutional response suppressed, displaced, generated, or damaged?
- Does the analysis clarify Palestine, or merely use Palestine to explain something else?

## 🌿 Working Principle

Expand without displacement.

Connect without collapse.

Scrutinise without collective suspicion.

Report the routes without losing the people and conditions from which they grow.
