# 🇵🇸 Palestine Remains Central  
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-17  
*Keeping Palestinian life, land, history, testimony, material conditions, and political agency at the centre of rhizomatic analysis.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The rhizome model traces the many routes through which Palestine enters public, institutional, professional, legal, and democratic life.

Those routes matter.

They help explain why people continue marching, why attempts at containment often generate new disputes, and why Palestine cannot be separated neatly from decisions made by institutions elsewhere.

But the routes are not the centre.

Palestine is.

Palestinian people are not an analytical device through which other societies examine:

- their own democratic health;
- their media systems;
- their professional ethics;
- their institutional contradictions;
- their political identities;
- or the moral character of their citizens.

Those questions belong in the cluster because they are materially connected to Palestine.

They must not be allowed to displace the Palestinian life, harm, history, survival, and political agency from which those connections arise.

The rhizome describes how the issue travels.

It must not become a method for travelling away from Palestine.

---

## 🇵🇸 The Subject Is Palestine

This pack begins with a practical question:

**Why are people still marching for Palestine?**

The answer cannot rest only on what the marches have come to represent elsewhere.

People continue marching because the originating Palestinian conditions have not reached a legitimate resolution.

These conditions include:

- killing and injury;
- mass displacement;
- destruction of homes and civilian infrastructure;
- restrictions on movement;
- detention and imprisonment;
- separation of families;
- loss of land;
- attacks on cultural and communal continuity;
- barriers to humanitarian relief;
- political exclusion;
- and the denial or erosion of credible routes towards safety, rights, accountability, and self-determination.

The precise conditions differ across Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem, Israel, refugee communities, prisons, border systems, and the wider Palestinian diaspora.

They should not be compressed into one undifferentiated account.

But the wider movement does not exist merely because Palestine has become symbolically useful.

It exists because Palestinian life and political claims remain materially at stake.

## 🧍 People Before Symbol

Palestine carries enormous symbolic weight.

It can signify:

- anti-colonial struggle;
- resistance;
- displacement;
- international solidarity;
- religious attachment;
- failures of international law;
- racial hierarchy;
- state violence;
- exile;
- return;
- and the survival of a people across borders.

Symbols can support memory and mobilisation.

They can also consume the people they claim to represent.

A person may become:

- a photograph;
- a casualty figure;
- a symbol of innocence;
- a symbol of resistance;
- evidence in somebody else’s argument;
- or a test of another person’s political morality.

This can flatten Palestinian people into roles.

Children become the only publicly grievable Palestinians.

Adults are required to prove perfect innocence.

Political actors are treated as either pure representatives or complete disqualifications.

Families become images of suffering without histories, disagreements, occupations, beliefs, ambitions, or political views of their own.

Palestinians should not have to become universally symbolic before their lives become recognisable.

They are people before they are proof.

---

## 🗺️ Land, History, And Material Conditions

Palestine cannot be kept central through identity language alone.

The analysis must remain connected to:

- land;
- homes;
- water;
- borders;
- roads;
- agriculture;
- movement;
- electricity;
- healthcare;
- education;
- employment;
- property;
- archives;
- religious sites;
- family networks;
- and the physical conditions through which a people remains socially and politically alive.

These are not background details.

They are part of the structure of the conflict.

A reporting frame that recognises Palestinian identity while ignoring material conditions may acknowledge a people symbolically while failing to describe what is happening to them.

Likewise, a purely humanitarian frame may record hunger, injury, and displacement without explaining the political arrangements through which they are produced or sustained.

Palestinian centrality requires both:

- attention to immediate human need;
- and attention to the systems governing land, rights, movement, resources, representation, and power.

---

## 🗣️ Testimony And Political Agency

Palestinians must remain visible as witnesses to their own conditions.

This means more than including an occasional quotation.

It means recognising Palestinian people as:

- historians;
- journalists;
- doctors;
- lawyers;
- organisers;
- researchers;
- artists;
- workers;
- parents;
- children;
- political thinkers;
- community members;
- and participants in debates about their own collective future.

Testimony is not only the description of suffering.

It may include:

- analysis;
- accusation;
- disagreement;
- memory;
- humour;
- strategy;
- cultural knowledge;
- political demand;
- and refusal.

Palestinian voices should not be included only when they are emotionally legible to an outside audience.

Nor should they be required to speak with one voice.

There are profound differences among Palestinians concerning:

- leadership;
- political settlement;
- resistance;
- religion;
- nationalism;
- class;
- gender;
- governance;
- international intervention;
- diaspora politics;
- and the failures of Palestinian political institutions.

Preserving Palestinian agency means allowing that plurality to exist without treating disagreement as evidence that Palestinians have no collective political claims.

---

## 🕊️ Humanitarian Concern Is Necessary But Insufficient

Humanitarian language can establish urgent recognition of:

- civilian suffering;
- hunger;
- disease;
- displacement;
- medical collapse;
- and the destruction of basic conditions for survival.

That recognition matters.

But humanitarian framing can also narrow the political field.

It may present Palestinians primarily as:

- recipients of aid;
- vulnerable civilians;
- displaced populations;
- patients;
- refugees;
- or bodies requiring temporary relief.

These descriptions may be accurate and necessary.

They become insufficient when they obscure questions of:

- rights;
- representation;
- responsibility;
- land;
- freedom of movement;
- return;
- political status;
- accountability;
- and collective agency.

A person can need food, medicine, shelter, and protection while also holding political rights and political demands.

Relief should not become a substitute for justice.

Compassion should not require depoliticisation.

---

## 🪞 When Solidarity Becomes Self-Referential

Solidarity movements inevitably reveal things about the societies in which they operate.

Palestine may expose disputes concerning:

- policing;
- protest rights;
- workplace speech;
- university governance;
- media credibility;
- charitable regulation;
- arms exports;
- procurement;
- political funding;
- racism;
- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- and institutional consistency.

These disputes are real.

They deserve examination.

But solidarity becomes self-referential when Palestine is gradually reduced to the occasion for a domestic audience to discuss itself.

This happens when:

- protest coverage becomes entirely about police tactics;
- campus reporting becomes entirely about university reputation;
- legal analysis becomes detached from Palestinian conditions;
- Jewish participation is treated as more politically meaningful than Palestinian participation;
- solidarity is evaluated primarily through the moral development of non-Palestinian participants;
- professional disputes become the whole story;
- or institutions describe their own difficulty responding as the central harm.

The domestic consequences do not need to be ignored.

They need to remain connected to the reason they arose.

A branch may grow far from the visible centre.

The reporting should still be able to trace the route back.

---

## 🗞️ How Reporting Recentres The Domestic Audience

Reporting frequently transforms distant suffering into a story about the audience presumed to matter most.

In British coverage, the central questions may become:

- whether British politicians are embarrassed;
- whether British protesters are disruptive;
- whether British institutions appear neutral;
- whether British Jews feel represented;
- whether British Muslims are integrating;
- whether universities can protect their reputations;
- whether police have sufficient powers;
- or whether political parties can manage internal division.

These are legitimate domestic questions.

They are not automatically the most important questions in every story.

A report can contain extensive discussion of Palestine while still centring Britain.

This occurs when Palestinians appear mainly as:

- the cause of British disagreement;
- the recipients of British charity;
- the subject of British foreign policy;
- the object of British protest;
- or the background to British institutional conflict.

Palestine remains central when reporting asks not only how events affect domestic institutions, but also:

- what those institutions are doing;
- whom their decisions affect;
- which Palestinian conditions those decisions sustain or challenge;
- and how Palestinians understand the consequences.

## 🧭 The Solidarity Mirror

Solidarity often includes an element of self-recognition.

People see their own histories, values, fears, or political commitments reflected in another struggle.

This can deepen connection.

It can also create distortion.

A movement may recognise Palestine through:

- anti-colonial history;
- Black liberation;
- Jewish historical memory;
- Indigenous struggle;
- labour organising;
- feminist politics;
- queer politics;
- disability justice;
- religious ethics;
- or experiences of policing and state surveillance.

These connections can be real and politically useful.

But the mirror must not replace the view.

The question is not only:

> What does Palestine teach us about ourselves?

It is also:

> What are Palestinians saying about Palestine?

Solidarity should create additional routes towards Palestinian life and political agency.

It should not turn Palestinians into a surface onto which others project a complete account of themselves.

---

## ✡️ Jewish Debate Must Not Displace Palestinians

Jewish identity, Jewish safety, antisemitism, Zionism, anti-Zionism, non-Zionism, diaspora politics, and the political representation of Jews are deeply connected to public arguments about Palestine.

These questions require serious and careful treatment.

Jewish people are not politically uniform.

Jewish fear should not be dismissed because it is politically inconvenient.

Jewish dissent should not be erased because it complicates claims of communal consensus.

Antisemitism within or around Palestinian solidarity should not be minimised.

But Jewish political debate must not become the only debate through which Palestine is considered legitimate.

A familiar pattern places Jewish participants at the centre of Palestinian solidarity because their presence is treated as:

- moral permission;
- evidence against antisemitism;
- a more surprising political story;
- or a form of authentication for Palestinian claims.

Jewish solidarity can matter without functioning as certification.

Palestinian testimony does not require Jewish endorsement to become politically real.

Likewise, the safety and plurality of Jewish communities should not require Palestinian disappearance.

Both can be held without making either population an instrument for managing the other.

---

## 🧱 Palestinians Are Not A Single Political Object

Keeping Palestine central does not mean constructing a single authoritative Palestinian voice.

Palestinians differ by:

- geography;
- citizenship and legal status;
- generation;
- class;
- religion;
- gender;
- political affiliation;
- family history;
- experience of displacement;
- and relationship to Palestinian institutions.

People living in Gaza do not occupy the same legal or material position as Palestinians in the West Bank.

Palestinian citizens of Israel face different structures from refugees in Lebanon, Jordan, Syria, or elsewhere.

Diaspora communities do not have one relationship to return, statehood, resistance, religion, or national identity.

These distinctions should be preserved.

At the same time, fragmentation should not be used to deny shared histories, relationships, collective claims, or forms of peoplehood.

Plurality does not eliminate political existence.

---

## ⚖️ Political Agency Includes Conduct That Requires Scrutiny

Palestinian centrality does not require romanticisation.

Palestinian organisations, leaders, institutions, and political actors can:

- make harmful decisions;
- exercise coercion;
- reproduce inequality;
- suppress dissent;
- target civilians;
- misuse resources;
- fail their constituencies;
- or pursue strategies open to legal and moral criticism.

Treating Palestinians as political actors means applying serious standards to Palestinian political conduct.

But scrutiny should be specific.

It should not convert the actions of:

- one organisation;
- one armed group;
- one authority;
- one speaker;
- or one political faction

into permanent collective liability for an entire population.

Political agency is not the same as collective guilt.

Nor should Palestinian wrongdoing be used to make Palestinian rights conditional on flawless representation.

No population must produce perfect leaders before civilians become protectable or political claims become discussable.

## 🔍 The Test Of Centrality

A node may contain many pages about domestic institutions and still keep Palestine central.

The test is not word count alone.

Ask:

- Is the originating Palestinian condition clearly identified?
- Are Palestinians present as people rather than symbols?
- Is Palestinian testimony included as analysis as well as suffering?
- Are material conditions visible?
- Are domestic decisions connected to their consequences?
- Are Palestinians allowed political plurality?
- Is humanitarian language preserving or removing agency?
- Are solidarists becoming the main characters?
- Is another population being asked to authenticate Palestinian claims?
- Can the route through the wider issue still be traced back to Palestine?
- Would the node remain meaningful if the domestic controversy disappeared?
- Does the analysis help readers understand Palestine, or merely use Palestine to understand themselves?

If the wider route has become more visible than the people and conditions from which it began, the node needs recentering.

---

## 🪴 What Recentering Requires

Recentering does not always require adding a generic Palestinian quotation at the beginning or end of a piece.

It may require changing the structure of the analysis.

That could mean:

- beginning with the Palestinian condition rather than the domestic reaction;
- identifying the material consequences of an institutional decision;
- distinguishing Palestinian constituencies rather than using one collective label;
- including Palestinian analysis throughout;
- naming land, movement, detention, health, family, and political status;
- explaining how domestic institutions are materially connected;
- refusing to make solidarity the main story;
- or asking what Palestinians themselves identify as meaningful change.

Recentering is not decorative representation.

It is a decision about what the work understands itself to be about.

---

## ❓ Working Questions

When assessing whether Palestine remains central, ask:

- Which Palestinian people, places, or conditions are involved?
- What material issue originated the wider connection?
- Whose testimony is being treated as authoritative?
- Are Palestinians present only as victims?
- Are Palestinian political demands visible?
- Which differences among Palestinians need to be preserved?
- Has humanitarian language displaced rights and agency?
- Has a domestic institution become the main character?
- Is solidarity being described primarily through the identity of the solidarists?
- Are Jewish voices being asked to authenticate Palestinian claims?
- Is Palestinian wrongdoing being examined specifically or converted into collective liability?
- Can the reader trace every wider route back to a concrete Palestinian reality?
- What would meaningful change look like to Palestinians most directly affected?
- What has the analysis made visible, and whom has it made disappear?

---

## 🌿 Working Principle

The rhizome may spread through many institutions, identities, professions, and political struggles.

But the purpose of tracing those roots is to understand how they connect to Palestinian life—not to replace Palestinian life with the story of everyone who has become connected to it.
