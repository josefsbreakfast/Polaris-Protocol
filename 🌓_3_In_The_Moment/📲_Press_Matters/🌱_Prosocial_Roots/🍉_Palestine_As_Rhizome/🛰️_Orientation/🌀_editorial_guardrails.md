# 🌀 Editorial Guardrails  
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-17  
*Operational standards for reporting, drafting, comparison, representation, and harm reduction across the Palestine As Rhizome pack.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node establishes the editorial guardrails for **🍉 Palestine As Rhizome**.

The pack traces how Palestine connects to:

- public institutions;
- legal systems;
- professional duties;
- protest movements;
- media practice;
- Jewish and Muslim communal life;
- anti-racist and anti-war organising;
- policing;
- surveillance;
- arms production;
- public procurement;
- and democratic legitimacy.

These connections create analytical value.

They also create risks.

A connected model can become:

- so broad that Palestine disappears;
- so symbolic that Palestinian people become evidence in somebody else’s argument;
- so defensive that legitimate scrutiny is avoided;
- so careless that distinct histories collapse into one another;
- or so focused on controversy that the originating harm becomes background scenery.

The purpose of these guardrails is not to make the work timid.

It is to make the work accurate enough to remain useful under pressure.

The pack should be capable of naming:

- mass harm;
- displacement;
- state violence;
- antisemitism;
- anti-Palestinian racism;
- Islamophobia;
- movement failure;
- political manipulation;
- institutional inconsistency;
- protest misconduct;
- and abusive uses of security language

without turning any population into a permanent suspect class.

---

## 🇵🇸 Keep Palestine And Palestinians Visible

Every node should remain connected to a concrete Palestinian reality.

This may include:

- life and death;
- land;
- homes;
- displacement;
- detention;
- movement;
- healthcare;
- food and water;
- family separation;
- cultural continuity;
- political status;
- representation;
- return;
- rights;
- and collective agency.

Domestic institutional disputes matter because they affect, respond to, or arise from those conditions.

They should not replace them.

A node requires recentering when Palestine functions mainly as:

- the occasion for a British political dispute;
- a symbol of institutional hypocrisy;
- a test of Jewish or Muslim identity;
- a backdrop to protest policing;
- evidence of media failure;
- or a stage on which solidarists demonstrate their own morality.

The wider route should always remain traceable.

Ask:

> What Palestinian person, place, condition, right, or political claim does this analysis ultimately concern?

If that question cannot be answered, the node may have travelled too far from its subject.

## 🧍 People Before Proof

Do not treat Palestinian people primarily as:

- casualty figures;
- images of suffering;
- symbols of innocence;
- symbols of resistance;
- moral tests;
- or evidence used to authenticate another argument.

Children should not become the only publicly grievable Palestinians.

Adults should not have to demonstrate perfect innocence before their lives are treated as protectable.

Palestinians should be visible as:

- witnesses;
- analysts;
- professionals;
- organisers;
- workers;
- family members;
- political thinkers;
- cultural participants;
- and people with ordinary, complex lives.

Testimony should include more than pain.

It may include:

- history;
- accusation;
- political analysis;
- humour;
- disagreement;
- refusal;
- strategy;
- memory;
- and demands for the future.

A person is not more real because their suffering has become useful to an outside audience.

## 🔎 Separate Claim, Evidence, Inference, And Finding

Use clear distinctions between:

### Claim

Something asserted by:

- a witness;
- campaign;
- official;
- institution;
- political actor;
- organisation;
- or media source.

### Evidence

Material that supports, complicates, or contradicts a claim.

This may include:

- documents;
- images;
- testimony;
- data;
- contracts;
- official records;
- forensic material;
- or contemporaneous reporting.

### Inference

A conclusion drawn from available evidence that has not itself been directly established.

Inference should be identified as inference.

### Finding

A conclusion formally reached by a competent body, such as:

- a court;
- tribunal;
- inquiry;
- regulator;
- investigatory mechanism;
- or recognised fact-finding process.

A finding should still be described with:

- its date;
- scope;
- jurisdiction;
- evidential basis;
- and any relevant limits or later developments.

Do not allow repeated assertion to become apparent corroboration.

Do not convert absence of public evidence into proof that no evidence exists.

Do not present uncertainty as equivalence between a well-supported account and an unsupported denial.

---

## 🗓️ Date Claims And Preserve Sequence

Palestine coverage changes quickly.

Every time-sensitive claim should be dated.

This includes:

- casualty figures;
- displacement estimates;
- legal proceedings;
- diplomatic positions;
- aid access;
- ceasefire proposals;
- sanctions;
- arms-export decisions;
- protest restrictions;
- organisational status;
- and official terminology.

Preserve sequence.

A statement made before new evidence, a legal ruling, or a policy change should not be presented as though it responded to later developments.

Ask:

- What was known at the time?
- What changed afterwards?
- Which claims were corrected?
- Which institutional positions remained unchanged despite new information?
- and which current descriptions rely on outdated facts?

Temporal hygiene is especially important where a movement is accused of ignoring changing circumstances.

The reporting should establish whether circumstances actually resolved the underlying issue.

---

## 🏷️ Attribute Contested Language

Terms such as:

- terrorism;
- extremism;
- resistance;
- self-defence;
- genocide;
- apartheid;
- occupation;
- pogrom;
- riot;
- intimidation;
- neutrality;
- security;
- hate;
- radicalisation;
- and public order

may have legal, scholarly, institutional, political, or rhetorical meanings.

Do not allow contested terms to enter the text as neutral description without examination.

Where relevant, identify:

- who is using the term;
- which definition they are applying;
- whether the term has a specific legal status;
- what conduct it includes;
- what conduct it excludes;
- and what consequence follows from accepting the label.

Prefer precise descriptions of conduct where a broad label would obscure more than it reveals.

For example, identify:

- what was said;
- what was done;
- who was targeted;
- what evidence exists;
- which law or rule is engaged;
- and what harm followed.

A politically powerful word should not be asked to perform the whole analysis.

---

## 🥁 Represent Protest Proportionately

Do not infer the character of a whole movement from:

- one sign;
- one slogan;
- one speaker;
- one arrest;
- one organisation;
- one social-media post;
- or one inflammatory incident.

Equally, do not erase harmful conduct merely because it is unrepresentative.

Distinguish among:

- organisers;
- affiliated organisations;
- regular participants;
- occasional attendees;
- speakers;
- observers;
- counter-protesters;
- unaffiliated actors;
- and people exploiting the event for another purpose.

Ask:

- How common was the conduct?
- Was it endorsed by organisers?
- Was it challenged?
- Was it visible before reporting selected it?
- Did it affect others’ safety or participation?
- and what proportion of the event does it reasonably represent?

Crowd size, disruption, arrest, and spectacle should not replace motive.

A protest report should explain:

- what people were responding to;
- what remained unresolved;
- what demands were being made;
- and why participants believed marching was still necessary.

---

## 🧭 Distinguish Movement, Network, Organisation, And Population

Do not use these categories interchangeably.

### Population

A people or demographic group.

Palestinians, Jews, Muslims, Israelis, students, and residents are not organisations.

### Movement

A broad field of political participation that may contain multiple organisations, tendencies, and unaffiliated people.

### Network

A set of relationships through which information, people, resources, or strategies travel.

### Organisation

A defined body with:

- membership;
- leadership;
- governance;
- funding;
- public positions;
- or formal responsibility.

### Event

A particular march, meeting, encampment, occupation, vigil, or action.

Responsibility should follow the structure that actually exists.

Do not assign the conduct of one organisation to an entire population.

Do not assume that every attendee endorses every organiser.

Do not imply that decentralisation eliminates leadership, funding, influence, or accountability.

Map the relationship rather than inventing one.

## 🪢 Treat Antisemitism Seriously And Specifically

Antisemitism can occur:

- within solidarity movements;
- around protests;
- in political rhetoric;
- through conspiracy;
- through collective blame;
- through harassment;
- through historical distortion;
- or through attacks on Jewish people and institutions.

It should not be minimised because acknowledging it is politically inconvenient.

At the same time, allegations of antisemitism should be examined rather than treated as self-proving.

Ask:

- What was said or done?
- Who was targeted?
- Was Jewish identity, ancestry, religion, or collective culpability invoked?
- Was a political institution or ideology being criticised?
- Was the criticism expressed through antisemitic imagery, conspiracy, or collective blame?
- What definition is being used?
- What context changes the meaning?
- and what institutional action is being justified through the allegation?

Do not make Jewish safety conditional on political agreement.

Do not treat all Jewish people as represented by:

- the Israeli state;
- Zionist organisations;
- anti-Zionist organisations;
- communal leadership bodies;
- or individual Jewish participants.

Jewish plurality should be preserved.

But Jewish debate must not displace Palestinians as the subject of the pack.

---

## 🇵🇸 Treat Anti-Palestinian Racism Seriously And Specifically

Anti-Palestinian racism may appear through:

- denial of Palestinian peoplehood;
- collective suspicion;
- assumptions of inherent violence;
- demands for exceptional proof of innocence;
- exclusion of Palestinian testimony;
- dehumanising language;
- political erasure;
- punishment for Palestinian identity or expression;
- or the treatment of Palestinian rights as uniquely conditional.

It may overlap with:

- Islamophobia;
- anti-Arab racism;
- hostility to refugees;
- counter-terrorism frameworks;
- and wider racialised security practices.

These categories should not be collapsed automatically.

Name the specific form of harm.

Do not treat Palestinian speech as presumptively dangerous because it concerns:

- resistance;
- return;
- displacement;
- Zionism;
- state violence;
- or Palestinian national identity.

At the same time, political speech remains open to scrutiny where it:

- targets civilians;
- promotes collective blame;
- uses antisemitic claims;
- threatens others;
- or supports unlawful harm.

The standard should be conduct and evidence, not identity-based presumption.

---

## ☪️ Do Not Use Islamophobia As A Generic Substitute

Many people involved in Palestine solidarity are Muslim.

Many Palestinians are Muslim.

But Palestine should not be reduced to a Muslim issue.

Palestinians also include:

- Christians;
- people of other religious traditions;
- secular people;
- and people whose political identity cannot be explained through religion alone.

Islamophobia should be named where Muslim identity, practice, communities, or perceived Muslimness materially shapes treatment.

Anti-Arab racism, anti-Palestinian racism, counter-terrorism suspicion, and religious discrimination may overlap.

They are not always identical.

Avoid frameworks that make Muslim participation appear inherently imported, communal, irrational, or security-relevant while treating other political participation as ordinary citizenship.

---

## ✡️ Preserve Jewish Plurality Without Recentering It

Jewish people hold a wide range of positions concerning:

- Zionism;
- anti-Zionism;
- non-Zionism;
- Israel;
- Palestine;
- diaspora;
- religious duty;
- nationalism;
- safety;
- and political representation.

Do not describe “the Jewish community” as though one institution or public figure can establish a complete communal position.

Identify:

- who is speaking;
- whom they represent;
- how representation is established;
- what disagreement exists;
- and whose voices are excluded.

Jewish participants in Palestinian solidarity should not be used primarily as:

- moral permission;
- proof that antisemitism is absent;
- unusual human-interest material;
- or authentication for Palestinian testimony.

Their participation can matter in its own right.

It should not become the condition under which Palestinian claims are considered credible.

---

## 🕸️ Compare Without Collapse

Every comparison should identify:

- the exact feature being compared;
- the type of connection;
- the evidence establishing the route;
- the differences that remain essential;
- and the limited conclusion the comparison supports.

Do not collapse distinct:

- histories;
- legal statuses;
- institutions;
- populations;
- scales of harm;
- or forms of political agency.

Avoid using comparison primarily to add emotional force.

Historical analogy should direct readers towards evidence.

It should not replace evidence.

In particular:

- do not reduce the Holocaust to one image, method, or site;
- do not require contemporary harm to reproduce an earlier atrocity brick for brick before it becomes serious;
- do not treat the presence of one shared mechanism as proof of total equivalence;
- and do not turn suffering into competitive moral ownership.

Connection identifies a route.

It does not erase the ground on either side.

---

## ⚖️ Do Not Invent Symmetry

Fairness does not require every story to contain matching halves.

Connected harms may differ in:

- power;
- scale;
- legal status;
- institutional reach;
- material consequence;
- and capacity for enforcement.

Do not balance:

- state policy against an individual slogan;
- military power against protest disruption;
- institutional repression against interpersonal discomfort;
- or mass harm against reputational difficulty

as though the categories are automatically equivalent.

This does not mean that harm by less powerful actors should be ignored.

It means each action should be assessed according to:

- what occurred;
- who acted;
- who was affected;
- which power was exercised;
- and what consequences followed.

Consistency of standards is not the same as symmetry of presentation.

---

## 🗣️ Preserve Disagreement Without Manufacturing Confusion

Palestinian solidarity contains real disagreement.

Participants may differ over:

- political settlement;
- Zionism;
- nationalism;
- armed resistance;
- sanctions;
- electoral strategy;
- protest tactics;
- language;
- religion;
- and relations with existing institutions.

Do not treat plurality as proof that the movement has no coherent concern.

Do not conceal disagreement in order to present false unity.

Identify:

- the minimum shared centre;
- the major points of dispute;
- which positions are dominant;
- which are marginal;
- and which questions remain unresolved.

Avoid false balance between:

- evidence and denial;
- legal finding and unsupported assertion;
- or widespread conduct and an isolated anecdote.

Disagreement is information.

It should be mapped, not manufactured.

---

## 🧠 Explanation Is Not Endorsement

Explaining why a slogan, tactic, political position, or organisation exists does not require endorsing it.

Likewise, condemnation does not remove the need for explanation.

A useful account may examine:

- historical conditions;
- material incentives;
- political failures;
- institutional repression;
- collective memory;
- grief;
- fear;
- ideology;
- and strategic calculation.

These factors can help explain conduct without erasing:

- agency;
- responsibility;
- legal accountability;
- or moral judgement.

Do not use context to absolve.

Do not use condemnation to avoid context.

Prevention and public understanding require both.

---

## 📸 Use Images Without Consuming Suffering

Images can establish:

- scale;
- damage;
- public participation;
- institutional conduct;
- and human consequence.

They can also expose, humiliate, endanger, or consume the people depicted.

Before using an image, consider:

- dignity;
- consent;
- identification risk;
- retraumatisation;
- family wishes;
- cultural and religious context;
- repetition;
- age;
- graphic content;
- and whether the image contributes information beyond emotional force.

Do not use:

- injured bodies;
- dead children;
- grieving families;
- detainees;
- arrests;
- or distressed protesters

as decorative proof.

Do not assume that an image is ethically available merely because it circulates widely.

Where possible, explain:

- who created it;
- when;
- where;
- under what conditions;
- and what has been independently verified.

The most visually dramatic material should not automatically determine the entire frame.

---

## 🧾 Numbers Require Context

Casualty figures, crowd estimates, arrests, aid volumes, polling, and financial totals should be:

- sourced;
- dated;
- attributed;
- and explained.

Identify:

- who collected the figure;
- what definition was used;
- what period it covers;
- which population is included;
- what uncertainty exists;
- and whether later revisions occurred.

Do not imply precision that the source cannot support.

Do not discard all figures merely because perfect verification is impossible.

Where sources differ, explain:

- why;
- how significantly;
- and what remains clear across the estimates.

Numbers should clarify human and institutional conditions.

They should not replace them.

---

## 🛡️ Examine Security Claims Rather Than Assuming Them

Security concerns may be real.

They may involve:

- threats;
- harassment;
- violence;
- weapons;
- intimidation;
- targeting;
- public-order risk;
- or risks to particular communities and institutions.

Do not dismiss a concern merely because authorities, political opponents, or unpopular organisations have raised it.

But security language should not be treated as self-justifying.

Ask:

- What specific threat is alleged?
- What evidence supports it?
- Who is at risk?
- How likely and severe is the harm?
- What intervention is proposed?
- Is it lawful and proportionate?
- Is it applied consistently?
- What less restrictive alternatives exist?
- What new harms might the response create?
- and how will the intervention be reviewed?

A broad claim of safety should not conceal:

- collective suspicion;
- political censorship;
- discriminatory enforcement;
- or the removal of legitimate routes for participation.

---

## 🌱 Treat The Response As Part Of The System

The response to protest, speech, or organising is not merely the end of the story.

It may become a new cause.

Track whether institutional action produces:

- humiliation;
- exclusion;
- fear;
- legal disputes;
- professional discipline;
- financial harm;
- public distrust;
- political withdrawal;
- wider mobilisation;
- or new solidarity networks.

This does not mean every restriction strengthens a movement.

Repression can exhaust, isolate, injure, and silence people.

But removal should not be mistaken for resolution.

Ask:

- Did the response reduce danger?
- Did it displace the activity?
- Did it create a new grievance?
- Did it widen the constituency?
- Did it make lawful participation appear futile?
- and did it preserve a credible route back into democratic life?

---

## 🏛️ Name Institutions And Decisions Precisely

Avoid vague formulations such as:

- “the authorities”;
- “the community”;
- “the media”;
- “universities”;
- “Jewish leaders”;
- “pro-Palestine groups”;
- or “security officials”

when a more precise actor can be identified.

Name:

- the institution;
- office;
- organisation;
- decision-maker;
- policy;
- date;
- jurisdiction;
- and scope of authority.

Distinguish between:

- government;
- police;
- prosecution;
- court;
- regulator;
- charity;
- university management;
- student body;
- political party;
- broadcaster;
- campaign;
- and informal network.

Precision prevents responsibility from dissolving into atmosphere.

---

## 💷 Follow Material Relationships

Where a node concerns:

- arms;
- investment;
- procurement;
- lobbying;
- insurance;
- donations;
- taxation;
- pensions;
- trade;
- or charitable funding,

show the route.

Identify:

- the parties;
- the transaction;
- the value where known;
- the time period;
- the relevant decision;
- the benefit;
- the exposure;
- and the claimed or documented consequence.

Do not imply financial connection merely from social or ideological proximity.

Do not treat indirect connection as direct control.

Do not describe funding as suspicious solely because it is foreign, communal, political, or charitable.

Follow the relationship carefully enough that the reader can see what is established and what remains inference.

---

## 🧑‍⚕️ Respect Professional Duties And Limits

Doctors, journalists, lawyers, academics, civil servants, teachers, artists, charity workers, and other professionals may encounter Palestine through their work.

Reporting should distinguish:

- professional obligation;
- personal political expression;
- institutional policy;
- regulatory requirement;
- protected conscience;
- employment restriction;
- and conduct outside professional scope.

Do not imply that professional identity requires political silence.

Do not imply that political commitment excuses breaches of professional duty.

Ask:

- What duty applies?
- To whom is it owed?
- Which rule governs it?
- Was the rule applied consistently?
- What evidence of harm exists?
- and is disciplinary action proportionate?

Professional ethics should not be converted into a vague demand for neutrality.

Neutrality itself may require definition.

---

## 🧱 Do Not Make Rights Conditional On Perfect Representation

Civilian protection, political rights, equality before law, and access to humanitarian relief should not depend on a population producing:

- flawless leaders;
- perfect slogans;
- ideal representatives;
- universally acceptable protest tactics;
- or complete internal unity.

Specific wrongdoing requires specific scrutiny.

It should not create permanent collective liability.

Likewise, criticism of collective punishment should not require denial of:

- violence;
- antisemitism;
- coercion;
- political repression;
- or harmful conduct by Palestinian organisations and actors.

Rights do not depend on romanticisation.

Accountability does not require dehumanisation.

---

## 🚫 Avoid The Permanent Suspect Class

Do not frame an entire population as presumptively:

- dangerous;
- disloyal;
- extremist;
- deceptive;
- foreign;
- collectively responsible;
- or incapable of ordinary democratic participation.

This applies to:

- Palestinians;
- Jews;
- Muslims;
- Israelis;
- Arabs;
- protesters;
- students;
- refugees;
- and other groups drawn into the field.

Risk assessment should be:

- behaviour-specific;
- evidence-based;
- proportionate;
- reviewable;
- and attentive to institutional bias.

A reporting frame can widen the field of suspicion even when it avoids explicit slurs.

Watch for assumptions embedded in:

- sourcing;
- headline language;
- image choice;
- demands for condemnation;
- definitions of neutrality;
- and whose fear receives immediate credibility.

---

## 🧭 Preserve Routes Back Into Democratic Life

A prosocial reporting framework should remain firm about harm.

It should also ask whether institutions preserve legitimate routes for:

- protest;
- complaint;
- legal challenge;
- professional dissent;
- political participation;
- public mourning;
- community safety;
- and policy change.

Where these routes fail, people may conclude that:

- lawful participation is futile;
- institutions recognise only pressure;
- public rules are applied selectively;
- or belonging is conditional on silence.

Do not confuse social cohesion with the suppression of visible disagreement.

Cohesion requires enough legitimacy for disagreement to remain inside public and democratic life.

## ✅ Pre-Publication Check

Before publishing or finalising a node, ask:

### Palestinian Centrality

- Are Palestinians present as people and political actors?
- Is the originating Palestinian condition clearly identified?
- Are land, material conditions, rights, or political claims visible?
- Has a domestic institution become the main character?

### Evidence

- Are claims, evidence, inference, and findings distinguished?
- Are time-sensitive facts dated?
- Have repeated assertions been mistaken for independent corroboration?
- Are uncertainties stated proportionately?

### Language

- Are contested labels attributed?
- Is conduct described precisely?
- Have collective nouns created false uniformity?
- Has passive language concealed responsibility?

### Protest

- Is one incident being used to characterise a whole movement?
- Has harmful conduct been examined without exaggerating its prevalence?
- Are motive and unresolved demands explained?
- Have organisers, participants, populations, and organisations been distinguished?

### Racism And Communal Harm

- Has antisemitism been treated seriously and specifically?
- Has anti-Palestinian racism been treated seriously and specifically?
- Has Islamophobia been identified where relevant rather than used generically?
- Has Jewish plurality been preserved without displacing Palestinians?
- Is any population being turned into a permanent suspect class?

### Comparison

- What exact feature is being compared?
- What evidence establishes the connection?
- Which differences remain essential?
- Is analogy clarifying the issue or adding emotional force?
- Has historical specificity been preserved?

### Security And Institutional Response

- Is the security claim supported by evidence?
- Is the response lawful, necessary, and proportionate?
- Are comparable cases treated consistently?
- Could the response generate additional harm or new roots of mobilisation?
- Is a route back into legitimate participation being preserved?

### Images And Human Dignity

- Does the image add information?
- Are dignity, consent, and identification risk considered?
- Is suffering being consumed as spectacle?
- Has the most dramatic image distorted the wider account?

### Final Test

- What will this framing make easier to believe?
- What will it make easier to justify?
- What will it make easier to ignore?
- Who becomes more human through this account?
- Who risks disappearing?

---

## 🌿 Working Principle

The work should be able to name harm without widening collective suspicion, trace connection without collapsing history, and scrutinise solidarity without allowing the scrutiny to replace Palestine itself.

Accuracy is not distance from moral consequence.

It is the discipline required to describe that consequence without reproducing it.
