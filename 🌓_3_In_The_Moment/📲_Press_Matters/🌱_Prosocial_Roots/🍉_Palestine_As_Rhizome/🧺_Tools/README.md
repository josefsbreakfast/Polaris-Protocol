# 🧺 Tools  
**First created:** 2026-08-02 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-02  
*Practical methods for reconstructing events, testing claims, mapping institutions, describing movements, following outcomes, assessing effects, and preserving uncertainty.*

## 🛰️ Orientation

Good analysis requires more than the right political question.

It requires a method that preserves:

- actors;
- evidence;
- time;
- authority;
- uncertainty;
- procedure;
- consequence;
- and correction.

This cluster is the practical methods layer for **🍉 Palestine As Rhizome**.

Neighbouring clusters explain what to analyse.

This cluster helps the reader do the work.

It provides reusable structures for:

- reconstructing events;
- testing claims;
- recording sources;
- mapping institutional decisions;
- comparing principles;
- describing movements;
- following procedural outcomes;
- assessing repression effects;
- tracking correction;
- and governing coalition work.

Its governing proposition is:

> Good analysis requires more than the right political question. It requires a method that preserves actors, evidence, time, authority, uncertainty, and consequence.

The central question is:

> What is the smallest reliable tool capable of answering the question without creating unnecessary risk?

Structure improves discipline.

It does not manufacture certainty.

## 🧭 The Cluster Boundary

This cluster contains practical instruments.

It does not replace:

- reporting;
- legal analysis;
- historical interpretation;
- professional judgment;
- political reasoning;
- or direct evidence.

A worksheet may make assumptions visible.

It cannot make weak evidence strong.

A completed field is not proof that the proposition named by the field is true.

This cluster should not become:

- a loose list of generic journalism tips;
- a replacement for evidence;
- a data-hoarding exercise;
- an intelligence dossier;
- a speculative network-mapping system;
- a collection of unexplained templates;
- or a promise that structured tables remove political judgment.

It routes readers toward appropriate methods drawn from the wider branch.

## 🧱 Core Distinctions

### Record Is Not Finding

A worksheet records material.

It does not automatically establish the conclusion suggested by the pattern.

### Field Is Not Fact

A table heading may ask whether coordination existed.

Completing the field does not prove that it did.

### Unknown Is Not Empty

Unknown information should be recorded as unknown, not silently omitted or guessed.

### Source Is Not Verification

A source log records who made the claim.

Verification requires separate assessment.

### Precision Is Not Accuracy

A detailed timestamp, label, or classification may still be wrong.

### Pattern Is Not Causation

Repeated timing or similarity may justify investigation without proving one cause.

### Comparison Is Not Equivalence

A matrix tests relevant similarities and differences.

It should not collapse whole histories.

### Public Is Not Harmless

Publicly accessible information can become harmful when aggregated.

### Completeness Is Not Safety

A more complete actor map may expose vulnerable people without improving public-interest analysis.

### Tool Output Is Not Publication Copy

Working notes may contain:

- unresolved allegations;
- personal data;
- uncertainty;
- source-sensitive material;
- or speculative questions

that should not be published directly.

### Structure Is Not Neutrality

A template reflects choices about:

- what matters;
- what is counted;
- what remains visible;
- and which relationships are considered relevant.

Those choices should be explicit.

## 🧭 Choose The Tool By The Question

### “What exactly is being claimed?”

Use a **claim, source, and confidence register**.

### “What happened, and in what order?”

Use an **event timeline and reconstruction sheet**.

### “Who possessed which power?”

Use an **actor, authority, and institutional-chain map**.

### “What procedural stage has been reached?”

Use a **procedural-status tracker**.

### “Was the same principle applied consistently?”

Use a **comparator and consistency matrix**.

### “Who or what does this person represent?”

Use a **movement actor and mandate map**.

### “How representative was this incident?”

Use an **incident-attribution test**.

### “What happened after the headline?”

Use a **follow-up and correction register**.

### “Did a correction reach every downstream record?”

Use a **correction-propagation audit**.

### “What did institutional pressure produce?”

Use a **repression-effects map**.

### “Can this coalition act together safely?”

Use a **coalition floor, centre, ceiling, and conduct agreement**.

The tool should follow the question.

The question should not be reshaped merely because a familiar template is available.

## 🧪 Confidence And Status

Confidence and status should be kept separate.

### Confirmed

Supported by direct, reliable evidence.

### Strongly Supported

Multiple credible sources or records point in the same direction.

### Reported

Attributed to a named source but not independently verified.

### Inferred

Reasoned from evidence but not directly established.

### Unclear

The available information is incomplete or contradictory.

### Disputed

Credible sources provide materially different accounts.

Procedural status may be certain while the underlying allegation remains unproven.

For example:

- procedural status: `arrested`;
- confidence: `confirmed`;
- underlying allegation: `reported`;
- guilt: `not established`.

This prevents procedural certainty from becoming substantive certainty.

A confidence label should never replace the evidence note.

## ⬜ Unknown-Value Discipline

Blank fields are dangerous.

A blank cell may mean:

- not applicable;
- not checked;
- unavailable;
- withheld;
- disputed;
- pending;
- or genuinely unknown.

Use explicit values.

Recommended values include:

- `unknown`
- `not yet checked`
- `not publicly available`
- `not applicable`
- `withheld`
- `disputed`
- `pending`
- `no evidence found`
- `source inaccessible`
- `requires follow-up`

Absence should be interpretable.

Unknown should not be converted into a negative finding.

`No evidence found` means the search did not identify evidence.

It does not always mean the underlying event or relationship did not exist.

## 🔗 Source Discipline

A source register should preserve:

- who produced the material;
- when it was produced;
- whether it is primary or secondary;
- whether it directly supports the claim;
- whether the source has been independently checked;
- whether the material is complete;
- and whether a later correction exists.

Useful source types may include:

- official record;
- court document;
- policy;
- direct statement;
- interview;
- contemporaneous image or video;
- professional record;
- organisational publication;
- reputable reporting;
- participant account;
- commentary;
- or anonymous claim.

These categories are not automatic rankings.

A direct source may still be:

- mistaken;
- partial;
- self-interested;
- or strategically framed.

A secondary source may provide necessary verification or context.

The tool should record the source’s relationship to the claim rather than relying only on prestige.

## 🔒 Privacy And Anti-Targeting Rules

This section governs the entire cluster.

Collect only what is necessary.

Prefer:

- organisational;
- institutional;
- role-based;
- and decision-based mapping

over personal mapping.

Avoid unnecessary collection or publication of:

- private contact details;
- home addresses;
- personal locations;
- vulnerable identities;
- immigration status;
- health data;
- informal contact chains;
- private relationship data;
- security practices;
- or speculative identity classifications.

Distinguish:

- public-interest accountability;
- source verification;
- working context;
- and curiosity.

Do not collect a person merely because they can be found.

Record them only where their identity is necessary to understand:

- authority;
- responsibility;
- evidence;
- direct harm;
- or a public role.

Consider aggregation risk.

One public fact may be harmless.

A combined dataset of:

- identity;
- location;
- affiliation;
- employment;
- and recurring attendance

may expose a person to serious risk.

Separate:

- private working notes;
- publication-ready material;
- source-protected material;
- and records requiring deletion.

Movement legibility should not become an operational targeting aid.

---

## 🗃️ Retention And Deletion

A tool should not create indefinite data retention by default.

Before collecting personal or sensitive information, identify:

- why it is needed;
- who may access it;
- how long it should be retained;
- whether it can be minimised;
- whether identifiers can be removed;
- and what event should trigger deletion.

Delete or anonymise material that:

- no longer serves the defined question;
- creates unnecessary exposure;
- duplicates safer organisational data;
- or cannot be responsibly verified.

Preservation may be necessary for:

- legal records;
- public accountability;
- source protection;
- correction;
- or historical documentation.

That need should be stated rather than assumed.

---

## 📝 Publication Review

Before converting a tool output into prose, ask:

- Has every allegation retained its status?
- Are confidence labels visible where needed?
- Have unknowns been preserved?
- Is chronology being mistaken for causation?
- Is one actor being made representative of a wider group?
- Are procedural actions being mistaken for findings?
- Are private people exposed unnecessarily?
- Are corrections included?
- Does the prose go farther than the worksheet supports?
- Have contrary sources been recorded?
- Is the source complete?
- Has Palestinian material reality remained visible?
- Could the table itself create harm if published?
- Does the audience need the raw data, a summary, or only the conclusion?
- Has obsolete or disproven material been removed?

The publication layer should usually be narrower than the working layer.

## 🧭 Minimum Viable Tool

The best tool is not always the most elaborate.

Use the smallest structure capable of preserving the key facts.

A minimum viable record may contain:

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Question | What are we trying to establish? |
| Claim | What is being asserted? |
| Source | Who or what supports it? |
| Status | Confirmed, reported, inferred, disputed, unclear |
| Actor | Who acted or spoke? |
| Authority | What role or power did they possess? |
| Date | When did the event or statement occur? |
| Consequence | What immediately followed? |
| Unknowns | What remains unresolved? |
| Follow-up | What should be checked next? |
| Privacy | What should not be published? |

A short reliable note is better than a large speculative table.

## 🔄 Moving Material Between Tools

One item may pass through several tools.

For example:

```text
claim register
      ↓
event timeline
      ↓
institutional chain
      ↓
procedural-status tracker
      ↓
follow-up register
      ↓
correction-propagation audit
      ↓
effects map
```

The item should retain:

- its identifier;
- original source;
- confidence;
- status;
- date;
- and uncertainty

as it moves.

Do not silently upgrade:

- reported to confirmed;
- associated to affiliated;
- arrested to guilty;
- present to representative;
- sequential to causal;
- or corrected to repaired.

## 🧿 Common Failure Modes

### Template Substitution

The form replaces substantive investigation.

### Field Inflation

A field is treated as important merely because the template contains it.

### False Completion

A completed table creates the appearance of certainty.

### Silent Guessing

Unknown information is filled from assumption.

### Status Collapse

Arrest, charge, finding, guilt, and outcome are merged.

### Actor Collapse

Participant, organiser, speaker, organisation, coalition, and movement are merged.

### Causal Collapse

Sequence, association, contribution, and direct cause are merged.

### Comparator Collapse

Cases are treated as equivalent because they share moral importance.

### Record Hoarding

Information is retained without a continuing analytical need.

### Aggregation Harm

Individually public facts are combined into a dangerous personal profile.

### Publication Leakage

Private working notes are published without review.

### Correction Failure

A later outcome is recorded locally but not propagated into summaries, timelines, or downstream records.

## ❓ Working Questions

Before using a tool, ask:

- What precise question am I trying to answer?
- What is the smallest suitable method?
- What conclusion can this tool support?
- What conclusion can it not support?
- Which fields are genuinely required?
- What is the source for each entry?
- Is the claim direct, indirect, reported, inferred, disputed, or unknown?
- Have status and confidence been separated?
- Have blank fields been explained?
- Is the timestamp the event time, publication time, or observation time?
- Am I mapping an actor, an organisation, a relationship, or a hypothesis?
- Does the actor possess relevant authority?
- Is a comparator actually comparable?
- Am I treating sequence as causation?
- What contrary evidence exists?
- What follow-up is required?
- Does a later correction alter earlier entries?
- Which downstream records require repair?
- Am I collecting personal information unnecessarily?
- Could aggregation create risk?
- Should part of the record remain private?
- When should the data be deleted or anonymised?
- Does the final prose go farther than the tool supports?
- Has Palestinian life, rights, material condition, and political agency remained visible?

## 🧿 Editorial Guardrails

Do not use templates as substitutes for judgment.

Do not collect information without a defined question.

Do not build speculative network maps.

Do not turn uncertainty into blank space.

Do not convert attributed claims into verified facts.

Do not treat procedural action as guilt.

Do not use precise formatting to disguise weak evidence.

Do not treat all fields as equally important.

Do not force incomparable cases into one matrix.

Do not convert public data into a targeting resource.

Do not retain sensitive personal information indefinitely.

Do not publish raw working notes without review.

Do not infer identity from appearance, name, dress, location, or politics.

Do not treat one source as independent confirmation of itself through repetition.

Do not let confidence labels replace explanation.

Do not describe a correction as repair unless the practical and downstream consequences were addressed.

Do not make the tool more complicated than the question requires.

The best tool is sometimes a short note explaining that the evidence is insufficient.

## 🌿 Working Principle

Ask a defined question.

Choose the smallest tool capable of answering it.

Preserve:

- the source;
- the status;
- the actor;
- the authority;
- the time;
- the uncertainty;
- the consequence;
- and the correction.

Collect no more personal information than the analysis requires.

Stop where the evidence stops.

Structure the question.

Preserve the uncertainty.

Follow the record.

Correct downstream.

Map power without mapping people for harm.

A tool should make overclaiming harder, not merely make the claim look organised.
