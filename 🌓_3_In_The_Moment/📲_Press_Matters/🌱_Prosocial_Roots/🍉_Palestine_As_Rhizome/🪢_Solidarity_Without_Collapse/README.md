# 🪢 Solidarity Without Collapse  
**First created:** 2026-08-01 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-01  
*How people can act together for Palestine without pretending to share one identity, one ideology, one organisation, one history, or one political settlement.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Solidarity does not require sameness.

Coalition does not require silence.

Accountability does not require collective blame.

Palestinian centrality does not require Palestinian uniformity.

People enter Palestinian solidarity through different:

- identities;
- histories;
- moral traditions;
- institutions;
- professions;
- political commitments;
- and experiences of recognition.

They may agree that:

- civilians require protection;
- humanitarian access must be sustained;
- collective punishment is unacceptable;
- international law should be applied;
- arms relationships require scrutiny;
- political expression must remain possible;
- or Palestinian people possess rights and political agency.

They may disagree about:

- Zionism;
- anti-Zionism;
- statehood;
- one-state or two-state arrangements;
- armed resistance;
- boycott strategy;
- electoral engagement;
- religious meaning;
- organisational alliances;
- rhetoric;
- protest tactics;
- and the appropriate long-term settlement.

A functioning coalition does not erase those differences.

It identifies:

- what is shared;
- what remains unresolved;
- what participants retain autonomously;
- what conduct is incompatible with participation;
- how responsibility is attributed;
- and how disagreement can be repaired, bounded, or separated.

This cluster examines how solidarity can remain broad without collapsing distinct people, histories, identities, organisations, and political positions into one undifferentiated bloc.

Its governing proposition is:

> Solidarity creates shared obligation without requiring shared identity, complete agreement, or ownership of another people’s struggle.

---

## 🪢 What Collapse Means Here

Coalition collapse is not only the breakdown of cooperation.

It also occurs when difference is flattened until participants are treated as interchangeable.

### Historical Collapse

Different histories are treated as the same history.

### Political Collapse

Participation is taken to imply agreement on every demand, tactic, organisation, or political future.

### Organisational Collapse

A network, march, coalition, and individual organisation are treated as one command structure.

### Identity Collapse

Jewish, Muslim, Palestinian, Arab, Christian, queer, disabled, feminist, student, labour, and anti-war participants are treated as fixed political blocs.

### Moral Collapse

One group’s suffering is used to settle another group’s political claims.

### Responsibility Collapse

Responsibility spreads from decision-makers to an entire:

- people;
- religion;
- nationality;
- electorate;
- workforce;
- or diaspora.

### Evidential Collapse

Shared political commitment is treated as permission to lower standards of evidence.

### Strategic Collapse

Agreement on an immediate demand is treated as agreement on every long-term aim or tactic.

### Boundary Collapse

Solidarity is interpreted as requiring tolerance of:

- racism;
- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- anti-Palestinian racism;
- harassment;
- intimidation;
- dehumanisation;
- deliberate misinformation;
- or collective blame.

Solidarity without collapse is not solidarity without limits.

## 🧱 Core Distinctions

### Solidarity Is Not Identity

Supporting another people does not make someone a member or representative of that people.

### Coalition Is Not Consensus

Participants may coordinate around a limited demand without agreeing on the whole political field.

### Shared Demand Is Not Shared Analysis

Two groups may support the same action for different:

- moral;
- legal;
- strategic;
- religious;
- professional;
- or political reasons.

### Participation Is Not Endorsement Of Everything Present

Attending one march or signing one statement does not automatically endorse every:

- speaker;
- slogan;
- organisation;
- incident;
- or political programme.

### Autonomy Is Not Disloyalty

Coalition members may retain their own:

- language;
- traditions;
- memberships;
- campaigns;
- internal debates;
- and red lines.

### Disagreement Is Not Sabotage

Some disagreement is evidence of real political participation rather than coalition failure.

### Boundary Is Not Purity

A coalition may exclude harmful conduct without demanding ideological uniformity.

### Accountability Is Not Collective Blame

Individuals and organisations should answer for their conduct.

Responsibility should not spread automatically to everyone sharing an identity, route, organisation, or event.

### Safety Is Not Comfort

A coalition cannot guarantee freedom from:

- disagreement;
- criticism;
- grief;
- anger;
- political tension;
- or unfamiliar language.

It should protect against:

- harassment;
- threats;
- dehumanisation;
- discrimination;
- coercion;
- and foreseeable exclusion.

### Palestinian Centrality Is Not Palestinian Uniformity

Keeping Palestine central does not mean inventing one authorised Palestinian position.

Palestinian disagreement must remain visible.

## 🧭 Shared Centre, Protected Difference

A durable coalition needs more than goodwill.

It needs a structure.

### Shared Centre

The coalition identifies the limited claims or actions around which coordination occurs.

These may include:

- civilian protection;
- humanitarian access;
- ceasefire;
- opposition to collective punishment;
- legal accountability;
- arms scrutiny;
- freedom of protest;
- protection of Palestinian political agency;
- or a specific institutional demand.

### Protected Difference

The coalition records what participants are not required to agree upon.

This may include:

- political ideology;
- religion;
- national identity;
- final settlement;
- organisational affiliation;
- preferred tactic;
- historical interpretation;
- and wider foreign policy.

### Autonomous Speech

Participants may speak from their own route without claiming to represent the entire coalition.

### Shared Conduct Floor

The coalition establishes minimum standards against:

- racism;
- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- anti-Palestinian racism;
- dehumanisation;
- harassment;
- threats;
- collective blame;
- and deliberate misinformation.

### Attribution Discipline

Statements and actions are attached to the person or body responsible.

### Disagreement Process

The coalition decides how disputes will be:

- raised;
- evidenced;
- heard;
- mediated;
- recorded;
- and, where necessary, separated.

### Exit Without Erasure

A participant may leave or decline an action without their earlier solidarity being rewritten as fraudulent.

## 🎯 Coalition Floor, Centre And Ceiling

A coalition benefits from defining three levels.

### Coalition Floor

The minimum commitments required for participation.

These may include:

- recognition of Palestinian civilian and political rights;
- rejection of collective punishment;
- commitment to evidence;
- opposition to racism and antisemitism;
- opposition to Islamophobia and anti-Palestinian racism;
- and adherence to agreed conduct.

### Coalition Centre

The specific demand or action being pursued together.

This should be clear enough that participants and observers can understand what the coalition is actually doing.

### Coalition Ceiling

The point beyond which the coalition does not claim agreement.

For example, a ceasefire coalition may not possess one shared position on:

- constitutional settlement;
- religious sovereignty;
- long-term state structures;
- every form of resistance;
- or every diplomatic strategy.

The floor prevents ethical collapse.

The centre enables coordinated action.

The ceiling prevents false unity.

## 🗣️ Representation Discipline

Coalitions need clear distinctions among different kinds of speech.

A person may be:

### Speaking Personally

Expressing their own position.

### Speaking From Lived Experience

Describing experience connected to:

- identity;
- family;
- profession;
- religion;
- disability;
- displacement;
- or political participation.

### Speaking For An Organisation

Acting under a defined mandate from a named body.

### Speaking For A Coalition

Communicating an agreed position within the coalition’s authorised scope.

### Reporting Another Group’s Position

Accurately describing what another person or organisation has publicly stated.

### Claiming To Represent A Population

Claiming authority to speak for an entire people, faith, nationality, diaspora, profession, or constituency.

These are not interchangeable.

A Jewish participant may speak as a Jewish participant without representing Jews.

A Palestinian participant may speak from Palestinian experience without representing all Palestinians.

A clinician may speak from medical expertise without defining the coalition’s entire political programme.

An organiser may describe agreed demands without controlling every participant.

A speaker should identify:

- the capacity in which they speak;
- the mandate they possess;
- the limits of that mandate;
- and whose position remains distinct.

## 🧩 Agreement Without Total Consensus

A coalition should distinguish among:

### Shared Commitment

A position participants affirm together.

### Protected Disagreement

A question participants are permitted to hold open while continuing shared action.

### Autonomous Position

A view held by one person or organisation outside the coalition’s mandate.

### Abstention

A participant declines to endorse or join one action without leaving the entire coalition.

### Red Line

A conduct or position considered incompatible with continued participation.

### Unresolved Governance Question

A dispute about process, representation, tactics, access, or decision-making that requires internal resolution.

Not every disagreement needs to be solved.

Some need to be:

- named;
- bounded;
- documented;
- and prevented from being falsely presented as settled.

A coalition becomes brittle when it demands silence.

It becomes incoherent when it cannot identify any shared centre.

## 🧱 Boundaries Without Purity Tests

Coalition discipline requires protection against:

- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- anti-Palestinian racism;
- anti-Arab racism;
- anti-Black racism;
- misogyny;
- homophobia;
- transphobia;
- ableism;
- harassment;
- sexual intimidation;
- threats;
- dehumanisation;
- glorification of civilian harm;
- collective punishment;
- and deliberate fabrication.

But boundaries can also be weaponised.

A complaint should not automatically establish misconduct.

Terms should be:

- defined;
- evidenced;
- contextualised;
- applied consistently;
- proportionate;
- reviewable;
- and open to correction.

A useful boundary process asks:

- What exactly was said or done?
- Who was affected?
- What context matters?
- Was the conduct isolated or repeated?
- Was it authorised, tolerated, or corrected?
- What immediate protection is required?
- What response is proportionate?
- Can the person understand and repair the harm?
- Does the conduct make continued participation unsafe or impossible?

Boundary enforcement should protect coalition integrity without becoming an ideological purge.

## 🪞 Accountability Without Collective Blame

When misconduct occurs, the coalition should identify:

- the actor;
- the conduct;
- the authority they held;
- the organisation involved;
- the people affected;
- the response;
- and the scale of responsibility.

Possible responsible actors may include:

- an individual participant;
- a speaker;
- an organiser;
- a steward;
- a member organisation;
- a coalition governing body;
- or an institution interacting with the coalition.

Responsibility should travel only as far as evidence supports.

One participant’s conduct does not automatically establish:

- the position of the crowd;
- the policy of the organisation;
- the culture of the coalition;
- the view of an identity group;
- or the nature of the movement.

But coalition structures may acquire responsibility where misconduct is:

- authorised;
- repeated;
- protected;
- ignored after warning;
- or structurally enabled.

The correct question is:

> Who is responsible, what authority did they hold, and how far does that responsibility properly travel?

## 🧭 Disagreement, Repair And Exit

Not every coalition conflict can or should be resolved through indefinite discussion.

A reusable process is:

### 1. Identify The Conduct Or Claim

What exactly happened?

### 2. Attribute It Correctly

Who said or did it?

What authority did they possess?

### 3. Identify The Harm

Was the problem:

- factual;
- discriminatory;
- threatening;
- exclusionary;
- organisational;
- strategic;
- procedural;
- or interpersonal?

### 4. Assess Scale And Pattern

Was it:

- isolated;
- repeated;
- authorised;
- tolerated;
- corrected;
- or structurally embedded?

### 5. Request Correction Or Boundary

What change is required?

### 6. Protect Affected Participants

What immediate support, access, separation, or safety is needed?

### 7. Decide The Coalition Consequence

Possible outcomes include:

- clarification;
- correction;
- apology;
- education;
- mediation;
- changed procedure;
- removal from a role;
- suspension;
- organisational separation;
- or exit.

### 8. Avoid Collective Transfer

Do not attribute the incident to everyone sharing the person’s:

- identity;
- religion;
- nationality;
- organisation;
- route;
- or presence at the same event.

Repair is not always possible.

Exit may become necessary where:

- threats continue;
- discrimination is defended;
- fabrication is repeated;
- governance becomes abusive;
- affected participants remain unsafe;
- or the shared centre no longer exists.

A coalition should permit exit without rewriting every earlier contribution as fraudulent.

## 🇵🇸 Palestinian Centrality Without Ownership

Palestinian centrality means that solidarity remains accountable to:

- Palestinian life;
- Palestinian testimony;
- Palestinian history;
- Palestinian land;
- Palestinian rights;
- Palestinian political agency;
- and the material consequences of action.

It does not mean:

- one authorised Palestinian position;
- outside organisations claiming political ownership;
- religious communities converting memory into title;
- humanitarian actors centring their own rescue;
- diasporas making Palestinian history a vehicle for their own identity;
- or coalition partners using Palestine mainly to validate another struggle.

Palestinians may disagree about:

- political leadership;
- armed resistance;
- diplomacy;
- statehood;
- one-state and two-state futures;
- religion;
- secularism;
- return;
- governance;
- international intervention;
- and movement strategy.

Outside solidarity should not require political uniformity as the price of recognition.

The coalition should ask:

- Which Palestinian voices are present?
- Which are absent?
- Who selected them?
- What authority do they claim?
- Are internal disagreements visible?
- Has one faction been treated as the whole people?
- Has outside recognition become control?
- What material consequence will the coalition’s action have for Palestinians?

Support is not ownership.

Centrality is not possession.

## ❓ Working Questions

When building or assessing a coalition, ask:

- What is the shared centre?
- What is the coalition floor?
- What is the coalition ceiling?
- Which disagreements remain protected?
- What lies outside the coalition’s mandate?
- Who may speak for the coalition?
- Who may speak for an organisation?
- Who is speaking personally?
- Is lived experience being mistaken for authority over everyone sharing that identity?
- Is participation being mistaken for endorsement of every speaker, slogan, or tactic?
- What conduct is incompatible with participation?
- Are boundary terms defined?
- Is a complaint being treated as a finding?
- Is the response proportionate and reviewable?
- Who is responsible for the incident?
- What authority did they hold?
- Does responsibility travel farther than the evidence?
- Is one person being made representative of an entire group?
- What repair is being requested?
- What protection do affected participants need?
- Is mediation appropriate?
- Is separation necessary?
- Can someone abstain or leave without being erased?
- Are Palestinian voices present?
- Are Palestinian disagreements visible?
- Has outside solidarity become ownership?
- What material effect will the coalition’s action have?
- Does the coalition preserve evidence, autonomy, difference, and political agency?

## 🧿 Editorial Guardrails

Do not present coalition plurality as incoherence.

Do not present disagreement as proof of bad faith.

Do not demand that marginalised participants perform perfect unity.

Do not treat one person as representative of an identity group.

Do not require Jewish participants to validate Palestinian rights.

Do not require Palestinians to provide one unified political line.

Do not use queer, feminist, disability, religious, or professional concerns as tests of whether Palestinian civilians deserve protection.

Do not treat criticism of an organisation as hostility to an entire people.

Do not excuse real misconduct because the coalition faces repression.

Do not use misconduct to discredit every participant.

Do not replace accountability with public humiliation.

Do not replace repair with indefinite suspicion.

Do not use boundaries as purity tests.

Do not use plurality as an excuse to avoid conduct standards.

Do not assume that attending one event establishes endorsement of everything present.

Do not treat a crowd, movement, coalition, network, and organisation as interchangeable.

Do not spread responsibility from:

- an actor;
- speaker;
- organiser;
- organisation;
- or incident

to an entire:

- religion;
- nationality;
- diaspora;
- profession;
- or movement

without evidence.

Do not let coalition process displace Palestinian life, rights, history, land, safety, and political agency.
