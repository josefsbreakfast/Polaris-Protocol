# 🍉 Palestine As Rhizome  
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-17  
*Reporting frameworks for understanding why Palestine remains a durable site of protest, political identification, institutional dispute, and interconnected public concern.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

**Palestine As Rhizome** examines why people are still marching for Palestine.

It uses the rhizome as an analytical model for understanding how an unresolved struggle has developed multiple connected routes through public life.

Palestine is not being reduced to those connections. Palestinian life, history, testimony, material conditions, and political agency remain central.

The model instead helps explain why the movement cannot be understood as one isolated issue with one root, one constituency, or one route towards political engagement.

The persistence of the movement is sometimes described as:

- fashion;
- ideological obsession;
- imported conflict;
- identity performance;
- organised extremism;
- foreign manipulation;
- or an inability to move on.

These descriptions usually fail because they imagine Palestine as a self-contained issue whose public relevance should naturally diminish over time.

A rhizome does not grow from a single visible trunk. It travels through multiple roots, connections, breaks, and points of emergence.

Palestine now connects to questions including:

- civilian protection;
- international law;
- colonial history;
- racism;
- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- protest rights;
- policing;
- arms exports;
- university governance;
- media credibility;
- humanitarian access;
- religious and diasporic identity;
- workplace speech;
- public procurement;
- surveillance;
- democratic accountability;
- and the consistency of institutions that claim to uphold universal principles.

People may therefore arrive at the same march through very different routes.

They do not need to share one ideology, identity, analysis, or desired political settlement to understand Palestine as unfinished.

---

## 🌱 Why The Rhizome Model Matters

A movement with one root may weaken when that root is removed.

A rhizomatic movement persists because each attempt to contain it may activate another part of the network.

For example:

- attempts to suppress protest can turn a foreign-policy issue into a civil-liberties issue;
- institutional silence can turn humanitarian concern into a crisis of professional ethics;
- inconsistent legal standards can turn solidarity into a question of democratic legitimacy;
- attacks on Palestinian testimony can draw attention to media representation;
- antisemitic incidents can generate demands for safer and more disciplined solidarity;
- accusations directed at Jewish dissenters can open wider arguments about Jewish identity and political representation;
- arms sales can connect protest to taxation, employment, procurement, and public accountability;
- and the treatment of students, workers, doctors, journalists, lawyers, or artists can create new constituencies with their own reasons to remain involved.

The movement is therefore not sustained by one slogan.

It is sustained by an expanding set of unresolved relationships.

## 🗞️ Why This Belongs Under Press Matters

Journalism often asks why Palestine still receives exceptional public attention.

That question becomes misleading when it assumes that attention would naturally disappear if protesters were behaving rationally.

A better reporting question is:

**What remains unresolved, and what has happened since the original protest began?**

Coverage can obscure persistence by:

- repeatedly treating demonstrations as isolated weekend events;
- reporting crowd size without reporting motive;
- searching for one representative ideology;
- privileging the most inflammatory sign or speaker;
- separating protest policing from the reason for the protest;
- framing institutional repression as evidence that the movement itself is dangerous;
- treating changing slogans as incoherence rather than adaptation;
- or assuming that people who continue marching have failed to receive new information.

Protest is itself a form of information.

Its repetition indicates that participants do not believe the underlying matter, or the institutions responsible for addressing it, has reached a legitimate conclusion.

## 🧭 Core Principles

### Persistence Is Evidence

The continuation of protest does not prove that every claim made within the movement is correct.

It does show that the issue has not been socially or politically resolved.

Repeated mobilisation should be examined rather than treated as a behavioural defect.

### One March Can Contain Many Routes

Participants may be motivated by:

- Palestinian family or identity;
- humanitarian concern;
- anti-racism;
- religious conviction;
- Jewish ethical or anti-Zionist commitments;
- professional duties;
- international-law concerns;
- opposition to arms sales;
- experiences of policing;
- mistrust of media coverage;
- student or trade-union politics;
- or a wider belief that democratic institutions apply their principles selectively.

These routes overlap without becoming identical.

### Connection Is Not Equivalence

Palestine may connect to other histories and struggles without those histories becoming interchangeable.

Comparison can identify:

- shared institutions;
- repeated techniques;
- common legal questions;
- similar media frames;
- or transferable forms of solidarity.

It must not erase historical specificity or use one population’s suffering merely as a metaphor for another.

### Solidarity Is Not Uniformity

A durable movement will contain disagreement.

Participants may differ over:

- political settlements;
- Zionism;
- nationalism;
- religion;
- armed resistance;
- sanctions;
- electoral strategy;
- protest tactics;
- language;
- and relations with existing institutions.

The presence of disagreement does not mean the movement has no coherent centre.

That centre may be a narrower refusal to accept continued mass harm, dispossession, impunity, or political abandonment.

### Repression Can Grow The Root System

Restrictions may reduce visibility in one place while producing new activity elsewhere.

A campus encampment may become:

- a legal case;
- a trade-union dispute;
- an academic-freedom campaign;
- a donor-governance question;
- a policing controversy;
- a media story;
- or a professional-regulation dispute.

Suppression does not necessarily eliminate political meaning.

It may redistribute it.

### Institutional Consistency Is Part Of The Issue

People assess Palestine not only through events in Palestine but through the conduct of their own institutions.

They ask:

- Which civilian deaths are grieved?
- Which legal rulings are treated as authoritative?
- Which allegations receive immediate belief?
- Which forms of collective punishment are recognised?
- Which protests are protected?
- Which speech is disciplined?
- Which weapons are supplied?
- Which relationships are disclosed?
- and whether stated universal principles remain universal when politically costly.

A perceived double standard can become a root of mobilisation in its own right.

### Marching Is Also About The Marchers’ Own Society

People may march about events abroad while making claims upon institutions at home.

These claims can concern:

- government policy;
- taxation;
- arms exports;
- policing;
- media standards;
- university governance;
- charitable status;
- workplace rules;
- party discipline;
- public appointments;
- or the meaning of citizenship.

Describing Palestine as merely foreign can conceal the domestic decisions through which the public is already connected to it.

## 🌿 Possible Internal Packs

### 🥁 Why They Are Still Marching

A direct reporting pack addressing:

- unresolved harm;
- continuing political decisions;
- changing participant motivations;
- movement durability;
- and the difference between repetition and stagnation.

### 🕸️ The Root Map

Map the movement’s connected concerns:

- humanitarian;
- legal;
- religious;
- diasporic;
- anti-racist;
- labour;
- student;
- feminist;
- queer;
- medical;
- journalistic;
- environmental;
- economic;
- and anti-war.

The purpose is not to claim that all concerns are the same.

It is to show how they meet.

### 🏛️ The Institutions At Home

Track how Palestine enters domestic public life through:

- Parliament;
- local government;
- policing;
- courts;
- universities;
- schools;
- hospitals;
- charities;
- cultural bodies;
- broadcasters;
- political parties;
- and professional regulators.

### 🗞️ The Protest Reporting Gap

Examine recurring weaknesses in coverage:

- event-based rather than process-based reporting;
- crowd spectacle;
- motive compression;
- selective quotation;
- decontextualised slogans;
- protester typologies;
- absent Palestinian voices;
- and failure to explain why the mobilisation continues.

### ⚖️ Universal Until Tested

Examine claims of inconsistency concerning:

- international law;
- human rights;
- civilian protection;
- freedom of expression;
- protest rights;
- professional ethics;
- and institutional neutrality.

### 🪢 Solidarity Without Collapse

Develop reporting and movement guardrails that allow connections to be drawn without:

- historical flattening;
- antisemitism;
- Islamophobia;
- atrocity comparison as competition;
- appropriation;
- or the erasure of Palestinian political agency.

### 🌱 What Repression Produces

Track how containment can generate new roots through:

- arrests;
- disciplinary proceedings;
- employment consequences;
- censorship;
- surveillance;
- protest restrictions;
- funding pressure;
- and institutional reputational management.

### 🛤️ What Would Allow The Marches To End

Ask what participants would recognise as meaningful movement towards resolution.

This may include:

- an end to immediate mass harm;
- credible humanitarian access;
- accountability;
- policy change;
- cessation of material support;
- recognition of Palestinian rights and political agency;
- protection for public dissent;
- and evidence that institutions have learned rather than merely waited for attention to fade.

## ❓ Working Questions

- What specific events or decisions are people responding to now?
- Which original grievances remain unresolved?
- What new grievances have been created by the response to protest?
- Through which personal, professional, religious, or political route did participants arrive?
- Which domestic institutions connect the public to events in Palestine?
- What do protesters believe conventional political routes have failed to achieve?
- How has the composition of the movement changed?
- What disagreements exist within it?
- What remains its minimum shared centre?
- Which comparisons clarify, and which erase important differences?
- How are antisemitism and Islamophobia being identified and addressed?
- Does coverage explain motive, or merely catalogue conduct?
- Has repression reduced harm, displaced it, or generated new constituencies?
- What conditions would make participants believe that marching was no longer necessary?
- What would a legitimate political conclusion look like to those most directly affected?

## 🧿 Editorial Guardrails

Do not use the rhizome model to make Palestinians disappear beneath the causes attached to them.

Palestinian life, testimony, political agency, history, and material conditions remain central.

Do not imply that every person marching shares the same analysis.

Do not treat the presence of antisemitism as either:

- proof that the entire movement is antisemitic;
- or an inconvenience that must be minimised for strategic reasons.

Do not treat accusations of antisemitism as inherently cynical.

Examine the claim, its evidence, its context, and the institutional use being made of it.

Apply the same discipline to allegations of:

- extremism;
- support for terrorism;
- Islamophobia;
- racism;
- intimidation;
- foreign influence;
- and threats to public order.

Do not confuse the explanation of persistence with endorsement of every tactic, slogan, organisation, or political demand.

Do not reduce Palestinian solidarity to a therapeutic stage in somebody else’s political development.

The connections matter, but Palestine is not merely a screen onto which other societies project their anxieties.

---

## 🌿 Working Principle

People are not still marching because nothing has changed.

They are still marching because harm continues, political responsibility remains active, and each attempt to close the subject has exposed another root through which it enters public life.
