# 💄 The Camden Crew and VAWG

**First created:** 2026-09-06 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-06\
*Why Government's credibility on misogyny depends not only on what it
says about violence against women and girls, but on whether its
principles survive proximity to power.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This is not an argument that:

> `Labour hates women.`

Nor is it an argument that previous governments did nothing about
violence against women and girls.

The more interesting history runs in almost the opposite direction.

Across successive governments, policy has increasingly recognised that
violence against women and girls cannot be understood only as a
collection of isolated offences.

The relevant system includes:

-   prevention;
-   perpetrator behaviour;
-   institutional competence;
-   survivor support;
-   education;
-   safeguarding;
-   policing and prosecution;
-   online environments;
-   the social conditions in which abuse becomes normalised.

By 2025--26, the current Government's own language is unusually
systemic.

Its cross-government VAWG strategy is structured around:

``` text
PREVENTION AND EARLY INTERVENTION

RELENTLESS PURSUIT OF PERPETRATORS

SUPPORT FOR THOSE AFFECTED
```

That is a serious ambition.

It also creates the standard against which Government should be judged.

``` text
Government says:

prevention
pursuit
support
root causes
institutional competence
whole-society response
        ↓
therefore
        ↓
those principles should remain operative
when the difficult case is politically close
to Government itself
```

The 7 September 2026 debate on e-petition 746640 arrives at an awkwardly
useful moment for testing that proposition.

**Question answered:** Why does Government's credibility on misogyny
depend not only on what it says in Monday's debate, but on whether it
applies its principles when accountability becomes politically
inconvenient?

---

## 🗓️ This Did Not Begin in 2026

The current VAWG framework sits at the end of a longer policy
development.

Over time, government and criminal-justice policy has increasingly had
to see connections between:

-   domestic abuse;
-   sexual violence;
-   stalking;
-   harassment;
-   coercive control;
-   exploitation;
-   institutional response;
-   prevention.

The conceptual movement matters:

``` text
individual incident
        ↓
pattern of offending
        ↓
pattern + institutional response
        ↓
prevention + perpetrator behaviour
+ survivor support
```

By the early 2020s, the wider policy environment also included:

-   the statutory domestic-abuse framework;
-   stalking and harassment;
-   sex-based harassment in public;
-   online safety;
-   major questions about policing standards and institutional misogyny;
-   universities and educational environments;
-   growing recognition that online and offline harms can interact.

The state progressively learned to describe **systems**.

That history matters because Petition 746640 is also describing a
system.

---

## 🏛️ The 2025--26 Government Raises the Stakes

The current Government's December 2025 strategy, *Freedom from Violence
and Abuse: a cross-government strategy to build a safer society for
women and girls*, commits to a ten-year programme intended to halve
VAWG.

The Commons Library briefing for the petition debate describes three
primary objectives:

1.  **prevention and early intervention**;
2.  **relentless pursuit of perpetrators**;
3.  **support for those affected by violence and abuse**.

It also records Government's recognition of:

-   entrenched sexist attitudes;
-   misogynistic content online;
-   violent pornography;
-   inadequate tools for parents and teachers;
-   online influences that can contribute to a culture normalising abuse
    and control.

This is important.

Government is not approaching Monday's debate from a position of:

> `We do not accept the petitioners' diagnosis.`

It already accepts a substantial part of the systems analysis.

That makes the next question unavoidable:

> **What does that analysis require of Government itself?**

# 💄 War Paint

A VAWG strategy is easiest to endorse when the perpetrator is safely
elsewhere.

The institutional test comes when:

-   somebody is powerful;
-   somebody is useful;
-   somebody is a colleague;
-   allegations create political consequences;
-   accountability conflicts with another institutional objective;
-   women asking questions become inconvenient.

That is not uniquely a Labour problem.

It is one of the oldest problems in institutional safeguarding.

> **The quality of a VAWG system is partly revealed by what happens when
> safeguarding and power point in opposite directions.**

This is where the political context around the petition becomes
relevant.

The Government is not merely asking the country to believe that it
understands institutional misogyny, survivor confidence and perpetrator
accountability.

It is asking institutions across the country to **change their
behaviour** accordingly.

That creates a reciprocal obligation.

## 🏘️ Why "The Camden Crew"?

**The Camden Crew** is Polaris shorthand.

It is not the name of a formal organisation, faction or conspiracy.

Here it refers to the unusually dense political and social geography
around Camden, Holborn & St Pancras and adjacent Labour networks that
has become particularly salient in the political events surrounding
September 2026.

The point is not guilt by association.

It is almost the opposite.

> **Dense networks make good institutional tests because friendship,
> loyalty, professional relationships, political usefulness and
> safeguarding obligations can coexist.**

Knowing somebody does not make you responsible for their behaviour.

Working alongside somebody does not make you responsible for their
behaviour.

Belonging to the same political network does not make you responsible
for their behaviour.

But people exercising institutional power **are responsible for what
they do with credible information, conflicts, safeguarding
responsibilities and accountability mechanisms available to them**.

That distinction is essential.

Otherwise legitimate scrutiny becomes association mapping rather than
institutional analysis.

## 👠 Women Inside Institutions Are Not Decorative Safeguards

There is a recurring institutional temptation:

``` text
male institutional failure
        ↓
women become publicly visible
        ↓
women demonstrate institution cares
        ↓
underlying accountability remains unresolved
```

No.

Women holding senior office is good.

Women participating in VAWG policy is good.

Women defending survivors is good.

Women exercising political authority during moments of institutional
instability can be extremely important.

But:

> **women cannot function as reputational shock absorbers for male
> institutions.**

Nor should female politicians be expected to carry personal
responsibility for every man's behaviour.

The constructive ask is narrower and more serious:

> **use the power you actually possess.**

When political conditions create an opening for accountability, take it.

When a male colleague or institution needs challenging, do not make the
women closest to the issue perform all of the challenging.

When safeguarding and political convenience conflict, do not quietly
redefine safeguarding until the conflict disappears.

That is not a demand for female perfection.

It is a demand that institutional power actually be used.

---

## 🪞 "Relentless Pursuit" Is Quite a Strong Phrase

Government chose it.

That makes it useful.

If the VAWG strategy promises:

> **relentless pursuit of perpetrators**

then Parliament can reasonably ask what *relentless* means
institutionally.

It cannot coherently mean:

``` text
relentless pursuit
unless embarrassing

relentless pursuit
unless politically useful

relentless pursuit
unless well connected

relentless pursuit
unless the institution
would rather move on
```

This does **not** mean:

``` text
allegation = guilt
```

Indeed, procedural fairness becomes more important when political stakes
are high.

A credible system needs:

``` text
allegation
   ↓
safe reporting
   ↓
competent assessment
   ↓
appropriate investigation
   ↓
fair process
   ↓
evidence-based outcome
   ↓
accountability where warranted
```

The alternative to institutional protection is not trial by Twitter.

It is **functioning institutions**.

## 🌱 Prevention Means Before the Criminal Threshold

Government's strategy explicitly emphasises prevention and early
intervention.

That matters because misogyny cannot become relevant only after somebody
commits an offence capable of prosecution.

Prevention necessarily operates in environments where:

-   degrading attitudes become normal;
-   boundaries are repeatedly crossed;
-   women stop reporting because nothing happens;
-   apparently low-level behaviour accumulates;
-   organisations reward silence;
-   online communities reinforce grievance;
-   people learn that status provides insulation.

That does not mean criminalising everything upstream.

It means:

> **a prevention strategy needs interventions other than criminal
> prosecution.**

Those can include:

-   training;
-   workplace standards;
-   safeguarding;
-   education;
-   platform design;
-   professional accountability;
-   early support;
-   institutional learning.

These are not peripheral to Petition 746640.

They are how prevention works.

## 📱 Government Now Explicitly Recognises the Online Part

The 2025 VAWG strategy recognises online misogyny as part of the
contemporary environment in which violence and abuse occur.

The Commons Library briefing highlights Government's concern with:

-   misogynistic content online;
-   violent pornography;
-   effects on young people;
-   insufficient tools for parents and teachers;
-   the normalisation of abuse and control.

That is a significant conceptual development.

Government cannot simultaneously say:

``` text
online environments
shape attitudes and behaviour
```

and treat online misogyny as wholly separate from VAWG policy.

But recognising an information environment does not justify collapsing:

``` text
offensive speech
        ↓
misogyny
        ↓
radicalisation
        ↓
extremism
        ↓
criminality
```

into one category.

Government has to do the harder thing:

> **intervene proportionately at different points in the system.**

See [📣 Men With Microphones](./📣_men_with_microphones.md) and the
wider [🌱 Thanks Algorithm](../🌱_Thanks_Algorithm/README.md) cluster.

## 🧠 Credibility Is Operational

Government credibility on VAWG does not require ministers to be morally
flawless.

It does not require every allegation involving somebody politically
adjacent to result in guilt.

It does not require women in government to answer personally for men.

It requires something much less theatrical:

-   consistent standards;
-   functioning reporting mechanisms;
-   evidence preservation;
-   fair investigation;
-   transparent management of conflicts;
-   protection against retaliation;
-   institutional learning;
-   implementation;
-   willingness to act when findings are uncomfortable.

In other words:

> **VAWG credibility is infrastructure.**

That matters much more than whether everybody says the correct sentence
on the correct awareness day.

## 🧯 Do Not Make Women Carry the Whole Thing

There is another obvious failure mode:

``` text
men create problem
        ↓
women asked to identify problem
        ↓
women asked to confront men
        ↓
women absorb political backlash
        ↓
men congratulate themselves
on supporting women
```

No thank you.

Women MPs and ministers should use the power they possess.

So should:

-   male MPs;
-   male ministers;
-   officials;
-   party officers;
-   prosecutors;
-   police leaders;
-   institutional decision-makers;
-   platform executives;
-   editors;
-   educators.

The burden of recognising and responding to misogyny cannot itself be
allocated according to misogynistic assumptions about who performs care
work.

> **Solidarity is not: women, please fix misogyny.**

That leads directly to [🪩 Solidarity Is
Multifaceted](./🪩_solidarity_is_multifaceted.md).

## 🧱 Institutional Loyalty Is Not Institutional Protection

There is a useful distinction here.

Healthy institutions need loyalty.

They need:

-   colleagues who trust one another;
-   confidentiality where appropriate;
-   procedural fairness;
-   resistance to malicious allegation;
-   willingness to defend people who have been treated unjustly.

But institutional loyalty becomes dangerous when it means:

``` text
protect the institution
        =
prevent embarrassing information
from producing consequences
```

A stronger form of loyalty asks:

> **What response leaves this institution safer, fairer and more
> trustworthy after the crisis than before it?**

Sometimes that means defending somebody.

Sometimes it means investigating them.

Sometimes it means removing them.

Sometimes it means admitting that the institution itself failed.

A VAWG strategy that cannot accommodate those possibilities is not yet
an institutional strategy.

## 🔄 The Same Standards Have to Survive Political Reversal

The principle developed in [📣 Men With
Microphones](./📣_men_with_microphones.md) applies here too.

Rules built for people we dislike eventually govern people we like.

Institutional standards work the same way.

If safeguarding procedures should be rigorous when allegations concern
an opposition politician, they should remain rigorous when allegations
concern somebody politically useful to Government.

If procedural fairness matters for an ally, it also matters for an
opponent.

If survivor protection matters when politically convenient, it matters
when politically disastrous.

``` text
ALLY
 ↓
same process
same evidential discipline
same safeguarding principles
same accountability standards
 ↑
OPPONENT
```

The point is not political purity.

It is institutional durability.

---

## 📊 A Strategy Has to Be Measurable Against Difficult Cases

The Government's VAWG programme can produce many legitimate outputs:

-   legislation;
-   strategies;
-   guidance;
-   programmes;
-   training;
-   institutional structures.

But its credibility will ultimately depend on outcomes.

Those include:

-   whether women can report safely;
-   whether repeated warning signs are recognised;
-   whether powerful people receive the same scrutiny as less powerful
    people;
-   whether institutions preserve evidence;
-   whether retaliation is prevented;
-   whether survivors receive meaningful support;
-   whether lessons change future practice.

The difficult cases matter because they are **stress tests**.

Systems that work only when nobody important is inconvenienced are not
robust systems.

## 🎯 Questions Worth Asking on 7 September

These questions connect the petition debate to the Government's own VAWG
framework without requiring MPs to prejudge any individual allegation or
case.

-   **How does Government understand the relationship between its VAWG
    commitment to prevention and the petition's concern about
    misogynistic online environments?**
-   **What does "relentless pursuit of perpetrators" require from
    institutions before, during and after criminal proceedings?**
-   **How is institutional accountability incorporated into the VAWG
    strategy rather than treated solely as a policing question?**
-   **What protections exist for women and survivors who raise concerns
    about powerful or well-connected people?**
-   **How does Government ensure that political, professional or
    institutional proximity does not alter safeguarding standards?**
-   **What role are men in positions of power expected to play in
    challenging misogyny and protecting institutional accountability?**
-   **How will Government avoid placing responsibility for reform
    disproportionately on women working inside the institutions being
    asked to change?**
-   **What lessons from previous VAWG strategies have materially changed
    the 2025--26 programme?**
-   **Which interventions are genuinely new, rather than renamed or
    reorganised versions of previous commitments?**
-   **How will Government know whether prevention is working before harm
    reaches the criminal threshold?**
-   **How does its analysis of online misogyny connect to education,
    safeguarding, policing and platform regulation in practice?**
-   **Where does responsibility sit when a case crosses those
    institutional boundaries?**
-   **What should Parliament expect Government itself to model if it
    asks schools, police forces, universities, platforms and communities
    to challenge misogyny consistently?**

These are not demands for a predetermined answer.

They are tests of whether Government's principles remain coherent when
moved from strategy documents into institutions.

---

## 💄 Core Finding

> **The current Government has inherited and expanded a VAWG policy
> tradition that increasingly understands violence as a systems problem:
> prevention, perpetrator behaviour, institutional competence, online
> environments and survivor support all matter.**

That is progress.

It also raises the standard against which Government itself should be
judged.

The petition debate therefore does not take place outside Government's
wider political and institutional context.

It is a live test of the same principle:

> **Can institutions apply their safeguarding and accountability
> standards when doing so is inconvenient to the institution itself?**

Because if the answer is yes only when the difficult man is somebody
else's difficult man, we have not built a VAWG system.

We have built a communications strategy.

Fortunately, institutional accountability does not require women to
fight every battle alone.

> **Solidarity has more than one job description.**
