# 🪩 Solidarity Is Multifaceted

**First created:** 2026-09-06 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-06\
*Whole-society action does not mean everybody saying the same thing. It means people using the power, expertise and tools they already have to leave more room for somebody else to act.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The Government's VAWG strategy describes violence against women and girls as requiring a **whole-of-government and whole-of-society response**.

Take that seriously.

Because:

> **whole society does not mean women do more unpaid safeguarding work.**

Nor does solidarity require:

``` text
same identity
+
same politics
+
same analysis
+
same preferred law
+
same personal risk
=
permission to care
```

People can agree that women and girls deserve greater safety while disagreeing substantially about:

-   hate-crime law;
-   speech offences;
-   policing;
-   prisons;
-   platform regulation;
-   pornography;
-   sex and gender;
-   protest;
-   surveillance;
-   safeguarding;
-   the appropriate limits of state power.

That is not necessarily a coalition failure.

A democratic coalition should be able to contain disagreement.

**Question answered:** If misogyny is a whole-society problem, what does whole-society solidarity actually require?

---

## 🪩 Solidarity Has More Than One Job Description

The organising principle is simple:

``` text
survivors
    ≠
women MPs
    ≠
male MPs
    ≠
police
    ≠
prosecutors
    ≠
teachers
    ≠
platforms
    ≠
researchers
    ≠
journalists
    ≠
friends / families / communities
```

These groups do not have identical information.

They do not possess identical powers.

They should therefore not have identical jobs.

The useful question is:

> **What can you do from where you actually stand?**

Solidarity can be direct.

It can also be structural, procedural, technical, evidential or enabling.

Sometimes the most useful contribution to somebody else's problem is simply doing your own job extremely well.

---

## 👠 Women With Institutional Power

Women inside institutions should not be used as evidence that the institution is safe.

But women with power **can use that power**.

That might mean:

-   asking questions male colleagues would prefer not to answer;
-   insisting that safeguarding remains on the agenda;
-   resisting quiet reputational management;
-   protecting junior women;
-   making sure survivor support is actually funded;
-   asking what happened to recommendations;
-   insisting that completed reviews are published;
-   refusing to allow "women and girls" to become decorative policy language.

Crucially:

> **this is political agency, not female housekeeping.**

Women are not responsible for fixing men.

They are responsible for how they exercise their own authority.

See [💄 The Camden Crew and VAWG](./💄_the_camden_crew_and_vawg.md) for the institutional accountability problem this creates inside Government itself.

## 👔 Men Have Jobs Too

Male solidarity with women cannot consist primarily of:

> `I support women who challenge other men.`

Sometimes **you are the man who needs to challenge the other man**.

Men may be better placed to:

-   confront misogyny in male-only spaces;
-   challenge friends;
-   interrupt professional protection;
-   refuse degrading jokes or narratives before women enter the room;
-   notice when a colleague is repeatedly making women uncomfortable;
-   support fair investigation without treating it as betrayal;
-   take political heat that would otherwise fall on female colleagues;
-   model disagreement without humiliation;
-   talk to boys and younger men without treating them as latent monsters.

And especially:

> **do not wait for a woman to become sufficiently distressed that her distress supplies your evidence that something was wrong.**

The early-warning signal should not have to become a casualty before the institution responds.

But there is another form of useful male solidarity which is less theatrical and, quite often, more useful.

Men do not always need to come into women's lane.

Sometimes they need to use the tools already on their desk.

## 🧪 Solidarity Can Produce Control Cases

Solidarity does not require everybody to investigate the same problem for the same reason.

Sometimes somebody working on an apparently separate accountability problem produces exactly the comparison another group needs.

Recent bundles of Written Parliamentary Questions from Mike Wood are useful in this way.

Taken together, questions across different departments and subjects can reveal an administrative baseline:

``` text
QUESTION A
        ↓
department answers promptly

QUESTION B
        ↓
records located + substantive answer

QUESTION C
        ↓
minister identifies responsible body

QUESTION D
        ↓
deadline passes / answer deferred /
responsibility becomes difficult to locate
```

The answered questions become **control cases** for the unanswered ones.

That changes the analytical question from:

> *Why hasn't Government answered this yet?*

to something potentially much more precise:

> **What distinguishes the questions Government can answer normally from the questions that repeatedly encounter delay, fragmentation, unusual handling or uncertainty about ownership?**

That is useful whether the original concern is:

-   VAWG;
-   political finance;
-   national security;
-   government transparency;
-   ministerial conduct;
-   something else entirely.

The work can expose the **administrative baseline**.

Once there is a baseline, exceptional handling becomes easier to identify.

An opposition backbencher asking a sufficiently large number of conventional questions can therefore produce something close to a natural experiment in government responsiveness.

If twenty questions travel through ordinary machinery and three hit molasses, the three become more interesting.

That is solidarity even where solidarity was not the original motive.

## 🧭 You Do Not Need the Same Motive to Produce the Same Public Good

Imagine:

``` text
feminist MP
asks about survivor protection

opposition backbencher
asks about departmental records

civil-liberties lawyer
tests statutory safeguards

journalist
chases an unpublished report

auditor
asks where the money went

committee
asks who authorised something
```

They may have completely different motives.

They may disagree about almost everything else.

But collectively they can produce:

``` text
visibility
+
comparison
+
institutional memory
+
accountability
```

Nobody has to join the same campaign.

Nobody has to adopt somebody else's political identity.

Sometimes the useful contribution is simply:

> **doing your own institutional job properly enough that somebody else can use the evidence it produces.**

Institutional usefulness is not proportional to seniority.

A backbencher asking sufficiently precise questions may contribute more to accountability in a particular case than a much more powerful politician expressing much stronger rhetorical solidarity.

That gives us a useful measure:

> **Solidarity should be measured partly by what becomes possible because you acted.**

## 🛠️ Men Do Not Need to Go Miles Out of Their Lane

There is a version of allyship that accidentally asks everyone to become an amateur specialist in everybody else's problem.

That is neither necessary nor particularly efficient.

Sometimes the useful intervention is:

> **do your own job well, and leave the system more governable for the people working on adjacent harms.**

There are examples elsewhere in [📖 Previously on the Internet](../📖_Previously_On_The_Internet/README.md).

Ed Miliband's interventions around AI are useful here.

He does not need to approach frontier AI regulation carrying glitter and announcing that he has arrived on behalf of cyberfeminism.

If work within his own remit:

-   constrains commercial power;
-   creates meaningful scrutiny of frontier systems;
-   establishes that powerful technology companies can be challenged by democratic government;
-   produces disclosure or accountability mechanisms;
-   prevents commercial interests becoming the automatic trump card;
-   creates political space in which other ministers, MPs, regulators and civil society can ask harder questions;

then that work has value for women concerned about AI-generated misogyny **regardless of why he did it**.

Misogynistic generation and distribution do not occur in a separate feminist computer.

They occur inside the same commercial technological systems being contested for reasons including:

``` text
energy
competition
copyright
labour
safety
children
national infrastructure
market power
environmental impact
        +
misogyny and gendered harm
```

Every successful assertion that:

> `No, actually, Government is allowed to govern this technology.`

changes the bargaining environment for the next person.

Men do not need to go miles out of their lanes to be helpful.

They need to leave useful infrastructure behind.

## 🌱 Solidarity Can Be an Enabling Condition

There is a useful distinction.

### Direct solidarity

``` text
I see your problem.
I am helping with your problem.
```

### Enabling solidarity

``` text
I am doing my own job.
        ↓
my intervention changes
an institution / market / evidential record
        ↓
you now have more leverage
to address your problem
```

Mike Wood's questions can create administrative control cases.

Ed Miliband's AI work can create regulatory and political room.

A competition regulator can reduce concentrated platform power.

An environmental campaign can force disclosure about infrastructure.

A copyright case can reveal how an AI company operates.

A trade union can establish workplace protections that subsequently help women experiencing harassment.

A civil-liberties lawyer can constrain an overbroad state power that would otherwise later be used against feminist protesters.

None of these people needs to adopt **misogyny** as the principal reason for their work.

Their work can still alter the ecology in which misogyny is contested.

> **Solidarity can be an externality of doing your own job properly.**

That is not lesser solidarity.

It may be some of the most durable solidarity available.

## 🧰 Bring Your Own Tools

Polaris will take ten men who can do a Mike or an Ed over a hundred men who know all the correct words.

That is not because words are worthless.

Norm-setting matters.

Public support matters.

Sometimes somebody simply needs to say the bloody thing.

But compare the outputs:

``` text
100 × “I stand with women”
        ↓
useful cultural signal

10 × competent intervention
        ↓
questions on the record
control cases
commercial constraints
regulatory precedent
institutional leverage
better evidence
room for somebody else to move
```

Mike does not have to become a feminist policy specialist.

Ed does not have to become a cyberfeminist theorist.

Their motives do not need to be converted into ours before their work counts.

That is a healthier coalition model because it does not demand ideological assimilation.

The principle is:

> **You do not have to come into our lane. Bring us something useful from yours.**

If you are an MP: ask the bastard question.

If you are an engineer: build the safer architecture.

If you are a regulator: regulate.

If you are a competition lawyer: break open the market problem.

If you are a journalist: find the document.

If you are a teacher: notice the kid disappearing down the rabbit hole.

If you are a civil-liberties lawyer: make sure the powers supposedly protecting women cannot become arbitrary powers over everybody.

If you work on frontier AI: create the opening through which women and others can subsequently challenge misogynistic generation and distribution.

If your work gives somebody elsewhere **evidence, precedent, leverage, institutional capacity or room to manoeuvre**, it counts.

> **Do not show us how well you have learned the vocabulary. Show us what became easier for somebody else because you did your job well.**

## 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Men and Boys Are Not Merely the Target Population

Government discourse can easily slide from:

``` text
some boys encounter misogynistic ecosystems
```

into:

``` text
boys are the misogyny problem
```

That is both analytically bad and strategically useless.

Boys and men can also be:

-   friends;
-   brothers;
-   partners;
-   bystanders;
-   moderators;
-   teachers;
-   organisers;
-   creators;
-   researchers;
-   whistleblowers;
-   counter-speakers;
-   people themselves harmed by coercive models of masculinity.

A boy looking for dating advice does not need to be treated as a pre-criminal.

He might need **better internet**.

See [🌱 He Only Wanted Dating Advice](../🌱_Thanks_Algorithm/🌱_he_only_wanted_dating_advice.md).

## 🕯️ Survivors Do Not Owe You Front-Line Service

Survivor testimony matters.

Survivor expertise matters.

But:

> **survivorship is not compulsory public service.**

A survivor can contribute by:

-   speaking publicly;
-   speaking privately;
-   writing;
-   advising;
-   giving evidence;
-   supporting another survivor;
-   building something;
-   doing nothing at all.

All are legitimate.

Nobody should have to repeatedly expose their worst experiences in order to make institutions perform functions those institutions already possess the power to perform.

And survivor disagreement is allowed.

Two survivors can want the same reduction in harm and disagree completely about:

-   policing;
-   sentencing;
-   hate-crime legislation;
-   censorship;
-   anonymity;
-   restorative justice;
-   platform governance.

Neither loses survivor status because the other has a different policy theory.

## 🌈 Solidarity Does Not Require Identical Women

"Women and girls" contains enormous differences in:

-   race;
-   religion;
-   disability;
-   class;
-   sexuality;
-   gender history;
-   migration status;
-   age;
-   geography;
-   political belief;
-   relationship to police and other state institutions.

Those differences affect both **exposure to harm** and **confidence in proposed solutions**.

Somebody may simultaneously want:

``` text
stronger protection from misogynistic abuse
        +
strong protection for political speech
        +
less discriminatory policing
        +
better investigation of violence
```

Those demands are not inherently contradictory.

They are exactly why legislation needs precision.

## ✡️☪️ Solidarity Across Communities Cannot Mean Competitive Injury

This is especially relevant given the wider public-order and hate-crime context in which Petition 746640 is being debated.

The current hate-crime architecture is responding to concerns involving different protected groups and communities.

There is a very bad way to process that:

``` text
your protection
        ↓
must reduce
        ↓
my protection
```

That produces competitive vulnerability.

A better question is:

> **Can we build legal and institutional standards we would trust when somebody else is the protected person and somebody we sympathise with is the accused?**

This connects directly to the rule developed in [📣 Men With Microphones](./📣_men_with_microphones.md):

> **case law travels.**

Solidarity therefore includes defending procedural safeguards even when today's defendant is deeply unsympathetic.

Tomorrow's defendant may not be.

## ⚖️ You Can Disagree About the Law and Still Be in the Coalition

Petition 746640 itself proposes legal change.

People acting in good faith can disagree about whether particular proposals are:

-   necessary;
-   sufficient;
-   too narrow;
-   too broad;
-   appropriately drafted;
-   better handled through criminal law;
-   better handled through regulation;
-   better handled upstream.

For example:

``` text
Person A:
extend criminal liability

Person B:
improve existing enforcement

Person C:
regulate platform distribution

Person D:
invest in education

Person E:
strengthen survivor services

Person F:
do several of these,
but keep speech thresholds narrow
```

Those people do not necessarily oppose one another.

They may be working on different parts of the same system.

> **Solidarity is compatible with argument.**

Badly designed law passed in the name of women is not automatically feminist law.

Good intentions do not remove the need for:

-   proportionality;
-   evidence;
-   clarity;
-   enforceability;
-   procedural fairness.

## 📣 Speech Is One Place Solidarity Gets Difficult

Someone can say something misogynistic without committing an offence.

Someone else can defend that person's legal right to say it while also saying:

> *mate, that was misogynistic.*

Those positions are compatible.

``` text
LEGAL QUESTION

should the state punish this?
        ≠

SOCIAL QUESTION

should anybody challenge this?
```

We need both questions.

Otherwise every social problem gets pushed toward criminal law because nobody has developed a functioning intermediate response.

See [📣 Men With Microphones](./📣_men_with_microphones.md).

## 📲 Platforms Have a Different Form of Power

Platforms do not need to decide whether a speaker deserves prison before deciding whether their recommender system should repeatedly introduce that speaker to fourteen-year-olds.

Their solidarity function is structural.

It includes:

-   recommender design;
-   friction;
-   reporting systems;
-   moderation;
-   user controls;
-   safety-by-design;
-   research access;
-   transparency;
-   enforcement consistency.

Again:

``` text
speaker responsibility
        ≠
platform responsibility
```

A functioning system can examine both without pretending they are identical.

See [📲 Online Safety and Platforms](../🌱_Thanks_Algorithm/📲_online_safety_and_platforms.md).

## 📰 Journalists Have a Job

Journalistic solidarity is not:

> never report allegations because they might hurt somebody.

Nor is it:

> publish allegations as established fact because the cause is important.

It means doing the journalism.

That includes:

-   evidential discipline;
-   protecting vulnerable sources;
-   checking institutional claims;
-   distinguishing allegation from finding;
-   following boring procedural developments;
-   asking where reports went;
-   tracking whether promises were implemented;
-   noticing patterns across apparently separate cases;
-   correcting errors.

And importantly:

> **do not make the survivor perform the entire investigation for you.**

Institutions leave records.

Find them.

---

## 🏫 Educators and Universities Have a Job

The petition explicitly mentions survivors in education and young people.

Educational institutions are not just locations where Government can deliver a healthy-relationships lesson.

They are themselves institutions with:

-   safeguarding responsibilities;
-   disciplinary systems;
-   reporting structures;
-   welfare provision;
-   accommodation;
-   societies;
-   digital communities;
-   staff power;
-   reputational incentives.

Their solidarity has to include **institutional competence**, not simply awareness campaigns.

A poster saying:

> `misogyny has no place here`

is considerably less useful if the reporting pathway underneath it is broken.

---

## 🧑‍⚖️ Police and Prosecutors Have a Job

Their contribution is not to solve misogyny.

It is to do criminal justice properly.

That means:

-   understand the law;
-   recognise relevant hostility where evidence supports it;
-   distinguish criminality from lawful ugliness;
-   investigate competently;
-   preserve evidence;
-   apply charging standards;
-   communicate properly with victims;
-   respect defendants' rights;
-   learn from failed cases.

This is why the petition's training demand matters.

A legal reform nobody understands operationally is not much of a reform.

## 🏛️ Government Has the Coordination Job

Government decomposes the problem into:

``` text
Home Office
MoJ
DfE
Ofcom
police
CPS
safeguarding
VAWG policy
```

Fair enough.

But somebody then needs responsibility for whether those parts reconnect.

Government solidarity therefore includes:

-   coordination;
-   funding;
-   publication;
-   measurement;
-   legislative coherence;
-   implementation;
-   answering Parliament;
-   identifying gaps rather than merely pointing to adjacent programmes.

> **Whole-of-government policy requires somebody to care about the spaces between departments.**

See [🧮 How Government Translated the Petition](./🧮_how_government_translated_the_petition.md).

## 🧶 Communities Have Informal Power Too

Not everything needs a statute.

Communities can:

-   create better norms;
-   offer alternative social spaces;
-   intervene early;
-   make misogyny less socially rewarding;
-   support isolated people;
-   build healthier routes into intimacy and friendship;
-   provide alternatives to grievance communities;
-   teach conflict without humiliation;
-   make belonging available without requiring an enemy.

The answer to harmful online community is not necessarily:

> **no community.**

Sometimes it is:

> **better community.**

## ❤️‍🩹 Care Is Infrastructure Too

If somebody is:

-   lonely;
-   humiliated;
-   frightened;
-   socially isolated;
-   traumatised;
-   rejected;
-   angry;

there are multiple possible pathways from that state.

A society interested in prevention should care about whether people can reach:

-   friends;
-   healthcare;
-   therapy;
-   community;
-   meaningful work;
-   accessible technology;
-   creative spaces;
-   relationships;
-   somewhere they can belong without learning to hate somebody else.

That is not sentimentalism.

It is upstream systems design.

## 🪩 The Coalition Can Contain Conflict

A useful coalition might contain:

-   feminists who favour stronger hate-crime law;
-   civil-liberties lawyers worried about speech;
-   survivors who want stronger policing;
-   survivors deeply distrustful of police;
-   religious women;
-   queer women;
-   trans people;
-   women critical of gender politics;
-   men worried about boys;
-   teachers;
-   police;
-   abolitionists;
-   prosecutors;
-   platform engineers;
-   researchers.

That coalition will argue.

Good.

> **The objective is not perfect ideological alignment. It is reducing harm without building machinery we cannot defend when power changes hands.**

That is democratic solidarity.

## 🧪 The Reciprocity Test

Before supporting an intervention, ask:

``` text
Would I accept this evidential standard
if my political opponent controlled it?

Would I accept this speech threshold
if somebody I supported were speaking?

Would I accept this police power
if my community were being policed?

Would I accept this safeguarding standard
if the accused were my friend?

Would I accept this survivor standard
if the survivor disliked my politics?

Would I accept this platform rule
if it affected speech I valued?
```

If the answer changes solely because the identities change:

> **the principle needs more work.**

This is solidarity with future strangers as well as present allies.

-   **What does Government mean operationally by a "whole-society" response to misogyny?**
-   **What role does Government expect men and boys to play beyond being treated as subjects of intervention?**
-   **How will Government ensure that women and survivors are not required to carry the majority of prevention, disclosure and accountability work?**
-   **How will survivor expertise be incorporated without making repeated public disclosure the price of being heard?**
-   **How does Government accommodate legitimate disagreement among survivors about policing, sentencing and speech regulation?**
-   **What role should non-criminal interventions play where misogynistic conduct is harmful but lawful?**
-   **How will safeguards built into new legislation remain effective when politically unpopular defendants rely upon them?**
-   **What responsibilities sit with platforms independently of the liability of individual speakers?**
-   **What specific responsibilities do schools, colleges and universities have beyond education campaigns?**
-   **How will Government ensure police and prosecutors understand both the reach and the limits of any new law?**
-   **Who owns coordination when one case crosses education, safeguarding, policing, prosecution and platform regulation?**
-   **How will Government measure whether its whole-society approach distributes responsibility rather than merely distributing work back onto women?**

## 🪩 Core Finding

> **Misogyny may be gendered. Responsibility for reducing it is not exclusively female.**

Women can use institutional power.

Men can challenge men.

Boys can build healthier cultures.

Survivors can contribute without becoming public property.

Police can investigate properly.

Prosecutors can apply the law precisely.

Educators can build competent safeguarding systems.

Platforms can change distribution.

Journalists can investigate institutions.

Government can coordinate the machinery.

Communities can make healthier belonging possible.

And people working on apparently unrelated problems can create the evidence, leverage, precedent, capacity and room that somebody else needs.

> **Solidarity is not an identity. It is a property of relationships between actions.**

Your action can help somebody whose politics, priorities and reasons differ from yours because it creates:

``` text
evidence
+
leverage
+
precedent
+
capacity
+
safety
+
room to manoeuvre
```

You do not even have to be working on the same problem.

> **You just have to leave more possibility behind you than you found.**

Solidarity does not mean everybody doing the same thing.

It means everybody doing their bit without quietly assigning the hardest, riskiest and most exhausting parts back to the people already carrying the harm.

And that gives us the handoff to Macdonald.

Government commissioned an expert to examine part of **its** job.

The report is finished.

Survivors, petitioners, women MPs and the public should not have to reconstruct its conclusions by divination.

> **Government can publish the bloody report.**
