Yes. `🪩_solidarity_is_multifaceted.md` fits very well here.

It gives the cluster a necessary constructive counterweight: male solidarity is not one performance, and it does not require men to speak *for* women. It can look like asking better questions, using institutional access, challenging other men, changing norms in male spaces, backing survivor-centred policy, noticing risk, making room, funding work, doing the boring implementation, or simply refusing to laugh along.

Mike Woods’ questions are a good worked example because they let us show **solidarity as institutional practice** rather than just sentiment. The node can ask: what does useful allyship look like when you have parliamentary, professional, media, organisational, or social power that women around you may not?

I’d frame it around a few modes: **interpersonal, cultural, institutional, political, practical, and protective**. The key line is probably:

> **Solidarity is not a personality trait. It is a set of behaviours.**

And the disco ball is perfect because the whole point is that solidarity has **multiple facets reflecting the same commitment in different directions**.
