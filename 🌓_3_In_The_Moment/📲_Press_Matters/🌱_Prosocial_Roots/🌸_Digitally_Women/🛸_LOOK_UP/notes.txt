And the short node briefs:

### `README.md`

**The prevention cluster.** `Thanks, Algorithm!` established that misogyny can form part of an online grievance and radicalisation ecology with offline consequences. `LOOK UP` asks what happens when those risks become observable. Introduce reporting, behavioural warning signs, survivor knowledge, safeguarding, pattern recognition and information-sharing. The central proposition is simple: prevention requires institutions to become interested **before conduct reaches the point at which criminal justice has an obvious offence to process**.

### `🛸_the_warning_signs_are_visible_from_space.md`

**Sometimes the difficulty is not absence of signal but failure to assemble what is already visible.** Use the deliberately ridiculous title to examine cases where warning behaviour accumulates across people, platforms, institutions or time without becoming one intelligible risk picture. Stalking, fixation, misogynistic hostility, coercion, sexual misconduct, threats and escalating grievance may each be recorded separately. The node asks when a collection of individually dismissible facts becomes a pattern that somebody ought to recognise.

This is the big handoff from `🌱_Thanks_Algorithm/`: *okay, you've established the ecology—what might it look like from the outside?*

### `🌱_before_the_offence.md`

**Safeguarding starts earlier than sentencing.** Map the upstream intervention chain:

> behaviour → disclosure/reporting → recording → assessment → safeguarding/referral → intervention → investigation/prosecution where appropriate.

Clarify that “before the offence” does **not** mean predicting crimes from thoughts or punishing people for lawful beliefs. It means recognising behaviour and circumstances relevant to established safeguarding and violence-prevention practice before the most serious outcome occurs. This is where stalking, coercive control, harassment, escalating threats and other behavioural precursors become especially important.

### `👁️_misogyny_as_a_risk_signal.md`

**What changes if misogyny is treated as potentially informative rather than merely offensive?** Examine when persistent misogynistic hostility, gendered grievance, entitlement, fixation or rigid domination narratives may contribute to a wider behavioural risk picture. Keep the distinction from `misogyny = future violent offender` absolutely clear. The useful question is whether misogyny can add information **in combination with behaviour, context and escalation**, particularly given its recurrence in several violence-associated environments.

This is probably one of the cluster's key policy nodes.

### `🔗_information_has_to_travel.md`

**A warning sign known only to the wrong person is not much of a safeguard.** Examine the plumbing: what gets recorded, who can access it, when information can or should be shared, where referrals go, and how relevant fragments move between policing, prosecutors, schools/universities, safeguarding services, health, employers/regulators and specialist VAWG services.

The governing question:

> **Who knew what, and was there a route by which the person capable of acting could know it too?**

This should eventually cross-link heavily into the institutional material in `♻️_Re_Recommendations/`.

### `🧩_pattern_recognition_not_incident_processing.md`

**A system can process every incident and still miss the person or pattern.** Distinguish incident-level decision-making from longitudinal and cross-source analysis. Repeated low-level reports, multiple victims, different jurisdictions, different categories of misconduct or fragments held by separate institutions can each look insufficient alone while becoming highly significant together.

This is where the Polaris **information-plumbing / whole-ecology** work really earns its keep.

And it gives us a useful test:

> **Did the system ask whether this incident met its threshold, or whether this incident changed what was already known?**

### `🏫_schools_colleges_and_universities.md`

**Young people encounter these ecosystems somewhere, and educational institutions are already part of the intervention environment.** Connect the petition's explicit concern about students and anti-feminist radicalisation to safeguarding, relationships/sex education, behavioural concerns, peer cultures, online conduct, staff training, student support and referral pathways.

Importantly, treat education as more than somewhere to deliver an “Andrew Tate is bad” assembly. Schools, colleges and universities can be places where **changes in behaviour become visible**, victims disclose, peer groups observe escalation, and healthier models of masculinity and relationships can be built.

### `🩸_survivor_support_and_reporting.md`

**The person experiencing the pattern may be the first person who can actually describe it.** Examine reporting systems, credibility, repeat disclosure, trauma-aware processes, practical support and what happens to information after somebody reports. Survivor support isn't an optional kindness attached to enforcement: inaccessible, unsafe or futile reporting systems also deprive institutions of information necessary for prevention and pattern recognition.

This is also where we can explicitly resist the tendency to make women repeatedly provide **unpaid institutional pattern-recognition services** while each new recipient treats their account as a fresh anecdote.
