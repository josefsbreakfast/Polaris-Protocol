# 🛸 LOOK UP
**First created:** 2026-09-06 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-06  
*Early prevention is an information-ecology problem as much as a criminal-law problem.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder begins with a simple instruction:

> **Look up.**

When serious harm occurs, institutions understandably reconstruct the final incident:

```text
What offence occurred?

What evidence exists?

What threshold was crossed?

What should happen now?
```

Those questions matter.

They are not the whole prevention problem.

Before the final offence, there may already have been:

- behavioural change;
- repeated boundary violations;
- grievance;
- fixation;
- targeting;
- online and offline movement;
- disclosures;
- low-level reports;
- peer observations;
- institutional contact;
- previous interventions;
- fragments held in different places.

The warning signs do not necessarily arrive labelled:

```text
⚠ FUTURE SERIOUS HARM ⚠
```

They arrive as ordinary pieces of information.

The question is whether the surrounding ecology can do anything useful with them.

---

## 🛸 The Folder Argument

`LOOK_UP` is not an argument for predicting future offenders from beliefs, browsing histories or unpopular speech.

It is not an argument for indiscriminate surveillance.

It is not an argument for collecting everything about everyone forever.

It asks a narrower and more practical set of questions:

```text
Can people recognise concerning change?

Can somebody ask for help before the worst outcome?

Can relevant information reach somewhere useful?

Can institutions preserve provenance and correction?

Can new information update old information?

Can apparently separate incidents be recognised as a pattern?

Can support and intervention occur proportionately?

Can survivors report without becoming the integration layer
for fragmented institutional systems?
```

Taken together, these are questions about **information ecology**.

Prevention depends not only on what information exists, but on:

- who encounters it;
- how it is interpreted;
- whether it can travel;
- whether its status survives travel;
- whether later information can change earlier assessments;
- whether somebody owns the combined picture;
- whether useful interventions exist;
- whether people still trust the reporting system enough to use it.

## 🛸 [The Warning Signs Are Visible From Space](./🛸_the_warning_signs_are_visible_from_space.md)

**Start here.**

The opening node asks what happens when warning information is not truly absent but distributed.

A serious outcome may be preceded by pieces held across:

- families;
- peers;
- schools;
- universities;
- workplaces;
- platforms;
- health services;
- safeguarding systems;
- police;
- specialist services.

The problem can therefore be less:

```text
NOBODY KNEW ANYTHING
```

and more:

```text
EVERYBODY KNEW
A DIFFERENT LITTLE THING
```

This establishes the folder's central shift:

> **Do not only ask whether a warning sign existed. Ask whether the information ecology could assemble what existed into an intelligible picture.**

## 🌱 [Before the Offence](./🌱_before_the_offence.md)

If warning information can exist before a serious offence, the next question is:

> **What can legitimately happen then?**

This node maps the space between:

```text
DO NOTHING
```

and:

```text
CRIMINAL JUSTICE
```

That includes both formal and informal systems:

- family and friendship networks;
- education;
- pastoral support;
- safeguarding;
- specialist services;
- health and welfare systems;
- behavioural boundaries;
- proportionate referral and intervention.

The node also makes the capacity problem explicit.

If policy asks institutions to detect more, refer more and intervene earlier, then somebody has to:

```text
receive
+
assess
+
support
+
coordinate
+
follow up
```

Early intervention without response capacity is not an intervention architecture.

It is an instruction to generate a queue.

## 🥃 [Misogyny as a Risk Signal](./🥃_misogyny_as_a_risk_signal.md)

The third node asks what role misogyny can legitimately play in assessment.

Its answer is deliberately constrained.

Misogyny may sometimes provide useful context around:

- grievance;
- entitlement;
- fixation;
- targeting;
- boundary violations;
- escalation.

But:

```text
MISOGYNISTIC CONTENT
        ↓
DANGER
```

is not an adequate assessment model.

The node therefore treats misogyny as **potentially informative contextual data**, not as a predictive shortcut.

Its governing safeguard is:

> **A useful signal should improve behavioural assessment, not replace it.**

That distinction matters for both effective prevention and civil liberties.

---

## 🗺️ [Information Has to Travel](./🗺️_information_has_to_travel.md)

Useful information cannot change an assessment if it never reaches somewhere capable of using it.

This node moves from detection into information plumbing.

It asks:

- where concerns enter;
- where they go;
- whether the recipient can act;
- whether referrals are received as well as sent;
- whether provenance survives copying;
- whether corrections travel;
- whether institutions can preserve useful memory without preserving every suspicion forever.

The node rejects both extremes:

```text
EVERYTHING STAYS SILOED
```

and:

```text
GREAT NATIONAL GOSSIP DATABASE
```

The design problem is **purposeful movement with provenance, status, correction and proportionate access**.

## 🔮 [Pattern Recognition, Not Incident Processing](./🔮_pattern_recognition_not_incident_processing.md)

Once information can travel, somebody still has to reason with it.

This node distinguishes:

```text
Does today's incident
cross today's threshold?
```

from:

```text
Does today's incident
change what yesterday meant?
```

A system can process every individual incident correctly and still fail at the aggregate level.

New information may reveal:

- repetition;
- another victim;
- escalation;
- movement across settings;
- failed intervention;
- a contradiction of an earlier benign explanation.

Equally, new information may reduce concern by revealing duplication, mistaken identity, benign context or an earlier error.

So:

> **Updating must be bidirectional.**

Pattern recognition is not allegation arithmetic, and integration is not interpretation.

The node asks who, if anybody, owns the combined picture.

## 🍎 [Schools, Colleges and Universities](./🍎_schools_colleges_and_universities.md)

Education provides a concrete prevention setting.

Schools, colleges and universities can potentially combine:

```text
TEACH
+
SUPPORT
+
NOTICE
+
CONNECT
+
INTERVENE
```

They can also see people longitudinally while people are still changing.

That creates opportunities for:

- relationship and consent literacy;
- healthier alternatives to grievance communities;
- ordinary routes into support;
- peer concerns to reach useful doors;
- repeated observations to become a coherent safeguarding picture;
- proportionate intervention before criminal-justice thresholds are reached.

But the node rejects turning education into a surveillance system or every member of staff into an amateur threat assessor.

Its practical staff model is simpler:

```text
NOTICE
        ↓
LISTEN
        ↓
RECORD APPROPRIATELY
        ↓
KNOW WHERE TO TAKE IT
```

And its implementation question remains:

> **With what staff, training, time, framework and referral capacity?**

## 🩸 [Survivor Support and Reporting](./🩸_survivor_support_and_reporting.md)

The final substantive node turns the information architecture around.

Institutions need information.

But the survivor is not:

```text
a sensor

a database interface

a human copy-and-paste system

a substitute for institutional memory
```

Reporting is itself an intervention experienced by the person reporting.

This node therefore asks whether systems can:

- provide more than one reporting door;
- distinguish advice, support, recording and formal action;
- avoid unnecessary repeated retelling;
- preserve evidential status;
- support somebody without requiring a formal finding;
- recognise continuity;
- explain confidentiality and information-sharing boundaries;
- prevent uncontrolled circulation;
- tell the reporter what happens next.

It also makes the information-ecology consequence explicit:

> **Trust is part of information infrastructure.**

If reporting repeatedly produces avoidable harm, confusion or futility, people eventually stop supplying information.

The system then experiences silence without necessarily recognising that it helped produce it.

## 🕸️ The Whole Prevention Loop

Read together, the nodes describe a feedback system:

```text
warning information exists
        ↓
somebody notices
        ↓
a useful door exists
        ↓
information is recorded accurately
        ↓
information reaches an appropriate recipient
        ↓
provenance and status survive
        ↓
new information is compared with what was already known
        ↓
pattern / trajectory is assessed
        ↓
proportionate support or intervention occurs
        ↓
the response produces new information
        ↓
the picture updates
```

Any broken link can matter.

A system can have excellent detection and nowhere useful to refer.

It can have excellent services and inaccessible reporting.

It can collect enormous quantities of information and fail to connect any of it.

It can connect everything and lose provenance.

It can identify patterns and have no proportionate intervention available.

It can encourage reporting and then make reporting so costly that trust collapses.

Prevention is therefore a property of the **whole pathway**.

---

## 🧱 What This Folder Does Not Recommend

The architecture here should not be read as support for:

- ideological profiling;
- criminalising misogynistic belief as such;
- treating boys and men as presumptive offenders;
- universal behavioural surveillance;
- indefinite retention of unresolved allegations;
- automatic information sharing;
- algorithmic guilt scoring;
- counting duplicated reports as corroboration;
- making every welfare concern a disciplinary matter;
- replacing specialist judgement with databases;
- making survivors responsible for proving the entire institutional pattern.

The point of `LOOK_UP` is not:

> **Collect more data.**

It is:

> **Become better at recognising, routing, interpreting and responding to the information legitimately encountered in ordinary safeguarding systems.**

## 🧭 A Compact Institutional Test

When examining a prevention system, ask:

1. **What can people notice?**
2. **Where can they take it?**
3. **Can they describe behaviour without knowing the correct professional category?**
4. **Who receives the information?**
5. **Can that recipient do anything useful?**
6. **Does provenance travel with the information?**
7. **Can corrections travel too?**
8. **Can later information change an earlier assessment?**
9. **Can the system distinguish repetition from duplicated rumour?**
10. **Who owns a pattern spanning several services or settings?**
11. **What proportionate interventions exist before the highest threshold?**
12. **Does the response to intervention update the assessment?**
13. **Can support exist without enforcement success?**
14. **Does the person reporting understand what happens next?**
15. **Has the policy funded the capacity required to make any of this real?**

If those questions do not have answers, exhortations to:

```text
LOOK FOR THE WARNING SIGNS
```

are doing considerably less work than they appear to be doing.

## 🌱 The Prosocial Point

This folder sits inside `🌱_Prosocial_Roots` for a reason.

Prevention cannot only mean becoming better at identifying dangerous people.

It also means building environments in which:

- healthier relationships can be learned;
- loneliness can be answered without exploitation;
- rejection can be survived;
- peers can raise concerns without becoming investigators;
- families can seek help;
- people can accept boundaries and move on;
- support exists before crisis;
- interventions can work;
- mistakes can be corrected;
- concern can decrease as well as increase;
- people retain routes back into ordinary social life where appropriate.

A system that only knows how to escalate suspicion has misunderstood prevention.

The objective is not merely to identify bad outcomes earlier.

It is to create more opportunities for **better outcomes instead**.

## 🔭 Looking Forward

`LOOK_UP` ends at an important boundary.

Once institutions can:

- retain information;
- move it;
- combine it;
- identify relationships;
- assess trajectories;
- update risk;
- coordinate action;

a new set of governance questions appears.

Who can see the assembled picture?

Who controls it?

How long does it persist?

What happens when it is wrong?

What happens when several institutions disagree?

What does automation change?

When does useful institutional memory become sediment?

When does coordination become surveillance?

Those are not reasons to abandon information sharing or pattern recognition.

They are reasons to govern them.

The next problem is no longer merely:

```text
CAN THE SYSTEM SEE?
```

It is:

```text
WHAT HAPPENS
ONCE IT CAN?
```
