PURPOSE
Build an evidence-led research pack for:

🌓_3_In_The_Moment/📲_Press_Matters/🌱_Prosocial_Roots/🌸_Digitally_Women/♻️_Re_Recommendations/♻️_as_per_my_last_review.md

The node is intended to identify recurring institutional safeguards and recommendations across UK inquiries, reviews, inspections and major institutional investigations concerning:

- violence against women and girls (VAWG);
- predatory behaviour;
- sexual violence;
- domestic abuse;
- stalking and harassment;
- safeguarding;
- policing failures;
- police vetting and misconduct;
- institutional responses to repeated allegations;
- failures to connect information across cases/institutions;

Concentrate primarily on approximately 2015–September 2026.

Include older landmark material where a later review explicitly revisits, repeats or tests an earlier recommendation.

GEOGRAPHY
Primary focus: England and Wales / UK institutions.

Include devolved or international material only where it is directly relevant to a UK institutional recommendation or provides an unusually useful control/comparator.

Prioritise primary and authoritative sources:

1. statutory/public inquiries;
2. independent reviews commissioned by government or public bodies;
3. HMICFRS inspections/reports;

7. government reviews/action plans/responses;
8. official police/institutional reviews;
9. major safeguarding practice reviews where directly relevant;
10. academic or high-quality secondary sources only where needed to explain implementation or trace what happened afterwards.

RECURRENCE TEST

For every apparent repetition, classify it as one of:

A = substantially the same recommendation repeated
B = earlier recommendation extended or strengthened
C = same underlying failure but materially different proposed remedy
D = superficially similar but actually different problem
E = unclear / insufficient evidence

Explain the classification.

ACCURACY RULES

- Do not claim recurrence merely because two reports concern VAWG.
- Compare the actual failure mechanism and actual recommendation.
- Separate recommendation from acceptance.
- Separate acceptance from implementation.
- Separate implementation from embedding.
- Separate embedding from audit.
- Separate initial success from durability.
- Do not describe a recommendation as "ignored" without evidence.
- Do not use "lessons were not learned" as a substitute for tracing what actually happened.
- Preserve uncertainty where implementation evidence is unavailable.
- Prefer primary sources.
- Provide working URLs.
- Record page/paragraph/recommendation numbers wherever available.
- Note publication dates and relevant follow-up dates.
- Search through September 2026.
