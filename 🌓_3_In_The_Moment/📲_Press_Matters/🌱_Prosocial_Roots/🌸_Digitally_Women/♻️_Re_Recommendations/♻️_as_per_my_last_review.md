## 🛰️ Orientation

This node does **not** begin from the claim that nothing ever changes.

The stronger and more useful question is narrower:

> **Why did this particular failure mode remain possible after the earlier intervention?**

Across the material reviewed here, there is substantial evidence of real institutional change: new national operating models, revised professional practice, statutory vetting rules, specialist teams, action plans, additional audits and a new national VAWG/public-protection centre. There is also repeated primary-source evidence that a safeguard can be **accepted without being implemented; implemented in some forces but not others; technically installed but operationally awkward; trained once but not embedded; audited without findings being acted upon; or adopted nationally while still taking time to become routine practice**.

That distinction matters. The institutional history is not a loop in which nobody ever notices the problem. It is often a chain with a missing hinge.

**Research cut-off:** 6 September 2026.  
**Primary geography:** England and Wales; UK-wide material only where directly relevant.  
**Source hierarchy:** inquiries, HMICFRS, IOPC/College super-complaint investigations, government/Met responses and implementation material, statutory guidance and policing professional practice.

## RR-01 — Everyone’s Business / domestic-abuse inspection baseline

**ID:** RR-01  
**TITLE:** *Everyone’s Business: Improving the police response to domestic abuse*  
**DATE:** 27 March 2014  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** HM Inspectorate of Constabulary  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Police forces in England and Wales; domestic-abuse response  
**TRIGGER:** National inspection of police handling of domestic abuse  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/improving-the-police-response-to-domestic-abuse/

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- The 2014 inspection became the baseline for a sequence of later follow-up inspections rather than a one-off report.
- Later HMIC/HMICFRS work specifically revisited whether forces had strengthened risk identification, supervision, victim response and governance.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- The report required force and national action to improve the consistency and quality of domestic-abuse policing, including leadership, risk assessment, victim safety and monitoring.
- It also recommended that victim views inform Home Office monitoring of police response.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- SURVIVOR_RESPONSE
- ESCALATION_AND_RISK_ASSESSMENT
- INSTITUTIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Chief constables; Home Office; policing bodies as specified by individual recommendations.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Subsequent HMIC/HMICFRS progress inspections demonstrate that recommendations were formally taken forward, but acceptance was not uniform evidence of operational maturity.

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- 2015, 2017 and 2019 follow-up reports document substantial changes in domestic-abuse policing and continuing areas for improvement.
- The longitudinal series is important because it provides evidence that some earlier intervention did alter practice.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- Directly followed by 2015, 2017 and 2019 HMIC/HMICFRS progress reports.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- HMICFRS’s 2021 VAWG inspection explicitly placed later VAWG work in the context of the domestic-abuse reforms begun in 2014.
- Similar concepts—victim voice, risk identification, supervision and consistent national standards—continue to recur, but later VAWG reports cover a broader problem than domestic abuse alone.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- There is independent evidence of substantial implementation and improvement, but the continuing national inspection sequence shows durability and consistency required repeated assurance. This is not evidence that the intervention failed wholesale; it is evidence that implementation was a process, not an event.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED EXPLANATION:** PARTIAL_IMPLEMENTATION; IMPLEMENTED_BUT_NOT_EMBEDDED in some settings; later scope also genuinely broader.

## RR-03 — Rape Review and Operation Soteria

**ID:** RR-03  
**TITLE:** *End-to-End Rape Review* / subsequent Operation Soteria implementation  
**DATE:** 18 June 2021 onward; national operating model launched 2023  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** HM Government; policing/CPS partners; later HMICFRS inspection  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Rape and serious sexual offence investigation and prosecution  
**TRIGGER:** Collapse/serious deterioration in rape case progression and victim confidence  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/end-to-end-rape-review-report-on-findings-and-actions

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- The review identified systemic problems in rape case handling and led to a more suspect-focused, victim-centred and context-led investigative model.
- Operation Soteria’s design includes attention to repeat suspects and predatory patterns rather than placing investigative attention primarily on victim credibility.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- Develop and roll out a transformed investigative model; subsequently embodied in the Operation Soteria National Operating Model.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- PATTERN_RECOGNITION
- SURVIVOR_RESPONSE
- TRAINING
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Home Office; policing; CPS; College of Policing; relevant national Soteria structures.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Adopted nationally. HMICFRS’s 2024–25 annual assessment states all forces in England and Wales had formally adopted Operation Soteria as the standard for investigating rape and serious sexual offences.

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- HMICFRS reports that most forces had trained large numbers of investigators, specialist staff and first-line responders.
- This is operational evidence beyond publication of an action plan.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- HMICFRS was commissioned to inspect 19 early-adopter forces.
- Angiolini Part 2 subsequently found that Soteria was not being fully implemented in some forces.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- Angiolini Part 2 Recommendation 24 called for plans for **full, consistent and sustainable** implementation across all forces, with multi-year funding until fully implemented and evaluated.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- There is clear evidence of national adoption, training and substantive operational change. There is also primary-source evidence that the model was not yet fully or consistently embedded across all forces. The later recommendation is therefore about durability and standardisation, not proof that nothing changed.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED EXPLANATION:** PARTIAL_IMPLEMENTATION; LOCAL_NOT_SYSTEMIC_IMPLEMENTATION; FUNDING_OR_CAPACITY_CONSTRAINT where Angiolini’s funding recommendation bears on sustainability.

## RR-04 — Police-perpetrated domestic abuse super-complaint

**ID:** RR-04  
**TITLE:** *Police perpetrated domestic abuse: Report on the Centre for Women’s Justice super-complaint*  
**DATE:** 30 June 2022  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** HMICFRS, College of Policing and IOPC  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Police forces in England and Wales; domestic abuse where suspect is police officer/staff  
**TRIGGER:** Centre for Women’s Justice super-complaint  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-super-complaints-force-response-to-police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse/police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse-report-on-the-centre-for-womens-justice-super-complaint

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- The investigation identified **systemic weaknesses**.
- Too many PPDA allegations were not appropriately treated as complaints/conduct matters in line with statutory guidance and legislation.
- Police could not reliably describe the scale and nature of PPDA because recording and monitoring were inconsistent.
- Crucially for this node, the investigators noted that recent new initiatives could not yet be treated as evidence of improved case quality or outcomes because their reviewed cases pre-dated those changes.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- Recommendation 1 required chief constables to audit live PPDA cases and those closed in the preceding 12 months, take corrective action, and explain how their force had or would improve:
  - complaint/conduct handling;
  - compliance with APP or reasons for derogation;
  - monitoring;
  - impartial, joined-up criminal and conduct investigations;
  - victim engagement;
  - deployment decisions for officers under investigation;
  - embedding of the super-complaint findings.
- Other recommendations/actions addressed independent investigators, specialist victim support, national data, guidance and training.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- INFORMATION_SHARING
- SURVIVOR_RESPONSE
- VETTING_AND_MISCONDUCT
- ESCALATION_AND_RISK_ASSESSMENT
- INSTITUTIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY
- TRAINING
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Chief constables; NPCC; Home Office; MoJ; PCCs; College of Policing; IOPC, depending recommendation.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Formal responses were published. NPCC obtained responses from all Home Office forces; relevant government bodies accepted specified recommendations, including MoJ acceptance of recommendation 3(b).

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- NPCC follow-up described force audits and planned/actual changes.
- College and IOPC published further implementation updates in September 2024.
- Some forces developed specialist PPDA structures, flags and audit mechanisms.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- The recommendation itself built audit into implementation.
- National bodies published response updates, but the available public record is stronger on actions taken than on a fully independent, all-force test of durability.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- Casey’s 2023 review found continuing Met failures in misconduct intelligence, vetting, victim engagement and pattern recognition.
- The overlap is substantial in safeguard architecture but not identical in case type: Casey examined broader misconduct and police predation, not only PPDA.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- There is evidence of acceptance, audits and operational changes. There is insufficient independent evidence that the package became consistently durable across all forces, and the original report itself warned against treating newly announced measures as demonstrated improvement.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED EXPLANATION:** PARTIAL_IMPLEMENTATION; LOCAL_NOT_SYSTEMIC_IMPLEMENTATION; FRAGMENTED_OWNERSHIP; INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_TO_DETERMINE durability nationally.

## RR-05 — National vetting, misconduct and misogyny inspection

**ID:** RR-05  
**TITLE:** *An inspection of vetting, misconduct, and misogyny in the police service*  
**DATE:** 2 November 2022  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** HMICFRS  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Police vetting, counter-corruption and misconduct in England and Wales  
**TRIGGER:** Serious concerns about police integrity following high-profile cases including abuse by serving officers  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/an-inspection-of-vetting-misconduct-and-misogyny-in-the-police-service/

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- HMICFRS found too many vetting processes below expected standards, creating routes by which unsuitable people could enter or remain in policing.
- It also found insufficient proactive counter-corruption activity and missed opportunities to identify workforce risk.
- Subsequent Home Office material records that HMICFRS’s May 2023 rapid review found some good progress but continuing need to improve vetting standards.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- 43 recommendations were made across vetting, misconduct and counter-corruption, including stronger vetting standards and action on workforce risk.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- VETTING_AND_MISCONDUCT
- PATTERN_RECOGNITION
- INFORMATION_SHARING
- INSTITUTIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Chief constables, College of Policing, NPCC and other policing bodies according to recommendation.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Chief officers committed to addressing relevant recommendations; subsequent Vetting Code/APP and government reform work strengthened the framework.

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- Revised Vetting Code of Practice in July 2023.
- Later statutory vetting framework introduced in 2025 and amended regulations came into force in July 2026.
- This is material operational/legal change, not merely a policy announcement.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- HMICFRS rapid review, published May 2023.
- Casey, Home Office dismissals review and Angiolini provide further external testing of adjacent safeguards.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- Casey 2023 and Angiolini 2024/2025 identified continued failures or gaps in vetting, transfer information and treatment of adverse information.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- The regulatory architecture materially strengthened, but later inquiries demonstrate that the earlier framework had not yet produced uniformly effective vetting and workforce-risk management. The 2025/2026 statutory regime is too recent for evidence of long-term effectiveness.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED EXPLANATION:** PARTIAL_IMPLEMENTATION; WEAK_IMPLEMENTATION; LOCAL_NOT_SYSTEMIC_IMPLEMENTATION; later LEGAL/REGULATORY GAP addressed by subsequent reform.

## RR-06 — Casey Review

**ID:** RR-06  
**TITLE:** *Baroness Casey Review Final Report*  
**DATE:** March 2023  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** Baroness Louise Casey of Blackstock  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Metropolitan Police Service  
**TRIGGER:** Sarah Everard’s murder by Wayne Couzens and subsequent concern about Met culture, standards and misconduct  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://www.met.police.uk/police-forces/metropolitan-police/areas/about-us/about-the-met/bcr/baroness-casey-review/

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- Met vetting was insufficiently alert to warning signs including previous indecent exposure and domestic abuse.
- Transfer and re-vetting processes could rely heavily on prior clearance/self-declaration.
- In an Operation Rainier sample, adverse information linked to sexual misconduct/domestic abuse was not always shared from vetting into misconduct intelligence.
- Carrick’s history illustrates the pattern-recognition problem: allegations, non-crime incidents, arrests and cross-force information accumulated over years, but the Met only constructed the broader troubling pattern after the later rape charge.
- Casey also found a governance problem: audit findings repeatedly identified issues that were not adequately acted upon; audit was too often treated as a process to get through rather than an assurance mechanism.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- Recommendation 3: immediate changes to vetting, with end-to-end processes throughout service and a relentless focus on identifying predators who seek or exploit policing powers.
- Wider recommendations called for misconduct reform, ethical standards embedded from recruitment through supervision and misconduct, and stronger specialist vetting for firearms.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- INFORMATION_SHARING
- PATTERN_RECOGNITION
- VETTING_AND_MISCONDUCT
- ESCALATION_AND_RISK_ASSESSMENT
- INSTITUTIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Metropolitan Police Commissioner / MPS; MOPAC oversight where applicable.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Commissioner accepted the gravity of the findings and Met reform programmes were developed in response, including *A New Met for London* and intensified integrity/vetting work.

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- Met subsequently undertook large-scale checks/re-vetting and specialist public-protection / police-perpetrated abuse reforms.
- National reforms also changed the surrounding vetting/dismissal framework.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- MOPAC/HMICFRS and other external oversight continued.
- Angiolini later tested overlapping national safeguards and found uneven implementation in several areas.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- Angiolini Part 1/2 revisited vetting, transfers, adverse information, sexual offending warning signs and the ability of a devolved 40+ force system to implement national standards consistently.
- Carrick is separately within Angiolini Part 3; that report had not been published by 6 September 2026.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- There is evidence of substantial reform activity, but the breadth of Casey’s recommendations and later evidence of continuing national inconsistency make GREEN inappropriate. The key implementation question remains whether reforms became routine, supervised and independently assured over time.

**EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED EXPLANATION:** WEAK_IMPLEMENTATION of earlier controls; FRAGMENTED_OWNERSHIP; IMPLEMENTED_BUT_NOT_AUDITED/ACTED-UPON for some audit findings; STATUS_OR_REPUTATIONAL_DISTORTION is discussed in the institutional context but should be assigned only to individual cases where directly evidenced.

## RR-07 — Police officer dismissals review

**ID:** RR-07  
**TITLE:** *Police officer dismissals: Home Office review*  
**DATE:** 18 September 2023  
**AUTHOR_OR_BODY:** Home Office  
**INSTITUTION_OR_SCOPE:** Police disciplinary, performance and vetting-removal mechanisms in England and Wales  
**TRIGGER:** Casey interim findings; later accelerated by David Carrick’s convictions and concern about the ability to remove unsuitable officers  
**OFFICIAL_SOURCE_URL:** https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-officer-dismissals-home-office-review

**RELEVANT_FINDING:**
- The 2017/2023 professional-practice framework expected officers to maintain vetting, but legislation did not then explicitly require officers to hold and maintain vetting clearance throughout their careers.
- Stakeholders reported inconsistency and uncertainty about the mechanism for removing officers who could no longer hold minimum clearance.
- At the time of review, the Home Office had not been shown evidence of forces using the performance regulations to remove officers for vetting failure.

**RELEVANT_RECOMMENDATION:**
- Create a clearer and effective statutory route linking inability to maintain required vetting clearance to removal from policing.

**RECOMMENDATION_FAMILY:**
- VETTING_AND_MISCONDUCT
- INSTITUTIONAL_ACCOUNTABILITY
- IMPLEMENTATION_AND_AUDIT

**RECOMMENDATION_OWNER:** Home Office; College/NPCC and forces for implementation.

**FORMAL_RESPONSE:**
- Government announced reforms; the statutory scheme followed.

**IMPLEMENTATION_EVIDENCE:**
- Police (Vetting) Regulations 2025 placed a legal duty on officers to hold and maintain appropriate clearance and created a bespoke withdrawal/dismissal process.
- Statutory guidance issued 22 July 2025.
- Amended Vetting Regulations came into force 13 July 2026; College APP expressly states that regulations prevail over inconsistent APP.

**AUDIT_OR_FOLLOW_UP:**
- The statutory framework is operational, but insufficient time has elapsed by September 2026 for robust evidence of long-term effectiveness and consistency.

**NEXT_CASE_OR_LATER_REVIEW:**
- Angiolini Part 2 records the continued evolution of vetting recommendations and disagreement/partial implementation on some specific sexual-offence rules.
- The new statutory dismissal mechanism answers a concrete gap identified in 2023, but does not by itself prove all vetting-quality failures are solved.

**STATUS:** AMBER

**STATUS_JUSTIFICATION:**
- The chain reaches operational law and guidance, which is stronger evidence than an action plan. But the regime is too new to demonstrate durability/effectiveness, so it does not meet the user’s GREEN test.

# PART 3 — RECOMMENDATION-FAMILY CROSSWALK

## FAMILY: INFORMATION_SHARING

**EARLIEST_RELEVANT_REVIEW:**  
2014 domestic-abuse work establishes the broader need for joined-up victim/risk information; the strongest modern policing-specific receipts are 2022 PPDA, Casey 2023 and Angiolini 2024.

**SUBSEQUENT_REVIEWS:**  
PPDA super-complaint 2022 → Casey 2023 → Angiolini Part 1 2024 → stalking 2024 → Angiolini Part 2 2025.

**RECURRING_FAILURE_MODE:**  
Relevant information exists but does not reliably become usable institutional knowledge: it may sit in a different force, different record type, vetting file, misconduct system, non-crime incident, complaint route or principal-crime field.

**RECURRING_RECOMMENDATION:**  
Make adverse information searchable, transferable and visible to the people making risk, vetting, investigation and misconduct decisions; require joined-up investigation and retain prior vetting/adverse-history information.

**WHAT_CHANGED_BETWEEN_REVIEWS:**  
- More explicit national vetting information protocols.
- Transfer/re-vetting rules strengthened.
- Stalking recommendations specify searchable included classifications and interim intelligence reports.
- Digital dashboards/problem profiles increasingly used.
- National VAWG structures now explicitly intelligence-led.

**WHAT_APPARENTLY_DID_NOT_CHANGE:**  
Technology and force boundaries still matter. In 2025, the stalking implementation report recorded system-dependent compliance and “clear challenges” in sharing information between forces and accurate crime recording.

**EVIDENCE_OF_IMPLEMENTATION:**  
Angiolini reports stronger information retention/sharing and NPCC reported Recommendation 10 transfer safeguards implemented; stalking implementation work shows system modifications and interim recording solutions.

**EVIDENCE_OF_DURABILITY:**  
Incomplete. National independent proof that these arrangements consistently surface the right information in later live cases remains limited.

**OPEN_QUESTION:**  
Does the information merely exist and transfer, or is there evidence that supervisors/investigators routinely synthesize it into a perpetrator/risk picture before the next escalation?

## FAMILY: PATTERN_RECOGNITION

**EARLIEST_RELEVANT_REVIEW:**  
Domestic-abuse inspection series; clearer predatory-pattern examples appear in Casey and Soteria.

**SUBSEQUENT_REVIEWS:**  
Casey 2023 → stalking 2024 → Angiolini Part 2 2025; Soteria throughout.

**RECURRING_FAILURE_MODE:**  
Incidents are handled as separate transactions rather than as a course of conduct, repeat-suspect history or escalating pattern.

**RECURRING_RECOMMENDATION:**  
Use intelligence, problem profiles, repeat-suspect analysis and context-led investigation to identify serial/predatory behaviour.

**WHAT_CHANGED_BETWEEN_REVIEWS:**

**WHAT_APPARENTLY_DID_NOT_CHANGE:**  
Formal adoption does not guarantee routine synthesis at first-line or supervisory level. Angiolini 2025 says Soteria was not fully implemented in some forces.

**EVIDENCE_OF_IMPLEMENTATION:**  
All forces formally adopted Soteria; many staff trained. Some stalking forces created analyst posts, dashboards, problem profiles and scrutiny arrangements.

**EVIDENCE_OF_DURABILITY:**  
Not yet demonstrated nationally at the GREEN threshold.

**OPEN_QUESTION:**  
Which mechanism reliably forces a “look across the record” moment before a case closes, restrictions are lifted, vetting is renewed or a complaint is treated as isolated?

# PART 4 — REPEATED RECOMMENDATIONS

## 1. Vetting as an end-to-end career safeguard

**Sequence:** HMICFRS 2022 → Casey 2023 → Home Office dismissals review 2023 → Angiolini 2024/25 → statutory Vetting Regulations 2025/26.

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **B — earlier recommendation extended or strengthened.**

**WHY:**  
The underlying safeguard is stable—unsuitable/predatory personnel should be identified and removed—but later interventions progressively strengthen the remedy:
1. improve vetting quality;
2. treat vetting as end-to-end rather than entry-only;
3. create reliable re-vetting/transfer checks;
4. clarify adverse-information rules;
5. create a statutory duty to maintain clearance;
6. create a bespoke dismissal mechanism.

This is not five reports making an identical recommendation. It is a safeguard being hardened after earlier mechanisms proved incomplete or legally/operationally uncertain.

---

## 2. Turning multiple incidents into a perpetrator pattern

**Sequence:** domestic-abuse risk work → Casey/Carrick 2023 → Soteria → stalking 2024 → Angiolini Recommendation 28 (2025).

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **B/C depending the precise case.**

**WHY:**  
The underlying failure—isolated incident handling obscuring cumulative risk—is recurrent, but remedies differ:
- Soteria uses suspect-focused/context-led investigation;
- stalking requires course-of-conduct recognition, searchable recording and problem profiles;
- vetting requires adverse-history synthesis;
- Angiolini asks for standards explicitly detecting predatory perpetrators.

The pattern-recognition safeguard is substantially shared; the operational machinery is offence/context-specific.

---

## 3. Information must cross organisational/system boundaries

**Sequence:** PPDA 2022 → Casey 2023 → Angiolini Part 1 transfer/information recommendations 2024 → stalking digital/searchability recommendations 2024 → implementation reports 2025.

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **B — same safeguard extended into more explicit technical and governance requirements.**

**WHY:**  
Later reports move from “joined-up working” language toward specified transfer checks, searchable classifications, retained vetting failures, intelligence reports and system capability. The recommendation becomes more concrete because the earlier problem is not simply lack of information; it is lack of **usable, retrievable and decision-linked** information.

---

## 4. Audit cannot stop at publication of an action plan

**Sequence:** 2014 domestic-abuse follow-up architecture → PPDA Recommendation 1 (2022) → Casey audit findings (2023) → stalking Recommendation 11 + six-month updates (2024/25) → Angiolini Part 2 implementation audit (2025).

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **A/B.**

**WHY:**  
“A” applies to the repeated safeguard: inspect implementation and case quality.  
“B” applies where later reviews make the assurance loop more explicit—public action plans, case audits, metrics, victim feedback, problem profiles, national progress reports and reinspection.

---

## 5. Victim voice as operational evidence, not decoration

**Sequence:** 2014 domestic-abuse victim monitoring → 2021 VAWG → Soteria → PPDA 2022 → stalking 2024.

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **B.**

**WHY:**  
Later interventions strengthen the role of victim experience from consultation/monitoring into investigation design, specialist support, training, force-level feedback and quality assurance.

---

## 6. National adoption versus local consistency

**Sequence:** 2021 VAWG national-priority recommendation → 2023 Strategic Policing Requirement → Soteria national adoption → stalking force action plans → Angiolini 2025 critique of inconsistent national implementation.

**RECURRENCE TEST:** **C — same underlying implementation problem, materially different remedies.**

**WHY:**  
The failure is inconsistent local operationalisation. Remedies range from strategic designation to national operating models, force plans, national centres and potential use of mandated/statutory mechanisms. The institutional problem repeats, but the proposed machinery is not identical.

# PART 6 — GAPS

1. **Angiolini Part 2 full formal response:** a definitive consolidated government/policing acceptance table for every Part 2 first-report recommendation was not established in this research pass. Do not mark individual Part 2 recommendations “accepted” without checking the responsible body’s response.

2. **Angiolini Part 3 / Carrick:** Part 3 was established in March 2026, but no completed Part 3 report was identified by 6 September 2026. It should not be treated as if findings are available.

3. **Independent durability test of 2025/26 vetting regime:** the regulations and statutory guidance are operational, but their long-term effect on unsuitable-officer detection/removal is not yet independently demonstrated.

4. **Stalking post-implementation reinspection:** NPCC’s August 2025 report is an important progress source, but it is substantially based on force updates. A later independent national inspection demonstrating durability was not identified.

5. **PPDA national outcome evidence:** actions, audits and guidance updates can be traced; a comprehensive independent national test establishing whether victims now consistently receive impartial investigation remains difficult to establish from the located official material.

6. **Training competence versus completion:** stalking and Soteria provide training-volume evidence. Completion figures do not establish competent pattern recognition, risk escalation or victim response in live cases.

7. **Information-sharing effectiveness:** protocols and digital changes can be documented. Evidence that information is routinely *synthesised into decisions* is thinner than evidence that it can be stored/shared.

8. **Force-by-force variation:** national summaries necessarily blur local differences. A stronger claim about any individual force requires its own action plan, audit and inspection record.

9. **Green classifications are rare by design:** under the requested threshold—implemented **and** shown to remain operational/effective—recent reforms should stay AMBER until durability is independently evidenced.

# PART 7 — HIGH-VALUE FINDINGS FOR POLARIS

## 1. Angiolini Part 2 supplies the missing receipt: acceptance is not implementation.

More than 18 months after Part 1, the Inquiry recorded recommendations that remained incomplete and directly questioned the gap between broad public acceptance statements and actual delivery.

**Primary source:** [Angiolini Inquiry Part 2 First Report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692ee226a245b0985f0343ed/E03342246_Angiolini_Inquiry_Pt2_Accessible.pdf)

**Why it matters:**  
This is unusually clean evidence for the node’s central proposition. No rhetoric about “lessons learned” is required. The Inquiry itself checks the chain.

---

## 2. A national recommendation can be mostly implemented and still leave the original failure mode geographically possible.

Angiolini Part 1 Recommendation 1 was reported complete by 74% of forces by September 2025; 26% had not implemented it.

**Primary source:** [Angiolini Inquiry Part 2 First Report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692ee226a245b0985f0343ed/E03342246_Angiolini_Inquiry_Pt2_Accessible.pdf)

**Why it matters:**  
“Implemented somewhere” and “systemic safeguard” are different claims.

---

## 3. Devolution of operational policing can itself be an implementation variable.

Angiolini Part 2 identifies the ability of more than 40 chief constables to depart from national policy/guidance as a source of inconsistency and notes that members of the public cannot necessarily assume identical standards by postcode.

**Primary source:** [Angiolini Inquiry Part 2 First Report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692ee226a245b0985f0343ed/E03342246_Angiolini_Inquiry_Pt2_Accessible.pdf)

**Why it matters:**  
This supports **FRAGMENTED_OWNERSHIP** and **LOCAL_NOT_SYSTEMIC_IMPLEMENTATION** where the recommendation is national but not mandatory.

---

## 4. Casey demonstrates a “data existed; pattern did not become institutional knowledge” failure.

Carrick accumulated allegations, incidents and contacts over years. Casey records that only after the later rape charge did the Met look back across the wider record and establish the troubling pattern.

**Primary source:** [Baroness Casey Review Final Report](https://www.met.police.uk/police-forces/metropolitan-police/areas/about-us/about-the-met/bcr/baroness-casey-review/)

**Why it matters:**  
The safeguard required is not just more data. It is a mechanism that forces data to become a cumulative risk picture before the next serious event.

---

## 5. Casey also identifies the next failure in the chain: audit can exist without producing correction.

The Review found repeated audit findings were not adequately acted on and described a culture in which audit could be treated as something to get through rather than an assurance mechanism.

**Primary source:** [Baroness Casey Review Final Report](https://www.met.police.uk/police-forces/metropolitan-police/areas/about-us/about-the-met/bcr/baroness-casey-review/)

**Why it matters:**  
A functioning audit department is not evidence that an audited safeguard is functioning.

---

## 6. The PPDA super-complaint explicitly refuses to count new initiatives as proven improvement.

The joint investigators said recent changes were too new to assess through the cases they reviewed.

**Primary source:** [PPDA super-complaint report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-super-complaints-force-response-to-police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse/police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse-report-on-the-centre-for-womens-justice-super-complaint)

**Why it matters:**  
This is a primary-source precedent for the node’s rule: **announcement/action plan ≠ demonstrated operational implementation.**

---

## 7. The PPDA remedy itself contains an implementation architecture.

Recommendation 1 does not merely say “improve.” It requires case audits, corrective action, monitoring, joined-up investigation, victim engagement, deployment decisions and steps to embed findings.

**Primary source:** [PPDA super-complaint report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-super-complaints-force-response-to-police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse/police-perpetrated-domestic-abuse-report-on-the-centre-for-womens-justice-super-complaint)

**Why it matters:**  
Later comparisons can ask which specific link survived.

---

## 8. Stalking provides a literal technical example of the safeguard getting stuck between recommendation and infrastructure.

Recommendation 7 required crimes to be searchable even when not recorded as the principal crime. In 2025, some systems already supported this; others still needed digital development and were using interim flags, free text or intelligence workarounds.

**Primary source:** [NPCC stalking implementation report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/responses-to-the-super-complaint-report-on-the-police-response-to-stalking/npcc-report-on-the-progress-made-by-police-forces-to-act-on-the-recommendations-of-the-stalking-super-complaint)

**Why it matters:**  
Here, “connect the dots” has an actual database field underneath it. A very unromantic little hinge, but a hinge all the same.

---

## 9. Training can show measurable rollout without proving the safeguard is embedded.

Stalking e-learning completions increased sharply, yet the NPCC still recorded gaps in spread/quality and warned against appointing specialist SPOCs/SMEs without specialist training.

**Primary source:** [NPCC stalking implementation report](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/responses-to-the-super-complaint-report-on-the-police-response-to-stalking/npcc-report-on-the-progress-made-by-police-forces-to-act-on-the-recommendations-of-the-stalking-super-complaint)

**Why it matters:**  
Training completion is an implementation link, not the endpoint.

---

## 10. Soteria is evidence both of change and of the difference between adoption and embedding.

HMICFRS says all forces formally adopted Soteria and many staff were trained; Angiolini later says it was not fully implemented in some forces and calls for sustainable, consistent implementation and evaluation.

**Primary sources:**  
- [HMICFRS State of Policing 2024–25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68c1333feeb238b20672a8f9/state-of-policing-2024-25.pdf)
- [Angiolini Inquiry Part 2 First Report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/692ee226a245b0985f0343ed/E03342246_Angiolini_Inquiry_Pt2_Accessible.pdf)

**Why it matters:**  
This is probably the cleanest counterexample to “nothing changes.” Something changed. The later question is whether it became normal everywhere.

---

## 11. Vetting reform shows a recommendation chain reaching all the way into law.

The Home Office’s 2023 dismissals review identified uncertainty over removing officers who could not maintain vetting. The 2025 Vetting Regulations created a legal duty and dismissal route; statutory guidance followed; amended regulations were in force by 13 July 2026.

**Primary sources:**  
- [Home Office dismissals review](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-officer-dismissals-home-office-review)
- [Statutory guidance on withdrawal of police vetting clearance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/withdrawal-of-police-vetting-clearance/statutory-guidance-withdrawal-of-police-vetting-clearance-accessible)
- [College of Policing Vetting Code](https://www.college.police.uk/guidance/vetting-code-practice)

**Why it matters:**  
This is genuine implementation. The outstanding question is durability/effectiveness, not whether anyone acted.

---

## 12. The contemporary reform trend increasingly creates national machinery for consistency.

The National Centre for VAWG and Public Protection was created in 2025 as an intelligence-led national structure, and Angiolini assigns it ownership roles in later recommendations.

**Primary source:** [Home Office: New £13m police centre to tackle VAWG](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/new-13m-police-centre-to-tackle-violence-against-women-and-girls)

**Why it matters:**  
This looks like an institutional response to recurring fragmented ownership. Whether it solves that problem requires later audit.

---

## 13. The domestic-abuse inspection series is the necessary control case.

HMIC/HMICFRS repeatedly followed the 2014 inspection and documented material improvement as well as remaining gaps.

**Primary sources:**  
- [Everyone’s Business](https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/improving-the-police-response-to-domestic-abuse/)
- [Increasingly everyone’s business](https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/increasingly-everyones-business-a-progress-report-on-the-police-response-to-domestic-abuse/)
- [2017 progress report](https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/a-progress-report-on-the-police-response-to-domestic-abuse/)
- [2019 update](https://hmicfrs.justiceinspectorates.gov.uk/publications/the-police-response-to-domestic-abuse-an-update-report/)

**Why it matters:**  
The archive should not accidentally build a survivorship bias in which only failed safeguards are visible. Some interventions improved practice. The forensic question is why particular residual failure modes survived despite that broader improvement.

# 🧿 Working synthesis

The strongest cross-review finding is not:

> “They keep making the same recommendations.”

It is:

> **A relatively stable set of institutional safeguards keeps reappearing at different levels of specificity because the failure can migrate to the next link in the chain.**

A review may solve the policy gap but leave a training gap.  
Training may exist but leave a supervision gap.  
Supervision may exist but lack searchable data.  
Data may exist but remain split across systems.  
An audit may detect the problem but lack a forcing mechanism for corrective action.  
A national standard may exist but remain locally discretionary.  
A model may be adopted everywhere but not yet be routine everywhere.

That is a more demanding claim than “lessons were not learned,” because it requires a receipt for the missing hinge. It is also considerably harder for institutions to answer with another action plan.
