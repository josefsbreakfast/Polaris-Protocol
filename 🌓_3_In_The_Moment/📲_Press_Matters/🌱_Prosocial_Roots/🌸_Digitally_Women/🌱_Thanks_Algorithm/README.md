# 🌱 Thanks, Algorithm

**First created:** 2026-09-06 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-06\
*Misogyny predates the feed. Platform architecture can still change who
encounters it, how often, in what sequence, and with whom.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This folder is about a problem that becomes much harder to understand
when everything is blamed on **the algorithm**.

Misogyny did not begin with recommender systems.

Neither did:

-   male loneliness;
-   sexual entitlement;
-   anti-feminist organising;
-   coercive relationships;
-   extremist politics;
-   harassment;
-   violence.

But digital architecture changes the environment in which those things
circulate.

Platforms can alter:

-   discovery;
-   recommendation;
-   repetition;
-   social context;
-   community formation;
-   creator incentives;
-   visibility;
-   access.

That means two bad explanations need to be rejected at once:

``` text
the algorithm caused misogyny
```

and:

``` text
the technology is irrelevant because misogyny already existed
```

The useful question sits between them:

> **What changes when an old social problem enters a personalised,
> networked, commercially optimised information environment?**

## 🧭 Reading Route

The folder works as a sequence.

### 1. [🌱 Misogyny Is in the Roots](./🌱_misogyny_is_in_the_roots.md)

Start with the substrate.

Misogyny is not an exotic ideology possessed only by obviously
misogynistic people.

Women can internalise it.

Survivors can internalise it.

Institutions can reproduce it.

People can say or do misogynistic things without acquiring a permanent
identity called `misogynist`.

This is why Polaris usually prefers:

> **What did somebody say or do?**

over:

> **What are they?**

The distinction gives the rest of the folder somewhere sensible to
stand.

---

### 2. [👠 You Are Not Getting the Same Feed](./👠_you_are_not_getting_the_same_feed.md)

Then change the information environment.

Two people can use the same platform without receiving the same
internet.

Personalisation means users may encounter different:

-   creators;
-   communities;
-   framings;
-   levels of repetition;
-   routes between topics.

A parent, journalist, regulator or researcher opening a fresh account
may therefore fail to reproduce the environment experienced by another
user over time.

> **The platform may be shared. The feed may not be.**

---

### 3. [🥃 The Manosphere Ecosystem](./🥃_the_manosphere_ecosystem.md)

Then map the ecosystem.

`The manosphere` is useful shorthand but dangerous if treated as one
organisation or one coherent ideology.

The node separates overlapping cultures around:

-   dating;
-   masculinity;
-   self-improvement;
-   grievance;
-   status;
-   anti-feminism;
-   incel communities;
-   red-pill ideas;
-   influencer economies.

Some overlap heavily.

Some barely do.

Some users move between them.

Some do not.

The point is an ecology, not a single pipeline.

---

### 4. [🌱 He Only Wanted Dating Advice](./🌱_he_only_wanted_dating_advice.md)

Then look at the entrance.

People do not need to search for misogyny.

They may search for:

-   dating advice;
-   confidence;
-   fitness;
-   friendship;
-   money;
-   masculinity;
-   help with rejection;
-   somewhere to belong.

Those are legitimate needs.

The analytical hinge is what explanations, communities and behavioural
scripts become attached to them.

> **An ordinary need can be an entrance point without being evidence of
> radicalisation.**

That matters enormously for prevention.

If ordinary male distress is pathologised, grievance entrepreneurs get a
very easy recruitment advantage.

---

### 5. [📈 How the Feed Changes the Ecology](./📈_how_the_feed_changes_the_ecology.md)

Now introduce the distribution mechanics.

Separate:

-   creation;
-   discovery;
-   recommendation;
-   repetition;
-   reinforcement;
-   active searching;
-   community circulation;
-   creator optimisation.

This avoids the cartoon:

``` text
evil algorithm
      ↓
helpless user
      ↓
extremism
```

Users retain agency.

Creators make choices.

Communities circulate material.

Platforms still make consequential decisions about:

-   availability;
-   sequence;
-   frequency;
-   social context.

> **The feed does not have to create the ecosystem to change it.**

---

### 6. [🕸️ From Online Grievance to Offline Harm](./🕸️_from_online_grievance_to_offline_harm.md)

Then cross the screen/body boundary carefully.

The node maps several possible routes:

-   gendered entitlement becoming interpersonal behaviour;
-   parasocial fixation;
-   stalking and location exposure;
-   digitally networked sexual violence;
-   peer reinforcement;
-   swarm harassment;
-   image-based abuse;
-   ideological mobilisation.

It deliberately refuses:

``` text
bad content
    ↓
violence
```

Exposure is not belief.

Belief is not behaviour.

Behaviour is not automatically crime.

But digital life is still embodied social life.

> **The screen is not a causal magic portal. It is also not a wall.**

---

### 7. [🧠 Radicalisation Is Not a Sentencing Factor](./🧠_radicalisation_is_not_a_sentencing_factor.md)

Then stop the next analytical collapse.

Research and prevention may legitimately ask:

> `how did this pathway develop?`

Safeguarding may ask:

> `where could escalation be interrupted?`

A criminal court asks different questions about:

-   proved conduct;
-   motive;
-   hostility;
-   intent;
-   legally relevant aggravation and mitigation.

`Radicalised online` should not become a free-floating substitute for
evidence.

The node keeps:

-   prevention;
-   safeguarding;
-   investigation;
-   criminal attribution;
-   sentencing

in their proper lanes.

---

### 8. [📲 Online Safety and Platforms](./📲_online_safety_and_platforms.md)

Finally, ask what can actually be changed.

Platforms possess real infrastructural power over:

-   recommendation;
-   amplification;
-   discovery;
-   moderation;
-   friction;
-   reporting;
-   children's experiences;
-   evidence preservation;
-   research access;
-   creator incentives.

Those are legitimate objects of regulation.

Platforms cannot independently solve:

-   misogyny;
-   loneliness;
-   fascism;
-   abusive relationships;
-   safeguarding failures;
-   criminal violence.

> **Ask platforms to govern the infrastructure they actually control.
> Then make the rest of the system do its job too.**

## 🗺️ The Whole Argument

``` text
🌱 misogyny already exists in the culture
        ↓
👠 users do not receive identical information environments
        ↓
🥃 overlapping male-coded ecosystems organise material and belonging
        ↓
🌱 ordinary needs can provide non-ideological entrance points
        ↓
📈 feeds alter availability, sequence, repetition and reward
        ↓
🕸️ digital information can acquire embodied consequences
        ↓
🧠 prevention analysis must not become free-floating criminal aggravation
        ↓
📲 platforms have real but bounded intervention powers
```

No stage requires the next one.

That is important.

This is not:

> **THE PIPELINE.**

It is a map of mechanisms that can interact.

People:

-   refuse;
-   leave;
-   move sideways;
-   disagree;
-   mature;
-   find better communities;
-   encounter counter-speech;
-   never become violent.

A serious prevention model has to preserve those exits.

## 🧿 Do Not Confuse Visibility With Causation

A recurring problem in online-harm analysis is that digital evidence is
unusually visible.

We can often see:

-   posts;
-   searches;
-   likes;
-   follows;
-   recommendations;
-   group membership.

Other influences may be harder to reconstruct:

-   family;
-   friends;
-   relationships;
-   school;
-   workplace;
-   local culture;
-   economic circumstances;
-   offline political communities.

That can produce an evidential distortion:

> **the thing easiest to screenshot becomes the thing assumed to have
> caused everything.**

Digital evidence can be extremely important.

It still has to be interpreted.

## 🧯 Do Not Criminalise the Entrance Ramp

The folder repeatedly returns to one prevention principle:

> **ordinary need is not evidence of dangerousness.**

A boy looking for:

-   help dating;
-   friends;
-   confidence;
-   masculinity;
-   sexual information

has not committed an offence by needing help.

A man experiencing:

-   loneliness;
-   rejection;
-   divorce;
-   humiliation;
-   economic insecurity

has not thereby become an extremist.

Prevention becomes self-defeating if the people most in need of
prosocial alternatives learn that asking ordinary questions will make
institutions treat them as suspects.

The intervention has to be better than the grievance entrepreneur's
answer.

## 🛠️ The Intervention Surface

The folder identifies several different places where action can occur.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Layer                               Useful question
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  culture                             what misogynistic assumptions are
                                      already normalised?

  individual need                     what was the person actually
                                      looking for?

  community                           what belonging, vocabulary and
                                      status are being supplied?

  distribution                        what is becoming easier to find,
                                      repeat and move between?

  platform design                     what is recommended, amplified,
                                      monetised or made difficult?

  safeguarding                        what signs suggest a real person is
                                      becoming unsafe?

  criminal justice                    what conduct, motive or hostility
                                      has actually been proved?

  prevention                          what viable exit or alternative can
                                      be offered before harm occurs?
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The point is not to locate one master lever.

It is to stop asking one institution to perform every function.

## 🌱 Prosocial Roots

This folder sits under `🌱_Prosocial_Roots/` for a reason.

The goal is not merely:

> `identify bad men`

or:

> `delete bad content`

It is to ask what makes healthier social pathways viable.

That can mean:

-   better dating advice;
-   friendship;
-   community;
-   meaningful work;
-   emotional literacy;
-   relationship education;
-   consent;
-   accessible survivor support;
-   non-dominating models of masculinity;
-   platform architecture that does not reward predation;
-   institutions capable of recognising escalation;
-   technological environments designed around human flourishing rather
    than endless extraction.

The constructive technological lineage continues in:

[🌞 We Could Build Nicer
Things](../📖_Previously_On_The_Internet/🌞_we_could_build_nicer_things.md).

Because the choice is not:

``` text
harmful technology
vs
no technology
```

We can build different systems.

## 🏮 Footer

*Thanks, Algorithm* is a living cluster of the **Polaris Protocol**.\
It examines how pre-existing misogyny and ordinary human needs interact
with personalised feeds, networked communities, creator economies and
platform architecture. The cluster separates exposure from belief,
belief from behaviour, prevention from punishment, and platform
responsibility from social omnipotence so that intervention can be
directed at the mechanisms that actually exist.
