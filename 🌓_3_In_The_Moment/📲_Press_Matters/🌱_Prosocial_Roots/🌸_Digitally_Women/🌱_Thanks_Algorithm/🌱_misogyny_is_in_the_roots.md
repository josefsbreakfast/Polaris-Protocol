# 🌱 Misogyny Is in the Roots

**First created:** 2026-09-06 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-06\
*Misogyny does not begin at the boundary of an extremist community.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🛰️ Orientation

Misogyny repeatedly appears across manosphere, incel, some far-right and
fascist, and other violence-associated radical ecosystems.

That recurrence matters.

It does **not** mean that misogyny automatically produces extremism.

It does not mean that everybody who says something sexist is travelling
toward violence.

And it does not mean that all of these communities belong to one
movement or share one genealogy.

The more useful question is:

> **What work does misogyny perform inside particular grievance
> systems?**

To answer that properly, we have to begin further upstream.

Misogyny does not originate inside radical communities. We live in
societies in which misogynistic assumptions already circulate through
families, schools, workplaces, relationships, entertainment,
pornography, religion, politics, medicine, policing, advertising and
ordinary conversation.

Online communities inherit people from that world.

> **The internet did not invent the roots.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌱 Women Internalise Misogyny Too

Women are not raised outside gendered culture.

Neither are survivors. Neither are feminists. Neither are people
professionally responsible for protecting women.

A woman can reproduce a misogynistic argument. A survivor can reproduce
a misogynistic argument. A feminist can reproduce a misogynistic
argument. An institution established to protect women can reproduce
misogynistic assumptions.

None of this requires treating those people as secretly belonging to an
anti-woman political movement.

Internalisation is one mechanism through which culture reproduces
itself.

We learn gender inside societies which already contain assumptions about
femininity, sexuality, credibility, desirability, motherhood,
aggression, authority, vulnerability and the proper behaviour of women.
Some of those assumptions are misogynistic. They can be internalised
without being consciously endorsed.

> **Identity does not provide automatic immunity from culture.**

## 🗣️ What Did Someone Actually Say?

For this reason, Polaris usually prefers a narrower analytical route.

Ask:

-   What did somebody say?
-   What did somebody do?
-   What assumption does it reproduce?
-   What effect does it have?

Before reaching immediately for:

-   What **are** they?

`That argument is misogynistic` is a narrower claim than
`you are a misogynist`.

The former can often be evidenced directly. It also creates somewhere
for feedback to go.

People can recognise an inherited assumption, reconsider it, change
their mind, change their language and change their behaviour.

Criticism should ideally increase the possibility of correction.

This is not an excuse-making device.

Repeated behaviour matters. Deliberate behaviour matters. Power matters.
Refusal to correct after feedback matters. Organised misogyny matters.

The distinction simply prevents one proposition from automatically
becoming a total theory of a human being.

It also helps distinguish **ambient prejudice from ideological
cultivation**.

## 🌿 Why Call It a Root?

Misogyny can supply concepts that later systems do not need to invent.

For example:

-   women owe men sex;
-   men's social status is partly measured through access to women;
-   femininity is inferior;
-   women who reject men are cruel, manipulative or illegitimate;
-   women's sexual autonomy threatens male status;
-   women require control;
-   male dominance is natural;
-   female authority is suspicious;
-   women who violate gender expectations deserve punishment;
-   sexual humiliation can legitimately restore hierarchy.

These ideas exist in stronger and weaker forms throughout ordinary
culture.

Most people exposed to them do **not** become violent extremists.

But an ideological system looking for material about grievance,
entitlement, domination, status anxiety, rigid gender roles, sexual
control or dehumanisation does not necessarily have to begin from bare
earth.

> **The radical ecosystem does not have to plant every root. It can
> cultivate what is already there.**

## 🪴 Cultivation Is the Important Bit

Think less:

``` text
normal boy
    ↓
sees misogyny
    ↓
extremist
```

Think more:

``` text
gendered culture
      ↓
ordinary internalisation
      ↓
life experience
      ↓
grievance / insecurity / disappointment
      ↓
an explanatory community encounters it
      ↓
some explanations are socially rewarded
      ↓
grievance can become entitlement
      ↓
entitlement can become hierarchy
      ↓
hierarchy can legitimate domination
```

That sequence is not inevitable.

People can enter and leave at multiple points. Different communities
intervene differently. Protective relationships, competing explanations
and ordinary maturation matter too.

The analytical questions are:

-   Which ideas are being reinforced?
-   What interpretation is being attached to someone's experience?
-   What behaviour does the community reward?
-   What becomes normal after prolonged participation?

## 🧠 Grievance Needs an Explanation

Bad things happen to people.

Rejection hurts. Loneliness hurts. Humiliation hurts. Unemployment
hurts. Status loss hurts. Feeling unattractive hurts. Being frightened
about sex hurts. Feeling that adulthood has not delivered the life you
expected can hurt enormously.

None of those feelings is inherently misogynistic.

Political and social environments become important when they answer:

> **Why is this happening to you?**

Compare:

-   relationships are difficult;
-   dating norms have changed;
-   you are lonely;
-   your social skills need work;
-   economic insecurity is making adulthood harder;
-   you're having a shit year;

with:

-   women have been given too much power;
-   feminism stole the life you were owed;
-   women deliberately select against men like you;
-   women's autonomy caused your suffering;
-   restoring hierarchy would restore your status.

The emotional input may be genuine in both cases.

The **causal story** changes.

> **Pain is not ideology. The explanation attached to pain can become
> ideological.**

## 👑 Entitlement Is a Particularly Important Hinge

Desire is not entitlement. Loneliness is not entitlement. Wanting sex is
not entitlement. Wanting partnership is not entitlement. Wanting status
is not entitlement.

Entitlement enters when another person's autonomy is interpreted as an
illegitimate obstacle to something one is owed.

That makes misogyny useful to some grievance systems because rigid
gender hierarchy can transform:

``` text
"I want"
    ↓
"I deserve"

"she chose"
    ↓
"I was denied"

"relationships changed"
    ↓
"something was stolen"

"I am struggling"
    ↓
"women caused this"
```

Once autonomy itself becomes the grievance, coercive solutions become
easier to rationalise.

## 📊 Status Anxiety Has Gender

Masculinity can be socially measured through sexual success,
partnership, money, strength, dominance, professional status and
perceived respect from other men.

That means a relationship with women can become simultaneously intimate,
sexual, emotional and a status signal within male hierarchy.

Misogynistic systems can therefore promise not merely access to women
but restoration of **rank**.

This helps explain why apparently private sexual grievance can become
political.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🪞 Femininity Can Be Policed in Men Too

Misogyny does not only regulate women.

Systems organised around male superiority often require policing men
away from whatever has been coded feminine:

-   vulnerability;
-   dependence;
-   softness;
-   care;
-   fear;
-   emotional openness.

That can make misogyny costly for boys and men themselves.

If the available masculine response to pain is domination, anger or
status restoration, other responses become harder to inhabit.

A genuinely prosocial intervention therefore cannot merely say:

> **Be nicer to women.**

It also has to make available friendship, dignity, emotional vocabulary,
competence, belonging, non-dominating models of masculinity, and routes
through rejection and failure that do not require an enemy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌐 Different Ecosystems Can Use the Same Root Differently

Do not collapse incels, red-pill communities, men's rights activists,
pickup cultures, manosphere influencers, fascists, white supremacists,
religious fundamentalists and violent actors into one political
movement.

They are not interchangeable.

Their histories, objectives, membership and relationships to violence
differ.

The useful comparative question is narrower:

> **What work does misogyny perform inside this particular system?**

Possible answers include:

-   producing an out-group;
-   explaining personal disappointment;
-   restoring hierarchy;
-   policing masculinity;
-   generating solidarity between men;
-   legitimising sexual control;
-   providing humiliation rituals;
-   converting status anxiety into political grievance;
-   normalising domination;
-   dehumanising targets.

Recurrence across different ecosystems is analytically significant even
when genealogy is not shared.

## 🌱 Thanks, Algorithm

And **then** the algorithm enters.

The algorithm is not the root.

It did not invent misogyny, sexual entitlement, status anxiety, gender
hierarchy, loneliness or male grievance.

But recommender architecture can affect:

-   discovery;
-   repetition;
-   reinforcement;
-   adjacency;
-   social proof;
-   escalation;
-   speed.

The distinction matters:

``` text
misogyny exists
      ↓
communities organise it
      ↓
creators package it
      ↓
platform systems distribute it
      ↓
recommenders can alter
who encounters what next
```

That is a stronger analytical proposition than:

> `the algorithm makes boys misogynists`

The question for this cluster is what happens when an information
environment begins actively selecting, ranking and recommending which
adjacent environment a person encounters next.

> **The algorithm does not need to create the root in order to water
> it.**

## 🧯 Why This Matters for Prevention

If misogyny only becomes interesting after terrorism, murder, assault,
stalking or organised extremist affiliation, then we have deliberately
selected the latest possible intervention point.

Upstream work can examine:

-   entitlement;
-   dehumanisation;
-   coercive attitudes;
-   rigid gender hierarchy;
-   grievance narratives;
-   social rewards for domination;
-   communities normalising violence.

But upstream prevention should **not** mean treating every teenage boy
who says something sexist as a proto-terrorist.

That would be analytically absurd and potentially counterproductive.

> **Upstream does not mean pre-criminal.**
>
> It means understanding the social conditions and ideas that can become
> useful ingredients in more dangerous systems.

The point is not to widen the terrorism net until ordinary adolescence
falls inside it.

The point is to recognise opportunities for healthier intervention long
before a terrorism framework would be appropriate.

## 🌿 Because the Roots Are Cultural, Intervention Can Be Cultural Too

People learn misogyny.

People can also learn:

-   reciprocity;
-   consent;
-   emotional literacy;
-   respect for autonomy;
-   non-dominating masculinity;
-   tolerance of rejection;
-   ways of surviving humiliation;
-   ways of finding status through competence rather than control;
-   ways of disagreeing without dehumanising.

Communities can reward those things. Schools can teach them. Men can
model them. Women can model them. Media can normalise them. Platforms
can choose not to systematically reward their opposites.

None of that requires believing prejudice can be engineered out of
humanity.

It requires recognising that:

> **Social environments cultivate things.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌱 Roots Are Not Destiny

Misogyny matters upstream because it is both ordinary enough to be
widely available and adaptable enough to perform useful work inside very
different grievance systems.

Its presence does not tell us that somebody is an extremist.

Its recurrence across violence-associated ecosystems tells us that we
should understand what those ecosystems are doing with it.

The task is not to divide humanity cleanly into misogynists and
non-misogynists.

We are all formed inside gendered cultures, and none of us gains
automatic immunity through identity, politics or survivorship.

Ask instead:

-   What is being said?
-   What assumptions are being reproduced?
-   What behaviour is being rewarded?
-   What explanation is being offered for pain?
-   What happens when somebody challenges it?

> ## **ROOTS ARE NOT DESTINY.**
>
> But if you want to understand what grows, it helps to know what is in
> the soil.
