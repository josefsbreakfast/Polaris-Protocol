# 🌞 We Could Build Nicer Things
**First created:** 2026-09-05 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-05  
*The present technological settlement is not the only technological settlement available to us.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

A great deal of contemporary technology politics accidentally presents a false choice:

```text
today's technology
        ↕
   no technology
```

That is not the choice.

Criticising surveillance, addictive design, inaccessible systems, extractive platforms, exploitative labour, discriminatory models, environmentally expensive infrastructure or concentrated technological power does not require rejecting technological development.

There is a long constructive lineage running through:

- cyberfeminism;
- xenofeminism;
- feminist HCI;
- feminist science and technology studies;
- disability justice;
- design justice;
- accessibility;
- queer technological experimentation;
- community technology;
- mutual-aid infrastructure;
- rehabilitative technology;
- solarpunk.

These traditions disagree about plenty.

They nevertheless make one possibility difficult to ignore:

> **Systems can be built around different assumptions about bodies, relationships, autonomy, safety, accessibility, community and power.**

This node is about that possibility.

**Question answered:** Fine. What does the less-shit technological future look like?

---

## 🌱 Critique Is Not the Destination

Knowing why a technological system is harmful is useful.

It does not automatically tell us what should replace it.

Feminist technological politics cannot permanently remain:

```text
technology appears
      ↓
women get harmed
      ↓
document harm
      ↓
request mitigation
```

Women are not merely the people technology happens to.

Nor are disabled people, queer and trans people, racialised communities, survivors, poor people, patients or children.

They are also:

- builders;
- researchers;
- maintainers;
- organisers;
- legislators;
- artists;
- clinicians;
- hackers;
- moderators;
- carers;
- users;
- designers;
- critics;
- owners;
- sources of technical knowledge.

The constructive question is:

> **What happens when the people usually treated as edge cases participate in defining the system itself?**

## 🕹️ The Cyberfeminist Inheritance

[🕹️ Cyberfeminism Before the Algorithm](./🕹️_cyberfeminism_before_the_algorithm.md) supplies the historical opening.

Feminist technological politics once asked questions considerably larger than how a platform should moderate harassment.

It asked:

- What is a body?
- What is natural?
- What kinds of identities can technology enable?
- What kinds of relationships can networks produce?
- Who gets to inhabit technological space?
- What might technologically mediated life become?

Some early cyberfeminism was excessively optimistic about cyberspace.

That does not make technological imagination itself the mistake.

> **The correction to technological utopianism is not technological fatalism.**

---

## 🪬 Xenofeminism: Existing Arrangements Are Not Sacred

[🪬 Xenofeminism and the Engineered Body](./🪬_xenofeminism_and_the_engineered_body.md) supplies the next move.

If something is technologically constructed, institutionally maintained, historically contingent or politically organised, it can potentially be reorganised.

Antinaturalism matters because existing arrangements do not acquire moral authority merely by appearing inevitable.

Neither does technological intervention automatically produce liberation.

The practical question becomes:

> **What should we deliberately redesign?**

---

## 🧑‍💻 Feminist HCI: The Interface Has Politics

Human-computer interaction is not merely about whether a button is easy to find.

Design assumptions can encode ideas about:

- who the user is;
- what the user wants;
- what counts as success;
- which errors matter;
- what behaviour should be encouraged;
- how much attention should be captured.

Feminist HCI has developed ways of asking about participation, pluralism, embodiment, ecology, advocacy and the values built into design processes.

A system designed for an abstract default user will encounter actual humans eventually.

Better to include them earlier.

---

## 🔬 Feminist STS: The Machine Has a History

Feminist science and technology studies asks us to look beyond the artefact.

Technologies emerge from:

- institutions;
- funding;
- research priorities;
- labour;
- classification;
- cultural assumptions;
- political economies.

A model is not merely code.

A medical device is not merely hardware.

A platform is not merely an interface.

Ask:

- Who defined the problem?
- Who funded the solution?
- Who supplied the data?
- Who performed the labour?
- Who was imagined as the user?
- Who was missing?
- Who owns the resulting infrastructure?

> **Technology has politics before anybody switches it on.**

---

## 🧩 Design Justice: Start Somewhere Else

Design justice shifts attention away from designing *for* supposedly vulnerable communities and toward design processes in which affected communities possess meaningful power.

This matters because the person who experiences a system may know something its designer does not.

A survivor may recognise an abuse affordance.

A disabled person may recognise an access failure.

A sex worker may recognise where a safety intervention actually moves risk.

A clinician may recognise a biologically absurd abstraction.

A community organiser may recognise what happens when a platform changes one apparently minor rule.

These are not annoying anecdotes surrounding the technical work.

> **They are information about the system.**

---

## ♿ Accessibility Is Not an Accommodation Queue

Accessibility should not mean:

1. build the system;
2. discover disabled people;
3. bolt something onto it;
4. require each person to request an exception.

Designing for bodily and cognitive variation from the beginning can produce systems that are more flexible, legible, robust and forgiving.

Accessibility also extends beyond interfaces.

It includes:

- cost;
- transport;
- time;
- energy;
- language;
- sensory environment;
- cognition;
- care requirements;
- device compatibility;
- network access.

> **The person should not perpetually have to petition the machine for permission to exist in its environment.**

---

## 🫀 Care Can Be an Architectural Requirement

Care-centred design does not mean pastel interfaces, excessive anthropomorphism or software pretending to love you.

It means designing around the fact that users may sometimes be:

- frightened;
- exhausted;
- grieving;
- disabled;
- inexperienced;
- distracted;
- poor;
- caring for somebody else;
- in pain;
- having a shit day.

Ask:

- Can the person stop?
- Can they come back?
- Can they understand what happened?
- Can they undo an error?
- Can they leave?
- Does the system punish reduced engagement?
- Does it escalate unnecessarily?
- Does it preserve dignity when something goes wrong?

Care is therefore not merely a tone.

> **Care can be a system property.**

## 🫂 Community Technology

Not every useful technological relationship needs to run:

```text
person
  ↓
corporation
  ↓
another person
```

Technology can support lateral community capability through:

- community networks;
- local knowledge repositories;
- repair culture;
- maker spaces;
- mutual-aid tools;
- community archives;
- cooperative infrastructure;
- federated systems;
- locally governed digital spaces.

Community technology can preserve knowledge, memory, skills, relationships and resilience.

It can also fail.

Communities can reproduce hierarchy, exclusion, prejudice and informal power.

"Community" is not a magic ethical property.

But communities can possess forms of governance and knowledge that centralised platforms cannot reproduce.

## 🏳️‍🌈 Queer Technological Experimentation

Queer communities have repeatedly used technology in ways its builders did not necessarily anticipate.

This can include:

- pseudonymity;
- multiple identities;
- chosen-family infrastructure;
- community moderation;
- health information;
- sexual cultures;
- maker practices;
- alternative archives;
- mutual aid.

People excluded from default systems often become unusually skilled at repurposing, routing around, modifying, interpreting and rebuilding them.

Difference is therefore not merely something a designer must accommodate.

> **Difference can produce design knowledge.**

## ☀️ Solarpunk Cyberfeminism Exists, Guys

Solarpunk contributes something political theory frequently lacks:

> **somewhere desirable to look.**

Dystopia is very good at showing what could go wrong, what already has gone wrong and where power is accumulating.

It is less good at showing what ordinary life might feel like after people make different choices.

Solarpunk experiments with futures organised around combinations of:

- renewable energy;
- ecological repair;
- local infrastructure;
- community;
- technological usefulness;
- sufficiency;
- repair;
- coexistence.

It is not a policy programme.

It is political imagination.

That matters because people cannot deliberately build futures they are unable to imagine.

> **Political imagination does not have to begin with another dystopia.**

---

## 🌻 Yes, the Lesbian Greenhouse Is Within Scope

For avoidance of doubt, solarpunk cyberfeminism does not *require* everybody to retire to a lesbian greenhouse and make the computers disappear.

The lesbian greenhouse remains a perfectly valid deployment option.

Possible infrastructure:

- community-owned renewable energy;
- accessible raised beds;
- local compute;
- mesh networking;
- repairable hardware;
- mutual-aid infrastructure;
- excellent ventilation;
- tea;
- robots;
- tomatoes.

The computers may remain.

They can monitor the tomatoes.

## 📖 Cosy Reading: *Monk & Robot*

**Becky Chambers — *A Psalm for the Wild-Built* and *A Prayer for the Crown-Shy*.**

If you would rather encounter some of these ideas somewhere warm before reading another manifesto, go here.

The *Monk & Robot* duology is gentle solarpunk science fiction concerned with technology, ecology, work, purpose, autonomy, community and what people actually need.

It is also, importantly, **cosy**.

The books have a relatively low trigger interface compared with much speculative fiction. They do not depend heavily on violence, cruelty, sexual threat or relentless catastrophe to produce narrative stakes.

Difficult questions can remain difficult without the world becoming punishing to inhabit.

> **Sometimes political imagination needs somewhere to live.**

You are allowed to imagine wanting to live somewhere nicer before completing the policy framework for building it.

## 👷 Women Are Not Merely the People Technology Happens To

This matters especially in contemporary political discussion.

Women are too often positioned as:

- victims of online harms;
- people requiring safeguarding;
- populations requiring special protection.

Those experiences matter.

They are not the limit of women's relationship with technology.

Women can participate in technological governance through expertise in:

- engineering;
- medicine;
- finance;
- procurement;
- law;
- safeguarding;
- education;
- disability;
- community organising;
- research;
- lived experience.

Nobody needs to understand every layer of an LLM in order to ask:

- What did this cost?
- Who evaluated it?
- What evidence supports the procurement?
- What happens when it fails?
- What data does it use?
- What dependencies have been created?
- Can we roll it back?
- Who benefits?
- Who carries the liability?

An MP who understands procurement can ask questions an ML engineer does not.

A survivor may understand an abuse affordance the product team missed.

A disabled user may recognise a catastrophic access assumption invisible to the designer.

A clinician may recognise that a model's abstraction has removed the biologically important variable.

A finance specialist can ask whether supposedly inevitable infrastructure is economically sustainable.

> **Technological accountability does not belong exclusively to technologists.**

This is distributed technological governance.

---

## 🗳️ Accountability Is Not Anti-Innovation

Democratic scrutiny can be technologically constructive.

Parliamentary questions, procurement scrutiny, financial oversight, audit, journalism and regulatory investigation can expose assumptions invisible inside technical organisations.

Asking about:

- evaluations;
- conflicts;
- public contracts;
- error rates;
- infrastructure dependencies;
- environmental costs;
- rollback plans

is ordinary governance.

Technological firms should not get to define scrutiny of technological firms as hostility to technology.

> **Accountability is not anti-innovation.**

Sometimes accountability is how a society discovers what it actually wants innovation to do.

## ❤️‍🩹 Rehabilitated Tech: Change the Objective Function

This is where the cluster connects directly to:

`🌕_5_Long_Strategies/❤️‍🩹_Rehabilitated_Tech/`

The central proposition is stronger than making the machine nicer.

It is:

> **Change what the machine is for.**

Technological rehabilitation can mean changing:

- the objective;
- the research target;
- the computational model;
- the governance;
- the relationship between system and user.

---

## 💎 Worked Example: Therapeutic Sandbox

See `🌕_5_Long_Strategies/❤️‍🩹_Rehabilitated_Tech/💎_therapeutic_sandbox.md`.

This proposal treats AI as bounded practice infrastructure rather than an autonomous therapist or engagement machine.

Its architecture places simulated interaction inside a deliberately constrained system with clinical supervision, boundary-recognition gates, audit trails and ethical review. The intended endpoint is increased behavioural autonomy rather than maximised interaction.

The design logic becomes:

```text
engagement system
     ↓
How do we keep the person interacting?

care-centred system
     ↓
What does the person need from this interaction?

rehabilitative system
     ↓
What capability should the person possess
when they no longer need the system?
```

That final question matters.

> **A successful rehabilitative technology may make itself less necessary.**

---

## 😻 Worked Example: Women's Health as a Gateway

See `🌕_5_Long_Strategies/❤️‍🩹_Rehabilitated_Tech/🤖_AI_Beyond_AI/😻_gateways_through_womens_health.md`.

This changes the research target.

Instead of treating women's health as niche, messy or unusually difficult, ask whether its complexity contains unusually valuable scientific information.

Reproductive tissues can provide rich problems involving:

- remodelling;
- angiogenesis;
- immune interaction;
- regeneration;
- fibrosis;
- distributed signalling.

Those problems may have implications well beyond gynaecology.

The useful inversion is:

> **Designing from a marginalised body does not necessarily produce niche knowledge.**

Sometimes the supposedly marginal case contains information the supposedly general model was missing.

The governance matters too. More sophisticated modelling does not justify importing surveillance logic into medicine. Consent, auditability, participation and protection against coercive secondary use remain part of the research architecture.

---

## 🧬 Worked Example: AI-Assisted Systems Comprehension

See `🌕_5_Long_Strategies/❤️‍🩹_Rehabilitated_Tech/🤖_AI_Beyond_AI/🧬_neural_network_remodelling_gynaecology.md`.

This changes the computational method.

Instead of using AI merely to classify, predict or diagnose, computational systems can help researchers understand:

- nonlinear networks;
- temporal change;
- spatial relationships;
- mechanobiology;
- multimodal signalling.

The useful distinction is:

```text
AI diagnosis
     ↓
classify the patient

AI-assisted systems comprehension
     ↓
increase our ability to understand
the biological system
```

Ethical technology does not necessarily mean less capable technology.

> **Sometimes nicer means more intellectually serious.**

Model more context.

Preserve uncertainty.

Stop flattening complicated people or systems into crude categories.

Include variables the old system ignored.

Represent populations the old dataset excluded.

Make the model sufficiently auditable to discover when it is wrong.

## 🧠 Rehabilitating Capability

Some capabilities currently associated with frightening technological systems are capabilities society may genuinely need:

- pattern recognition;
- simulation;
- complex-system modelling;
- distributed knowledge synthesis;
- adaptive interfaces;
- network analysis;
- generative systems.

The political question is not simply whether these capabilities should exist.

Ask:

- What problem are they pointed at?
- What objective are they given?
- What information are they permitted to consume?
- Who governs their use?
- Who can challenge the output?
- Who benefits when they work?
- Who bears the consequences when they fail?

> **The capability is not the objective function.**

---

## 🧬 Nicer Can Mean More Intellectually Serious

Ethical design is sometimes imagined as subtractive:

- less data;
- less automation;
- less capability;
- fewer features.

Sometimes subtraction is exactly right.

Sometimes the ethical failure is **oversimplification**.

Better technology can require:

- more context;
- better biological models;
- better uncertainty representation;
- richer datasets;
- interdisciplinary expertise;
- more representative populations;
- longitudinal understanding;
- better causal reasoning.

The aim is not maximal complexity for its own sake.

It is:

> **Do not simplify away the thing that matters merely because your system finds it inconvenient.**

---

## 🏘️ Alternative Platform and Community Governance

Different technology also requires different governance.

Relevant possibilities include:

- cooperatives;
- federated communities;
- community moderation;
- portable identity;
- interoperable systems;
- transparent rules;
- meaningful appeals;
- user representation;
- public-interest infrastructure;
- community ownership.

Governance is not administrative debris surrounding the product.

> **Governance is part of the technology.**

Moderation rules change behaviour.

Appeals systems change power.

Portability changes exit.

Ownership changes incentives.

Transparency changes accountability.

---

## 🚪 Exit Is a Design Feature

One of the simplest tests of a humane technological system is:

> **Can the person leave?**

Meaningful exit may require:

- data portability;
- interoperability;
- account deletion;
- alternative providers;
- local copies;
- open standards;
- non-retaliation;
- continued access to essential resources.

A system that provides enormous benefits but makes departure impossible has created dependence.

Dependence is not automatically bad.

Unaccountable dependence is dangerous.

---

## ⚠️ Nicer Does Not Mean Harmless

There is no politically pure technological architecture.

Community governance can reproduce hierarchy.

Care can become paternalism.

Safety can become containment.

Accessibility systems can become surveillance.

Therapeutic technology can create dependency.

Mutual-aid infrastructure can burn people out.

Local systems can exclude outsiders.

Open systems can be abused.

The goal is therefore not:

```text
design benevolent system
        ↓
politics disappears
```

It is:

```text
build systems
        ↓
keep power visible
        ↓
make power challengeable
        ↓
make correction possible
```

Useful guardrails include:

- transparency;
- auditability;
- feedback;
- plural governance;
- appeal;
- meaningful consent;
- reversibility;
- exit.

---

## 🌍 Build for Interdependence

Humans are not autonomous units occasionally inconvenienced by needing other people.

We live through:

- infrastructures;
- relationships;
- ecosystems;
- institutions;
- technologies.

A nicer technological politics therefore does not have to optimise everyone for independence.

It can make interdependence:

- safer;
- more reliable;
- more equitable;
- less humiliating;
- less coercive.

Sometimes the technological breakthrough is the prosthesis.

Sometimes it is the accessible bus.

Sometimes it is the community network.

Sometimes it is the payment system that does not exclude you.

Sometimes it is the database that knows less.

## 🌞 THE TECHNOLOGY IS NOT INEVITABLE

The present configuration is one configuration.

Different assumptions produce different machines.

Different objectives produce different optimisation.

Different research questions produce different knowledge.

Different governance produces different power.

Different ideas about bodies produce different environments.

> ## **THE TECHNOLOGY IS NOT INEVITABLE.**

We do not have to choose between harmful technology and no technology.

We can ask:

- What is this for?
- Who is this for?
- Who controls it?
- What does success mean?
- What happens when it fails?
- Can people leave?
- What capability does it create?
- What relationship does it produce?
- What kind of world does it make easier to inhabit?

And then:

> ## **WE COULD BUILD NICER THINGS.**
