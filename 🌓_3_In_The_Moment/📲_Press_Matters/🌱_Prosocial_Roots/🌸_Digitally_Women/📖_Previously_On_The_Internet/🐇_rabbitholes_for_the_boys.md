# 🐇 Rabbitholes For The Boys

**First created:** 2026-09-05 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-05  
*How ordinary male interests, commercial attention systems and deliberately constructed influence funnels can lead men and boys into very different information environments.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

**Question answered:** How are men and boys moved through online information environments?

- Start with the ordinary boy.
- He may be looking for:
  - dating advice;
  - help talking to women;
  - fitness;
  - weight loss;
  - money;
  - entrepreneurship;
  - gaming;
  - sport;
  - confidence;
  - fashion;
  - masculinity;
  - friendship;
  - sex;
  - mental-health advice;
  - somewhere to belong.
- None of those interests is pathological.
- None makes him a misogynist.
- None makes him destined for radicalisation.

- The problem is that these are also **excellent points of entry** for people selling:
  - products;
  - courses;
  - subscriptions;
  - communities;
  - status;
  - political explanations;
  - enemies;
  - identities.

- Some pathways develop organically.
- Some are produced by recommender systems.
- Some are commercial funnels.
- Some are deliberate ideological recruitment.
- Some combine all four.

> **The rabbit hole often begins with a reasonable question.**

## 🥺 He Only Wanted Dating Advice

- Treat this seriously.
- A teenage boy who searches:
  - “how do I get a girlfriend?”
  - “why don't girls like me?”
  - “how do I become more confident?”
- is asking ordinary developmental questions.

- The first explanatory frame matters.

- Possible answers include:
  - practise social skills;
  - develop friendships;
  - accept rejection;
  - look after yourself;
  - meet more people;
  - women have individual preferences;
  - relationships require reciprocity.

- Another answer is:
  - women are hypergamous;
  - feminism has deceived you;
  - attractive men monopolise women;
  - women lose value with age;
  - women must be controlled;
  - your unhappiness has a political enemy.

- Both can be presented as:
  - **dating advice**.

## 🛒 Before Ideology, Notice The Funnel

- A great deal of male-oriented online content is commercial.

- Typical funnel:

```text
free short-form content
        ↓
follow
        ↓
long-form video / podcast
        ↓
newsletter / Discord / community
        ↓
course / coaching / subscription
        ↓
higher-trust environment
```

- The creator has incentives to:
  - retain attention;
  - create repeat customers;
  - differentiate the in-group;
  - establish expertise;
  - offer progressively more valuable or exclusive material.

- This does not automatically produce extremism.
- But the architecture is extremely useful for ideological entrepreneurs too.

> **Attention funnels and ideological funnels can use the same plumbing.**

  - political creators.

- Participants disagree with each other.
- Creators compete.
- Audiences overlap.
- Vocabulary travels.

- A person does not need to formally “join the manosphere”.
- He can encounter its explanatory frames through adjacent content.

- One of the easiest ways to observe movement between communities is language.

- Terms can travel:
  - community → creator;
  - creator → podcast;
  - podcast → TikTok clip;
  - clip → mainstream social media;
  - social media → school;
  - school → group chat.

- Vocabulary can include:
  - alpha;
  - beta;
  - sigma;
  - Chad;
  - Stacy;
  - hypergamy;
  - body count;
  - high-value man;
  - high-value woman;
  - Red Pill;
  - blackpill;
  - cuck;
  - simp.

- Vocabulary does not prove ideological commitment.
- But vocabulary can carry:
  - assumptions;
  - classifications;
  - narratives;
  - models of gender relations.

> **Memes can transport a worldview before somebody has learned its name.**

## 📲 The Clip Is A Transport Mechanism

- Long-form content increasingly travels as:
  - clips;
  - shorts;
  - edits;
  - reaction videos;
  - stitched responses;
  - screenshots;
  - memes.

- A user does not need to watch:
  - a three-hour podcast
- to encounter:
  - its most provocative thirty seconds.

- Clips can detach claims from:
  - qualifications;
  - surrounding argument;
  - original audience;
  - source context.

- They also provide recruitment material for the longer ecosystem.

## 📈 Recommendation Changes The Ecology

- Recommendation systems do not need to:
  - create misogyny;
  - invent a community;
  - or consciously decide to radicalise somebody
- to change the environment.

- They can change:
  - frequency;
  - proximity;
  - discoverability;
  - repetition;
  - creator competition;
  - audience overlap.

- A niche creator can become adjacent to:
  - millions of ordinary searches.

## 🧲 Deliberate Recruitment Exists Too

- Do not explain every pathway as accidental recommendation.

- Political and ideological movements have long understood:
  - recruitment;
  - audience segmentation;
  - propaganda;
  - front groups;
  - cultural entry points;
  - youth outreach.

- Online environments make these techniques:
  - cheap;
  - scalable;
  - measurable;
  - iterative.

- Deliberate recruitment may use material that initially appears:
  - non-political;
  - funny;
  - self-improvement oriented;
  - transgressive;
  - masculine;
  - anti-establishment.

- The useful question is:
  - **who is deliberately directing whom toward what?**

## 🎮 Gaming, Humour And Shared Space

- Gaming communities are important because they combine:
  - recreation;
  - identity;
  - friendship;
  - competition;
  - voice chat;
  - forums;
  - streaming;
  - memes;
  - technical culture.

- Most gaming is:
  - gaming.

- But gaming environments can also provide:
  - social graphs;
  - trusted peers;
  - cultural language;
  - pathways into adjacent communities.

- Do not make:
  - **plays videogames**
- a risk factor.

- Analyse:
  - actual community;
  - actual behaviour;
  - actual information movement.

## 😂 Irony Is Useful Plumbing

- Humour lowers the cost of participation.

- A person can:
  - repeat the joke;
  - learn the vocabulary;
  - recognise the in-group;
  - test audience reaction
- without first declaring:
  - “I subscribe to this ideology.”

- Irony also provides deniability.

- But:
  - ironic use does not prove sincere belief;
  - sincere-looking use does not prove ideological depth.

- Track:
  - repetition;
  - context;
  - behavioural change;
  - community movement.

- **Pipeline describes a pathway, not destiny.**

## 🚪 There Are Off-Ramps

- Men and boys leave communities.
- They change their minds.
- They encounter:
  - better friends;
  - women;
  - other men;
  - competing creators;
  - different politics;
  - new hobbies;
  - ridicule;
  - empathy;
  - adulthood.

- Some communities themselves contain disagreement and corrective voices.

- Prevention should therefore include:
  - alternatives;
  - social belonging;
  - credible male voices;
  - digital literacy;
  - relationship education;
  - opportunities to exit without humiliation.

- Treating every boy who encountered bad material as irredeemably dangerous can:
  - reinforce isolation;
  - strengthen the in-group;
  - destroy an off-ramp.

## 🔮 Watch Movement, Not Just Content

For safeguarding, journalism and policy, useful questions include:

1. **What did he originally search for?**
2. **Who answered?**
3. **What problem did they tell him he had?**
4. **What vocabulary did he acquire?**
5. **Which creators became trusted?**
6. **Did he move platform?**
7. **Did he enter a private or higher-trust community?**
8. **Was money involved?**
9. **Were enemies introduced into the explanation?**
10. **Did attitudes toward women change?**
11. **Did behaviour change?**
12. **Did offline relationships deteriorate?**
13. **Who or what provided alternative explanations?**
14. **Where were the off-ramps?**

This is more informative than asking:

> **Did he watch Andrew Tate?**

## 🧠 The Analytical Point

The useful model is not:

> **the internet brainwashed the boys.**

Nor is it:

> **boys independently chose every idea they encountered.**

It is an ecology containing:

- human needs;
- commercial incentives;
- creators;
- communities;
- friends;
- platform architecture;
- recommender systems;
- deliberate ideological actors;
- cultural history;
- offline life.

Different combinations produce different pathways.

The policy question is therefore not merely:

> **How do we remove bad content?**

It is also:

> **Who is answering boys' ordinary questions, what answers are they giving them, how are those answers being distributed, and where are the better routes?**
