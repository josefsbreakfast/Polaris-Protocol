Job: Correct the historical framing.

Women didn’t suddenly arrive on an otherwise naturally male internet and begin experiencing online harm.

The node should follow women as builders, programmers, users, moderators, writers, artists, fans, gamers, sex workers, activists, researchers, community organisers and creators across successive digital architectures.

The useful chronology is technological rather than merely event-based:

early networks/forums
→ web communities
→ blogs
→ social networking
→ smartphones
→ platform consolidation
→ algorithmic feeds
→ creator economy
→ generative AI.

At every transition ask:

What became easier? What became harder? What became visible? Who gained power? Who acquired access to whom?

Dickwolves belongs somewhere in this historical neighbourhood once we find the existing Polaris node. It is useful precisely because pre-GamerGate online gender conflict helps prevent GamerGate from being treated as Year Zero.

Question answered: How did changing internet architecture change women’s participation, power and exposure?
