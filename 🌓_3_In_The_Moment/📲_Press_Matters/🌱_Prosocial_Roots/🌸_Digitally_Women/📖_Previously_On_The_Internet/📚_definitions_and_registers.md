# 📚 Definitions & Registers

**First created:** 2026-09-05 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-05
*A searchable, printable, grab-and-go field guide for translating between internet culture, feminist analysis, journalism, policy, safeguarding and law.*

## 🗺️ Orientation

The internet has dialects.

Law has another one. Government has several. Researchers have more. Journalists translate between them under deadline. People actually living online may describe the same phenomenon using language that sounds ridiculous until somebody translates what the ridiculous word is doing.

This node is designed as a **searchable, printable, grab-and-go reference**.

It can be read from start to finish, but it is primarily intended to be **consulted**: in a newsroom, briefing room, committee office, classroom, policy meeting, or even from the Chamber when somebody encounters a term, figure, platform, tactic or subculture they need explained quickly.

Each entry aims to answer four things fast:

- **What is it?**
- **What register does it belong to?**
- **Why does it matter here?**
- **What should it not be confused with?**

The difficulty ratings work like ski runs. They describe how much contextual knowledge is useful before attempting the terrain — **not** how dangerous, extreme or morally serious a term, person or community is.

| Run                    | Difficulty         | Rough translation                                                          |
|:-----------------------|:-------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 🟢 **Green**           | Beginner           | You need this to follow the conversation.                                  |
| 🔵 **Blue**            | Intermediate       | You need this to understand how internet ecosystems behave.                |
| 🔴 **Red**             | Advanced           | Context, history or specialist knowledge materially changes the meaning.   |
| 🖤💎 **Black Diamond** | Chronically online | Dictionary definitions will no longer save you. Context is the definition. |

A person can be 🟢 while a phrase associated with their community is 🖤💎. A behaviour can begin 🟢 and require 🔴 analysis once coordination, platform migration or offline escalation enters the picture.

This is not a threat scale.

It is an **internet-literacy scale**.

## 👠 A Note on Misogyny and Who Can Experience It

Polaris generally approaches misogyny through **perpetrator behaviour** rather than requiring the target to pass an identity test.

Misogyny includes anti-woman hostility, gender hierarchy, gendered entitlement, control, degradation, and hostility toward femininity or perceived womanhood.

Women are not a monolith. Misogynistic behaviour can target cis women and trans women; it can also be directed at trans men, non-binary people and others through the perpetrator’s hostility to perceived womanhood, femininity, gender non-conformity, or departure from a prescribed gender role.

This follows a useful principle familiar from anti-racist analysis:

> **Look at what the perpetrator is doing, what hierarchy they are enforcing, and what they believe they are targeting.**

The purpose here is violence prevention and accurate description, not competitive classification of victims.

| 💬 | **Shitposting** | Deliberately low-quality, absurd, provocative or context-heavy posting, often for humour or disruption. | Can be apolitical, political, ironic, sincere — or all four before lunch. |
| 🧠 | **Parasocial relationship** | A one-sided sense of social or emotional connection with a media figure, celebrity, creator or similar persona. | Not inherently unhealthy and not synonymous with fandom. |
| 🧠 | **Misogyny** | Hostility toward, degradation of, control over or enforcement of hierarchy against women or femininity. | Related to sexism, but not identical in every analytical or legal register. |
| 🧠 | **Sexism** | Prejudice, discrimination, stereotyping or unequal treatment on the basis of sex or gender. | Misogyny often describes a more specifically anti-woman or hierarchy-enforcing phenomenon. |
| 🧠 | **Anti-feminism** | Opposition to feminism, feminist movements or particular feminist goals. | Anti-feminism is not automatically misogyny, though the two can overlap. |
| 🧠 | **Manosphere** | Umbrella term for overlapping male-oriented online communities concerned with masculinity, dating, gender relations and/or male grievance. | Not one ideology, platform or organisation. |

| 💬 | **Red pill / red-pilled** | Metaphor, derived from *The Matrix*, for believing one has awakened to a hidden truth; in manosphere contexts often applied to particular beliefs about sex, gender and relationships. | Used in political subcultures beyond the manosphere. |
| 💬 | **Tradwife** | Internet term for a woman publicly embracing or performing a highly traditional wife/mother gender role, often as lifestyle content. | Aesthetic domesticity, religious conservatism, commercial influencing and political ideology can overlap but are not identical. |
| ⚖️ | **VAWG** | Violence against women and girls: a major UK policy and safeguarding category covering multiple forms of gendered violence and abuse. | Women are people, not merely a government acronym. |
| ⚖️ | **Safeguarding** | Systems and practices intended to protect people from abuse, exploitation and other forms of harm. | It does not begin only once criminal liability is obvious. |
| ⚖️ | **Coercive control** | A pattern of controlling or coercive behaviour within relevant intimate or family relationships; also used more broadly in abuse analysis. | Legal definitions and evidential requirements matter when describing an offence. |
| ⚖️ | **Stalking** | Repeated or persistent unwanted behaviour that can cause fear, alarm or distress and may create serious safety concerns. | Not simply “looking at someone's social media a lot”. |
| ⚖️ | **Harassment** | Repeated unwanted conduct capable of causing alarm or distress; legal and everyday meanings can differ. | Specify the conduct rather than relying on the label alone. |

## 🔵 Blue Runs

### 🔵 When the Internet Starts Behaving Collectively

“Online harassment” can conceal very different mechanisms.

#### Swarming

**Swarming** is useful here as an umbrella description for concentrated collective attention or action against a target.

The important question is:

> **What is the swarm actually being mobilised to do?**

| Form                             | What happens                                                                                                 | Why the distinction matters                                                  |
|:---------------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Comment/reply swarming**       | Large numbers flood replies, mentions, comments or DMs.                                                      | Can overwhelm a target while remaining largely inside the original platform. |
| **Report swarming**              | Many users report an account or content.                                                                     | Platform moderation infrastructure itself may become part of the contest.    |
| **Contact swarming**             | People contact an employer, university, client, family member, regulator, venue or other third party.        | The activity has escaped the original conversation.                          |
| **Review/reputation swarming**   | Coordinated or concentrated reviews, ratings, complaints or reputational attacks.                            | Can create professional or commercial consequences.                          |
| **Cross-platform swarming**      | Targeting follows somebody from one service to another.                                                      | Persistence becomes part of the behaviour.                                   |
| **Doxxing-enabled swarming**     | Personal information is circulated to facilitate further targeting.                                          | Increases the possible attack surface substantially.                         |
| **Threat/intimidation swarming** | Multiple participants send threats, sexualised abuse or intimidating material.                               | Scale can alter both impact and risk assessment.                             |
| **Institutional weaponisation**  | Participants make complaints or reports intended to cause coercive consequences through third parties.       | Institutions can become unwilling participants in harassment.                |
| **SWATting**                     | False emergency reports are made to provoke a police or other emergency response at a target’s location.     | This can create immediate physical danger.                                   |
| **Offline swarming**             | Online mobilisation contributes to physical attendance, confrontation, stalking or other embodied targeting. | The online/offline distinction has now become particularly unhelpful.        |

#### Dogpile, Brigade, Swarm

| Term          | Emphasis                                                                                             |
|:--------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Dogpile**   | The mass response itself.                                                                            |
| **Brigading** | Mobilisation from somewhere else toward a target, post or community.                                 |
| **Swarming**  | The broader concentration of collective action around a target.                                      |
| **SWATting**  | A specific tactic involving malicious false emergency reporting — not merely a very intense dogpile. |

> 📰 **Press note:** “She received negative comments online” may be technically true while concealing doxxing, employer contact, cross-platform pursuit and malicious reports to authorities. Describe the mechanism.

| Type | Term                                     | Working definition                                                                                                                 | Don’t flatten                                                                                  |
|:-----|:-----------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

| 🧨   | **Sockpuppet**                           | A false or additional online identity used to disguise who is speaking or create an appearance of independent participation.       | Not every anonymous account is a sockpuppet.                                                   |
| 🧨   | **Astroturfing**                         | Activity designed to create the appearance of organic grassroots support or opposition.                                            | Genuine grassroots mobilisation can also be highly organised.                                  |
| 🧨   | **Sealioning**                           | Persistent, ostensibly polite questioning performed in bad faith to exhaust, derail or demand endless justification from a target. | Asking difficult questions is not inherently sealioning.                                       |
| 🧨   | **Concern trolling**                     | Criticism or disruption presented as sympathetic concern for the target or cause.                                                  | Sincere internal criticism exists.                                                             |
| 💬   | **Dogwhistle**                           | Language carrying an additional meaning recognisable to a particular audience while remaining more innocuous to outsiders.         | Not every ambiguous phrase is coded speech.                                                    |
| 💬   | **Shadowban**                            | Internet term for reduced visibility imposed without an obvious account ban.                                                       | Users frequently infer shadowbanning from ordinary changes in reach; platform mechanisms vary. |
| 🧨   | **Deplatforming**                        | Removal or restriction of a speaker or community from a platform or service.                                                       | Does not necessarily eliminate the community; migration matters.                               |
| 💬   | **Rabbit hole**                          | Metaphor for increasingly deep or specialised content exposure or participation.                                                   | Not a technical synonym for radicalisation.                                                    |
| 💬   | **Echo chamber**                         | Environment in which similar views are repeatedly reinforced and contrary views are limited or discounted.                         | Real networks are often messier than the metaphor suggests.                                    |
| 💬   | **Irony poisoning**                      | Internet shorthand for prolonged ironic engagement blurring the boundary between parody and sincere belief.                        | Difficult to establish from individual posts alone.                                            |
| 💬   | **“Just asking questions” / JAQing off** | Performing neutral inquiry while repeatedly advancing insinuations without owning the underlying claim.                            | Genuine questions remain legal and useful; please continue asking them.                        |

## 🔴 Red Runs

### 🔴 You Are Now Analysing the Ecosystem

These terms need more than a dictionary definition because they make claims about **mechanism**.

| Concept                       | Working use                                                                                                                                                                   | Precision note                                                                                       |
|:------------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Networked misogyny**        | Misogyny produced, circulated or reinforced through interconnected users, communities, media and platforms.                                                                   | “Networked” does not necessarily mean centrally coordinated.                                         |
| **Participatory misogyny**    | Misogynistic meaning or targeting produced collectively through participation rather than one speaker acting alone.                                                           | Participants may contribute very different amounts and kinds of conduct.                             |
| **Networked harassment**      | Harassment emerging through interconnected participants, platforms or communities.                                                                                            | Identify what behaviour actually occurred.                                                           |
| **Ideological ecosystem**     | Interacting creators, audiences, communities, narratives, institutions and distribution systems through which ideas circulate.                                                | Often more useful than imagining one linear “pipeline”.                                              |
| **Algorithmic amplification** | Technical or platform processes increasing the distribution or visibility of particular material.                                                                             | Establish which system and evidence before asserting causation.                                      |
| **Recommender system**        | System selecting or ranking content for a user based on signals, models or platform objectives.                                                                               | One part of the information environment, not an autonomous political actor.                          |
| **Affordance**                | A feature or property of a technology that enables, encourages or constrains particular kinds of behaviour.                                                                   | A quote-post button, anonymity, forwarding and recommendation can create very different affordances. |
| **Attention economy**         | Framework treating human attention as a scarce resource competed for and monetised by media or platform systems.                                                              | Useful for understanding why outrage can have commercial value.                                      |
| **Context collapse**          | Different audiences and social contexts becoming compressed into one communication environment.                                                                               | A joke for one community may travel into an audience lacking its original assumptions.               |
| **Gendered disinformation**   | False or manipulated information using gendered narratives, stereotypes or attacks to target people or shape political or social perception.                                  | Misogynistic abuse and disinformation overlap but are not identical.                                 |
| **Status threat**             | Perceived loss or challenge to social position that may contribute to grievance or political identity.                                                                        | Does not automatically produce misogyny, extremism or violence.                                      |
| **Hegemonic masculinity**     | Analytical framework concerning culturally dominant ideals or forms of masculinity and their relationship to hierarchy.                                                       | Does not mean “masculinity is inherently toxic”.                                                     |
| **Male supremacism**          | Ideology asserting male superiority, dominance or entitlement to authority over women.                                                                                        | Stronger and more specific claim than “sexist”.                                                      |
| **Radicalisation pathway**    | Process through which beliefs, identity, social relationships or behaviour may become increasingly organised around extremist ideas or violence-supportive commitments.       | There is no universal conveyor belt.                                                                 |
| **Mainstreaming**             | Movement of ideas, frames or language from marginal or subcultural spaces into wider public discourse.                                                                        | Mainstream visibility does not prove public acceptance.                                              |
| **Ideological laundering**    | Repackaging ideas in forms more socially acceptable or legible to wider audiences.                                                                                            | Useful analytical shorthand; establish the actual transmission rather than assuming intent.          |
| **Cyberfeminism**             | Broad family of feminist practices and theories concerned with digital technologies, bodies, networks, identity and power.                                                    | Not one movement with one manifesto.                                                                 |
| **Xenofeminism**              | Technomaterialist feminist project associated particularly with Laboria Cuboniks and the proposition that technology can be deliberately repurposed toward emancipatory ends. | Not simply “feminism but futuristic”.                                                                |
| **Feminist HCI / STS**        | Feminist approaches to human-computer interaction and science and technology studies that examine how technologies embody, reproduce or can redesign social relations.        | Useful for asking not only how systems harm, but what else we could build.                           |

### 🔴 Jeffrey Epstein

Jeffrey Epstein belongs on the **Red run**, not because this hierarchy measures moral severity, but because understanding why he keeps appearing across apparently unrelated contemporary stories requires substantial chronology and source discipline.

His relevance to this cluster is broader than “internet figure”.

He sits at the intersection of:

- sexual exploitation;
- violence and abuse against women and girls;
- wealth and status;
- elite social and professional networks;
- institutional reputation;
- safeguarding failure;
- journalism and documentary disclosure;
- political relationships;
- information retention and release;
- and downstream investigations.

Further United States Department of Justice releases may clarify additional relationships and institutional connections. This is therefore an area where the map is **still being populated**.

#### Evidence Discipline

| Status                    | Meaning                                                                                                                        |
|:--------------------------|:-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Documented connection** | Reliable records establish some form of contact, relationship, meeting, communication, employment, travel or other connection. |
| **Reported connection**   | Credible journalism reports a relationship but primary documentation may not yet be available in Polaris.                      |
| **Inference**             | Available facts permit a hypothesis but do not establish it.                                                                   |
| **Unresolved lead**       | Material worth retaining for later checking against further documentary releases.                                              |

A photograph, contact-book entry, email, meeting, flight, social relationship or professional connection does **not by itself establish knowledge of, assistance with or participation in Epstein’s offending**.

The interesting analytical question is not:

> **Who can we draw a line to?**

It is:

> **What does the documented relationship establish, what institutional function did it have, and did anybody possess information that should have changed safeguarding or accountability decisions?**

### 🔴 Legal and Policy Translation

| Term                       | Meaning here                                                                                                                                                                              | Please do not say                                                                            |
|:---------------------------|:------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Hate crime**             | Umbrella description for several legal mechanisms involving hostility toward protected characteristics; England and Wales does not simply have one universal offence called “hate crime”. | “Misogyny is now a hate crime” without explaining which mechanism.                           |
| **Aggravated offence**     | A specified underlying offence becomes a statutory aggravated form where the relevant hostility requirements are established.                                                             | “The judge just adds some extra time.”                                                       |

| **Risk signal**            | Information potentially relevant to an assessment when considered with other behaviour and context.                                                                                       | “Misogyny predicts violence.”                                                                |

## 🖤💎 Black Diamond Runs

### 🖤💎 Unfortunately, Context Is Now Doing Most of the Work

Black Diamond vocabulary is not necessarily sinister.

It is vocabulary where somebody unfamiliar with the culture can read every individual word correctly and still misunderstand the sentence.

| Term                        | Approximate translation                                                                                                    | Why context matters                                                                         |
|:----------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Anon**                    | Anonymous user, especially in imageboard cultures.                                                                         | Persistent identity works differently in anonymous environments.                            |
| **OP**                      | Original poster / original post.                                                                                           | Meaning depends on context.                                                                 |
| **Normie**                  | Person perceived as outside a subculture or unfamiliar with its norms.                                                     | Can be playful, contemptuous or ideological boundary-making.                                |
| **Based**                   | Approval for saying or doing something perceived as unapologetic, authentic or ideologically correct.                      | What is being approved matters rather more than the word.                                   |
| **Cringe**                  | Embarrassing, socially awkward or contemptibly uncool.                                                                     | Can function as surprisingly powerful community norm enforcement.                           |
| **Cope**                    | Dismissal suggesting somebody’s explanation exists to protect them from an uncomfortable reality.                          | Often paired with “seethe”.                                                                 |
| **Seethe**                  | Mocking instruction or observation that an opponent is angry.                                                              | Performance of emotional dominance is often the point.                                      |
| **Touch grass**             | Log off and reconnect with ordinary offline reality.                                                                       | Occasionally excellent advice.                                                              |
| **NPC**                     | “Non-player character”; insult suggesting somebody lacks independent thought and merely repeats scripts.                   | Can encode dehumanising political attitudes depending on use.                               |
| **Simp**                    | Derogatory term for somebody — usually a man — seen as excessively deferential or devoted to a woman.                      | Often reveals assumptions about what men are entitled to receive in return for attention.   |

| **Edgelord**                | Person performing deliberately shocking or taboo opinions or humour.                                                       | Performance can still normalise, distribute or eventually merge with sincere ideology.      |
| **Post-irony / meta-irony** | Layers of performance where the distinction between sincere belief and parody becomes unstable or deliberately unknowable. | Welcome to the Black Diamond. Wear a helmet.                                                |

### 🖤💎 Irony Is Not an Evidential Magic Trick

One recurring difficulty in internet research is the proposition:

> **“But they were joking.”**

Sometimes they were.

Sometimes humour provides community bonding around a sincerely held belief.

Sometimes somebody sincerely believes part of the proposition but exaggerates it for entertainment.

Sometimes an outrageous belief is tested through irony before being stated sincerely.

Sometimes the audience contains people interpreting the same content in all of those ways simultaneously.

And sometimes it really is just a shitpost.

The analytical task is therefore not to declare that **irony is secretly sincerity**.

It is to examine:

- repeated behaviour;
- surrounding community norms;
- audience response;
- migration between ironic and explicit spaces;
- whether rhetoric changes over time;
- what actions accompany the speech;
- and what the speaker does when the joke leaves the screen.

## 🧭 Register Rules

Before borrowing a term from another discipline or subculture:

1.  **Ask who normally uses it.**
2.  **Ask what claim the term makes.**
3.  **Ask whether another register uses the same word differently.**
4.  **Distinguish description from mechanism.**
5.  **Distinguish association from coordination.**
6.  **Distinguish exposure from persuasion.**
7.  **Distinguish misogynistic speech from criminal speech.**
8.  **Distinguish a community from its loudest celebrity.**
9.  **Distinguish internet irony from evidence that nothing matters.**
10. **When in doubt, describe what actually happened.**

The last rule solves an astonishing number of problems.

### 🎙️ Podcasts, Interviews and Cultural Sources

Podcasts, videos, archived posts, interviews and other internet-native material belong in this project because the culture cannot be reconstructed solely from statutes and journal articles.

They should, however, be labelled according to what they can establish.

| Source type                                   | Best used for                                                                                      | Do not automatically treat it as                                    |
|:----------------------------------------------|:---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|:--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Podcast episode / creator interview**       | What a speaker said; self-presentation; rhetoric; audience framing; contemporary cultural language | Independent verification that the speaker’s factual claims are true |
| **Archived post / forum thread**              | Primary evidence of language, memes, community norms or a particular interaction                   | Evidence that every member of the community shared the same view    |
| **Video / livestream**                        | Performance, rhetoric, audience interaction and the circulation of ideas                           | A representative sample of a whole movement                         |
| **First-person interview / oral history**     | Lived experience, chronology, movement memory and leads for further research                       | Complete documentary reconstruction by itself                       |
| **Independent podcast journalism**            | Reporting, interviews, documentary leads and specialist investigation                              | A substitute for checking the underlying documents where available  |
| **Mainstream broadcast / podcast journalism** | Contemporary reporting, interviews and public framing                                              | Automatically superior to specialist or independent reporting       |

#### Source handling rule

When a podcast or cultural source matters, preserve enough metadata to find it again:

- programme or channel;
- episode/title;
- speaker or guest;
- publisher/host;
- publication date;
- timestamp where relevant;
- archive link where possible;
- and a short note saying **what proposition the source actually supports**.

This matters particularly for internet history. Episodes disappear. Channels are renamed. Posts are deleted. Platforms close. A source map that records only a naked URL is not much of an archive.

> 🎧 **Placeholder:** specific podcast, interview and cultural-source recommendations will be added as the relevant nodes are researched. Their role will be identified as **primary cultural material**, **first-person history**, **journalism**, or **analysis**, rather than treating all audio sources as one evidential category.
