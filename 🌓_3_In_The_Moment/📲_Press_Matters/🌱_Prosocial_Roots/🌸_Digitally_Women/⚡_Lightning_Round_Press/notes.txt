### `README.md`

**The deadline door.** A very short explanation of the whole `🌸_Digitally_Women` cluster, designed for readers who do not have time to read it. Tell them which file to open depending on what they need: questions, terminology, fact-checking, or primary sources. Link prominently back to `🗣️_explain_this_in_500_words_or_less.md`.

### `🗣️_questions_for_journalists.md`

**Questions worth asking instead of another generic “is social media harmful?” interview.** A bank of specific questions for ministers, MPs, police, prosecutors, Ofcom/platforms, schools/universities, researchers and campaigners. Organise by interviewee so somebody can grab five useful questions in thirty seconds. Pull especially from `🛸_LOOK_UP`, `♻️_Re_Recommendations` and `🧾_Petition_GC`.

### `📰_language_for_press_and_politicians.md`

**How to talk about this without flattening it.** Short preferred formulations, distinctions and traps: misogyny versus sex-based hostility; manosphere versus incel; association versus causation; online/offline; radicalisation versus criminality; survivor versus victim where context matters; and the cluster's perpetrator-behaviour approach to misogyny and gender-diverse targets. Also explain why different registers can all be useful rather than insisting everybody adopt one approved vocabulary.

This is where we can translate:

> **research language ↔ government language ↔ legal language ↔ Girl Internet ↔ words normal humans use.**

### `🧮_claims_check.md`

**The “can I actually say that?” file.** Fast verification of recurring claims, with something like:

> **Claim → status → precise formulation → source → date checked → caveat if materially necessary.**

Perfect for things such as: what section 145 does; whether it is in force; what section 66 covers; what the Law Commission recommended; when Macdonald reported; what government promised; what the VAWG strategy says about online misogyny; petition signatures/dates.

This should be deliberately boring and excellent.

### `🔖_source_map.md`

**Receipts without the treasure hunt.** Curated primary/authoritative sources grouped by subject rather than one enormous bibliography: petition and Parliament; legislation; Macdonald; Law Commission; Home Office/VAWG; Ofcom/Online Safety; policing/CPS; research evidence; then selected high-quality contextual reporting.

Each entry gets a one-line annotation saying **what that source is actually useful for**.
