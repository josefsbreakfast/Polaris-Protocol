# 🕯️ The Enlightenment Was Not Enlightened
**First created:** 2026-11-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-11-18  
*The contradictions of Enlightenment universalism, European racial classification and the intellectual histories that continue to shape academic authority.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The Enlightenment is often presented as the moment when reason, evidence, liberty and universal human dignity overcame inherited authority. Those achievements matter. So does the uncomfortable fact that influential Enlightenment thinkers also developed, circulated or defended racial hierarchies while European powers participated in colonial domination and enslavement. The contradiction is not a footnote to intellectual history; it is part of the history. [1–4]

**The editorial argument is not that every Enlightenment thinker was a racist, or that modern genetics is merely eighteenth-century philosophy in a lab coat.** It is that claims to universal reason can coexist with exclusions about whose experience counts, whose testimony is credible and whose humanity is presumed. Oxford's present-day controversies over race and academic authority are worth examining against that longer inheritance. [1–5]

## 🧭 Universalism, with exclusions

- Enlightenment thought was diverse and contested. Commitments to reason, toleration and liberty did not produce a single agreed position on slavery, empire, sex or race. [1, 2]
- Universal moral language sometimes coexisted with colonial economic structures and theories ranking human populations. That contradiction demands historical explanation, not a simplistic claim that Enlightenment philosophy inevitably produced later eugenics. [2–4]
- **Analytical test:** when an author writes of *man*, *humanity*, *reason* or *civilisation*, identify whom the argument actually includes and how that inclusion is qualified in the text. [1–4]

## 🧬 Classification is not neutral simply because it is systematic

### Linnaeus and natural history

Carl Linnaeus's eighteenth-century classification of humans incorporated geographical categories and, in influential editions, stereotyped behavioural and moral descriptions. These descriptions reflected cultural judgements rather than a demonstrated biological hierarchy. Linnaean taxonomy helped standardise natural history, but the application of such classifications to people requires scrutiny of the categories and their historical assumptions. [5, 6]

### Blumenbach and the complication of categories

Johann Friedrich Blumenbach's work helped popularise a fivefold classification of human varieties, including the term *Caucasian*. His positions were more complicated than a straightforward endorsement of separate human species: he argued for human unity while using typological categories that later acquired lives of their own. **Do not collapse monogenism, racial typology and nineteenth-century biological racism into one position.** [5, 7]

### From typology to measurement

Later craniometry, anthropometry and eugenics drew upon overlapping histories of human classification, but these were not one uninterrupted scientific programme. Francis Galton's nineteenth-century eugenics introduced explicit ambitions to shape human reproduction and population traits. The connections are historical and institutional, not proof that every contemporary study of genetic ancestry is eugenic. [6, 8]

## 🕯️ The philosophers' problem

### David Hume

In a notorious note to *Of National Characters*, Hume expressed racist claims about Black people's capacities. The passage belongs in any assessment of how empirical and sceptical philosophy could coexist with sweeping assertions made without adequate evidence. Quote only from a reliable edition, identify the version and note the note's revision history where relevant. [3, 9]

### Immanuel Kant

Kant's writings on race included hierarchical claims and theories of inherited human differences. These sit uneasily alongside the universal moral language associated with his philosophy. Scholarship debates the relationship between his racial anthropology, later revisions and his moral philosophy; it should not be resolved by declaring either that the racism is irrelevant or that every Kantian concept is therefore void. [4]

### Other Enlightenment positions

Some European intellectuals challenged enslavement, colonial injustice or the denial of rights. Condorcet's antislavery writing is one important example. The period contained criticism and resistance as well as rationalisations of domination. **The contradiction was visible to contemporaries; it is not an exclusively modern objection.** [1–3, 10]

## 🌍 The people who were never merely subjects of European debate

- **Ottobah Cugoano** condemned slavery and argued for the rights and freedom of enslaved people in *Thoughts and Sentiments on the Evil of Slavery* (1787). His work is an intellectual intervention, not merely testimony illustrating European philosophy. [11]
- **Olaudah Equiano** documented enslavement, commerce, religion and abolitionist politics in *The Interesting Narrative* (1789). Read the narrative as an authored political and literary argument as well as a historical source. [12]
- **Mary Wollstonecraft** exposed the exclusions of political universalism in relation to women's education and rights, although her work should not be made to stand in for all excluded groups. [1, 13]
- **The Haitian Revolution** demonstrated that enslaved and formerly enslaved people were political agents capable of transforming the language of liberty into revolutionary practice. It is not an appendix to European intellectual history. [2, 14]

**Research rule:** place colonised and abolitionist thinkers in the main intellectual chronology rather than in a token concluding paragraph headed “critics”.

## 🧪 From historical racial hierarchy to contemporary scientific claims

The historical material matters for the present debate because the language of objective inquiry has repeatedly been used both to challenge prejudice and to authorise it. That observation is a reason for **better scientific standards**, not a reason to reject empirical research. [5, 6, 8]

For claims about race, genetics and intelligence, distinguish:

- socially defined racial labels from genetic ancestry and population structure; [15, 16]
- within-population heritability from explanations for differences between populations; [15, 16]
- genetic association from causal explanation; [15, 16]
- genotype from phenotype and the developmental and environmental processes connecting them; [15, 16]
- descriptive scientific hypotheses from normative or reproductive programmes such as eugenics. [8, 16]

**A classification can be internally consistent and still be a poor description of the world.** An apparently quantitative argument is not rescued from confounding by the presence of numbers.

## 🏛️ Why Oxford belongs in this story

Oxford's institutions have participated in the history of empire, collecting and scholarship, while also hosting criticism of those traditions. The relevant question is not whether Oxford must answer personally for every historical philosopher. It is how a university with such intellectual authority teaches the limits of earlier claims to objectivity and responds when controversial racial theories are presented in a contemporary academic setting. [5, 17, 18]

Oriel College's decision to host Nathan Cofnas in November 2026 is a **present-day governance case**, not evidence that Oriel endorses eighteenth-century racial philosophy or Cofnas's scientific claims. The connection to this node is analytical: institutional prestige should never substitute for evidence, and legal permission to speak does not confer scientific credibility. [18, 19]

## ⚖️ Failure modes to avoid

| Failure mode | Corrective discipline |
|---|---|
| “The Enlightenment invented racism” | Trace earlier histories of prejudice, conquest and slavery as well as eighteenth-century changes in classification. [2, 5] |
| “All Enlightenment thinkers agreed” | Identify disagreements, abolitionists, revisions and contradictions. [1–4] |
| “Classification proves hierarchy” | Separate description, measurement, causal inference and moral judgement. [5, 6, 15] |
| “Historical resemblance proves a modern conspiracy” | Require documentary evidence of direct transmission, funding, citation or organisational ties. |
| “Genetics is eugenics” | Distinguish scientific research from programmes of reproductive selection or coercion. [8, 16] |
| “Only Europeans developed Enlightenment ideas” | Restore abolitionist, Black Atlantic and Haitian intellectual agency. [11, 12, 14] |

## 🧿 Closing proposition

The Enlightenment did not fail because reason was worthless. It failed, repeatedly and consequentially, when people claiming the authority of reason exempted their own assumptions about race, empire and humanity from serious scrutiny.

**The response is not less inquiry. It is inquiry that is willing to examine the categories, institutions and power relations through which its evidence is produced.**
