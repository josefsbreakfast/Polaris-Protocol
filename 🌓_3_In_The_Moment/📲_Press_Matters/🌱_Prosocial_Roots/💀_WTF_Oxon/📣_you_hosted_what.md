# 📣 You Hosted What?
**First created:** 2026-11-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-11-18  
*Documenting the Nathan Cofnas event at Oriel College, the institutional decisions surrounding it, and the unresolved questions of judgement, dignity and accountability.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

On 17 November 2026, Nathan Cofnas spoke at Oriel College, Oxford, at an event organised by the Oxon Universities’ Reform Society. The college distinguished permitting the event from endorsing its content. Protests took place outside. [S1][S2][S3]

The question is not simply whether Cofnas could lawfully speak. **It is why this event, in this setting, at this time, was considered an appropriate exercise of institutional judgement.**

This node separates documented events, institutional statements, contested allegations, editorial assessment and questions requiring further evidence.

## 📅 Event chronology

- **27 October:** *The Guardian* reported demands for Oriel to cancel the event amid objections to Cofnas’s views and allegations about the late Professor Jason Arday. [S4]
- **11 November:** *The Oxford Student* reported officials being questioned by student representatives about safety and freedom-of-speech obligations. [S5]
- **12 November:** Oxford student representatives issued a statement challenging the invitation and requesting further disclosure of legal and risk reasoning. [S6]
- **16 November:** Oriel published its explanation of the event arrangements and legal position. [S3]
- **17 November:** The event proceeded amid protests. Contemporary accounts differ in their estimates of attendance and describe the protest and security response differently; figures should therefore be attributed rather than harmonised into a false precision. [S1][S2]

## 🏛️ Who was responsible?

### Organisers

The **Oxon Universities’ Reform Society** organised the event. Oriel said the society was responsible for the talk’s content and moderation. [S3]

Do not conflate the society with the national Reform UK party, Oriel College, Oxford University or the Oxford Union without specific evidence of an organisational relationship.

### Oriel College

Oriel provided the venue and publicly defended the decision under its free-speech obligations. Its statement says it considered Equality Act duties, received legal advice, consulted student groups, coordinated security and welfare arrangements, and worked with university officials. It also declined to release its security risk assessment because of sensitivity. [S3]

**The evidence does not support saying that Oriel ignored equality duties or failed to seek legal advice.** The proper question is whether the process, weighting and eventual decision were adequate.

### University and student bodies

University officials had a role in arrangements, while student representatives raised concerns and requested scrutiny. The responsibilities of central university authorities and the college should be distinguished. [S3][S5][S6]

## 🕯️ Professor Jason Arday: timing and dignity

Cofnas publicly accused Professor Jason Arday of plagiarism. Arday denied wrongdoing; contemporary reporting describes Liverpool John Moores University as having upheld his doctorate following investigation. Arday died in August 2026. An inquest opened on 26 August and was adjourned to a later date. [S4][S7]

**Important boundaries:**

- An allegation is not an established finding.
- The opening of an inquest does not establish a cause of death or another person's responsibility.
- No causal claim about Cofnas and Arday’s death should be made without substantiated evidence.
- The precise procedural status of the inquest on 17 November should be checked against authoritative records before publication.

The editorial objection concerns **decorum, timing and institutional judgement**: did Oriel consider whether postponement would be appropriate given the recent death and unresolved formal proceedings? This question can be asked without alleging culpability for the death.

## 📣 What happened on 17 November?

Contemporary reporting describes Cofnas delivering his talk despite substantial protest. Protesters challenged the college’s decision and commemorated Arday. Some reports describe obstruction of entrances or attempts to enter restricted areas; specific conduct and numbers must be attributed to the reporting source. [S1][S2][S8]

The event involved several simultaneous exercises of expression: the speaker’s talk, the organisers’ advocacy, students’ objections and public protest. Lawful protest deserves protection; that does not automatically extend to preventing others from speaking.

A complete, authenticated recording or transcript is preferable to snippets when assessing exactly what Cofnas said.

## ⚖️ What Oriel actually said

Oriel’s 16 November statement is the key institutional primary source. The college said it had considered its legal duties and made practical arrangements, including consultation, security and welfare support. [S3]

**Established:** Oriel publicly acknowledged its free-speech and equality responsibilities.

**Not established by that statement alone:** the content of its legal advice; the full equality analysis; whether postponement was considered; the reasons alternatives were rejected; or whether all risks were appropriately weighed.

The existence of legal advice does not make a decision immune from criticism. Equally, non-publication of confidential advice or security material is not proof that the underlying work did not happen.

## 🧿 Institutional judgement: the alternatives

Questions for the college and organisers include:

- Was a **definite postponement** considered, rather than cancellation?
- Could the event have used a different format, independent academic moderator or structured expert response?
- What consultation occurred before the decision was final?
- Which changes to venue, access and security were implemented, and why?
- Did the assessment distinguish physical safety, equality, welfare, educational access and reputational risk?
- Was a post-event review commissioned, and will a non-sensitive account be published?

These are **options for examination**, not claims that any one option was legally required or necessarily feasible. For the statutory and proportionality analysis, see [⚖️ Balancing Rights and Duties](./⚖️_balancing_rights_and_duties.md).

## 🧩 Competing positions

**Oriel and supporters of the event:** Lawful expression must be protected even when unpopular; hosting does not equal endorsement. [S3]

**Students and critics:** The event raised serious concerns about race, academic standards, safety, the treatment of affected students and the timing following Arday’s death. [S5][S6][S1]

Both propositions can be true. The question is whether the institution exercised its powers with sufficient care, transparency and proportionality.

## 🛠️ Outstanding evidence requests

- Original booking request, applicable booking rules and approval pathway.
- A non-sensitive account of the legal and equality considerations.
- Whether postponement was considered and, if so, the documented reasons for rejecting it.
- The extent and timing of student consultation and any changes resulting from it.
- Any published post-event incident or welfare review.
- The status of the coronial proceedings from an authoritative source.
- An authenticated full transcript or recording of the talk.

**Evidence rule:** Absence of a public document is not evidence that no internal document exists. Record unanswered questions as unanswered questions.

## ⚖️ Closing position

Oriel has stated that it considered its legal duties, obtained advice and made arrangements for the event. That deserves to be recorded accurately. [S3]

But a statement that an event **could lawfully proceed** is not necessarily a complete answer to whether proceeding **then and there** was the wisest exercise of institutional judgement.

**Why this speaker, in this setting, at this time — and what alternatives were actually considered?**

*WTF, Oxon?*
