# 🌱 Prosocial Roots
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-06  
*Reporting frameworks for social cohesion, legitimate disagreement, public trust, material security, safeguarding, and responses that reduce rather than reproduce harm.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

**Prosocial Roots** looks at the conditions that help people remain part of a shared society, especially when trust is low, disagreement is intense, material security is weakening, or harm has already occurred.

It is interested in the roots of both conflict and cooperation:

- what gives people a credible place in public life;
- what makes disagreement feel survivable;
- what allows institutions to retain trust;
- what material conditions make participation possible;
- what warning signs can be recognised before harm escalates;
- what pushes people towards isolation, hostility, or destructive action;
- and what kinds of public response reduce harm rather than compounding it.

The aim is not to manufacture consensus or ask harmed people to become endlessly understanding.

It is to examine how societies preserve enough recognition, connection, material possibility, safety, and legitimate agency for people to disagree without treating one another as disposable.

The branch now approaches that problem through four substantial working packs:

```text
🌱 PROSOCIAL ROOTS
│
├── 🧾 THE COST OF LIFE
│      → CAN PEOPLE BUILD AND MAINTAIN A VIABLE LIFE?
│
├── 🍉 PALESTINE AS RHIZOME
│      → CAN UNRESOLVED POLITICAL SOLIDARITY REMAIN
│        LEGIBLE INSIDE DEMOCRATIC LIFE?
│
├── 🦷 POLITICAL VIOLENCE TODAY
│      → CAN SOCIETY CONFRONT POLITICAL VIOLENCE
│        WITHOUT MANUFACTURING A SIMPLER OR LARGER THREAT?
│
└── 🌸 DIGITALLY WOMEN
       → CAN INSTITUTIONS RECOGNISE GENDERED RISK EARLY
         WITHOUT TURNING PREVENTION INTO INDISCRIMINATE SUSPICION?
```

These are different subjects.

They share a systems question:

> **How do we take harm seriously enough to respond, while preserving the distinctions that stop the response becoming another source of harm?**

## 🗞️ Why This Belongs Under Press Matters

Journalism does more than describe social conflict after it happens.

Reporting can influence:

- which experiences are publicly recognised;
- whose fear is treated as credible;
- which disagreements remain discussable;
- whether whole communities are presented as threats;
- whether exceptional incidents become permanent identities;
- whether material pressure is treated as personal failure or public condition;
- whether warning signs become visible before a crisis;
- and whether public responses produce understanding, humiliation, solidarity, exclusion, or escalation.

This does not mean journalism is responsible for every social outcome.

It means reporting forms part of the environment in which people understand one another, institutions establish legitimacy, risks become visible, and political choices become possible.

Good reporting therefore needs to do more than identify the loudest conflict.

It needs to preserve:

```text
scale
context
provenance
causation
difference
material conditions
institutional responsibility
human agency
```

without using complexity as an excuse for paralysis.

## 🌱 What Prosocial Means Here

Prosocial does not mean polite, moderate, obedient, or free from conflict.

A prosocial response strengthens the capacity of people and institutions to:

- recognise harm accurately;
- protect those at risk;
- preserve human dignity;
- resolve conflict through legitimate means;
- maintain truthful public memory;
- support meaningful participation;
- preserve the material foundations of ordinary life;
- recognise risk without converting uncertainty into guilt;
- and prevent further harm without unnecessarily widening the field of enemies.

A response may be firm, angry, or coercive and still serve a prosocial purpose.

Equally, a response may sound compassionate while deepening exclusion, mistrust, precarity, or danger.

The question is not simply whether a response appears kind.

The question is what it helps grow.

## 🧭 Core Principles

### Recognition Is Not Endorsement

People can be recognised as members of society without their beliefs, demands, or conduct being approved.

Understanding why a position exists does not require accepting its proposed solution.

### Explanation Is Not Absolution

Understanding the conditions that produce harmful behaviour does not erase personal, political, or legal responsibility.

It makes prevention more realistic.

### Cohesion Is Not Consensus

Social cohesion does not require people to share one identity, culture, moral framework, or political conclusion.

It requires enough trust, restraint, material security, and shared infrastructure for serious disagreement to remain inside social and democratic life.

### Safety And Belonging Are Connected

People need protection from violence, coercion, and abuse.

They also need credible routes to dignity, participation, recognition, and a future worth investing in.

Neither can safely replace the other.

### Material Security Is Civic Infrastructure

Housing, food, energy, healthcare, transport, time, work, family life, and the ability to plan are not merely private consumer concerns.

They affect whether people can:

- participate;
- leave unsafe situations;
- care for others;
- change jobs;
- organise;
- recover;
- maintain relationships;
- and believe ordinary effort can still produce a viable future.

Private coping can conceal public deterioration.

Survival is not proof that a system is functioning well.

### Prevention Is Not Pre-Emptive Punishment

A warning sign is not a conviction.

A risk signal is not proof of future violence.

A concerning belief is not automatically criminal conduct.

But a system that can respond only after certainty arrives may be incapable of safeguarding.

Prosocial prevention therefore asks:

> **Which institution should respond to which information, at what threshold, with what authority, and with what safeguards?**

The aim is better discrimination, not indiscriminate suspicion.

### Public Trust Depends On Consistency

Institutions lose legitimacy when similar harms are described differently according to the identity, status, or political usefulness of those involved.

Consistency does not mean every case is identical.

It means differences should be explained rather than assumed.

### Connection Is Not Equivalence

People, institutions, movements, histories and harms can be connected without becoming interchangeable.

A shared mechanism does not erase historical specificity.

A common institution does not create identical politics.

A comparison is useful when it clarifies something.

It becomes destructive when it flattens the people or histories being compared.

### Difficult Subjects Should Not Be Abandoned

When mainstream institutions refuse to acknowledge a real concern, radical actors may become the only people offering recognition.

Opening a subject for examination does not give every answer equal factual or moral weight.

### Responses Can Become Causes

A badly designed reaction may increase the conditions associated with future harm:

- humiliation;
- social expulsion;
- collective blame;
- status competition;
- material insecurity;
- institutional mistrust;
- conspiracy;
- or the belief that lawful participation is futile.

The response must therefore be studied as part of the system, not merely as its conclusion.

## 🧩 Scope

This folder can hold work relating to:

- political violence and radicalisation;
- democratic disagreement;
- public trust and institutional legitimacy;
- loneliness, exclusion, and social abandonment;
- economic insecurity and household pressure;
- housing and the material foundations of participation;
- family formation and caring;
- intergenerational misunderstanding;
- race, class, religion, gender, and national belonging;
- protest and public order;
- persistent political mobilisation;
- community safety;
- misogyny and gendered harm;
- safeguarding and early intervention;
- social repair after violence or scandal;
- moral injury and collective grief;
- media narratives that intensify conflict;
- routes out of extremist or conspiratorial movements;
- restorative and preventive practices;
- civic participation;
- youth agency and meaningful adulthood;
- shared public spaces;
- information movement between institutions;
- and the conditions under which diverse populations continue choosing to remain a society.

The branch should remain open to cohesion issues that do not initially look like security questions.

A breakdown in social trust may first appear through:

```text
housing
healthcare
education
work
family life
protest
local media
professional institutions
digital environments
access to public systems
```

Likewise, a safeguarding problem may initially look like a school issue, an online dispute, a family concern, an employment problem, or a collection of apparently unrelated incidents.

The route by which a problem becomes visible should not determine whether the system is capable of understanding it.

## ❓ Working Questions

Work in this folder should generally ask:

- What is the underlying social problem?
- Who experiences the current arrangement as protective?
- Who experiences it as excluding, humiliating, precarious, or dangerous?
- What harm must be acknowledged clearly?
- What material conditions shape the available choices?
- What legitimate routes for agency currently exist?
- Where have people stopped trusting formal routes?
- What information is visible before the crisis?
- Who holds it?
- Can it travel lawfully and proportionately?
- Who is responsible for recognising the combined picture?
- What response is being proposed?
- What behaviour will that response reward?
- What will it make easier or harder next time?
- Does it reduce the number of enemies, or merely rename them?
- Does it protect people without treating whole populations as suspect?
- Which distinctions must survive the intervention?
- What would meaningful repair require?
- What helps people remain invested in a shared future?

## 🧿 Editorial Approach

Nodes should be:

- curious without becoming naïve;
- compassionate without becoming exculpatory;
- firm about harm without relying on contempt;
- alert to structural conditions without erasing agency;
- precise about scale and classification;
- willing to investigate warning signs without treating them as destiny;
- and willing to distinguish legitimate disagreement from dehumanisation.

Do not assume that social conflict is produced by misunderstanding alone.

Material interests, power, impunity, prejudice, scarcity, repression, and deliberate exploitation remain real.

Prosocial analysis should not turn oppression into a communication problem or demand reconciliation from people who remain in danger.

It should not turn poverty into a budgeting problem.

It should not turn protest persistence into a behavioural defect.

It should not turn political violence into the pathology of one ideology without evidence.

It should not turn misogynistic risk information into a magical predictor of future offending.

Where repair is not yet possible, the work may instead identify:

- minimum safety conditions;
- factual boundaries;
- routes for accountability;
- protections against retaliation;
- proportionate interventions;
- and what must stop before trust can reasonably be requested.

# 🧺 Current Packs

## 🧾 [The Cost Of Life](./🧾_The_Cost_Of_Life/)

### Material conditions for a viable life

**The Cost Of Life** examines what happens when the ordinary foundations of a viable life become steadily harder to secure.

Its concern is broader than a basket of prices.

It connects:

- food and energy;
- rent and mortgages;
- earnings and real purchasing power;
- healthcare;
- transport;
- childcare;
- debt;
- housing security;
- time poverty;
- family formation;
- the ability to leave unsafe situations;
- and the practical capacity to participate in society.

Its central distinction is:

> **The cost of living asks what things cost.  
> The cost of life asks what people must surrender in order to keep going.**

The pack treats economic insecurity as a cohesion issue without assuming that hardship mechanically produces one political outcome.

It asks what happens to trust, relationships, autonomy, planning, participation and democratic legitimacy when ordinary effort no longer appears capable of producing stability.

Use this pack when the primary question is:

> **Can people still build, maintain and imagine a viable life?**

## 🍉 [Palestine As Rhizome](./🍉_Palestine_As_Rhizome/)

### Persistent mobilisation around an unresolved Palestinian reality

**Palestine As Rhizome** examines why Palestine remains a durable site of protest, political identification, institutional dispute and interconnected public concern.

The rhizome is not Palestine itself.

It is the wider field of political relationships, solidarities, obligations, institutional connections, disputes and forms of participation that has developed around continuing Palestinian dispossession, mass harm, political abandonment and demands for accountability.

People may arrive at the same march through different routes:

- Palestinian life or identity;
- humanitarian concern;
- international law;
- religious or diasporic commitments;
- anti-racism;
- Jewish ethical or anti-Zionist commitments;
- professional duties;
- opposition to arms sales;
- protest rights;
- policing;
- university governance;
- media credibility;
- workplace speech;
- or institutional consistency.

Those routes overlap without becoming identical.

The pack therefore insists on several disciplines:

```text
PERSISTENCE REQUIRES EXPLANATION

CONNECTION IS NOT EQUIVALENCE

SOLIDARITY IS NOT UNIFORMITY

REPRESSION CAN REDISTRIBUTE POLITICAL MEANING

PALESTINIAN LIFE AND AGENCY REMAIN CENTRAL
```

It asks not simply why people are marching, but what remains unresolved, what new relationships have developed around the issue, how domestic institutions materially connect people to it, and what happens when attempts to suppress participation create additional political routes.

Use this pack when the primary question is:

> **How does an unresolved Palestinian reality continue to travel through democratic, institutional and social life without those connections replacing Palestine itself?**

## 🦷 [Political Violence Today](./🦷_Political_Violence_Today/)

### Accurate threat recognition without threat inflation

**Political Violence Today** is an evidence-first reporting and analysis pack on political violence, motive, classification, weapons culture, security responses, democratic practice and prevention.

It deliberately separates things public narratives often compress:

```text
POLITICAL TARGET
        ≠
POLITICAL MOTIVE

IDEOLOGY
        ≠
OPERATIONAL PROFILE

EXTREMISM
        ≠
TERRORISM
        ≠
ORDINARY CRIME

SMALL THREAT
        ≠
NO THREAT
```

The pack covers:

- cases and motives;
- language and classification;
- numbers and comparison;
- how violence becomes meaningful;
- targets, methods and effects;
- contested causes and public morality;
- weapon cultures and social permission;
- security, surveillance and spending;
- democratic practice;
- narrative and power;
- reporter tools;
- and structured sources.

It asks why violence occurs without treating explanation as absolution.

It takes rare threats seriously without describing them as dominant merely because they are politically salient.

It tests whether proposed security responses actually address the mechanism being described.

And it treats political violence as a recurring human problem rather than the unique pathology of one ideology or community.

Use this pack when the primary question is:

> **Can we identify, explain and prevent political violence without allowing classification or rhetoric to manufacture a simpler or larger threat than the evidence supports?**

## 🌸 [Digitally Women](./🌸_Digitally_Women/)

### Misogyny, digital environments and before-the-offence safeguarding

**Digitally Women** examines misogyny and digital information environments as problems spanning culture, safeguarding, law, institutions and system design.

Its central question is:

> **If warning signs can be visible before serious harm, what would institutions need to notice, connect and do differently?**

The branch includes:

- definitions and analytical registers;
- the live parliamentary and legal-policy context around Petition 746640;
- before-the-offence safeguarding;
- misogyny as a possible risk signal;
- schools, colleges and universities;
- survivor reporting;
- information movement;
- pattern recognition;
- recurring recommendations across UK VAWG and safeguarding reviews;
- institutional learning and recommendation decay;
- and rapid press tools.

Its core distinctions include:

```text
WARNING SIGN
        ≠
CRIME
        ≠
PROOF OF FUTURE VIOLENCE

EXPOSURE
        ≠
PERSUASION
        ≠
RADICALISATION
        ≠
OFFENDING

INFORMATION SHARING
        ≠
INFORMATION BECOMING USABLE KNOWLEDGE
```

The aim is neither to criminalise every concerning behaviour nor to wait for a completed offence before acknowledging that risk information existed.

It asks which systems should respond, at what threshold, with what authority, and with what safeguards.

For the shortest orientation, start with [🗣️ Explain This in 500 Words or Less](./🌸_Digitally_Women/🗣️_explain_this_in_500_words_or_less.md).

Use this pack when the primary question is:

> **Can institutions recognise gendered risk earlier and more accurately without turning safeguarding into indiscriminate suspicion or pre-emptive punishment?**

## 🕸️ What The Four Packs Have In Common

These packs are not four examples of the same problem.

They are four places where similar analytical disciplines become useful.

### 🧾 The Cost Of Life asks:

> **What happens when the material basis for participation erodes?**

### 🍉 Palestine As Rhizome asks:

> **What happens when an unresolved political reality develops many routes through domestic public life?**

### 🦷 Political Violence Today asks:

> **What happens when real violence becomes attached to broad threat categories and politically useful narratives?**

### 🌸 Digitally Women asks:

> **What happens when risk may be visible before criminal certainty exists, but the relevant information is fragmented across systems?**

Across all four, **Prosocial Roots** keeps returning to:

- evidence before narrative;
- explanation without absolution;
- recognition without endorsement;
- connection without equivalence;
- prevention without collective suspicion;
- proportionality without neglect;
- material conditions without determinism;
- institutional consistency;
- legitimate routes for agency;
- and responses whose consequences are examined rather than assumed.

That is the connective tissue.

## 🌿 Working Principle

A prosocial response does not pretend that conflict, scarcity, danger, or injustice are absent.

It creates better conditions for confronting them without unnecessarily producing new harms, new exclusions, or new permanent enemies.

That requires two things at once:

> **Take harm seriously enough to intervene.**

and:

> **Take distinctions seriously enough not to create more harm while doing it.**
