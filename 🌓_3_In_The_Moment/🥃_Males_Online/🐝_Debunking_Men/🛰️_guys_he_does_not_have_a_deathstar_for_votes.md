# 🛰️ Guys, He Does Not Have a Death Star for Votes
**First created:** 2026-10-22 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-23  
*Mel Brooks, Miyazaki, mushroom robots, actual orbital telecommunications, and why a billionaire's impressive infrastructure is not a magical ballot-box override.*

## 🛰️ Orientation

ELON, MATE.

ARE THESE SPACE LASERS FOR BROADBAND, OR HAVE YOU BEEN WATCHING TOO MUCH ANIME? 🌳

This node concerns a real and rather important distinction: **having extraordinary technology is not the same thing as having every extraordinary capability someone might imagine for it.** In particular, Starlink's optical communications links are not evidence that Elon Musk can alter votes from orbit. The United States has genuine election-security risks, including interference, disinformation, compromised systems and administrative disruption. Those risks deserve mechanisms, evidence, safeguards and scrutiny—not a science-fiction shortcut that makes everyone feel helpless.

Why now? Alex Gibney's documentary *Musk* reached cinemas in October 2026, shortly before the US midterms. Circulating excerpts of Ashley St. Clair discussing Musk's comments about an election and thousands of space lasers can sound alarming. The purpose here is not to criticise the filmmakers for examining those remarks. Nor is it to adjudicate St. Clair's relationship, or to accuse Musk of secretly changing votes. **It is to explain the engineering, distinguish prediction from control, and refuse the suggestion that a ballot is pointless because someone owns satellites.**

I haven't watched the entire documentary. Reporting provides more context than a circulating clip, but it is not a substitute for the full film or a complete exchange. Where the evidence stops, this node stops. Where the science begins, however, I am going to be quite annoying.

## ✨ Key Features

- **Cultural instruction first:** *Spaceballs*, *Castle in the Sky*, *Monk & Robot*, Zelda and *Toy Story* are not optional enrichment activities. They explain what we imagine technological power is *for*.
- **No woman-as-punchline:** an extraordinary power differential can make challenging technical authority difficult without proving a particular relationship was abusive.
- **The actual lasers:** Starlink's optical intersatellite links transmit data. They are real; the electoral Death Star is not demonstrated.
- **Maximum-capability stress test:** give a fictional Dark Helmet satellites, data, money, AI and a platform. What can he influence, disrupt or control—and what stops him?
- **Democratic resilience:** distinguish real security incidents from unsupported claims, and give people reliable, nonpartisan ways to check election procedures.

## 🎬 I. Mel Brooks Was Right, Obviously

We are starting with Mel Brooks because I have decided that we are starting with Mel Brooks. If you came here expecting to skip straight to electoral machinery, I am afraid you must first sit through my nerdery. This is the price of admission.

*Spaceballs* gives us Dark Helmet: a man with access to an absurd amount of machinery, an equally absurd sense of personal importance, and no guarantee that possession of a large apparatus translates into wisdom. The joke is not that engineering is silly. Engineering is wonderful. The joke is the assumption that a person who commands an impressive machine must therefore understand everything else.

For our purposes, **Light Helmet** is the satirical cousin: the space weeaboo with real rockets, real satellites and an apparent surplus of technological mythology surrounding him. The character is a joke; the specifications are not.

And before we do the specifications, Japan has something to say.

## 🌳 II. Laputa: Sir, There Is a Giant Fucking Tree

In Hayao Miyazaki's *Castle in the Sky* (1986), Muska sees the floating civilisation of Laputa as an opportunity for domination. There is ancient engineering, terrible destructive potential and an entire fantasy of command waiting to be claimed.

But Laputa is not only its weapons. It has gardens. Birds. A great tree. And robots whose remaining work includes tending living things.

**THE TREE WAS THE POINT, YOU ABSOLUTE WALNUTS.**

The distinction is not technology versus nature. The garden is being maintained *with* technology. The distinction is between technologies used to make other beings obey and technologies used to sustain a world in which other beings can flourish.

The fact that the antagonist is called **Muska** is an entirely unreasonable gift from the universe. No, this is not an allegation that a contemporary billionaire is secretly a Studio Ghibli villain. It is a joke about the persistent human temptation to see a floating city and immediately ask where the weapons controls are.

### 🍄 You could have Mosscap

Becky Chambers's *A Psalm for the Wild-Built* offers a gentler but equally pointed contrast. Sibling Dex, who is explicitly nonbinary, meets Splendid Speckled Mosscap: a robot named after a mushroom, interested in what humans need. Not what they can buy. Not what they can dominate. What they need.

The book does not require the reader to solve Dex's gender before proceeding with the philosophical questions. Nor does Mosscap have to conquer anybody to justify its existence. We can also think of the constructs and ancient machinery of newer *Zelda*: fantastical technological systems that can be used for exploration, repair, cultivation and play.

**YOU COULD HAVE MUSHROOM-NAMED GARDENING ROBOTS.**

Shiitake on canopy inspection. Enoki in the nursery. Button taking soil samples. Chanterelle checking fungal networks. A whole little civilisation of botanical maintenance. And yes, they could communicate through sophisticated networks.

**JUST MAKE THE TEA AND HAVE THE TEA AND TALK TO MOSSCAP, YOU FIEND.** 🍵

The point is not that billionaires are obliged to reproduce the plot of a novella. It is that technological possibility is larger than the imagination of domination.

## 🚀 III. He Has a Car That Falls With Style

In February 2018, SpaceX's Falcon Heavy launched a Tesla Roadster with a mannequin called Starman into a heliocentric trajectory. A genuinely extraordinary aerospace demonstration, carried out by large teams of people whose engineering work deserves recognition. [1]

**HE HAS A CAR THAT, LIKE BUZZ LIGHTYEAR, FALLS WITH STYLE.**

This matters because the debunk is not: *nothing impressive exists*. The impressive things exist. SpaceX operates launch vehicles. Starlink operates a large satellite communications system. Optical links between spacecraft are technically remarkable. Their reality is exactly why we must not substitute vague awe for mechanism.

| What someone says | What we should ask |
|---|---|
| 'We built it' | Has the capability been demonstrated, and under what conditions? |
| 'We're going to build it' | Is this a research objective, prototype, schedule or sales projection? |
| 'The system is powerful' | Powerful in what measurable sense: bandwidth, energy, coverage, reach, access? |
| 'It could affect an election' | By which physical, cyber, informational or administrative pathway? |
| 'He knew the result' | Was this forecasting, access to partial data, boasting or something else? |
| 'He controlled the result' | Where is the evidence of control over actual voting or tabulation processes? |

Ambition is not a crime. Hyperbole is not a specification. And confidence is not a substitute for evidence.

## 🦚 IV. The Woman Is Not the Engineering Problem

### What the documentary reporting actually establishes

The documentary is Alex Gibney's *Musk*, premiered at Venice in September 2026 and released theatrically in October. NPR's September reporting says it obtained and authenticated messages between Musk and Ashley St. Clair. St. Clair describes Musk referring to an **'anomaly in the matrix'** in connection with the 2024 presidential election and telling her he had **'over 10 thousand lasers in space'**. Asked whether she understood this to mean SpaceX, she answered, **'I don't know where else he has lasers.'** [2]

Those are short excerpts reported by NPR—not a complete reconstruction of the conversation. NPR also reported Musk's lawyer disputing the insinuation that the technology was used to interfere with voting. **Authenticated messages can establish that words were sent; they do not establish that the technical implication drawn from them is true.** The film's presentation and subsequent commentary also need to be distinguished from the underlying exchange.

This is an especially useful moment to resist the internet's favourite sport: finding a woman in a difficult situation and demanding that she have delivered a perfect technical cross-examination on the spot.

### Mid-20s woman; substantially older, extraordinarily wealthy man. Sadly not more intelligent, interesting, or mature for it.  

At the time of the 2024 conversations, St. Clair was in her **mid-20s** and Musk in his **mid-50s**. <!-- Look. He needs to hear it like that.--> The age gap is only one part of it. Add immense wealth, specialist institutional access, influence over a large platform, technical authority, and the social dynamics of an intimate relationship.

People will disagree about whether relationships with such disparities are acceptable, or inherently exploitative. **A substantial structural risk of exploitation is not proof that exploitation occurred.** Two people might be fond of one another and sincerely try to manage an inequality that remains difficult to manage. The inequality does not evaporate because the intentions are good.

Yes, literature by Austen and the Brontës depicts societies in which unequal marriages, economic dependence and expectations surrounding women's age were familiar. Historical normalisation does not make those asymmetries irrelevant today. Nor does a woman's political background remove her entitlement to speak for herself. St. Clair has publicly discussed her own political past and choices; this note is not a character assessment of her. [3]

I have not examined all of her testimony, and I'm not going to pretend I can resolve her private relationship from excerpts. Her account may contain experiences she considers good, bad, ambiguous, or all three. That is hers to describe.

What we *can* say is that people often defer to apparent experts, and that deference can become harder to challenge when the expert is also a much older, extraordinarily powerful intimate partner. This is not a diagnosis of either individual; it is a recognisable social and informational asymmetry.

She might not have felt able to ask. She might not have thought to ask. She might have asked and received an explanation omitted from the clips. **We do not know.**

I would not necessarily expect a woman in her mid-20s in that position to interrupt with an impromptu aerospace engineering viva.

Fortunately, I am older and considerably more annoying.

## 🔦 V. Congratulations, Light Helmet. You've Invented Telecommunications

### What Starlink says its lasers do

Starlink describes its space lasers as **optical intersatellite links**: systems that transmit digital information between satellites using light. Its technology page describes three optical links per satellite, capable of up to **200 gigabits per second** per link for the cited design. Starlink's 2025 progress report describes a constellation-wide optical mesh with **more than 24,000 lasers**—a network count, not 24,000 orbital weapons. Hardware generations and deployment details vary, so those figures should not be treated as eternal specifications for every spacecraft. [4][5]

A simplified mental model: **lots of little communications terminals that send information using light rather than radio waves.** The analogy is to fibre-optic communications without a physical fibre between the spacecraft, not to the Death Star's superlaser.

The laser's optical carrier is modulated to encode data. Narrow beams are aimed between moving satellites using pointing and tracking systems. Receivers detect the signals and the network routes information onwards. These links help satellites pass traffic across the constellation without always sending it immediately to a nearby ground station. NASA has described work with SpaceX to demonstrate optical relay services for other spacecraft. [6]

There are important distinctions here:

- **Optical crosslink:** laser carrying data between spacecraft.
- **Radio user link:** electromagnetic communications between satellite and compatible ground terminal.
- **Ground network:** gateways, terrestrial networks, internet routing and associated equipment.
- **Directed-energy weapon:** a different system whose relevant parameters would include delivered energy, beam control, dwell time and target effects.
- **Election compromise:** a cyber, physical or procedural pathway affecting election infrastructure, not a magical property of a laser.

A laser is not automatically a weapon. A communications system can be strategically significant without having an undocumented ability to rewrite ballots.

### The mechanism test

| Claim | What is actually supported | Missing step if someone claims election control |
|---|---|---|
| Starlink satellites use lasers | **Yes:** optical data links between satellites | None; that's the ordinary engineering |
| Those links can carry important information | **Yes:** high-bandwidth networking | Important information is not equivalent to privileged election access |
| Starlink can connect terrestrial users | **Yes:** through compatible terminals and network infrastructure | A specific path into a relevant election system |
| A network operator can influence connectivity | **Potentially:** service management and network dependencies matter | Evidence of the particular dependency, action and consequence |
| A satellite laser can simply change a vote | **Not demonstrated** | A plausible mechanism, access to the relevant system, and evidence of alteration |
| A satellite constellation can control every US election | **Not demonstrated** | Compromise across diverse jurisdictions, records, safeguards and procedures |
| Someone can predict an election confidently | **Yes, people can make forecasts** | Prediction does not prove interference or control |

The key distinction is **transport versus authority**. A system that can move packets does not thereby have permission to change whatever those packets might describe. A road can carry a ballot box; owning the road does not give you lawful or automatic control of the ballots.

### Prediction ≠ privileged access ≠ interference ≠ control

Campaigns, researchers and analysts can estimate outcomes from polling, turnout assumptions, historical data and other information. Those estimates can be confident, uncertain, accurate or wrong. An apparently prescient remark is not evidence of manipulating a vote count.

Equally, the presence of real-time data somewhere in a political operation would not, without further evidence, establish access to official voting-machine tabulations or the ability to modify them. Each escalation is a new claim requiring new proof.

A separate claim about unauthorised access to voting equipment should be assessed on its own evidence. An authorised penetration test is not the same as an illicit intrusion. And neither is established by someone saying **SPACE LASERS**.

## 🪖 VI. Give Dark Helmet the Entire Budget

Right. We have disposed of the imaginary orbital ballot-ray. Now let's ask the harder question.

Suppose a **fictional** Dark Helmet has enormous capital, satellites, a major social platform, advanced AI, large datasets, communications infrastructure, political access and very few scruples. What could such an actor plausibly do to a country the size of the United States?

This is a **capability and resilience exercise**, not an allegation that Musk has done any of the following.

| Domain | Plausible consequence | What constrains or detects it |
|---|---|---|
| Public narratives | Amplify misleading claims or confuse people about procedures | Independent reporting, competing channels, authoritative local guidance, public scrutiny |
| Political access | Seek disproportionate influence over agendas and institutions | Disclosure, law, competing interests, oversight, elections and courts |
| Communications | Disrupt users who depend on a particular network | Redundant networks, contingency plans, procurement safeguards, alternatives |
| Data analytics | Build sophisticated models of behaviour and turnout | Data gaps, uncertain assumptions, model error, no automatic control over individuals |
| Cyber intrusion | Exploit a specific vulnerable system if access is obtained | Segmentation, authentication, monitoring, forensic investigation, backups |
| Election administration | Cause local confusion, outages or delays | Local procedures, paper backups, emergency plans, independent officials |
| Ballot tabulation | Attempt interference where a system is vulnerable | Paper records, chain of custody, testing, audits, recounts and legal scrutiny |
| Certification | Pressure or confuse the processes that establish official results | Statutes, courts, multiple offices, documentation and public accountability |

The limitations are not magic either. No security system is perfect; different jurisdictions have different equipment, resources and practices. Serious interference can cause harm even without achieving total control. But **the difference between influence, disruption and comprehensive control is enormous**.

America is not one national voting computer with an exposed 'make candidate win' button. Election administration involves state and local offices, registration databases, electronic pollbooks, ballot-marking systems, scanners, paper ballots, unofficial reporting, canvasses, audits and certification. These are different systems with different access paths and safeguards. The US Election Assistance Commission describes pre-election testing, ballot reconciliation, secure custody, audits and other controls; CISA's election-security assessments emphasise redundancy and recoverability. [7][8]

That distinction is not comforting because concentrated power is harmless; it is useful because **we need to know what kind of power we're actually dealing with**.

## 🗳️ VII. Real Election Interference Is Real. That's Why We Need the Right Threat Model.

US authorities have documented interference attempts, disinformation and cybersecurity risks. In September 2024, the FBI and CISA specifically warned that false claims about hacked voter information could undermine confidence, noting that possession of voter-registration data did not itself demonstrate a breach. [9]

After the November 2024 election, CISA said it had **no evidence of malicious activity materially affecting the security or integrity of election infrastructure**. That was a dated official assessment of the 2024 election—not a promise that every future election will be flawless, and not a substitute for investigating specific new evidence. [10]

A useful taxonomy:

| Threat | What it means | What evidence matters |
|---|---|---|
| Influence operation | Attempts to shape beliefs or participation | Messaging networks, funding, distribution, attribution |
| Registration disruption | Errors or interference affecting voter records | Official records, logs, affected voters, remediation |
| Administrative disruption | Interruptions to polling or election services | Incident records, operational effects, recovery |
| Unauthorised access | Entry into systems or equipment without permission | Access logs, forensic evidence, authorisation records |
| Vote alteration | Improper changes to ballot recording or counting | Ballot records, audits, system forensics, chain of custody |
| Certification interference | Improper pressure or obstruction of official processes | Documented acts, legal filings, procedural records |

Reports about officials requesting access to voting equipment or asking for hacking must be handled case by case. **Was this an authorised security test? An alleged unauthorised intrusion? A political demand? What system, date and jurisdiction?** Without identifying the incident, we cannot responsibly pick an answer.

### What would change this node's conclusion?

Authenticated records of access, reproducible technical findings, forensic evidence of changed records, independent audits revealing discrepancies, or official investigations establishing a specific mechanism would all matter. The standard is not 'trust the billionaire' or 'trust the government'. It is **show the mechanism, show the evidence, show the checks**.

## 🗳️ VIII. If You're Worried About Voting, Here Are the Actual Options

This is **nonpartisan procedural information**, not advice on whom to support. Rules differ by state and sometimes locality; check the official source for your own jurisdiction rather than assuming a national rule.

| Concern | Practical action |
|---|---|
| 'Am I registered?' | Check your record through your state's official election site, reached via **Vote.gov**. |
| 'My registration looks wrong' | Contact your state or local election office promptly; ask about correction, deadlines and available remedies. |
| 'I can't vote on Election Day' | Check whether your state offers early in-person voting or absentee/mail voting, and the relevant deadlines. |
| 'Was my mail ballot received?' | Use your jurisdiction's official tracking system if available; ask about ballot-curing procedures where relevant. |
| 'I'm missing from the pollbook' | Ask election workers about your eligibility, the correct location and whether a **provisional ballot** is appropriate. |
| 'I heard the polling place moved' | Confirm the location and hours with the official election authority, not a circulating screenshot. |
| 'I want to know how results are checked' | Ask election officials about paper records, reconciliation, audits, recounts and certification. |
| 'Someone else needs practical help' | Share official registration information, explain deadlines and offer lawful, nonpartisan logistical assistance. |

The official **Vote.gov** site links to state registration and voting information. The **US Election Assistance Commission** explains early voting, mail voting, provisional ballots, audits and election-night reporting. Importantly, election-night numbers are **unofficial**; canvassing, verification and certification follow. [11][12]

Registration-roll errors can be serious whether accidental, administrative or deliberate. The right response is to identify the affected record, find the relevant legal remedy and document the issue—not conclude that the existence of a registration problem proves an orbital conspiracy.

No one needs to promise that every system is perfect. People need reliable information about the systems that actually exist, the remedies available when they fail, and the evidence required to establish misconduct.

## 🌳 IX. The Tree Was the Point

We began with Mel Brooks because he understood that having a giant machine doesn't necessarily mean you know what you're doing with it. We visited Laputa because Muska sees a weapon where a robot sees a garden. We met Mosscap because there are more interesting questions for intelligent machines than how to make people obey.

And we examined Starlink because its optical links are impressive **as optical links**. They are not an established mechanism for changing votes. That does not mean money, platforms, cyber operations or infrastructure dependence are irrelevant to democracy. It means those problems are real enough to deserve actual analysis instead of an imaginary Death Star.

If an extraordinary technical claim frightens you, you're allowed to ask how it works. If you don't know the physics, that is not a moral failing. If someone else is more powerful, more confident or closer to the machinery, that doesn't make their every implication true.

And if a woman in her mid-20s did not conduct an aerospace viva in the middle of an intimate conversation, I am not putting her on trial for it.

## 🐈‍⬛ Post-Credits Scene — Salem Saberhagen, Patient Zero of Posting Through It

There is a character in *Sabrina the Teenage Witch* called Salem Saberhagen.

Salem is a former witch who attempted world domination and was consequently sentenced to spend a century as a cat.

This is already an excellent premise.

But what makes Salem particularly interesting, in retrospect, is that **being turned into a cat did absolutely nothing to cure him of being a man on the internet.**

There is an episode in which Salem is caught pretending to be a woman in online chat rooms.

Why?

Attention.

[HE LIKES THE ATTENTION.](https://youtu.be/tt1Zc7Zi9AY?is=WPY5r0avkAO_n4Il)  

This is a character who has already attempted world domination, been stripped of his powers, and been reduced to living in a house with three witches who regularly tell him to behave.

And still.

**THE MAN IS POSTING.**

In the 1990s.

Before Twitter. Before X. Before the modern social-media ecosystem had developed its elaborate machinery for converting attention into money, status and political influence.

Salem was already there.

A disgraced would-be dictator, pretending to be somebody else online, because apparently the validation of strangers remained an irresistible prospect.

**SALEM SABERHAGEN. THE ORIGINAL TERMINALLY ONLINE MAN.**

And perhaps this is the final distinction worth making.

You can give somebody satellites. You can give them rockets. You can give them an enormous communications platform.

None of those things guarantees that they will become any less interested in what strangers think of them.

Nor does an appetite for attention demonstrate the ability to control the people providing it.

Salem didn't need a Death Star.

He needed somebody to close the laptop.

**HILDA. ZELDA. YOUR CAT IS ON THE INTERNET AGAIN.**
