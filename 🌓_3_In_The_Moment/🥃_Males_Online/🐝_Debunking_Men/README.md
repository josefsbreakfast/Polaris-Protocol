# 🐝 Debunking Men
**First created:** 2026-10-09 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-09  
*An evidence-led examination of confidently delivered claims that have encountered the unfortunate obstacle of reality.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Welcome to **🐝 Debunking Men**, a Polaris Protocol cluster dedicated to examining factual claims made by men who have apparently mistaken confidence for evidence.

The problem is not that men have opinions.

The problem is that some men have developed an extraordinary capacity to present scientifically, historically, technologically or statistically unsupported assertions as though they were established facts.

Occasionally, on stage.

With microphones.

And an audience.

🐝 Oh dear.

This cluster examines the claims themselves, separating demonstrable mechanisms from speculation, misinterpretation, pseudoscience and outright bollocks.

**The objective is not to prove that men are always wrong. It is to establish whether the particular man currently speaking is correct.**

Sometimes he might be.

We remain open to this possibility.

---

## 🔬 What We Examine

Claims may concern:

- Biology, endocrinology and neuroscience.
- Psychology, attachment and human behaviour.
- Medicine, reproductive health and sexual development.
- Evolutionary biology and genetics.
- Technology, artificial intelligence and cybersecurity.
- History, anthropology and social organisation.
- Economics, statistics and demographic trends.
- Scientific research presented without its methodological limitations.

The common denominator is not the subject.

It is the gap between **what someone confidently claims to know and what the available evidence actually establishes.**

---

## 🧪 Methodology

Each debunking should establish:

1. **The claim:** What was actually said, by whom, when and where?
2. **The proposed mechanism:** How is the claim supposed to work?
3. **The evidence:** What does the relevant research establish?
4. **The missing steps:** Which assumptions, causal relationships or contextual variables have been overlooked?
5. **The verdict:** Supported, partially supported, unsupported, contradicted or presently indeterminate.
6. **The sources:** Primary research and authoritative references wherever possible.

Claims should be examined in their original context.

Where evidence is incomplete, uncertainty should be preserved rather than replaced with a more entertaining conclusion.

**A bad argument does not become a good argument merely because its conclusion is convenient.**

Nor does an amusingly bad argument excuse bad factchecking.

🐝 Standards, gentlemen.

---

## ⚖️ Scope And Boundaries

This cluster primarily examines **factual and scientific validity**, rather than providing a comprehensive ethical, political or legal critique of every claim.

Those dimensions may be important and can be addressed elsewhere in Polaris.

Here, the central question is frequently much simpler:

**Would this actually work?**

A proposal can be unethical and scientifically ineffective.

These are separate findings.

Likewise, an accurate scientific observation does not automatically justify the political or social conclusion someone has attached to it.

## 🧬 Relationship To Debugging The Men

**👾 Debugging The Men** examines the wider systems, cultures, incentives and information environments that produce particular behaviours and beliefs.

**🐝 Debunking Men** examines whether the individual claims survive contact with evidence.

One investigates the machinery.

The other checks whether the machinery is talking bollocks.

Both are useful.
