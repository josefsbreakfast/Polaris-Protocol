## 2. 🪞 The Chirality of a Molecule That Isn't Chiral

A molecule's *molecular formula* tells us the numbers and types of atoms present. It does not tell us how they are connected, let alone how they are arranged in three-dimensional space.

- **Constitutional isomers** share a molecular formula but differ in atom connectivity.
- **Stereoisomers** share connectivity but differ in spatial arrangement.
- **Enantiomers** are stereoisomers related as non-superimposable mirror images.
- **Diastereomers** are stereoisomers that are *not* mirror-image pairs.

The familiar analogy is your hands. They have corresponding parts but cannot be perfectly superimposed. The word *chirality* comes from the Greek for hand. A tetrahedral carbon attached to four different substituents is one common source of chirality; it is not the only one. Some molecules are chiral without such a carbon, and some structures containing stereogenic centres can be achiral overall because of symmetry.

```text
      MIRROR-IMAGE IDEA — SCHEMATIC, NOT A MOLECULE

          LEFT         |         RIGHT
         /  /          |          \  \
        /__/           |           \__\
         \             |             /
          \            |            /

      Mirror-related shapes need not be superimposable.
```

That diagram teaches handedness, not a particular drug structure. An ASCII picture that looks impressively chemical while being wrong is not scientific communication; it is interior decoration.

And now the title's first trap: **parent fentanyl is achiral**. There is no stable pair of fentanyl enantiomers to separate. Some *fentanyl analogues* possess stereogenic centres, but those are different chemical structures and must not be quietly substituted into this discussion.

## 3. 🧪 The Actual Molecule

Fentanyl has the formula **C₂₂H₂₈N₂O**. It is a synthetic opioid of the 4-anilidopiperidine family, with two phenyl groups, a six-membered nitrogen-containing piperidine ring, and an amide. Here is a connectivity sketch based on the structural reference supplied for this node. It is *not* a bond-angle-accurate skeletal drawing.

```text
                  Ph
                   |
                  CH2
                   |
                  CH2
                   |
                   N
                 /   \
               CH2   CH2
                |     |
               CH2   CH2
                 \   /
                   CH
                   |
                   N — Ph
                   |
                C(=O)
                   |
                  CH2
                   |
                  CH3

 Ph = phenyl group (C6H5—), an aromatic benzene ring
```

Read the sketch as follows: the **upper N** is part of the piperidine ring and bears a two-carbon chain ending in phenyl; the **lower N** is attached to carbon 4 of that ring, a phenyl group and the propionyl carbonyl. The two routes around carbon 4 of the piperidine ring are equivalent in the parent structure, so that carbon is not a stereogenic centre. The amide nitrogen does not create a stable enantiomeric pair either.

If you want the aromatic hexagon on its own:

```text
        ______
       /      \
      /   ○    \
      \        /
       \______/

          R

  ○ denotes aromatic electron delocalisation;
  R denotes the attached remainder of a molecule.
```

**Figure accessibility note:** The first drawing is a labelled connectivity tree with a piperidine ring; the second is a schematic benzene hexagon with an aromatic circle and an attachment labelled R. Neither is a reaction scheme or a recipe.

Fentanyl was developed by Paul Janssen's research group in 1959 and has legitimate, important medical applications, including anaesthesia and selected severe-pain settings. Its clinical value is precisely why it is worth understanding properly, rather than treating its name as shorthand for magical compliance.

## 4. 🏅 The Nobel Prize Has Entered the Chat

And yes, the timing is rather beautiful. **The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, announced on 7 October, recognised Henri B. Kagan and Kenso Soai for work on asymmetric chemistry and molecular handedness.** Reporting from the announcement describes the contribution of their work to understanding and producing particular mirror-image molecular forms. This is a live scientific achievement, not an antique anecdote about Louis Pasteur.

There is a useful earlier landmark too: **Benjamin List and David W. C. MacMillan received the 2021 Chemistry Nobel for asymmetric organocatalysis**, methods using small organic catalysts to favour particular products, including selected stereoisomers.

Why does any of this matter medically? Because proteins, enzymes and receptors are themselves three-dimensional, often chiral systems. Mirror-image molecules can fit and interact differently. Their biological effects may differ substantially, slightly or not meaningfully at all; it is an empirical question, not something we settle by waving a structural formula at a patient.

So the Nobel Prize is not here as a shiny medal to compete with another shiny medal. It is here because **science continues to advance through patient, cumulative, international work**. A person does not get to skip that work by deciding they are a Renaissance man with a drone.

## 5. 🧠 Receptors Are Not Remote Controls

Fentanyl primarily activates **μ-opioid receptors**, encoded by **OPRM1**. These are G-protein-coupled receptors whose signalling can reduce neuronal excitability and neurotransmitter release. In simplified terms, activation of Gi/o pathways reduces adenylyl cyclase activity and cAMP signalling; associated channel effects can reduce calcium entry and increase potassium conductance in relevant neurons.

That is a real mechanism. It is not a behavioural command.

Pain signalling, the subjective experience of pain, analgesia, sedation, euphoria or dysphoria, respiratory depression and physical incapacitation are **not synonyms**. A drug may affect more than one; it does not follow that they arrive together in an operationally convenient order, or that the person behaves as an observer expects.

Nor is an individual who reports poor analgesic responses to opioids necessarily protected from dangerous opioid effects. Tolerance, comparatively poor analgesia and variable receptor response are not interchangeable. The same person can receive disappointing pain relief and still be at risk of sedation or respiratory harm.

The claim that the substance *can* act on the nervous system therefore does not demonstrate that it *will* make someone compliant. This is where our sibling oxytocin discussion belongs. Oxytocin is a nine-amino-acid peptide with important physiological and context-dependent social functions. It is not a universal affection or consent switch. Different molecule, similar error in reasoning: **receptor biology is not a complete theory of human behaviour**.

**THE RECEPTOR HAS NOT SIGNED THE OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS DOCUMENT.**

## 6. 🫀 ADME, or Why the Liver Is a Subcontractor

Pharmacologists describe **absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion** as ADME. It is a useful reminder that the drug does not simply teleport from an exciting product pitch to the outcome slide.

**Absorption.** Route and formulation matter. A medically characterised route cannot be assumed equivalent to arbitrary environmental contamination. **DRONE IS NOT A ROUTE OF ADMINISTRATION.** Nothing in the hypothetical tells us that the exposure would produce a consistent effect, and it would be dangerous to assume either reliable incapacitation or harmlessness.

**Distribution.** Fentanyl is lipophilic, meaning it partitions readily into lipid-rich environments; it is also a weak base, substantially ionised at physiological pH. Distribution between blood, brain and tissues is dynamic. It is not a personal savings account for analgesia.

**Metabolism.** The liver, particularly CYP3A4-mediated pathways, converts much of fentanyl into metabolites including **norfentanyl**, which is largely inactive as an opioid.

**Excretion.** Urine after fentanyl administration contains predominantly metabolites rather than a reliably active stockpile of unchanged parent drug. Urine from someone who has taken fentanyl and urine to which somebody has *added* fentanyl are different propositions. Neither proposition establishes a real drone programme.

## 7. 🫁 Anaesthesia Would Like a Word

Respiratory depression is one of the serious risks of opioids. Fentanyl can also, in some circumstances, be associated with muscle rigidity sometimes called *wooden chest syndrome*, complicating ventilation. Those risks do not turn it into a dependable incapacitation device. They turn an uncontrolled exposure into a potentially dangerous medical emergency.

Naloxone can reverse opioid effects, but it is not a substitute for competent airway support, monitoring and clinical judgement. Its duration of action may be shorter than the opioid effect, so recurrence can be a concern. Not every patient given fentanyl requires intubation; legitimate clinical use takes place in appropriate contexts with assessment and monitoring.

There is also the small matter of concurrent medication. The author's reported **venlafaxine 375 mg** is an example of information a clinician would need to assess. Serotonergic toxicity has been reported rarely in relevant combinations; a medication history is not a prediction that a particular complication will occur.

## 9. 🏛️ Two Historical Cases, Neither of Them a Business Case

This is where the room should become quiet for a moment. Real people died in the events below. They are not props for the couch-fort joke.

**Nebraska, August 2018.** Carey Dean Moore was executed by lethal injection using a four-drug protocol that included fentanyl citrate. This was an execution in a defined institutional setting, not evidence that fentanyl can be used to produce controllable group incapacitation. The protocol was controversial and the moral and legal debate over capital punishment does not disappear because one of the drugs also has legitimate medical uses.

**Moscow, October 2002.** Russian security forces used an incapacitating chemical agent during the Dubrovka theatre hostage crisis. A 2012 forensic study of samples from survivors found evidence of **carfentanil and remifentanil**, both fentanyl-related agents, while noting that their relative proportions could not be determined. The paper described **125 deaths** in connection with the agent and inadequate medical care. Other accounts give different casualty totals and causal descriptions; the essential lesson is not a falsely exact figure but the catastrophic gap between incapacitation and medical rescue.

| Case | Stated purpose | What the evidence supports | What it does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nebraska 2018 | Execution | Fentanyl appeared in a lethal-injection protocol | Safe or predictable environmental incapacitation |
| Moscow 2002 | End hostage siege | A fentanyl-related incapacitating agent was used; hostages died | A controllable, medically manageable general-purpose weapon |
| Karp's remark | Provocative revenge fantasy | He made the remark in an interview | A real drone, procurement contract, experiment or deployment |

The Moscow case is especially relevant to a recurring institutional problem: the people who decide to create an effect may not be the people equipped to manage its consequences.

## 10. ⚖️ Law Is Not an Optional Software Dependency

In the UK, fentanyl is controlled as a **Class A drug** under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 and falls within **Schedule 2** of the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001. Legitimate clinical use and appropriately authorised research exist within legal frameworks. Neither a prescription nor a research licence is a magic exemption from consent, safety or criminal law.

International humanitarian law, domestic criminal law, medical ethics and the **Chemical Weapons Convention** raise different questions. Whether a particular hypothetical act falls under a specific provision depends on purpose, facts and context. One cannot simply label a domestic revenge fantasy a battlefield operation and inherit an imaginary legal permission.

Nor does a drone confer a pharmaceutical manufacturing authorisation, a controlled-drug import licence or the right to dose unwilling people.

**THE DRONE IS NOT A CONTROLLED-DRUG IMPORT LICENCE.**

The legal and ethical question isn't whether a system is technologically feasible in some broad sense. It is whether anyone has a legitimate right to do the thing, and who is accountable if they do.

## 12. 🫂 Oxytocin and the Renaissance-Men Problem

Our parallel node, *Hugging, But Make It A Bomb*, considers another tempting leap: because oxytocin has documented physiological and context-dependent social effects, perhaps one can engineer affection, trust or compliance with it. No. Not from that premise alone.

Oxytocin is a peptide; fentanyl is a small synthetic opioid. They have different structures, receptors, pharmacology and clinical applications. The shared mistake is not chemical. It is epistemological.

```text
  MOLECULE HAS PROPERTIES
            |
            v
  EXPOSURE PRODUCES AN EFFECT?       [EVIDENCE REQUIRED]
            |
            v
  PERSON EXPERIENCES A GIVEN STATE?  [EVIDENCE REQUIRED]
            |
            v
  PERSON BEHAVES AS PREDICTED?       [EVIDENCE REQUIRED]
            |
            v
  OBJECTIVE IS ACHIEVED?             [EVIDENCE REQUIRED]
            |
            v
  ACT IS LAWFUL AND ACCOUNTABLE?     [SEPARATE QUESTION]
```

This is why *knowing the receptor* is not the same as *knowing the person*. It is also why consent cannot be derived from a structural formula.

## 13. 🫀 Leonardo's Cow

The Renaissance men have arrived. Lovely. Let's talk about Leonardo.

Around 1511, Leonardo da Vinci produced his extraordinary *Fetus in the Womb* drawing. It remains beautiful. It also includes anatomical features inferred from his earlier dissection of a **pregnant cow**. The Royal Collection Trust identifies the bovine placental features in the drawing and the limits of Leonardo's access to human material.

The animal was a cow, not a cat. That correction is part of the method: if we are going to complain about people failing to check their models against reality, we must be prepared to correct our own animal.

Leonardo was not an idiot and this is not a licence to laugh at early anatomists for failing to possess modern imaging. His achievement was enormous. **His evidence was incomplete, and an analogy crossed a species boundary.** The persuasive quality of the illustration did not make that inference correct.

### 🩺 Enter: Vesalius, Stage Left  

In 1543, Andreas Vesalius published *De humani corporis fabrica*, challenging anatomical knowledge inherited from Galen, whose descriptions of human bodies had relied substantially on dissections of animals, including monkeys and pigs. By examining actual human cadavers, Vesalius demonstrated that centuries of respected medical authority had preserved errors that direct observation could correct. Galen was an extraordinary physician; his conclusions were nevertheless constrained by the bodies he could examine.

🦊 **“GENTLEMEN, HAVE YOU CONSIDERED LOOKING AT THE ACTUAL PATIENT?”**

The lesson is not that earlier scientists were stupid. It is that **authority, however impressive, cannot substitute for checking whether your model describes the thing you claim it describes.**  


## 14. 🩺 Victorian Modesty, Observation and Women's Bodies

Now jump forward to nineteenth-century medicine. Some intimate examinations were conducted under strict conventions of modesty, with clothing or drapes obscuring the body and clinicians relying heavily on touch. Practices varied across time, place and practitioner; we should not pretend that every Victorian examination was identical or that privacy itself was the problem.

But conventions around what could be seen, said and examined could shape what was clinically known and how patients were treated. That matters particularly for pelvic and reproductive conditions, where embarrassment, power, gender and professional authority intersected.

The specific historical plate of a doctor examining beneath a skirt while avoiding direct visual observation remains **unidentified** in this node. We should locate it before naming an artist or claiming that it illustrates every case. A famous-looking image is not a source until we've found it.

The larger question is entirely contemporary: **what are we still doing awkwardly because we have not fully confronted the social conditions under which medical knowledge is produced and care is delivered?**

A person can have profound pelvic pain or prolapse and still encounter delays, discomfort, poor communication or inadequate access to assessment. The existence of medical knowledge does not guarantee that the patient receives competent, consensual, humane care.

And now the gentlemen can perhaps see why an allegedly universal technological revolution might be asked to look at something as unglamorous as gynaecological access and the logistics of healthcare appointments.

## 15. 🇬🇧 Brexit, COVID and the Cost of Broken Scientific Connections

I am not arguing that the American scientific tradition is bad. Quite the reverse: there are extraordinary scientists, institutions and collaborations on both sides of the Atlantic. The complaint is against the idea that technological ambition exempts its proponents from engaging with the other disciplines and institutions that make science useful.

Britain learned something painful about this after Brexit. Scientific collaboration did not cease, but uncertainty about programme participation, funding and institutional relationships mattered. The UK rejoined **Horizon Europe as an associated country from 1 January 2024**. That restoration was valuable; it did not reverse time or automatically recreate every disrupted relationship. Nor is Horizon Europe association the same thing as membership of the EU's medicines regulatory system.

COVID provided an even larger lesson. Genomic surveillance, vaccine research, clinical evidence, manufacturing and public-health decisions depended on cross-border cooperation. The scientific advances were extraordinary. Access to their benefits was unequal. A discovery can be successful while the systems distributing it fail people.

The relevant question is not whether all national regulators should disappear. They have responsibilities to their populations. It is whether **avoidable differences, duplication and delays** are causing preventable illness, interrupted treatment or deaths.

## 16. 🌍 Open Source Is Not the Same as Open Pharmaceutical Licensing

Silicon Valley is fond of discussing open-source software. Excellent. Can we use that enthusiasm to ask a more difficult question about medicines?

A drug's molecular formula is not a finished medicine. Neither is a patent, a published paper, a manufacturing licence, a marketing authorisation or a clinical guideline. These things interact, but they are not interchangeable.

| Layer | What cooperation or openness could improve | What must remain protected |
|---|---|---|
| Publications and chemical data | Reproducibility and independent scrutiny | Research integrity |
| Clinical evidence | Better comparative assessment | Patient privacy, trial ethics |
| Patents and licensing | Access for qualified manufacturers | Fair incentives and lawful rights |
| Manufacturing know-how | Reliable technology transfer | Quality, traceability, competent production |
| Regulatory submissions | Less duplication between authorities | Independent accountable judgement |
| Procurement | More reliable, equitable supply | Anti-corruption and quality controls |
| Pharmacovigilance | Earlier recognition of safety signals | Data provenance and patient confidentiality |

We already have partial tools: the **World Health Organization's prequalification programme**, international technical harmonisation, regulatory reliance and recognition, voluntary licences, patent pools and lawful compulsory-licensing mechanisms in relevant circumstances. They do different jobs. None makes clinical safety optional.

The **Medicines Patent Pool** is an example of negotiated licensing and access mechanisms, not a universal switch labelled OPEN SOURCE ALL MEDICINES. WHO prequalification assesses specified health products against quality, safety and efficacy standards; it is not a replacement for every national regulatory decision.

AI may be useful for comparing requirements, identifying duplicated evidence review, supporting pharmacovigilance and helping people navigate complex regulatory records. It may also introduce errors, biases or new accountability problems. **The model does not become the regulator merely because it can summarise the paperwork.**

Our aspiration should be fewer avoidable morbidity and mortality outcomes arising from regulatory fragmentation, without weakening the standards that protect patients. That is a design goal, not a promise that no person will ever die because two jurisdictions differ.
