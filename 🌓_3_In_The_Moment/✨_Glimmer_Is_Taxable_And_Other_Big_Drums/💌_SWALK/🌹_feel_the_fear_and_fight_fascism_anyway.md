# 🌹 Feel the fear, and fight fascism anyway
**First created:** 2025-10-23 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-11  
*A circular for frightened persons who remain inconveniently committed to behaving like human beings.*

---

## 📮 Circular to all frightened persons

**To all citizens awaiting the disappearance of fear before reporting for democratic duty:**

The Office regrets to announce that courage has not arrived in the expected condition.

The available specimens are damp, underslept, intermittently furious, and privately hoping somebody else has brought the map. Several require medication. One has lost a shoe. Another has been crying in the lavatory but remains, on every material question, correct.

This is normal.

Courage is not the absence of fear. It is the frequently unglamorous business of keeping hold of one’s ethical direction while fear, coercion, uncertainty, ridicule or danger attempt to seize the compass.

The Office is not presently recruiting martyrs. They are difficult to roster, alarming at meetings, and notoriously unavailable for the following Tuesday. We require living people: people who can tell the truth carefully, preserve relationships, check the evidence, bring the charger, make the tea, document what happened, and remain capable of recognising another human being as human.

You may report frightened.

Indeed, most of the useful people already have.

---

## 🚨 Fear has reported something

Fear is not automatically evidence of fragility. Sometimes it is the nervous system noticing instability, hostility or danger before the committee has agreed upon the minutes.

People who register a change early are often called oversensitive, dramatic or alarmist. Occasionally they are mistaken. Occasionally they have noticed the smoke while everyone else is still debating whether the smell is constitutionally significant.

Either way, smashing the alarm with a shoe is not a diagnostic method.

Fear may be responding to:

- a direct threat;
- selective or inconsistent punishment;
- public hostility or intimidation;
- being watched, monitored, scrutinised or misrepresented;
- uncertainty about how much attention one is receiving;
- previous trauma being activated by a present resemblance;
- exhaustion, isolation or several different risks becoming fused together;
- information that has not yet been checked.

To feel fear is not to fail. It may mean that the nervous system has refused to go numb.

It does not, however, mean that the nervous system has completed the inquiry.

---

## 🔍 The alarm is not the incident report

Fear is a smoke alarm, not the completed Fire Brigade report. One should neither silence it out of embarrassment nor permit it to identify the arsonist unaided.

This distinction matters because authoritarian pressure thrives at both extremes. One person is told that fear proves nothing and must therefore be ignored. Another is encouraged to treat fear as proof of every suspected cause. Both routes leave the frightened person easier to isolate, discredit or steer.

The approved procedure is less exciting:

1. **Notice the alarm.** What, precisely, changed?
2. **Stabilise the operator.** Food, medication, sleep, company and physical safety are not evasions of the question.
3. **Separate observation from interpretation.** What was directly seen, heard, received or recorded? What is inferred?
4. **Check for ordinary explanations without insisting upon them.** Technical fault, misunderstanding and coincidence are possibilities, not compulsory verdicts.
5. **Compare carefully.** Ask trusted people, preserve relevant material, and seek appropriate technical, legal or professional help where warranted.
6. **Choose a proportionate action.** The purpose is safety and clarity, not winning the Most Alarmed Citizen competition.

Respecting embodied knowledge does not require abandoning evidential discipline. Evidential discipline does not require treating the body as an unreliable junior clerk who must be kept away from the files.

Both may report to the same desk.

## 🦁 Courage stores: what has actually been issued

Courage is generally advertised in a cape and delivered in a cardigan.

It may consist of:

- saying one accurate thing when silence would be easier;
- asking a second person to look at the same evidence;
- accompanying somebody who should not have to attend alone;
- refusing to repeat a dehumanising claim;
- checking on the neighbour, colleague or comrade who has gone quiet;
- preserving a record without publishing it immediately;
- correcting an error before it becomes coalition folklore;
- accepting a lower-risk role and performing it reliably;
- making the meeting accessible enough that frightened and disabled people can actually remain in it;
- going home before exhaustion turns judgment into soup.

The ethical field is seldom cinematic. It is generally staffed by tired persons with unsuitable shoes, one functioning charger between them, and a firm conviction that somebody ought to check on Mrs Rosenberg.

That counts.

## 🐍 How frightened populations are separated

Authoritarian systems do not need every person to be fearless, loyal or convinced. It is often sufficient to make each person calculate risk alone.

The method is admirably economical:

- frighten ten people;
- punish one conspicuously;
- provide no reliable rule explaining why;
- ridicule anybody who notices the pattern too early;
- reward everybody else for calling the matter complicated;
- allow the remaining nine to operate their own unpaid compliance departments.

Ambiguity does much of the work. Inconsistent enforcement encourages self-censorship because nobody knows where the line is. Public mockery makes early warning socially expensive. Impossible demands for certainty postpone collective recognition. Isolation ensures that each frightened person assumes everybody else is coping splendidly.

Thus fear is produced socially and experienced privately. The citizen is then invited to regard the resulting distress as a personal defect.

It is a splendid arrangement for the system. The system causes the weather and invoices the umbrella.

Naming fear together interrupts that arrangement—but collective recognition is not collective certainty. A group can compare observations, acknowledge ambiguity and still agree upon sensible precautions. Democracy does not require everyone to produce identical theories before anyone is permitted to bring sandwiches.

## 🌊 Borrowing somebody else’s courage

Courage is rarely a private mineral deposit. Most people borrow it.

They borrow it from the person who answers the phone. From the friend who says, “Yes, that was strange; no, we do not yet know why.” From legal observers, translators, trade union representatives, faith communities, clinicians, archivists, aunties, group chats, songs, rituals and the one calm person who has remembered where the forms are kept.

Mutual reassurance is not cowardice. It is the civilian maintenance department preventing six frightened people from separately concluding that they have imagined the same fire.

Nor does solidarity require everybody to feel equally brave at the same time. One person speaks while another takes notes. One drives. One cooks. One checks the claim. One sits beside the person whose hands are shaking. Tomorrow the arrangement may reverse.

Humour matters here. So do music, friendship, cultural memory and ritual. Human beings metabolise fear socially, not mechanically. A joke does not make the danger unreal; sometimes it merely stops the danger becoming the only reality left in the room.

## 🧰 Approved forms of useful bravery

The Office has reviewed the standing assumption that courage means walking personally into every available machine-gun emplacement and found it wasteful.

The realm also requires archivists, drivers, cooks, witnesses, solicitors, stewards, researchers, carers, organisers, translators, first-aiders, technical chaps, emergency aunties and the person who remembers the password.

Useful bravery may therefore include:

- **reducing exposure:** choosing a safer channel, time, route or role;
- **asking for accompaniment:** fear shared with a trusted witness is often easier to assess and harder to exploit;
- **verifying before amplifying:** urgency is not a waiver from accuracy;
- **documenting before declaring:** some evidence improves when allowed to remain evidence for ten minutes;
- **handing over:** the safest or best-placed person need not be the first person who noticed;
- **setting a boundary:** declining an unnecessary risk is not collaboration with fascism;
- **doing one useful thing:** no citizen is required to become the entire resistance before tea.

Proportion matters. So does consent. So does the possibility that the person under pressure may know which forms of help will make matters worse.

One should not seize the steering wheel from a frightened survivor and call it solidarity.

---

## 🫀 Rest, retreat and continuation

“Continue anyway” does not mean “override the body until it files for independence.”

Rest is part of continuation. So are recovery, privacy, medicine, joy, sleep, refusal and periods in which somebody else carries the banner because your shoulder has gone peculiar.

Tactical retreat remains permitted. Even the British Army has occasionally moved backwards while insisting it was reorganising the geography.

Leaving an unsafe situation may be courageous. Delaying publication may be responsible. Declining to debate a person committed to humiliating you may preserve energy for work that matters. Turning off the telephone is not always surrender; sometimes it is the communications officer taking a constitutionally mandated bath.

The question is not whether every action looks brave from outside. The question is whether it helps preserve life, truth, dignity, relationship and the capacity to act again.

Courage should make a future more possible. If the only plan consumes every person asked to carry it, Headquarters has confused resistance with kindling.

---

## 📯 Final instruction from the Office

You are not required to feel fearless.

You are not required to be calm enough to reassure the people frightening you. You are not required to turn pain into perfect rhetoric, uncertainty into silence, or survival into a public performance of limitless stamina.

You are asked, where possible and without sacrificing yourself for theatrical effect, to keep hold of the human thread:

check what you know;
say what you can;
protect what must be protected;
ask for company;
make room for somebody else’s fear;
rest before collapse;
return when able.

History is not moved only by the fearless. It is moved by frightened people who continue to recognise one another, pass the map between them, and refuse to let terror perform all the introductions.

Report frightened. Report tired. Report with biscuits.

But do report to one another.
