*A field circular for persons awaiting a perfectly homogeneous anti-fascist coalition before joining it.*

## 📯 Circular to the Temporarily Undecided

**To:** all persons still inspecting one another’s ideological paperwork while authoritarianism unloads outside  
**From:** the Office for Beaches, Barricades and Emergency Sandwiches  
**Subject:** whether we might cooperate before the building catches fire  
**Classification:** terribly British, but not exclusively so

The Office regrets to announce that survival may depend upon people who do not move, think, pray, organise, vote, speak or make tea exactly as you do.

It may even depend upon people you do not especially like.

This is inconvenient. A committee has been formed.

History, having no respect for a well-curated social circle, repeatedly places incompatible people in the same emergency and asks whether they can keep one another alive long enough to have the argument later. Durable resistance to organised harm rarely emerges from purity, perfect agreement or a matching set of lapel badges. It emerges from imperfect cooperation, specialist labour, local knowledge, mutual reliance and somebody remembering the spare batteries.

This node concerns solidarity as civic endurance: not romantic militancy, not compulsory friendship, and not an instruction to ignore serious harm within a coalition. It is an instruction to distinguish between **a disagreement requiring procedure** and **a fascist movement requiring opposition**.

One need not agree on the precise future administration of the biscuit tin before preventing fascists from marching past the house.

## 🗺️ Three Historical Dispatches

### Dunkirk: Headquarters discovers that civilians own boats

The popular memory of Dunkirk often arrives as a spontaneous flotilla of plucky civilians sailing across the Channel on instinct, sandwiches and national character.

The reality was more organised and therefore more useful.

Operation Dynamo was a military evacuation. Naval planning, larger vessels and command structures were essential; many of the “little ships” were crewed by naval personnel, while civilian owners and crews also took part. Small craft could reach shallow water and ferry troops to larger ships offshore. The famous civilian contribution did not replace state capacity. It joined it at the precise point where the existing machinery needed more shapes and sizes of boat.

The Navy had the command structure. Civilians had vessels. France had unfortunately developed a rather immediate requirement for both.

The lesson is not that institutions are unnecessary. It is that, during an emergency, institutions and communities may have to stop being precious about who is permitted to count as infrastructure.

### D-Day: courage acquires a timetable

D-Day is often compressed into the visible landing: men, beaches, landing craft, gunfire. This is rather like describing an orchestra as “several chaps having a difficult evening with brass.”

The operation depended upon multinational command, intelligence, deception, resistance networks, meteorology, engineering, manufacturing, translation, medicine, transport, signals, logistics and a quantity of paperwork capable of achieving independent statehood. Different countries, services, professions and resistance groups did not become identical. They became coordinated.

Some people crossed beaches. Others forecast the weather, disrupted railways, decoded messages, repaired engines, treated casualties, moved supplies or ensured that the correct object reached the correct coast at approximately the correct hour. None of this is less real because it photographs badly.

The lesson is that courage without coordination can become a very moving way to lose. Coalitions need more than admirable feeling. They need routes, roles, contingencies and somebody authorised to ask why the fuel has gone to Kent.

### Cable Street: the neighbourhood declines the parade

In October 1936, Oswald Mosley’s British Union of Fascists planned to march through the East End of London. Jewish residents, trade unionists, socialists, communists, anarchists and other local allies—including many Irish working-class residents—did not first merge into one serene political consciousness. They shared an immediate conclusion: the fascists should not be permitted to use the neighbourhood as a theatre of intimidation.

The coalition was broad because the threat was legible across difference. People brought their own histories, organisations, arguments, languages, methods and reasons for being there. Cooperation did not erase those distinctions. It made them temporarily interoperable.

This is a useful word: **interoperable**. One does not require every fire engine to have the same opinions. One requires the hoses to connect while the street is burning.

Cable Street does not provide a universal template for every present-day confrontation. It does provide a durable civic memory: communities need not wait for fascism to become polite, administratively complete or universally inconvenient before refusing it space.

## 🦊 Solidarity Is Often Grim, Actually

I hate to be the bearer of bad news.

(Lies; I do rather enjoy ruining a day.)

The lives of “the left” are rarely a Bohemian dream or a romantic tryst with poets. Most solidarity work looks significantly less cinematic than advertised.

Often it looks like:

- sourcing food without funding the harm under discussion;
- checking in again, after the dramatic bit has ended;
- helping somebody navigate bureaucracy;
- sharing transport;
- translating systems, notices or very cross emails;
- proofreading forms;
- arranging childcare or disability access;
- carrying boxes;
- finding a competent solicitor;
- or spending forty minutes locating the one shop that stocks the correct dates without funding an occupation.

This is the ordinary kit of solidarity: repetitive, logistical, occasionally irritating and deeply unglamorous.

*We do not especially need more people interested only in their own performance of goodness.*

If Glimmer is taxable, solidarity is almost certainly under audit. The bureaucracy of virtue prefers forms over function:

> “Submit evidence of inter-sector cooperation in triplicate.  
> Attach receipts for kindness.  
> Declare mutual aid exceeding the annual threshold.”

But genuine coordination—the Dunkirk kind, the D-Day kind, the Cable Street kind—rarely fits neatly into administrative categories. It is multilingual, imperfect, improvised and held together largely by exhausted people continuing to report for duty.

Usually it smells faintly of petrol, bread, damp paperwork and stress.

On better days, tea.

## 🌳 Embodied Interdependence

Human relationships are not regrettable lapses in personal self-sufficiency. No citizen has yet been issued with an individual railway, weather service, immune system and emergency auntie.

Communities exchange information, resources, warnings, skills and care continuously. Like root networks or microbial ecologies, they survive through difference, redundancy and mutual adjustment—not because every component performs the same task.

In civic life, this can resemble an immune response: harmful conduct is detected, warnings travel, resources gather and damage is contained.

**Important memorandum from Historical Stores:** the harmful object is coercive conduct, organised cruelty and the machinery that sustains it—not a category of human beings. Authoritarian movements have repeatedly described unwanted populations as disease. The Office has checked this distinction twice because history contains several very bad chaps who did not.

Interdependence therefore requires more than togetherness. It requires boundaries, repair and enough trust to exchange warnings without turning every error into a public execution.

That means remaining in one another’s presence over time. It means continuing after the mood, march or media cycle has passed. It also means accepting that coalition work includes persons who are wrong about several things, badly organised, and in possession of an objectionable opinion about tea.

Headquarters confirms that they may nevertheless be useful against fascism.

This is not an instruction to tolerate abuse, racism, antisemitism, Islamophobia, misogyny, ableism or dehumanisation for the sake of a photogenic alliance. A coalition incapable of protecting its own people is not displaying admirable unity. It has merely moved the danger indoors.

The work is to build procedures strong enough to distinguish sabotage from disagreement, correction from humiliation, and necessary boundaries from the thrill of throwing somebody out.

Interdependence is not weakness. It is how societies remain survivable.

## 🕯️ The World to Come Requires Facilities

Judaism speaks of *Olam HaBa*: the world to come. One labours toward a future one may never personally enter.

Related long-horizon ethics appear across many traditions. Each generation inherits structures it did not build, alters some of them, and passes the remainder onward with a note apologising for the wiring.

The world to come is not constructed solely by decorated officers or charismatic speakers. Somebody must translate the notice, bring the sandwiches, remember who cannot use the stairs, check the source, sit with the frightened person, update the rota and determine why the designated safe room has been locked by Facilities.

Roles change: specialist or volunteer, paid or unpaid, visible or invisible, front-line or administrative. Prestige is a poor measure of whether harm was reduced or dignity preserved.

The point is to leave behind structures slightly kinder and more defensible than the ones inherited—not because medals are waiting, but because civilisation is cumulative.

Some forms of meaning must be built slowly enough that nobody gets credit for them.

This is frustrating for the publicity department. The work continues.

## 🧰 Coalition Stores: Minimum Issue

In Britain’s continuing reckoning with racism, radicalisation and democratic strain, broad civic coalitions will be required: artists, lawyers, clergy, teachers, coders, organisers, carers, journalists, librarians, trade unionists, neighbours and the aunties running WhatsApp fact-check patrols.

“Broad coalition” must mean more than assembling these persons in one photograph. Before deployment, the Office recommends issuing:

### A shared factual baseline

Agree what is known, what is alleged, what remains uncertain and which sources can update the picture. A coalition unable to revise a claim will eventually be commanded by its most exciting rumour.

### Accessible communications

Provide readable notices, translation, disability access, multiple contact routes and sensible information security. Do not build the democratic resistance entirely inside an app that half the participants cannot use and the other half have muted.

### Material support

Arrange food, transport, childcare, mobility access, emergency funds and places to rest. Morale is affected by ideals; attendance is affected by the last bus.

### Legal and professional support

Know who can observe, document, advise, refer and respond when something goes wrong. “Surely somebody handles that” is not a safeguarding protocol. It is the opening sentence of an inquiry.

### Protection across difference

Set explicit boundaries against antisemitic, Islamophobic, racist, misogynistic, ableist and other dehumanising conduct. Do not ask targeted people to absorb harm quietly in order to preserve the coalition’s excellent vibes.

### A disagreement procedure

Establish how errors are corrected, complaints heard, decisions reviewed and people restored where repair is possible. Not every incorrect sentence requires exile; not every pattern of harm can be solved by a very warm meeting.

### A conduct boundary

State what the coalition will and will not do. Democratic defence must remain proportionate, lawful, evidence-led and directed against harmful conduct and coercive systems—not human categories.

### A persistence rota

Plan for the week after the rally, the month after the scandal and the morning everybody wakes up tired. Authoritarian projects rely upon exhaustion. The aunties have therefore requested a spreadsheet.

## 🧮 Standing Orders for Imperfect Allies

1. **Cooperate without pretending to be identical.** Uniformity is not solidarity. It is usually branding.
2. **Assign jobs according to capacity.** The loudest person need not hold the map.
3. **Correct without manufacturing a spectacle.** Public humiliation is an unreliable educational technology.
4. **Protect the people carrying disproportionate risk.** They are not decorative evidence of coalition breadth.
5. **Keep boundaries around dehumanisation and abuse.** “Broad” does not mean structurally defenceless.
6. **Permit evidence of improvement.** A person or institution must be allowed to behave better without receiving an unearned clean bill of health.
7. **Continue after the photograph.** Fascism has regrettably declined to operate only during office hours.

At minimum, a democratic coalition must remain capable of agreeing that systems organised around cruelty, dehumanisation or permanent exploitation should not pass uncontested into normality.

Nobody has to become best friends. Everybody does have to stop polishing their individual moral bayonet long enough to notice whether the bridge is still standing.
