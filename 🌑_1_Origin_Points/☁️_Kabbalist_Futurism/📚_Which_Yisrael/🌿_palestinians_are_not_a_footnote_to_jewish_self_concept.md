# 🌿 Palestinians Are Not A Footnote To Jewish Self-Concept  
**First created:** 2026-08-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-18  
*Why a Jewish-forward conversation about Yisrael, Zionism, identity, memory, and safety must keep returning to the people who bear the material consequences of those political projects.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

This cluster is deliberately Jewish-forward.

It is trying to solve a Jewish language problem.

It asks:

- what do Jews mean when we say *Yisrael*?
- why do Jews mean different things by Zionism?
- why can two Jews hear the same political sentence differently?
- how can Medinat Yisrael become incorporated into self-concept?
- what forms of Yisrael exist outside or beyond identification with a modern state?
- how do Jewish histories of persecution, diaspora, refuge, sovereignty, and communal survival shape political interpretation?

Those are real questions.

They deserve serious treatment.

But they create an obvious danger.

We can become so interested in:

> **what Israel means to Jews**

that Palestinians become:

> **the context in which Jews are having an interesting argument about ourselves.**

No.

Palestinians are not context for Jewish identity formation.

They are not an edge case.

They are not a final ethical test added after the Jewish philosophy has been completed.

They are people whose lives, land, homes, families, political rights, movement, safety, displacement, imprisonment, loss, and futures have been materially affected by political projects built around particular answers to Jewish questions about Yisrael.

That fact has to remain visible throughout the cluster.

---

## 🌿 Palestinians Are Not Supporting Characters

There is a recurrent structure in Jewish discussion about Israel:

**Jewish history**  
↓  
**Jewish trauma**  
↓  
**Jewish safety**  
↓  
**Jewish self-determination**  
↓  
**Jewish statehood**  
↓  
**and then, eventually, Palestinians**

That ordering is understandable if the conversation begins inside Jewish self-concept.

It is also dangerous.

Because Palestinians do not enter the story after Jewish identity has already been defined.

They were already there.

Their history does not begin at the moment Jewish political thought notices them.

Their relationship with the land does not become real only when it becomes relevant to a Jewish argument about return.

Their political rights do not arise as a limitation upon Jewish aspiration.

They possess their own history.

Their own attachments.

Their own collective memory.

Their own political claims.

Their own interior worlds.

The analytical order therefore has to change.

Not:

> **What does Yisrael mean to Jews, and what problems does that create with Palestinians?**

But:

> **What does this Jewish political meaning become when it is made materially real in a place where Palestinians already live?**

That is a different question.

---

## 🏠 A Philosophical Homeland Meets A Material Home

Jewish relationships with Eretz Yisrael can be:

- theological;
- historical;
- ancestral;
- liturgical;
- cultural;
- emotional;
- philosophical;
- familial;
- linguistic;
- spiritual.

Those relationships can be real.

They can be profound.

They can survive centuries of absence, migration, persecution, longing, prayer, and textual memory.

But the political problem begins when:

> **this place is sacred to me**

becomes:

> **therefore this place should be politically organised around my collective sovereignty.**

At that point, another set of questions becomes unavoidable.

Who lives there?

Who owns land?

Who has legal title?

Who has citizenship?

Who can return?

Who cannot return?

Who controls the army?

Who controls movement?

Who controls planning?

Who controls borders?

Who controls residency?

Who can build?

Who can stay?

Who can be removed?

Who has equal political power?

A philosophical homeland becomes a material political structure.

And material political structures act on bodies.

→ See: [🏠 When A Philosophical Homeland Is Someone Else's Home](./🏠_when_a_philosophical_homeland_is_someone_elses_home.md)

## 🧠 Jewish Self-Concept Cannot Be The Final Unit Of Analysis

The concept of **Atzmi Yisrael** is useful because it helps explain why criticism of Medinat Yisrael may become psychologically self-threatening for some Jews.

But the concept becomes dangerous if it turns into:

> **therefore Palestinian political claims must be filtered through Jewish psychological comfort.**

That cannot work.

Suppose a Jewish person experiences:

> **Medinat Yisrael is necessary for my sense of Jewish safety.**

That is psychologically meaningful.

But it does not settle:

- Palestinian freedom;
- Palestinian equality;
- Palestinian sovereignty;
- Palestinian return;
- Palestinian property;
- Palestinian movement;
- Palestinian citizenship;
- Palestinian safety.

Otherwise the structure becomes:

**Jewish historical vulnerability**  
↓  
**Jewish psychological need**  
↓  
**state structure required to preserve that need**  
↓  
**Palestinian rights become conditional upon Jewish self-stability**

That is not a defensible endpoint.

## 🌉 Identity Explains Stakes

Identity can tell us:

> **why this matters so much to someone.**

It can tell us why a proposition feels:

- frightening;
- destabilising;
- sacred;
- insulting;
- threatening;
- morally urgent.

It cannot automatically tell us:

> **what everyone else is required to surrender.**

That distinction is central.

A Jewish person's identity can explain why Jewish sovereignty matters to them.

It cannot by itself determine what political system Palestinians must live under.

A Palestinian person's identity can explain why return or land matters to them.

It does not by itself answer every political question concerning Israeli Jews either.

Identity explains stakes.

Rights, power, history, law, and political arrangements still have to be analysed.

---

## 🕯️ Palestinian Life Is Not A Jewish Moral Examination

There is another version of the problem.

Critical or anti-Zionist Jews can also turn Palestinians into objects inside Jewish self-concept.

The structure becomes:

> **I oppose Palestinian suffering because that demonstrates the ethical integrity of my Judaism.**

That may contain sincere Jewish ethics.

But if we are not careful, Palestinian people still end up doing the same job:

> **helping Jews decide what sort of Jews we are.**

That is not enough.

Palestinian rights do not depend upon:

- whether Torah supports them;
- whether Jewish ethics supports them;
- whether Jewish history makes Jews empathetic;
- whether anti-Zionist Jews need them as evidence of moral differentiation;
- whether Zionist Jews can reconcile them with Jewish statehood.

Palestinians do not need to pass through Jewish moral philosophy in order to become people.

Their personhood is prior to our interpretation of it.

---

## 🧿 “Look How Jewish My Solidarity Is” Is Still About Us

There can be a subtle narcissism even in apparently ethical Jewish political speech.

A Jew says:

> **My Jewishness teaches me to stand with Palestinians.**

That can be beautiful.

It can also quietly centre:

> **my Jewishness.**

The sentence to keep available is:

> **Palestinian liberation does not become legitimate because it resembles my preferred interpretation of Judaism.**

Jewish traditions can motivate solidarity.

They can deepen it.

They can make it feel continuous with Jewish ethical life.

Good.

But the test is not:

> **does solidarity make me a better Jew?**

The test is:

> **what is happening to Palestinians?**

---

## 🌿 Palestinian Testimony Is Not Just Evidence In A Jewish Debate

Another flattening happens when Palestinian testimony is consumed mainly as:

> **information capable of changing Jewish minds.**

Palestinian testimony may indeed change Jewish minds.

It has.

It can destabilise inherited narratives.

It can force confrontation with omitted history.

It can produce moral and political transformation.

But Palestinian people are not primarily witnesses in an internal Jewish trial.

Their testimony is also:

- family memory;
- political history;
- documentation;
- mourning;
- claims to justice;
- accounts of state power;
- accounts of displacement;
- accounts of survival;
- accounts of ordinary life.

Jewish political development is one possible consequence of encountering that testimony.

It is not the purpose of the testimony.

---

## 📖 The Long Way To No Still Cannot Become The Main Story

This cluster includes a node about Jewish people who reach anti-Zionism after a long process of study, grief, communal conflict, and reconstruction.

That matters.

It helps explain why some Jewish anti-Zionist positions become very firm.

But there is an asymmetry.

A Jewish person's political awakening may be:

> **painful.**

A Palestinian person's family may have been:

> **displaced.**

Those are not the same kind of harm.

A Jew may experience:

- loss of communal belonging;
- grief over an inherited narrative;
- family conflict;
- identity reconstruction.

Those experiences can be serious.

But they must not become so narratively compelling that the Palestinian material history producing the political confrontation disappears behind the Jewish psychological response to learning about it.

→ See: [📖 The Long Way To No](./📖_the_long_way_to_no.md)

## 🪞 Jewish Grief And Palestinian Loss Are Not Interchangeable

This needs particular care.

A Jewish person may grieve:

- the collapse of an idealised Israel;
- the loss of trust in communal institutions;
- a childhood narrative;
- a belief in Israel as a uniquely moral project;
- a political identity.

A Palestinian may grieve:

- a person;
- a home;
- a village;
- land;
- family separation;
- displacement;
- imprisonment;
- exile.

Both are grief.

They are not therefore equivalent objects.

Psychological pain should not flatten material asymmetry.

---

## 🏛️ The State Is Not Merely A Symbol

The cluster repeatedly distinguishes **Medinat Yisrael** from other forms of Yisrael for a reason.

Medinat Yisrael is not merely:

- a symbol of survival;
- a Jewish aspiration;
- a spiritual projection;
- an internal object;
- a philosophical answer.

It is a state.

States exercise power.

They:

- legislate;
- police;
- imprison;
- tax;
- regulate land;
- control borders;
- maintain militaries;
- define citizenship;
- allocate resources;
- create legal categories;
- recognise or refuse rights.

So when Palestinians encounter Medinat Yisrael, they are not necessarily encountering:

> **the Jewish idea of Israel.**

They may be encountering:

> **a checkpoint.**

> **a court.**

> **a soldier.**

> **a permit system.**

> **a planning authority.**

> **a prison.**

> **a border.**

> **a settlement.**

> **a government.**

The psychological meaning of the state to Jews cannot replace the material form of the state for Palestinians.

---

## 🧠 Different Referents, Different Stakes

This is one reason the same sentence can land so differently.

Consider:

> **Israel protects Jews.**

A diaspora Jew may hear:

> **There is somewhere Jews can go if other societies become unsafe.**

A Palestinian may hear:

> **The state exercising power over my life is justified as somebody else's protection.**

Those are not competing emotional reactions to one neutral object.

They reflect different relationships to power.

Likewise:

> **Jewish self-determination**

may mean:

**collective emancipation**

to one person.

To another:

**the political structure through which my own self-determination was constrained.**

That is not merely a vocabulary error.

Sometimes two populations understand one another perfectly and still have conflicting claims.

Shared language does not eliminate politics.

---

## ⚡ 1967 Makes The Asymmetry Especially Visible

1967 is a particularly sharp example.

For many Jews, especially in communities carrying vivid memories of persecution and the Shoah, the Six-Day War could become:

> **proof that Jews were no longer helpless.**

It could mean:

- survival;
- military competence;
- agency;
- protection;
- relief;
- pride.

For Palestinians, the same war is inseparable from:

- military occupation;
- territorial control;
- displacement;
- life under Israeli power.

One event.

Two historical relationships.

The Jewish psychological meaning is real.

The Palestinian material meaning is real.

Neither should erase the other.

But the presence of state power means they are not symmetrical.

One population's experience of:

> **we can finally protect ourselves**

may be experienced by another through:

> **the power being used over us.**

## 🌉 Jerusalem Shows The Same Problem

Jerusalem can be:

- prayer;
- longing;
- memory;
- orientation;
- sacred history;
- future restoration;
- an interior spiritual geography.

Jerusalem is also:

- neighbourhoods;
- homes;
- municipal policy;
- policing;
- land;
- residency;
- planning;
- political sovereignty;
- Palestinian life;
- Israeli life.

A Jewish discussion can say:

> **Jerusalem has lived inside us for centuries.**

That may be true.

A Palestinian resident can say:

> **I live here.**

That is not a less sophisticated claim.

The metaphysical density of a place does not reduce the rights of the people physically inhabiting it.

→ See: [🌉 Philosophical Jerusalem And Actual Jerusalem](./🌉_philosophical_jerusalem_and_actual_jerusalem.md)

## 🏠 Homeland And Home

There is a useful distinction here.

**Homeland** can stretch across:

- history;
- ancestry;
- religion;
- memory;
- collective imagination.

**Home** can be:

- the kitchen;
- the bedroom;
- the olive tree;
- the street;
- the neighbour;
- the key;
- the property boundary;
- the place somebody expects to return to at night.

These categories can overlap.

They can also conflict.

When:

> **my homeland**

meets:

> **your home**

the answer cannot be supplied by whichever claim is more emotionally or spiritually elaborate.

Political systems have to deal with both people.

---

## 🕸️ Settler Colonialism Is Not Merely A Word Fight

This is one place where the cluster needs discipline.

There is substantial argument around applying **settler-colonial** analysis to Zionism and Israel.

That argument matters.

Terminology affects comparison.

Historical models have limits.

But there is a danger of making the terminology itself the entire political discussion.

The more useful question is:

> **What material structures is the framework trying to describe?**

For example:

- settlement;
- land acquisition and control;
- demographic transformation;
- sovereignty;
- displacement;
- replacement or restructuring of political authority;
- differentiated rights;
- control of territory.

Those structures need to be examined whether one ultimately accepts, modifies, or rejects the analytical label.

Otherwise:

> **Is this settler colonialism?**

becomes another semantic trench in which everyone can hide from:

> **What happened to the people already there?**

---

## 🌿 Palestinian Personhood Is Not Contingent Upon The Correct Historical Label

Likewise:

Palestinian rights do not depend upon every scholar agreeing whether the relevant history is best described as:

- settler colonialism;
- nationalism;
- ethnic conflict;
- colonialism;
- occupation;
- partition;
- dispossession;
- some combination.

Those categories can matter enormously for analysis.

But a person's:

- home;
- body;
- liberty;
- family;
- political rights

do not vanish while historians argue about the taxonomy.

---

## ⚖️ Antisemitism Cannot Be Solved By Silencing Palestinians

This is another boundary.

Palestinian political speech can contain antisemitism.

That must be named when it happens.

Palestinian suffering does not make antisemitic conspiracy, collective blame, attacks on Jews, or other antisemitic structures harmless.

But preventing antisemitism cannot mean:

> **Palestinians may criticise Medinat Yisrael only in ways that preserve every Jewish listener's existing self-concept.**

That would make Palestinian political speech structurally impossible.

The standard has to distinguish:

**hostility towards Jews as Jews**

from:

**hostility towards, criticism of, resistance to, or rejection of a state or political ideology.**

That distinction can be difficult.

It remains necessary.

→ See: [⚖️ IHRA, JDA And Which Yisrael?](./⚖️_ihra_jda_and_which_yisrael.md)

## 🧿 Jewish Fear Still Matters

None of this requires treating Jewish fear as fake.

Antisemitism is real.

Jewish historical vulnerability is real.

Jewish fears about mass violence, exile, expulsion, and failed protection are not inventions.

Jewish people can reasonably worry about:

- violence against Jews;
- attacks upon synagogues;
- dual-loyalty accusations;
- conspiracy theories;
- coded antisemitism;
- political movements that blur Israel and Jews;
- threats to Israeli civilians;
- threats to family.

The answer is not:

> **stop having Jewish fear because Palestinians suffer more.**

That is not how human beings work.

The task is:

> **do not make one population's fear the mechanism through which another population's rights disappear.**

---

## 🪞 Trauma Explains. It Does Not Allocate Rights.

This cluster keeps returning to a simple principle:

> **psychology explains behaviour; it does not automatically justify political outcomes.**

Historical trauma can explain:

- attachment to refuge;
- attachment to sovereignty;
- defensive cognition;
- fear of statelessness;
- fear of political dependence.

It does not by itself allocate:

- territory;
- citizenship;
- property;
- borders;
- military authority;
- political supremacy.

Trauma is morally relevant.

It is not a land registry.

## 🥚 Palestinian Reality Is Not The Same Problem As Jewish Diversity

This distinction matters inside the cluster architecture.

The node on Ashkenormativity asks:

> **Which Jews have been omitted from our model of Jewish life?**

This node asks something different:

> **What happens when a Jewish-forward model begins treating Palestinians as another population whose experience needs to be “included”?**

Palestinians are not another Jewish diversity category.

They are not the next item after:

- Ashkenazi;
- Mizrahi;
- Sephardi;
- Black Jews.

They are a distinct people whose political relationship with Zionism and Medinat Yisrael includes the experience of state power, land, displacement, and contested sovereignty.

These are separate analytical obligations.

→ See: [🥚 Ashkenormativity And Allied Problems](./🥚_ashkenormativity_and_allied_problems.md)

## 🧭 Ask The Material Question

Every node in this cluster should periodically stop and ask:

> **What does this mean materially for Palestinians?**

If we are discussing:

### Jewish safety

Ask:

> **What political arrangements are being justified in the name of that safety?**

### Return

Ask:

> **Who else has a claim to return?**

### Homeland

Ask:

> **Who currently lives there?**

### Sovereignty

Ask:

> **Whose sovereignty is limited?**

### Demography

Ask:

> **What mechanisms maintain the desired demographic structure?**

### Security

Ask:

> **Who experiences the security apparatus as protection, and who experiences it as coercion?**

### Jerusalem

Ask:

> **Who has residency, citizenship, housing, planning rights, and political power in the actual city?**

### Jewish self-determination

Ask:

> **What form of Palestinian self-determination can coexist with it?**

If the answer repeatedly becomes:

> **we will get to that later**

the cluster is going wrong.

## 🌿 The Ethical Minimum

This node does not require every Jewish reader to reach the same position on:

- Zionism;
- statehood;
- one state;
- two states;
- confederation;
- binationalism;
- sovereignty;
- return.

It asks for something more basic.

Do not make Palestinians disappear inside Jewish abstraction.

Do not turn:

> **Palestinian life**

into:

> **the Palestinian question.**

Do not turn:

> **Palestinian homes**

into:

> **the problem of competing narratives.**

Do not turn:

> **Palestinian political rights**

into:

> **a challenge to Jewish identity.**

Those reframings can become methods of distance.

## 🌱 The Conversation Can Still Be Jewish

There is a fear that once Palestinian reality is centred properly, the Jewish philosophical question has to disappear.

It does not.

We can still ask:

- what does Yisrael mean?
- what does diaspora mean?
- what is Jewish peoplehood?
- what does Jewish safety require?
- what does Jewish memory obligate?
- what is Zionism?
- what does Eretz Yisrael mean?
- what is Yisrael she-be'atzmi?
- what happens when Medinat becomes self?

Those questions remain.

The difference is that they cannot be allowed to pretend they occur in an empty landscape.

Jewish philosophy does not become less Jewish when it notices other people.

Quite the reverse.

---

## 🔮 Yisrael She-be'atzmi Does Not Solve This Either

There is also a temptation to imagine that the cluster's interior-Yisrael concept provides the morally clean answer.

It does not.

A person can derive terrible politics from interior traditions.

Jewish ethics are interpreted.

Texts are interpreted.

History is interpreted.

Yisrael she-be'atzmi can provide a standpoint from which Medinat Yisrael is judged.

It does not guarantee the judgement will be correct.

And Palestinian rights still cannot depend upon whether Jews successfully develop the right internal theology.

## 🫱 A Note To Non-Jewish Readers

There is a related mistake outsiders can make.

Once they recognise that this cluster is Jewish-forward, they may decide:

> **Good, Jews should solve this among themselves.**

No.

Not entirely.

Some of the conceptual problem is intra-Jewish.

The political problem is not.

Palestinians are not waiting outside a Jewish seminar room for us to finish defining Israel.

States operate through:

- governments;
- allies;
- foreign policy;
- arms;
- trade;
- law;
- diplomacy;
- international institutions.

Non-Jewish political actors therefore have their own responsibilities.

Do not use:

> **Jews disagree**

as an excuse to suspend judgement indefinitely.

And do not use one Jewish voice as permission to ignore Palestinian testimony.

→ See: [🫱 How Not To Flatten Jewish Plurality](./🫱_how_not_to_flatten_jewish_plurality.md)

## 🌉 Meeting On The Bridge Means Palestinians Are On The Map

This cluster often talks about:

> **meeting on the bridge.**

That metaphor is primarily about Jews developing enough shared vocabulary to understand what one another mean.

But there is an obvious limit.

We cannot construct a bridge where the entire map contains:

**Zionist Jews**  
↔  
**anti-Zionist Jews**

and Palestine exists somewhere underneath as terrain.

Palestinians are not the bridge.

They are not the landscape.

They are not the thing being argued over.

They are political subjects.

That has to shape the map.

---

## 🧿 A Useful Diagnostic

Whenever a discussion becomes intensely focused on Jewish identity, ask:

> **Where are the Palestinians in this sentence?**

Not because every sentence must mention Palestinians.

But because patterns matter.

If we have spent:

- five pages on Jewish fear;
- four pages on Jewish identity;
- three pages on Jewish history;
- two pages on Jewish disagreement;

and:

> **Palestinians appear only as the reason the Jews are upset with each other**

then something has gone badly wrong.

## 🧭 The Point

This cluster exists because Jewish people genuinely need better language for talking to one another about Yisrael.

That matters.

It matters for:

- antisemitism;
- communal conflict;
- political communication;
- Jewish safety;
- Jewish futurity.

But the conversation cannot become so sophisticated about Jewish interiority that it forgets what happens outside the Jewish self.

A person can carry:

**Eretz Yisrael**

inside memory.

A Palestinian can carry:

**the key to a house.**

A person can experience:

**Medinat Yisrael**

as protection.

A Palestinian can experience:

**Medinat Yisrael**

as state power.

A Jew can undergo a profound intellectual struggle over:

> **what Zionism means to me.**

A Palestinian may be dealing with what political projects called Zionism have meant materially for their family.

Those experiences belong in the same analysis.

They are not interchangeable.

And they do not carry the same kind of power.

So the cluster needs one rule that it keeps returning to:

> **Jewish interiority cannot be allowed to erase Palestinian exterior reality.**

And another:

> **Palestinians do not exist to help Jews decide who we are.**

We should work out who we are.

We should argue.

We should differentiate Yisrael from Yisrael.

We should confront antisemitism.

We should understand why our histories produce different political intuitions.

And while we do that:

**Palestinian lives remain lives.**

Not footnotes.
