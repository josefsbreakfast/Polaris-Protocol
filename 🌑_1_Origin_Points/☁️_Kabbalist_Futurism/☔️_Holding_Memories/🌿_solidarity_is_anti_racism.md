# 🌿 Solidarity Is Anti-Racism

**First created:** 2026-10-07 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-07
*If you want to inherit a tradition of solidarity, you have to practise the solidarity that made it worth inheriting.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

There is an apparent puzzle sitting inside contemporary British Jewish politics.

Parts of establishment Jewish leadership are reaching towards the language of **solidarity**, anti-fascism, Cable Street, collective protection and the Jewish East End. In October 2026 the Board of Deputies is itself running **Cable Street 90: A Festival of Jewish Culture, History & Solidarity**, describing the anniversary as a legacy of Jewish courage, resistance and people acting together.

At almost exactly the same moment, the Board of Deputies and the Jewish Leadership Council have publicly condemned the Green Party's "Zionism is racism" motion in extraordinarily strong terms and called upon wider civil society to change how it relates to the Green Party.

Meanwhile, some Jews whose political traditions have spent decades safeguarding Cable Street as a memory of anti-fascism, diasporism, socialism, anarchism, internationalism and anti-racist solidarity are looking at this new institutional enthusiasm and going:

**Hang on.**

That reaction can look like gatekeeping.

Sometimes it probably is.

Boundaries can become walls. Political communities can become sectarian. People can mistake having carried a memory for owning it. No Jewish faction gets permanent copyright over Jewishness, anti-fascism or Cable Street.

But there is another possibility which needs to be understood before the gatekeeping itself can be understood.

**Memory can be custodial.**

People can feel precious about memories because somebody paid for them.

The question being asked is therefore not necessarily:

> Are you Jewish enough to remember Cable Street?

It may be:

> **Do you understand what the memory asks of the people who inherit it?**

This node makes a simple proposition:

**Solidarity is not something Cable Street proves you possess. Solidarity is what Cable Street asks you to practise.**

And solidarity, if it is to mean anything beyond an aesthetic, has to include anti-racism beyond ourselves.

## 🧱 1. The Awkward Bit About Cable Street

On 4 October 1936, Oswald Mosley's British Union of Fascists intended to march through the East End of London. The proposed march was understood by local Jewish residents as intimidation in an area already experiencing fascist agitation and antisemitic violence.

The resistance was not improvised out of nowhere on the morning itself.

The **Jewish People's Council Against Fascism and Anti-Semitism** had organised locally. Four days before the march it wrote to the Home Secretary asking to present a petition carrying 100,000 signatures gathered in two days. Its proposed deputation included representatives of the Jewish People's Council alongside Labour MP James Hall, London Trades Council secretary A. M. Wall and Father John Groser.

But Jewish institutional leadership did not speak with one voice.

The *Jewish Chronicle* warned Jews before the march to stay away, arguing that Jews caught up in disorder would assist antisemitism and Jew-baiting. Later *Jewish Chronicle* retrospectives have stated plainly that both the Board of Deputies and the paper advised Jews to remain indoors.

That matters.

It also needs to be handled without constructing cartoon villains.

There were at least two recognisable models of Jewish safety in collision.

### The establishment model

The logic can be reconstructed cautiously as:

**confrontation → public disorder → Jews blamed → antisemitism intensifies → Jewish safety decreases**

This was not an incomprehensible fear.

British Jewish establishment politics had developed inside a society in which Jewish acceptance was historically conditional, uneven and classed. Institutional access, respectability and the ability to negotiate with power had often been treated as protective resources. Contemporary historical writing describes a real gulf between better-off, respectable communal leadership and parts of the working-class Jewish East End.

That history deserves examination, including the longer inheritance of elite Jewish representation to British political power and the extremely narrow routes through which a small number of Jewish families had previously acquired legitimacy.

But that is context, not absolution and not a monocausal theory.

We should not lazily write:

> COURT JEWS CAUSED CABLE STREET.

History is more interesting than that.

### The East End model

The competing logic was:

**fascist mobilisation → territorial intimidation → retreat demonstrates that intimidation works → fascist capacity increases → Jewish danger increases**

Many East End Jews rejected the advice to stay home.

Cable Street veteran Max Levitas later remembered the disagreement in brutally simple terms: the Board had wanted Jewish people to remain at home; the East Enders disagreed because, in his view, staying home made the fascists stronger.

The people who rejected institutional advice then assumed the physical risk of that decision themselves.

That distinction matters enormously for what came afterwards.

The Board was not expelled from Jewish history because it advised caution in 1936.

But neither can the later institution approach the memory as though there was never an argument about how Jewish safety should be built.

**The argument is part of the inheritance too.**

## 🫱🏽‍🫲🏻 2. Cable Street Was A Relationship

Cable Street cannot be reduced to:

> Jews defeated fascists.

The anti-fascist mobilisation included Jewish residents and organisers, communists, socialists, trade unionists, Catholics, Irish East Enders and other local and visiting opponents of fascism.

The Irish story is particularly useful because it refuses mythology.

There was significant Irish participation in the resistance. There were longstanding Irish communities in the East End, including dock-working communities in Wapping and Shadwell. But Mosley also tried to recruit Irish support and found some.

Good.

Keep the complication.

Solidarity becomes less meaningful if we rewrite every neighbouring community as having possessed immaculate politics.

The point is not that every Jew, Irish person, communist, Catholic, trade unionist or East End resident agreed.

The point is that enough people built enough relationship to recognise a common danger and act together.

**Solidarity did not require sameness.**

It required people to know who was standing beside them.

That is the inheritance.

## ☔️ 3. Holding A Memory Means Holding An Obligation

A memory is not merely a fact which survived.

Communities also remember:

- who appeared;
- who disappeared;
- who warned;
- who listened;
- who protected;
- who endangered;
- who took the risk;
- which strategies failed;
- which relationships held;
- what somebody paid so that something else could continue.

That makes memory part of the apparatus through which a community enters its future.

This is one of the propositions of **Holding Memories**.

**Memory is not luggage carried from the past. Memory is active infrastructure.**

Some British Jews kept Cable Street as more than heritage.

They kept it inside Jewish socialist traditions, Jewish anarchist traditions, Bundist and diasporist traditions, communist histories, anti-fascist organising, anti-racist organising and, in different combinations and at different times, anti-Zionist, non-Zionist and post-Zionist Jewish life.

These are not synonyms.

They disagree with one another.

They have their own failures, exclusions and historical baggage.

But some of their people have spent decades keeping alive a proposition which now matters very much:

> **Jewish safety can be relational.**

It can be built with neighbours.

It can be built in diaspora.

It can be built through reciprocal minority solidarity.

It can be built by making an attack on somebody else's racialised existence part of our own safety problem.

That is not the only Jewish model of safety.

But it is a Jewish model of safety.

And the people who kept it alive did work.

## 🌱 4. The Memory Kept Moving

The East End did not stop having racial politics when Jewish migration patterns changed.

By the 1970s, racist violence in the area was heavily directed towards South Asian communities. Contemporary and later histories describe the period using the racist phrase "Paki-bashing": violence which was nominally targeted at Pakistanis but operated through broad racial coding and endangered people who could be read as South Asian more generally.

In May 1978, Altab Ali, a young Bangladeshi textile worker, was murdered near Brick Lane in a racist attack.

Thousands marched.

Bangladeshi youth organisations developed self-defence, protest and anti-racist organising. UCL's history of British Bangladeshis in the East End records alliances between Bangladeshi youth organisations, the Anti-Nazi League and Jewish East End activists.

The Altab Ali Foundation itself describes the **Battle of Brick Lane 1978** as part of this history of community resistance.

The connection to Cable Street is not something Polaris has invented because it makes a satisfying paragraph.

Later East End anti-racist commemoration has explicitly connected the two.

**The memory travelled.**

Cable Street did not remain sealed inside Jewish heritage.

It became part of a wider East End vocabulary of what communities might do when fascists or racists attempt to make a neighbourhood unsafe.

That means nobody now gets to rewind the memory until only Jews remain in it.

The solidarity was the point.

## 🌿 5. Solidarity Is Anti-Racism

If solidarity is going to mean anything, it cannot mean:

> **Understand the complexity of my minority while I flatten yours.**

An anti-racist Jewish politics has to remain capable of recognising antisemitism.

Obviously.

It also has to remain capable of recognising anti-Palestinian racism, Islamophobia, anti-Arab racism, racism towards South Asian communities and other forms of racialisation without experiencing their recognition as subtraction from Jewish safety.

No racial exemption certificates.

No:

> We have suffered, therefore our analysis of everybody else is automatically correct.

No:

> They have suffered, therefore antisemitism inside their movement cannot be real.

No:

> A Jew did something racist, therefore Jews are responsible.

No:

> A Palestinian did something antisemitic, therefore Palestinians are responsible.

No collective racial punishment.

Different harms have different histories, distributions, institutions and power.

Recognising more than one harm does not require pretending they are identical.

It requires retaining enough human resolution to see people.

**Jewish safety cannot sustainably be purchased with somebody else's exposure.**

## 🗝️ 6. The Door Is Not The Wall

This is where the gatekeeping starts making more sense.

A wall says:

> You cannot enter.

A door says:

> **There is a relationship involved in entering.**

Someone approaching radical Jewish spaces after years of political hostility may encounter:

- scepticism;
- questions;
- refusal;
- reading recommendations;
- delayed engagement;
- requests to speak to somebody else first;
- "not now";
- "not me";
- "please understand this before we continue";
- "please stop doing that".

That does not prove every boundary is fair.

Gatekeeping can become factional.

A gatekeeper can confuse themselves with the whole community.

A political group can establish an ideological test which nobody outside it can satisfy.

Cultural fluency can be performed by people who remain dangerous.

A sincere newcomer can be rejected because the system produces a false positive.

**The protocol can fail.**

But the existence of an entrance condition is not itself proof that a community opposes solidarity.

Sometimes it means that solidarity is understood as a relationship rather than a badge.

## 🥳 7. Your Extremely Strict Uncle Has Arrived At The Party

Translate all of this into a considerably less politically heightened situation.

You are at a party.

You have been here for ages.

You have worked this room.

You have friends here. You have had arguments here. Somebody in this room looked after you when things were absolutely fucking dreadful. You have looked after somebody else. People know which jokes you make. You know who needs warning before a difficult conversation. You know who is currently fragile. Everyone has broadly established where the chairs are.

Then:

**UNCLE HAS ARRIVED.**

Your extremely strict uncle.

And Uncle does not quietly come over and say hello.

Uncle begins **working the fucking room**.

Your immediate response is not:

> I HAVE RENOUNCED MY FAMILY.

It is:

> **For fuck's sake, Uncle.**
>
> **Do not make me look uncool here.**
>
> **I have worked this room.**
>
> **What the fuck are you doing?**

Because you love Uncle.

You also know Uncle.

You know what he is like when somebody mentions something he does not approve of.

You know the joke he is going to make.

You know which conversational grenade he is about to mistake for an icebreaker.

And crucially:

**you know the guests.**

Somebody here has history with Uncle that Uncle does not understand.

Somebody has recently been through something awful.

Somebody will happily argue with him but would like five minutes' warning.

So:

> Uncle.
>
> **Kitchen.**

We need to have a conversation in the house first.

Not because Uncle can never come to the party.

Because we would quite like Uncle to come to the party **without being a fucking bellend to the guests**.

This is not no love.

It is quite a lot of love.

It is:

> Mate. We are related.
>
> **I know what we are like.**

You are not being excluded from relationship.

**You are being asked to enter relationship as though other people are already in the room.**

## 🇬🇧 8. Unfortunately, We Are Also British

There is another trap here.

It would be very easy to describe every harmful habit in this story as uniquely Jewish, uniquely Zionist or imported wholesale from Israel.

That is far too convenient for British people.

British Jews are also, inconveniently, **British**.

We can inherit habits from the society which socialised us.

Imperial-centre habits.

Institutional confidence.

Assuming that our political vocabulary is the neutral vocabulary.

Beginning with constitutional structures and maps rather than asking the people living somewhere how they understand their home.

Assuming that because we can explain the historical reasons for our position, we have finished listening.

Treating another population's relationship to land as sentiment while our preferred legal-administrative description becomes reality.

Expecting to adjudicate the political legitimacy of people whose lives are being discussed.

Britain has some form here.

For me personally, recognising these habits has had at least as much to do with recognising my British socialisation as with asking what I inherited through Zionism.

I have had to do that work in relation to Palestine.

I am still doing it.

And I can watch the same problem appear when I think about Ireland and the north of Ireland.

I may have identities which give me access to histories of oppression.

That does not magically remove the fact that I am British.

It does not remove the histories of British power which may enter a room with me.

Decolonisation therefore cannot mean:

> BAD PERSON LEARNS COLONIALISM EXISTS → BECOMES GOOD PERSON.

It is more irritating than that.

It is continuous error correction.

Sometimes:

> Oh.
>
> **There I fucking am again.**

And then you do some more work.

## 🧭 9. Sometimes Somebody Else Leads

There are situations involving the north of Ireland where I expect people who live there, and people more directly situated in those histories, to lead.

There are conversations in which I do not expect everybody to feel safe with me.

That is uncomfortable.

Mostly because consciously doing it is still relatively new.

But somebody's caution around me is not automatically a judgement that I am morally irredeemable.

Sometimes it is information about the relationship between our positions.

Someone may:

- know something I do not;
- understand the consequences of British state power more intimately;
- need to speak among people with shared experience before speaking with me;
- trust me for one conversation but not another;
- want me present but not leading;
- not trust me yet.

I do not cure that by announcing that I am one of the good British people.

I build the relationship.

I listen.

I learn where I am useful.

I accept where I am not.

And sometimes I wait outside the fucking room.

**Sometimes history entered the room before I did.**

Being asked to wait outside one room is not the same as being expelled from the house.

That principle has to work in every direction or it is not a principle.

## 🫂 10. Bring It To Us First

There is a Jewish version of this which matters enormously now.

The principle is **not**:

> Jews must never criticise Jews in public.

Jewish history would collapse laughing.

It is narrower:

> **Where the dispute is substantially between Jews, and routing that dispute immediately through a powerful non-Jewish information system foreseeably exposes other Jews or neighbouring minorities, try the Jewish relationship first where it is safe and appropriate.**

If the Board has a serious problem with Jewish anti-Zionists, talk to Jewish anti-Zionists.

If anti-Zionist Jews believe establishment Jewish institutions are participating in anti-Palestinian racism, bring that challenge to those Jews where possible.

If Jewish Greens and Jewish communal institutions profoundly disagree about Zionism, build the Jewish conversation rather than immediately converting the disagreement into British national press content.

And there is humility required from those of us making that request.

You may think we did not always do this well enough.

You may think anti-Zionist Jews became impatient.

You may think we did not come to you enough.

You may think particular organisations were not given a meaningful opportunity to answer.

You may think we sometimes mistook disagreement for refusal.

Fine.

**Tell us.**

That is itself the Jewish-to-Jewish conversation we are asking for now.

My own experience of Jewish opposition to Israel, Zionism and the destruction of Gaza has not felt like leaving Jews in order to complain about Jews to somebody else.

It has repeatedly felt like an argument **with other Jews because they are still our community while we disagree with them**.

That experience should not be silently promoted into a statistical claim about every anti-Zionist Jew.

But the relational ethic matters:

> **We brought our disagreement to you because you were still ours while we disagreed with you.**
>
> If you disagree with us now, extend us the same relationship.
>
> **Come and argue with us before you go and tell Britain about us.**

## 🗣️ 11. Lashon Hara Is Not An Abuse Shield

Jewish traditions around speech are considerably more sophisticated than:

> Do not say bad things about Jews.

Leviticus 19 itself puts several difficult obligations beside one another: do not go about as a talebearer; do not stand idly by the blood of your neighbour; rebuke your neighbour; do not hate your kin in your heart.

Later Jewish traditions develop those tensions extensively.

There is a strong tradition around **tochacha** --- rebuke --- and around beginning correction privately and without gratuitous humiliation. Rambam's treatment of rebuke describes telling the person directly what they have done and initially doing so privately and calmly.

That supports something important in this node:

**relationship-first correction has Jewish roots.**

But it cannot be converted into:

> Jewish leadership must absorb all damaging information before anybody else may know.

Nor:

> The communal reputation is more important than the endangered person.

The prohibition on harmful speech sits beside the prohibition on standing idly by blood for a reason worth taking seriously.

There are circumstances in which preventing harm, warning people and seeking accountability require information to travel.

So separate the cases.

### Political disagreement

Try relationship first where practicable.

### Ordinary interpersonal conflict

Prefer proportionate relational resolution where safe.

### Institutional disagreement

Give affected people and institutions meaningful opportunity to hear, answer and repair where appropriate.

### Abuse, violence, exploitation or serious safeguarding danger

**Protect the endangered person.**

Jewish leadership cannot become a sacrificial shock absorber into which vulnerable Jews are required to transfer risk.

The racism of outsiders belongs to the outsiders.

The wrongdoing of an abuser belongs to the abuser.

The person being harmed does not acquire responsibility for privately carrying the interaction between those two dangers.

This node is about **routing with care**.

It is not communal omertà.

## 📰 12. The British Press Is Not The Community Meeting

The British press is not an empty room.

Once a dispute enters it, neither party controls the resulting information system.

A Jewish disagreement can become:

``` text
Jewish disagreement
        ↓
headline
        ↓
party-political weapon
        ↓
racialised national controversy
        ↓
Palestinians / Muslims exposed
        ↓
Jews exposed
        ↓
original disagreement becomes almost irrelevant
```

That does not mean never speaking to journalists.

It means asking:

> **What is the purpose of transmitting this information through this channel?**

If somebody is endangered and publicity protects them, finds other victims, exposes serious wrongdoing or breaks an institution's refusal to act, external transmission may itself be protective.

If two Jewish political constituencies disagree about Zionism and one recruits the national press before seriously attempting the Jewish conversation, that is a different routing decision.

Same channel.

Different function.

Different ethical calculation.

The colloquial rule is:

> **If you have got a bone to pick which could be used to pick on other Jews, bring the bone to the other Jews before you hand it to the British press.**

Where safe.

Where possible.

Where nobody is being asked to absorb abuse in silence.

**Sequence, not secrecy.**

## 🌡️ 13. Right Now: Dial Down The Heat

This matters in October 2026.

The Board of Deputies and Jewish Leadership Council jointly condemned the Green Party motion commonly summarised as "Zionism is racism". Their statement called it an antisemitic platform and asked wider civil society to relate differently to the Green Party.

There are Jewish Greens who oppose the motion.

There are Jews who support it.

There are Jews who agree with large parts of it but are uncomfortable with particular wording.

There are Jews for whom the words "Zionism is racism" strike directly into an understanding of Zionism as refuge from persecution.

There are Jews for whom Zionism names a political system inseparable from Palestinian dispossession and racial hierarchy.

Those disagreements are real.

They are not going to disappear because somebody wins a press cycle.

The immediate request to establishment Jewish leadership therefore does **not** need to be:

> Become anti-Zionist by Friday.

Nor:

> Pretend Jewish fear is fake.

Nor:

> Stop criticising the Green motion.

It can be much smaller and much more useful.

**Start containing the British press response.**

Dial down the heat around Zack Polanski.

Dial down the heat around Mothin Ali.

Dial down collective suspicion towards Palestinians, Muslims, Greens, anti-Zionist Jews, non-Zionist Jews and everybody else who can become collateral material in the story.

Distinguish individual wrongdoing from communities.

If antisemitism occurs, name the antisemitism.

If anti-Palestinian racism occurs, name the anti-Palestinian racism.

Recognise Jewish disagreement rather than presenting one Jewish political position as the only available Jewish position.

Talk to Jewish opponents before talking about them.

Decline some invitations to escalate.

Redirect journalists towards a wider range of Jewish voices.

And if all you can do initially is **distract the fucking thing**, distract it.

Attention is a resource.

Institutional attention can redirect media attention.

Containment buys room for somebody else to breathe.

## ♻️ 14. The Racism Feedback Loop

There is a dangerous loop available here:

``` text
controversial political proposition
        ↓
genuine Jewish fear
        ↓
institutional condemnation
        ↓
press amplification
        ↓
Palestinian / Muslim / political-opponent exposure
        ↓
racist hostility and resentment
        ↓
Jews collectively associated with institutional response
        ↓
antisemitic hostility
        ↓
greater Jewish fear
        ↓
stronger institutional reaction
        ↺
```

Nobody has to intend the complete loop for the loop to operate.

That is the cybernetic problem.

The useful question is therefore not only:

> Who started it?

It is:

> **Which connection can I break?**

Language.

Amplification.

Collective blame.

Press engagement.

Attribution.

Direct relationship-building.

Public solidarity with somebody who is being racialised despite political disagreement.

There are multiple intervention points.

Use one.

## 🪜 15. Follow The Breadcrumbs

There is no de-Zionisation entrance examination.

There is no:

``` text
READ SIX BOOKS
        ↓
APOLOGISE CORRECTLY
        ↓
RECEIVE BUNDIST MEMBERSHIP CARD
```

People unlearn political assumptions messily.

One person says:

> Read this.

Someone else:

> Talk to her.

Someone else:

> I cannot have this conversation with you right now.

Someone else:

> These people occupy more middle ground between us. Start there.

Someone says:

> This book helped me understand what I was missing.

Another says:

> Please stop using that phrase around me until you understand its history.

Fine.

Use the breadcrumbs.

Build a trail.

Some of the people you approach will refuse.

Some are in too much pain.

Some will be suspicious.

Some will talk later.

Some may become bridges now.

Nobody owes reconciliation on schedule.

And do not march directly up to Palestinians demanding that they mediate an unresolved Jewish argument because you have suddenly discovered curiosity.

There is work Jews can do with Jews first.

Then bridge-building.

Then inter-community dialogue where people actually want it.

Palestinians do not exist to perform emotional labour for a Jewish crisis about Zionism.

## 😭 16. Sometimes You Have To Go And Cry About It

Here is the less glamorous part.

Anti-racist learning can fucking hurt.

Someone tells you something you said was racist.

You think:

> That is unfair.

Or:

> They misunderstood me.

Or:

> I WAS TRYING TO HELP.

Or:

> 😭 SHE CALLED ME A BITCH.

Fine.

Have the cry.

The important information-routing question is:

**where do you put the cry?**

The person you hurt does not automatically become responsible for comforting you about discovering that you hurt them.

Go somewhere safe.

Cry like a little bitch because it hurted your feelings.

Complain to an appropriate friend.

Go for a walk.

Eat something.

Sleep.

Let your nervous system finish submitting its 47-page defence bundle.

Then perhaps:

> Oh.
>
> Yeah.
>
> **Wow, I was a fucking bitch, huh?**

And you go back.

> I understand what you were telling me now.
>
> That was shitty.
>
> I am sorry.
>
> I am working on it.

Sometimes you go back and still disagree.

That is allowed too.

The objective is not submission.

It is becoming capable of receiving painful information **without immediately making the sender pay for having transmitted it**.

**Feelings are allowed. Routing matters.**

## 🐦 17. The Pigeonhole Is Not Safety

At first, all of this can feel less safe.

You do not control every room.

Someone may correct you.

Someone may be angry.

You may discover you caused harm.

You may have to wait outside.

You may not be able to defend yourself immediately.

Surely the old model was safer?

The old model can look like this:

``` text
my group protects me
        ↓
I protect my group
        ↓
criticism threatens my group
        ↓
criticism threatens me
        ↓
DEFEND
```

The problem is that everybody ends up guarding their own pigeonhole.

The relational model is different:

``` text
I protect other people
        ↕
other people protect me
        ↕
correction does not automatically terminate relationship
        ↕
nobody has to pretend their own group cannot cause harm
```

Consciously entering that can be uncomfortable.

In my experience, it also feels **much safer**.

Not because everything is settled.

The place is in flux.

Theoretically, it should feel less secure.

But I have more routes to safety.

If antisemitism appears, I am not dependent solely upon Jews noticing it.

If anti-Palestinian racism appears, Palestinians are not solely responsible for fighting it.

If Islamophobia appears, Muslims do not stand alone.

If British state power harms somebody, I do not need to pretend my Britishness makes me the correct leader in order to remain useful.

**The resilient system is not the node which never receives painful information. It is the network capable of receiving error signals, metabolising them, repairing damage and remaining connected.**

The pigeonhole is not safety.

It is one route.

A network has many.

## 🕊️ 18. If Israel Becomes Profoundly Unstable

This section needs evidential discipline.

On 5 October 2026, *Drop Site News* reported, citing two United States sources who described its contents, that an August classified CIA Red Cell assessment titled **Fracture From Within** examined risks including civil conflict or state breakdown in Israel.

The underlying assessment has not been published.

The CIA did not comment to *Drop Site*.

So this node does **not** say:

> The CIA says Israel is definitely going to collapse.

It says:

> **There is attributed reporting that US intelligence analysts have modelled severe internal-conflict scenarios.**

That matters as part of the information environment.

It also makes a broader contingency question reasonable.

It would be prudent for governments and relevant international bodies to think seriously about civilian protection in the event of severe Israeli state fragmentation or loss of control.

It would be surprising if competent security institutions never considered such scenarios.

But prudence is not evidence.

This node does **not** presently claim public evidence that NATO has a specific operational plan for Israeli civil war or state collapse.

Keep those categories separate.

## 🫀 19. Worry About Our Family --- And Everybody Else's

If Israel became profoundly unstable, Jewish people would worry about Jews there.

Of course.

People have parents there.

Children.

Cousins.

Friends.

People they love.

Nobody needs to apologise for worrying about family.

But an anti-racist conception of Jewish solidarity has to remain capable of noticing unequal vulnerability.

In January 2024, the International Court of Justice did not rule that genocide had been proven. At the provisional-measures stage it found that at least some of the rights claimed by South Africa were plausible, including the right of Palestinians in Gaza to be protected from acts of genocide and related prohibited acts, and it found urgency in the risk of irreparable prejudice.

That legal finding should not be inflated into something it did not say.

But the material vulnerability of Palestinian civilians matters independently.

A severe breakdown of state authority would create civilian-protection concerns across Israel and the occupied Palestinian territories.

The risk would not be evenly distributed.

Populations already subjected to displacement, deprivation, military occupation, racialised dehumanisation or restricted movement may enter a crisis with fewer protective resources.

So:

**protect everybody.**

Israeli Jews.

Palestinian citizens of Israel.

Palestinians in East Jerusalem.

Palestinians in the West Bank.

Palestinians in Gaza.

Bedouin communities.

Migrant workers.

Refugees.

Everybody.

Universal protection does not require pretending everybody begins from the same position.

Our family being frightened does not stop us noticing whose body is underneath the boot.

And Jewish leadership advocating to governments for civilian protection across the whole population would itself be a meaningful practice of solidarity.

## 💍 20. We Are Not Your Plan B

There is an emotionally difficult possibility underneath some of this.

If Israel becomes less politically stable, diasporic models of Jewish safety may suddenly become more interesting to people who previously regarded them as naive, marginal or insufficient.

Those models did not preserve themselves.

People kept them.

They kept Bundism.

Diasporism.

Jewish socialism.

Jewish anarchism.

Anti-fascism.

Jewish-Palestinian solidarity.

Anti-Zionist, non-Zionist and post-Zionist possibilities.

Again: these traditions are not interchangeable.

But some of their people maintained Jewish futures in which diaspora could be home and Jewish safety could be built through reciprocal relationships with neighbours.

Some did that while being mocked.

Some lost friendships.

Some lost institutional belonging.

Some were treated as traitors.

Some have spent years trying to tell other Jews that another Jewish future exists.

So if geopolitical instability suddenly makes that future look useful:

**we are not merely your Plan B.**

This is where the bride metaphor belongs.

A bride does not ask for commitment because she wants to punish somebody for having had another life.

She asks because a shared future requires considerably more than being useful when somebody's previous future falls apart.

You can come frightened.

You can come uncertain.

You can come still calling yourself a Zionist.

You can come disagreeing with half the room.

You can come without having worked everything out.

But if what you are asking for is relationship:

**commitment will be expected.**

When standing beside us becomes inconvenient again, will you still be there?

---

## 🤝 21. Commitment Does Not Mean Conversion

Commitment is not:

- ideological purity;
- compulsory declarations;
- ritual humiliation;
- instant renunciation of Zionism;
- pretending uncertainty has disappeared;
- agreeing with every anti-Zionist Jew;
- agreeing with every Palestinian;
- never fucking up again.

It is:

- consistency;
- curiosity;
- sustained anti-racist behaviour;
- willingness to repair;
- learning histories you previously ignored;
- protecting people with whom you disagree;
- accepting that trust takes time;
- remaining when there is no reputational reward.

**Solidarity is tested when solidarity costs something.**

That includes accepting correction from people you would previously have expected to teach.

It includes allowing somebody else to lead.

It includes tolerating a room you are not in.

It includes admitting that a political tradition you dismissed may have preserved something you now need.

That is not humiliation.

It is relationship.

---

## 🏺 22. Xenia, Sukkot And The Accountable Guest

There are parallel traditions which help make this legible.

The ancient Greek idea of **xenia** is not Jewish law, and should not be smushed into it. But it provides a useful comparative language for hospitality as a reciprocal relationship.

A guest is welcomed.

A host has obligations.

The guest also has obligations.

The existence of hospitality does not mean:

> Stranger arrives → stranger acquires unlimited authority over household.

Jewish traditions of hospitality offer their own vocabulary.

Abraham becomes an archetype of hospitality across the Abrahamic traditions.

Sukkot makes dwelling itself temporarily strange: permeable, provisional, open to the world.

Later traditions of **ushpizin** ritualise the invitation of guests into the sukkah.

None of this requires the host to cease caring for everybody already inside.

That matters.

A hospitable household has obligations towards the new guest.

It also has obligations towards the guests who arrived earlier.

So if somebody says:

> Why are you gatekeeping me? I thought this was solidarity.

The answer may sometimes be:

> **Because solidarity includes the people already in the room.**

Welcome and boundaries are not opposites.

---

## 🍽️ 23. The Extremely Scientific Food Test

There are, obviously, sophisticated methods for measuring inter-community trust.

There is also:

**Are you being invited for the food?**

More importantly:

**Are you being invited for the good food?**

And at the most advanced stage:

**Are people becoming mildly offended because you have eaten six things and are clearly wasting away before their eyes?**

This is data.

Knafeh.

Potato breads.

Apple pie.

Knishes.

Butterbrot.

Seven courses you were not informed were seven courses.

You:

> I am so close to being in a food coma, my friend.

Host:

> **You have hardly eaten anything.**

Congratulations.

**You may have achieved community.**

This is not literally a scientific measure.

Nobody should publish a cross-cultural Knafeh Trust Index and blame Polaris.

But the joke works because food invitations can encode several of the things this node has spent an unreasonable number of words describing:

**proximity + trust + hospitality + cultural sharing + repeated contact + expectation of future contact**

There is:

> Here is a biscuit because you are physically present.

And then there is:

> No, no. Sit down.
>
> You need to try this.
>
> My aunt makes it differently.
>
> Have you had this one?
>
> Wait.
>
> **MAM---**

Different fucking metric.

You do not fill in the anti-racism form and receive Level Seven Knafeh Clearance.

You listen.

You show up.

You fuck up.

You accept correction.

You defend somebody when they are not there.

You learn when you are the guest.

You learn when to leave the room.

You come back.

Eventually somebody is yelling at you because near-terminal carbohydrate saturation apparently constitutes insufficient eating.

**Relationship produces relationship.**

## 🌈 24. The Payoff Is More People

This matters because anti-racism cannot be described only as homework.

If the entire proposition is:

> Read more.
>
> Feel ashamed.
>
> Get corrected.
>
> Wait outside.
>
> Cry privately.
>
> Apologise.
>
> Repeat forever.

then understandably everybody wants to go home.

There is a payoff.

**More people become your people.**

You acquire more routes to safety.

More people whose rights you will defend.

More people who will defend yours.

More houses where you are expected.

More people who notice when you are missing.

More cultural worlds you can approach respectfully.

More tables.

More food.

It turns out that giving up the exhausting project of defending your own pigeonhole does not necessarily leave you culturally homeless.

It can do the opposite.

You become embedded in a network larger than the pigeonhole ever permitted.

And yes, there will be times when somebody tells you that you have been a bitch.

There will be times when you tell somebody else.

There will be tears.

There will be arguments.

There will be political disagreement.

There will also, apparently, be knishes.

**Preliminary results are extremely encouraging.**

## 🕯️ 25. Repairing The Cable Street Relationship

Return to the Board of Deputies.

The point of acknowledging 1936 is not:

> Your predecessors were cowards, therefore you are illegitimate forever.

That would reproduce exactly the frozen politics this node is arguing against.

Institutions change.

Communities change.

People learn.

Memory changes hands.

A meaningful contemporary acknowledgement could instead say:

- our institutional predecessors advised Jews not to participate in the confrontation;
- many East End Jews rejected that advice;
- they were joined by neighbours and political allies;
- their resistance became an important part of British Jewish memory;
- later anti-racist communities carried and extended that memory;
- our institutional relationship to the history has changed;
- we recognise why people who kept this tradition alive may experience our arrival as complicated;
- we want to understand what solidarity requires now.

Maybe there is an apology.

Maybe there is a conversation before an apology.

Maybe the most useful first act is listening.

The point is not ritual confession.

It is demonstrating:

> **We understand why you might be cautious about us claiming this inheritance before we have talked about how we got here.**

That is an entirely different entrance.

And if the people holding the memory say:

> Not yet.

that may have to be tolerated.

Some people are not ready.

Some people are too hurt.

Some people will never want the relationship.

Others may say:

> Fine. Kitchen first.

That is where bridges begin.

---

## 🌧️ 26. You Cannot Inherit Only The Shelter

Cable Street offers beautiful things.

Courage.

Belonging.

Pride.

Collective protection.

Jewish continuity.

Anti-fascist legitimacy.

A story in which people refused to be intimidated and their neighbours stood beside them.

But the inheritance also contains:

risk.

obligation.

neighbours.

reciprocity.

anti-racism.

You cannot inherit only the shelter.

If you invoke the people who stood at Cable Street, the memory leaves a question behind:

> **Who are you prepared to stand beside now?**

Not:

> Which historical photograph would you like to stand inside?

Not:

> Which tradition would look useful on the institutional website?

Who are you prepared to stand beside **now**?

When they are unpopular.

When they disagree with you.

When protecting them costs political capital.

When the newspaper wants another quote.

When you are frightened.

When they are frightened of you.

When nobody has yet worked out the perfect language.

That is the inheritance.

---

## ☔️ 27. Holding Memories

The people who maintained alternative Jewish futures were not preserving museum curiosities.

They were maintaining possible routes.

That does not make them infallible.

It does not give them ownership of Jewishness.

It does not mean every anti-Zionist Jew has behaved well.

It does not mean every Zionist Jew has behaved badly.

It does not mean one faction gets permanent control of the door.

It means that when history makes an old route newly interesting, the people who kept that route passable are part of the information required to navigate it.

Ask them what they learned.

Ask where they fucked up.

Tell them where they hurt you.

Let them tell you where you hurt them.

Read what they recommend.

Offer them what you think they have missed.

Protect the people standing around the conversation from becoming collateral damage.

Do not ask Palestinians to referee unresolved Jewish trauma.

Do not ask vulnerable Jews to absorb danger for communal reputation.

Do not mistake discomfort for expulsion.

Do not mistake access to the British press for access to truth.

Do not mistake the door for the wall.

You are allowed to approach.

You are allowed to disagree.

You are allowed to be frightened.

You are allowed to fuck up.

You may eventually help carry the memory.

But first:

> **Come into the kitchen.**
>
> **We have got some things to talk about.**

And eventually:

> **Come back to the party.**

---

## 🌿 28. Coda --- Solidarity Is Anti-Racism

We are not asking you to stop being Jewish.

We are not asking you to stop worrying about Jews.

We are not asking you to stop loving Israel if that is where you are.

We are not asking you to pretend that antisemitism is imaginary.

We are asking you to discover that worrying about everybody else does not necessarily leave less safety for us.

**It can build more of it.**

That is the promise inside the mess.

Not purity.

Not perfect politics.

Not everybody agreeing.

A larger network of people who understand that somebody else being racialised is not an opportunity to retreat into our own pigeonhole.

A network in which we can correct one another without automatically terminating the relationship.

A network in which we can protect one another without demanding sameness.

A network in which people sometimes lead and sometimes follow.

A network in which people sometimes wait outside the room.

A network in which Uncle eventually makes it back out of the kitchen.

A network in which somebody's mother has decided that seven courses are a light snack.

**Solidarity is anti-racism.**

**Commitment will be expected.**

**Snacks will probably follow.**

*Polaris has not established whether distributed anti-racist solidarity reliably increases access to international carbohydrates. Preliminary results remain extremely encouraging.*
