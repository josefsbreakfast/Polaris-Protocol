# 🥮 Mooncakes And Oranges
**First created:** 2026-09-24 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-24  
*On embodied diplomacy, learned taste, Florida citrus, pandas, human-controlled AI, and the apparently radical proposition that two great countries might both be allowed to succeed.*

---

## 🌕 Orientation — Guys. Make It Happen.

On 24 September 2026, Donald Trump and Xi Jinping stood in Washington talking about some very large things.

Trade. American farmers and ranchers. Security. Technology. AI and “super intelligence”. Competition. Peace. What decisions made now might mean in ten, twenty, thirty years.

They were also doing all of this immediately around Mid-Autumn Festival: a period in which the moon, reunion, food, family, seasonal time and shared cultural memory carry meanings which cannot be reduced to a line in a diplomatic communiqué.

And Donald Trump, approaching the end of his remarks, reached for the United States Marines.

He explained that the Marines had adopted **“gung-ho”** from China, and rendered its meaning as **“working together”.**

This is not quite a dictionary definition. It is, however, a remarkably Trump-shaped survival of the underlying signal:

> **工合 / industrial cooperative**  
> ↓  
> cooperation  
> ↓  
> teamwork  
> ↓  
> US Marines  
> ↓  
> **GUNG HO**  
> ↓  
> 🇺🇸 **HELL YEAH, LET’S FUCKING GO**  
> ↓  
> Donald Trump  
> ↓  
> **“It means working together.”**

Correct enough direction.

The important thing is not that information crossed systems without changing.

It very obviously changed.

The important thing is:

> **The signal survived.**

And then Xi got up and, in considerably more diplomatic language, essentially said:

**Yes. Working together.**

This node is about what happens when diplomacy stops being treated only as words exchanged between abstract states and is returned to the bodies, foods, animals, memories, photographs, journeys, technologies and material systems through which human beings actually experience one another.

## 🇨🇳📈 + 🇺🇸📈 — The Big Thing

The most important shorthand from the visit may be this:

> ### 🇨🇳📈 ≠ 🇺🇸📉
>
> ### 🇺🇸📈 ≠ 🇨🇳📉
>
> ### 🇨🇳📈 + 🇺🇸📈 = potentially compatible

This does **not** mean:

> 🇨🇳🤝🇺🇸 = identical interests

It does not mean:

> 🇨🇳🤝🇺🇸 = no competition

And it definitely does not mean:

> 🇨🇳🤝🇺🇸 = everybody likes everybody now

The proposition is narrower, harder and much more important:

> **My success does not inherently require your failure.**

Xi has framed the relationship in explicitly non-zero-sum terms, saying that the “great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation” and “making America great again” can go hand in hand, and describing a desired relationship with cooperation as the mainstay, moderate competition and manageable differences. [Chinese government/Xinhua coverage of Xi’s arrival](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202609/24/content_WS6ab51bd9c6d00ca5f9a0d6d7.html)

That is politically clever because neither leader has to announce that his own country should become less successful for the sake of the other one.

> 🇺🇸: **I want America to succeed.**
>
> 🇨🇳: **Understandable. I want China to succeed.**
>
> 🇺🇸: **Can those things coexist?**
>
> 🌏🌎: **FOR THE LOVE OF GOD PLEASE INVESTIGATE THAT POSSIBILITY.**

There will be genuinely zero-sum disputes. There are scarce resources, military contingencies, protected technologies and incompatible strategic objectives. Nothing useful is gained by pretending otherwise.

The danger is turning **every** improvement in the other system into information about your own impending defeat.

China develops a better technology → American loss.

America expands semiconductor manufacturing → Chinese containment.

Chinese living standards rise → American relative decline.

American agricultural exports rise → Chinese loss.

Once two enormous adaptive systems learn to process the other side’s success primarily as threat, they can manufacture an escalating feedback loop out of perfectly ordinary evidence of development.

### Shorthand

> **Competition does not require existential interpretation.**

## 🏛️ America Has Remembered That China Also Has Context

There is something worth giving the White House credit for here.

This visit is not culturally blank.

The state dinner has been designed around American ingredients with Chinese influence. Its opening sparkling wine deliberately recalls the same American wine served during Nixon and Zhou Enlai’s 1972 **“Toast to Peace”.** The White House itself has made that historical continuity explicit. [White House state dinner programme](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/09/first-lady-melania-trump-releases-details-on-the-state-dinner-with-his-excellency-xi-jinping-president-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-and-madame-peng-liyuan/)

That matters because one recurring American strategic failure is remarkably simple:

> **You cannot expect everybody else’s information environment to revolve around you.**

Other countries have context.

They have historical memory. Festivals. Food. Humour. Grievances. Rituals. Stories about themselves. Stories about America. Expectations about respect. Different associations attached to the same words, objects and actions.

The 1972 wine does not solve a modern strategic disagreement.

It says something smaller:

**We remember that this relationship has a history.**

The Marine reference does something similar.

A Chinese linguistic object entered an American military institution, acquired an American life, and remained available decades later as evidence that these two societies have not merely observed one another from sealed containers.

> 🇺🇸: **Oh. You also have context.**
>
> 🇨🇳: **Yes.**
>
> 🇺🇸: **We have attempted to read some of it.**
>
> 🇨🇳: **This is preferable.**

## 🌕 The Moon Is Not Just A Rock

The timing around Mid-Autumn Festival is symbolically rich whether or not every available meaning was deliberately engineered in Washington.

That distinction matters.

> **Intended information ≠ available information.**

### 🇺🇸 Translation, not equivalence

For a British or American reader, simply saying **“Mid-Autumn Festival is an important Chinese holiday”** risks making the calendar feel much smaller than it is. There is no clean Western equivalent, and the point is not to pretend that Mid-Autumn is Chinese Christmas, Thanksgiving or the Fourth of July.

But if you are trying to understand the *weight of the scheduling choice*, borrow pieces from several familiar reference points:

- **Thanksgiving:** harvest, food, homecoming and family reunion.
- **Christmas:** the emotional weight attached to togetherness, absence and being home for a culturally saturated family occasion.
- **Fourth of July:** the symbolic significance of an American president choosing to spend a nationally meaningful date on a major state visit abroad.

Then imagine an American president choosing to be in China across that kind of calendar moment.

> **The festivals are not equivalent. The comparison is about what it means for a head of state to be somewhere else at a culturally saturated moment in the national calendar.**

That gets closer to the informational weight of Xi being physically in the United States around Mid-Autumn than the thin translation **“important festival”** does.

It also produces two different readings of the same itinerary:

> 🇺🇸: **The Chinese president has come for an important state visit.**
>
> 🇨🇳: **The president is in America during Mid-Autumn.**

Same dates. Different information.

And this is embodiment too. Calendars are not culturally empty grids. Seasons, foods, astronomical cycles, memories, family routines, travel, ritual and expectations accumulate around dates until **when something happens becomes part of what happened**.

> 🌕 **A date can carry embodied information before anybody says a word.**

An audience does not require the transmitter to have consciously encoded every possible meaning before that meaning can become available in reception.

The moon is an unusually clean example.

> 🌕 Astronomer: astronomical body  
> 🌕 Engineer: destination  
> 🌕 Calendar: clock  
> 🌕 Farmer: seasonal information  
> 🌕 Festival: ritual  
> 🌕 Separated family: something both can see

The moon has not changed.

The relationship between observer and moon has.

Across East and Southeast Asia, distinct autumn and lunar traditions attach food, gathering, harvest, ancestry, beauty, time and reunion to the moon. Those traditions are not interchangeable. Chuseok is not simply Chinese Mid-Autumn Festival with a Korean flag stuck on it; Tsukimi and Tết Trung Thu have their own histories and practices.

But this is precisely why the moon is useful.

A single physical object can participate in many information environments without requiring those environments to become identical.

### Shorthand

> **Same moon. Different information.**

And, appropriately:

> 🌏 🌎 🌍 = **same planet, different face presented to the observer.**

## 🥮 Give Donald The Fucking Mooncake

Now we arrive at pudding.

This is serious scholarship.

A traditional lotus-seed-paste mooncake enters a body whose sensory expectations have already been trained by thousands of previous encounters with food.

The eater does not receive:

`SWEETNESS = 0.67`

The eater receives flavour through a history.

Sweetness, dairy, fat, pastry, texture, salt, egg, familiarity, smell, expectation, childhood food and the immediate social setting all participate in what the food becomes **to that eater**.

A British or American person can appreciate lotus paste and simultaneously notice that it does not behave exactly like the category of **SWEET WESTERN DESSERT** their mouth has learned to predict.

This is not a defect in lotus paste.

It is information about the observer.

And therefore, for Donald Trump:

# CUSTARD MOONCAKES.

Not because Chinese food needs to be Americanised until the American cannot detect China.

The opposite.

Find something already within the enormous Chinese culinary repertoire which creates an easy bridge into the recipient’s existing sensory model.

> 🇨🇳: “It is a mooncake.”
>
> 🇺🇸: “Okay.”
>
> 🇨🇳: “It has sweet custard inside.”
>
> 🇺🇸: “…Go on.”

Give him several varieties.

This is not the **Correct Appreciation Of Chinese Culture Examination**.

The useful question is:

> **Which mooncake does Donald actually like?**

He is allowed to like the custard one.

Communication has not failed because two mouths prefer different fillings.

### Shorthand

> **Translation ≠ homogenisation.**

And:

> **You do not have to like every mooncake to like mooncakes.**

## 🍫 WHY DOES YOUR CHOCOLATE TASTE LIKE THAT?

The Western palate is not the culture-free control group.

Hershey’s is useful precisely because many Europeans encounter it and immediately discover that **American chocolate has a context too**.

Penn State’s discussion of chocolate flavour describes controlled lipolysis of milk fats in Hershey’s production, producing butyric acid and the distinctive tangy or “goaty” note which many Europeans notice while Americans raised with the flavour may simply experience it as familiar chocolate. [Penn State — “Finding the Flavor of Chocolate”](https://www.psu.edu/news/research/story/finding-flavor-chocolate)

So:

> 🇬🇧: **WHY DOES YOUR CHOCOLATE TASTE LIKE THAT?**
>
> 🇺🇸: **Why doesn’t yours?**

The useful chain is not **Americans have bad chocolate**.

It is:

> **material conditions**  
> ↓  
> **food-processing technology**  
> ↓  
> **sensory environment**  
> ↓  
> **repeated exposure**  
> ↓  
> **familiarity**  
> ↓  
> **preference**  
> ↓  
> **culture**

The larger history of American dairy preservation, transport, refrigeration, rail distribution and industrial food production is worth following carefully rather than reverse-engineering a neat story from the modern flavour. But the broader methodological point already holds.

Bodies learn environments.

Industrial solutions can become tastes.

Tastes can become expectations.

Expectations can become national food cultures.

Then another body arrives a century later and goes:

**what the fuck is going on with the chocolate?**

### Shorthand

> **No mouth is culture-free.**

## 🍊 Florida Has Entered The Chat

China has mooncakes.

America grows citrus.

Florida can, in fact, provide the diplomatic mandarins. UF/IFAS documents Florida tangerines and mandarins, including satsumas; Florida tangerines are available from September, and some early cultivars mature from late September. [UF/IFAS — Florida Fresh: Tangerine](https://floridafresh.ifas.ufl.edu/PlantDetails/Details/550) [UF/IFAS — The Satsuma Mandarin](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/CH116)

This makes the orange much better than a generic attempt to Google **Chinese Lucky Gift** and reproduce somebody else’s symbol back at them.

It can be American **and** legible in a Chinese cultural environment.

And agriculture is already part of the substantive relationship being discussed.

So the object can simultaneously be:

🍊 American agriculture  
🍊 Florida  
🍊 food  
🍊 trade  
🍊 gift  
🍊 smell  
🍊 taste  
🍊 prosperity / auspicious associations in relevant Chinese contexts  
🍊 and, unfortunately, Donald Trump

One joke.

**One.**

> 🇺🇸: “Florida oranges.”
>
> 🇨🇳: “Thank you.”
>
> 🇺🇸: “People say I know something about orange.”
>
> 🇨🇳: “Yes, Donald.”

**DO NOT EXPLAIN THE JOKE FOR FOURTEEN MINUTES.**

The orange is also the economic abstraction becoming material again.

A trade document says:

`agricultural market access`

The orange says:

> soil → tree → farmer → harvest → packing → transport → market → hand → mouth

### Shorthand

> **You can eat a trade relationship.**

## 🐼 China Deploys The Pandas

And then China brought pandas.

Of course it did.

In the live speech coverage on 24 September, Xi moved from shared Chinese-American wartime history to people-to-people exchange, announcing an invitation for **100,000 young Americans** to visit China for study exchange programmes over five years. He then announced that two pandas would meet Americans in their new home at Atlanta’s zoo.

That sequence matters.

> 🇺🇸 **100,000 young Americans → China**
>
> 🐼🐼 **China → Americans**

The symmetry is not literal.

The informational movement is nevertheless gorgeous.

An American who has never been to China can know China through American journalism, politics, films, defence discourse, Chinese state communications, social media, diaspora communities, products, algorithms, academic work and inherited stories.

An American who spends meaningful time there acquires another category:

> **embodied primary experience**

They have walked down streets.

Eaten food.

Caught trains.

Got lost.

Spoken to people.

Been bored.

Been surprised.

Encountered ordinary life.

Discovered which expectations survived contact with reality and which did not.

None of that requires them to approve of the Chinese government.

It means their model of China now contains information that cannot be completely substituted by information **about** China delivered from elsewhere.

### Shorthand

> **Information about a place ≠ information acquired inside a place.**

And then the panda performs the inverse movement.

A child in Atlanta does not receive a seminar on Chinese foreign policy.

They receive:

> 🐼 **China is also this.**

A living creature. A public encounter. A memory. Something cute enough to survive ideological abstraction.

🐼 has been assigned to Track II diplomacy.

## 🤖 Keep The Humans In The Loop

This is where the node stops being cute.

In the 24 September speech coverage, Xi argued that China and the United States have responsibilities around AI: developing it for good, keeping it under human control and ensuring it benefits people. Trump, immediately before him, had foregrounded security, technology and “super intelligence”, saying decisions made now could shape peace and prosperity for decades.

The juxtaposition matters.

> 🤖 **more capable machines**
>
> +
>
> 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 **more capable human relationships**

If China and America expect decades of competition involving AI, semiconductors, autonomous systems, cyber capabilities, military technology, space and machine-speed information systems, deliberately reducing their human communication bandwidth would be an extraordinarily stupid companion strategy.

### Shorthand

> **More machine capability requires more human legibility, not less.**

Or, more compactly:

> 🤖📈 + 🧑‍🤝‍🧑📉 = **bad fucking plan**

The 100,000-person exchange proposal therefore belongs in the AI section as much as it belongs in the diplomacy section.

It is relationship infrastructure.

People who have actually inhabited one another’s environments provide additional pathways through which each society can model the other.

That does not eliminate conflict.

It makes the model harder to reduce to **ENEMY SHAPE DETECTED**.

## 📺 Someone Has To See The Fucking Orange

There is another information system in the room even when its members are not physically in the room:

**the press.**

The White House is currently engaged in its own fights over press access and media participation. That argument has its own legal, democratic and institutional dimensions and does not need to be completely litigated here.

For Embodied Information Ecology, the immediate systems question is simpler:

> **How does an event become available to a person who was not there?**

The chain might look like:

> **event**  
> ↓  
> **observer**  
> ↓  
> **recording**  
> ↓  
> **editorial interpretation**  
> ↓  
> **distribution**  
> ↓  
> **audience**  
> ↓  
> **experienced information**

A government can spend enormous effort constructing ceremony, historic references, food, speeches, visual symbols and cultural gestures.

But if it simultaneously damages the independent transmission infrastructure through which those events become observable, it creates a contradiction in its own information strategy.

There is also a very obvious alternative to trying to force favourable reception:

> **Do something worth photographing.**

Feed Donald the custard mooncake.

Give Xi the Florida oranges.

Let photographers photograph the fucking oranges.

Let Chinese journalists explain them to Chinese audiences.

Let American journalists make the obvious Donald-is-orange joke.

Let Florida agricultural media talk about the growers.

Let different observers receive different information from the same event.

### Shorthand

> **You cannot control reception.**
>
> **You can improve what there is to receive.**

That is not the same thing as propaganda.

Independent observers remain independent.

The point is that prosocial behaviour creates prosocial facts which other people are then free to interpret.

## 🪢 Exchange Does Not Necessarily Diminish

Now put the objects back on the table.

🥮 An American eats Chinese food and remains American.

🍊 A Chinese visitor receives an American agricultural product and remains Chinese.

✈️ An American studies in China and does not thereby cease to belong to America.

🐼 A panda lives in Atlanta and China has not suffered geopolitical panda defeat.

🍫 A British person learns why Hershey’s tastes strange to her without requiring Americans to stop liking Hershey’s.

📚 A Chinese phrase enters the US Marines, becomes American slang, changes meaning and still carries a trace of the relationship that moved it.

Interaction can add information to both systems.

### Shorthand

> **Exchange ≠ loss.**

And therefore, again:

# 🇨🇳📈 + 🇺🇸📈 = potentially compatible.

Not guaranteed.

Not universal.

Not an excuse to ignore genuine conflicts of interest.

A proposition worth preserving as an available possibility.

## 🕊️ Diplomacy Is For People Who Disagree

Diplomacy is not the prize states receive after proving they like each other.

It is infrastructure for living on the same planet without requiring that condition.

> 🇺🇸: “I disagree with you.”
>
> 🇨🇳: “Likewise.”
>
> 🌏🌎: “Excellent. You have correctly identified why we built diplomacy.”

States cannot simply decide that another great power is annoying and therefore move to another planet.

For the foreseeable future, everybody is still here.

So it is entirely possible to maintain:

**competition + communication**

**deterrence + cultural exchange**

**strategic disagreement + trade**

**national ambition + bounded cooperation**

**AI development + human contact**

**different political systems + fucking dinner**

None of this requires pretending that serious disagreements do not exist.

It requires refusing to make disagreement the only information the relationship is capable of carrying.
