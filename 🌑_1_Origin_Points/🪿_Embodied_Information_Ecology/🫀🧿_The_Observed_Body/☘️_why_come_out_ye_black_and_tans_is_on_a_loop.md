## 🛰️ Orientation

*Come Out Ye Black and Tans* is on a loop.

Again.

This is not because I have mistaken myself for Ireland.

It is not because every experience of institutional intrusion is equivalent to colonial occupation, because it obviously fucking is not.

It is not because a rebel song is evidence about Prince Andrew.

It is because, as a survivor, **all of this feels very invasion-like to me, psychologically**.

That distinction matters.

The song is not evidence about the things I am researching.

**The fact that I keep putting it back on is information about what researching them feels like.**

Unfortunately, my nervous system has declined to submit a properly referenced literature review explaining its playlist choices.

It has instead selected:

**COME OUT YE BLACK AND TANS AGAIN.**

Peer review remains pending.

## 🫀 1. The Body Recognises The Grammar

There are many different kinds of intrusion.

Sexual violation is not the same thing as political occupation.

Medical intrusion is not the same thing as surveillance.

Institutional scrutiny is not the same thing as military force.

Information extraction is not the same thing as somebody physically entering your space.

Historical analogy is not historical equivalence.

But different events can contain a recognisable **grammar**.

Something outside me crosses a boundary.

It possesses more power than I do.

It behaves as though its reason for crossing the boundary is more important than my experience of being crossed.

I cannot reliably make it stop.

That is upstream of a great many more precise categories.

The body may first register:

**INTRUSION.**

This matters for Embodied Information Ecology because information theory can make observation sound wonderfully clean:

`observer → observes object → acquires information → processes information`

The observed body has several additional questions.

**Who the fuck are you?**

**Why are you looking at me?**

**Who said you could come in?**

**Where is this information going?**

**Who else gets to see it?**

**What happens to me because you have it?**

**Can I make you leave?**

And, apparently:

**WHY ARE YOU NEAR MY HORSE?**

That last question is not strictly necessary to the model.

It has nevertheless survived peer review.

## 👣 2. Those Loving English Feet

One of the lines that catches hardest is:

> "And those loving English feet, they walked all over us"

The important collision for me is not simply **English / Irish**.

It is:

**loving**

against:

**walked all over us**.

That is an extraordinarily recognisable structure if you have experienced coercion presented as benevolence.

*We are helping.*

*We are protecting you.*

*This is necessary.*

*We are concerned.*

*We are doing this for your own good.*

Meanwhile:

**YOU ARE STANDING ON ME.**

Intent and experienced effect are different information.

An institution can understand itself as protective while the person underneath experiences intrusion.

A professional can understand a question as routine while the person being questioned experiences loss of control.

A state can understand observation as security while the observed population experiences the same observation through histories of policing, coercion, surveillance or political inequality.

Neither perspective magically deletes the other merely by existing.

That is part of what it means for information to be embodied.

The observer does not get sole authorship of what observation means.

## 👀 3. Where Are The Sneers And Jeers?

Then the song turns around and asks:

> "Where are the sneers and jeers
> That you loudly let us hear?"

This is a different operation.

**The observed object looks back.**

That is important.

So much institutional life involves the powerful party observing the less powerful one.

Was she credible?

Was she compliant?

Was she calm enough?

Was she too calm?

Did she report correctly?

Did she remember correctly?

Did she behave correctly?

Did she understand the process?

Was her reaction proportionate?

The body becomes a document everybody else is apparently authorised to annotate.

And then accountability reverses the direction of scrutiny.

**You were extremely fucking confident when I was the one answering questions.**

How are *you* getting on?

Tiny survivor acquires clipboard.

> "Interesting."
>
> *writes something down*
>
> "Please continue."

This is not necessarily revenge.

It is **reciprocity of scrutiny**.

The person who has spent years being made legible to systems gets to ask whether the systems are equally willing to become legible to her.

Where are the sneers now?

Where is the certainty now?

Where is the enormous institutional confidence when the question changes from:

**What happened to you?**

to:

**What did you know, what did you record, where did the information go, and what the fuck did you do with it?**

## 🏃 4. Come Out Where I Can See You

Then comes the demand:

> "Come out ye Black and Tans
> Come out and fight me like a man"

For me, the psychologically important word is:

**out**.

Come out.

Come where I can see you.

Come out of the institutional maze.

Come out from behind the unexplained decision.

Come out from behind the person who says somebody else holds the record.

Come out from behind the record that says another department was responsible.

Come out from behind the information advantage.

Come out from behind the authority to observe without being observed back.

**COME OUT.**

And then the song gives the body another thing it does not always get in real life.

The frightening thing retreats.

The boast about the green and lovely lanes of Killashandra is not interesting to me because I require a literal military reenactment in County Cavan.

It is interesting because the enormous thing is suddenly **capable of running away**.

That changes scale.

Trauma can make danger enormous and the self tiny.

The song tells a story in which the supposedly enormous thing can be named, mocked, confronted and made to leave.

That is not quite domination.

It is closer to **successful expulsion**.

The information produced by my body is not automatically yours because you have an institution, a form, a title, a lanyard or an Important Reason.

## 🪭 6. Sometimes You Need An Island

There is a much older cultural technology sitting behind this.

Irish political culture has repeatedly personified Ireland as a woman. The *aisling* tradition could render Ireland as a female figure whose condition stood for the condition of the country. Later political and literary works could use intimate, familial and gendered relationships to make political conditions emotionally legible.

That tradition is complicated.

Woman-as-country can turn actual women into symbols rather than people. Romantic nationalism can make feminine suffering useful to male political action. The metaphor has its own politics and its own limits.

But the information technology inside it is interesting:

**a political relationship can be rendered as an intimate relationship because both contain questions of trust, possession, abandonment, obligation, betrayal, access and boundary.**

The translation can also run the other way.

A survivor can borrow a political language of territory and expulsion to make an intimate experience of violated boundaries large enough to see.

That does not mean:

**Ireland is my trauma.**

It means:

**my body recognises this grammar.**

Sometimes **"please respect my personal boundaries"** is simply not geographically ambitious enough.

Sometimes you need an island.

There is also a separate history of political censorship in Ireland, including twentieth-century broadcasting restrictions on material associated with organisations advocating political violence. That history should not be lazily collapsed into the much older *aisling* tradition, or used to pretend every love song was secretly a coded republican communiqué.

The more interesting point is broader.

Cultures under censorship, political pressure or contested authority become very good at carrying political meaning through forms that can do more than one job at once.

Love.

Loss.

Betrayal.

Longing.

The bastard man who promised everything, took everything and fucked off.

Woman.

Country.

Home.

And sometimes the survivor encounters that cultural machinery and thinks:

**oh.**

**I can use this.**

## 👑 7. FOR FUCK'S SAKE, ANDREW

Its recent history contains simultaneously:

republican and loyalist political violence;

major policing reform;

## 🌐 9. A Rhyme Is Not An Edge

The digital field itself can change.

That is an observation, not yet an explanation.

New documents become public.

Journalists write about them.

Parliamentary questions create new authoritative text.

Government responses add another layer.

Old documents become newly salient.

Search engines re-index.

Entity associations strengthen.

Users search along new paths.

Journalists notice those paths.

More text is produced.

The navigable information environment changes even where the underlying historical event has not.

One particularly strange example is **Hillsborough**.

There is Hillsborough Castle in County Down: a royal residence and an important Northern Irish governmental site.

There is also the Hillsborough disaster and the enormous British history of campaigning over institutional truth, police accounts, disclosure, accountability and candour that follows that name.

So a search environment containing:

`Hillsborough + government + records + disclosure + police + accountability`

can begin retrieving two entirely different histories into conceptual proximity.

That does **not** create a historical edge between them.

It creates a retrieval edge.

This distinction matters.

> **A rhyme is not an edge.**

But a rhyme can still affect the information environment.

It can affect what a researcher sees next.

It can affect which old document suddenly becomes visible.

It can affect which analogy becomes cognitively available.

It can affect what a journalist searches.

It can affect which entities become adjacent in future reporting.

It can affect what the observed body repeatedly encounters.

The archive has not merely gained documents.

**Its navigable topology has changed.**

## 🕸️ 10. The Data Field Can Move

This is particularly noticeable if you have been watching the same information environment longitudinally.

A person arriving today can search a term and report:

**this is what the internet says.**

A person who has repeatedly searched the same institutional terrain can sometimes observe something different:

**this is not what used to come back.**

That observation should be recorded without immediately inventing a cause.

The field can move because the underlying public knowledge changed.

It can move because reporting changed.

It can move because a new document made old entities newly adjacent.

It can move because a title creates persistent SEO association.

It can move because a search engine changed.

It can move because the user's own behaviour changed what was retrieved.

It can move because several of these things happened together.

The disciplined statement is:

> **I observed the field shift. I have not thereby established what
> caused the shift.**

That is still data.

In network terms, adding one document does not merely add one node.

A new public connection can shorten the retrieval distance between many existing nodes.

`release → reporting → indexing → searching → rediscovery → reporting → indexing`

The observer is inside the loop.

This is one reason an information environment can begin to feel haunted without requiring a ghost.

Sometimes the ghost is just SEO.

Sometimes the ghost is a title.

Sometimes the ghost is a newly indexed archive.

Sometimes the ghost is the British state having kept several centuries' worth of cupboards.

And sometimes you open one of those cupboards and---

### 👑 ANDREW, STOP ADDING SUPPORTING MATERIAL.

## 🧿 11. The Evidentiary Boundary Is Also Mine

The joke only works if the evidence stays disciplined.

Feeling invaded is not evidence that every suspected relationship exists.

Algorithmic association is not institutional association.

Institutional association is not personal association.

Personal association is not participation in wrongdoing.

A title is not a conspiracy.

A military affiliation is not intelligence activity.

A search result is not corroboration.

A repeated allegation is not necessarily an independently repeated source.

A negative records search is not always proof that an event did not happen.

An incomplete archive is not proof that somebody deliberately destroyed it.

**A rhyme is not an edge.**

These distinctions are not concessions to institutional comfort.

They are **my boundaries around my own evidence graph**.

The survivor does not regain informational sovereignty by filling uncertainty with whatever story feels most emotionally satisfying.

That simply gives somebody else another opportunity to tell her she cannot distinguish what she knows from what she fears.

No.

I can say:

**this feels fucking invasive**

and:

**I do not presently know whether X caused Y**

at the same time.

I can say:

**I am sick of seeing this man**

without saying:

**this man committed every offence in the archive.**

I can say:

**the information environment keeps putting him in my field**

without saying:

**he controls the field.**

That is not contradiction.

That is boundary competence.

**You do not get to invade my evidence graph either.**

## 🛡️ 13. Successful Expulsion Is Not Domination

This is perhaps the most important distinction in the node.

The fantasy is not conquest.

It is not:

**now I get to walk all over you.**

It is:

**stop walking over me.**

It is not:

**now I control your information.**

It is:

**you do not automatically get mine.**

It is not:

**I want permanent access to you.**

It is:

**I want the capacity to make you leave.**

This is why the song's emotional usefulness is closer to boundary restoration than revenge.

The threatening thing can be confronted.

The threatening thing can be made visible.

The threatening thing can lose its aura of inevitability.

The threatening thing can retreat.

The field can become a field again.

The horse can go back to doing whatever the horse was doing before the British constitutional settlement inexplicably arrived beside it.

## 🎶 14. Sometimes The Observed Body Needs Music That Looks Back

The song performs several operations for me.

**You walked over us.**

The violation is named.

**Where are the sneers now?**

Observation reverses.

**Come out.**

Hidden or asymmetric power is summoned into visibility.

**You ran.**

The enormous thing becomes capable of retreat.

That is why reducing the loop to **anger** misses something.

It is doing boundary work.

It is doing scale work.

It is doing observation work.

It is giving the observed body a temporary position from which it is not merely the object being looked at.

**I am looking back.**

And perhaps that is why political music can sometimes do something ordinary therapeutic language cannot.

It does not politely ask the body to imagine a boundary.

It gives the boundary fields, roads, houses, people, history, weather, jokes, enemies, songs and somewhere for the intruder to fucking leave *to*.

The historical struggle is not my personal trauma.

My personal trauma is not Ireland.

Northern Ireland is not a metaphorical dumping ground for my feelings about Andrew.

Andrew's title does not make Northern Ireland responsible for him.

The evidence does not get bent to accommodate the joke.

But the body is allowed to recognise a grammar.

It is allowed to notice what the information environment keeps doing.

It is allowed to be sick of the eijit middle child.

It is allowed to laugh.

It is allowed to look back.

And it is allowed, sometimes, to require a country-sized metaphor for a boundary it was never allowed to have.

Anyway.

**ANDREW.**

**HORSE.**

**DISTANCE.**

Thank you.
