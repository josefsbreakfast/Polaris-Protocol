# 📋 Exhibit B: Windows Fucking Vista
**First created:** 2026-09-16 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-16  
*Microsoft, the product was already good.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Before explaining that the next technological revolution requires more compute, more hardware, more interface, more integration, and more of the user’s attention, we would like to submit a previous experience for the record.

**Windows. Fucking. Vista.**

This is not an argument that old technology is automatically better.

It is an argument that a technology company can achieve something extremely valuable—

**everybody knows how the fucking thing works**

—and then mistake that achievement for stagnation.

## 📋 The Product Was Already Good

Windows 95 and 98 had done something important.

They made the graphical personal computer increasingly ordinary and legible to enormous numbers of people.

People learned where things were.

Schools taught it.

Offices built workflows around it.

Software accumulated around it.

Hardware accumulated around it.

Repair shops learned it.

Families learned it.

That accumulated familiarity had value.

XP eventually consolidated an even more valuable state:

**people knew how to use the fucking computer.**

The computer was becoming infrastructure.

People increasingly wanted to think about the document, spreadsheet, photograph, game, website, printer or actual work rather than about the operating system itself.

That is not failure.

**That is the operating system succeeding.**

---

## 🍄 XP, Inexplicably Given Mushrooms

Then came Vista.

The complaint is not simply that Vista looked different.

The complaint is that the machine suddenly seemed extremely interested in demonstrating that it was a machine.

Glass.

Transparency.

Animations.

Aero.

More visual depth.

More conspicuous interface.

More hardware appetite.

More opportunities for the user to become aware of the operating system.

Microsoft appeared to have taken XP, fed it mushrooms, made it extremely interested in translucent surfaces, and increased its dietary requirements.

> **Britain, traumatised by Aero Glass:**
>
> And how much RAM does the shiny thing want?

This is the important distinction:

**visual novelty is not the same thing as user value.**

Additional computational complexity is not free merely because the resulting interface looks more technologically advanced.

---

## 🍎 If I Wanted Sexy I Would Buy Mac OS

There was already a company selling computers partly through beauty, industrial design, visual coherence, and tightly integrated hardware and software.

It was called Apple.

That was fine.

Microsoft had another proposition.

**It runs everything. Everybody knows it. Your weird peripheral probably works. Your employer uses it. Your files are there. Excel is there. Get on with it.**

That is an extraordinarily good product proposition.

So:

> **I JUST NEED IT TO FUNCTION.**
>
> **IF I WANTED SEXY I WOULD BUY MAC OS.**

Microsoft did not need to win an Apple beauty contest to keep people using Excel.

It needed Windows to remain an extremely good place to use Excel.

**We never moved to Apple spreadsheets.**

The customer had already picked you.

Calm the fuck down.

---

## 🧱 Installed Human Competence Is Part Of The Installed Base

The installed base is not merely hardware and software.

It includes human beings.

It includes:

- muscle memory;
- training;
- documentation;
- organisational procedures;
- support knowledge;
- repair expertise;
- peripheral familiarity;
- accumulated workarounds;
- confidence;
- the knowledge that **the button is over there because the button has always been over there**.

Every substantial redesign spends some of that accumulated competence.

Sometimes that cost is worth paying.

Interfaces should change when there is a sufficiently good reason.

But the cost does not become zero merely because it never appears on the software company’s invoice.

**Installed human competence is infrastructure.**

Treat it accordingly.

---

## 🟪 What The Fuck Are These Boxes

Windows 8 supplied the sequel.

The desktop user switched on the computer and encountered a full-screen Start environment built around large tiles.

The design made considerably more sense in a world of touchscreens.

There was only one minor difficulty.

A great many Windows users were sitting at desks with:

**a mouse.**

Hence:

> **WHY IS MY DESKTOP A FUCKING BATHROOM TILE DISPLAY.**
>
> **I HAVE A MOUSE.**

The problem was not that touch interfaces existed.

Touch interfaces are useful.

The problem was treating a new interaction paradigm as though existing users had ceased to possess the interaction hardware and workflows around which their computers were already organised.

Sometimes the future arrives.

Sometimes the customer is still using a mouse.

**Design for the customer who exists.**

---

## 🔄 Excuse Me, What Do You Mean “Shut Down”?

Later Windows versions supplied another excellent lesson.

Fast Startup changed ordinary shutdown behaviour so that shutting down could preserve system state needed to accelerate the next startup, while **Restart** remained the route to a more complete fresh Windows boot.

The engineering motivation is comprehensible.

The semantic result is magnificent.

> **Windows:** Shut down?
>
> **User:** Yes.
>
> **Windows:** Excellent. I shall not completely do that.
>
> **User:** No. Shut. Down.
>
> **Windows:** You probably mean Restart.

Sometimes Sandra is shutting down the computer because **the bastard needs shutting down**.

Something is misbehaving.

A driver is confused.

The machine has been running for ages.

Sandra would like to invoke the ancient and respected technical procedure:

**TURN IT OFF AND ON AGAIN.**

Do not optimise away the intuitive meaning of the fucking button.

## 🧮 The Administrative Footprint Of Innovation

Every unnecessary change can itself become admin.

Button moved?

Find button.

Workflow redesigned?

Relearn workflow.

Feature removed?

Find workaround.

File format changed?

Convert files.

Account required?

Create and manage account.

Hardware unsupported?

Replace hardware.

New interface?

Teach everyone where the fucking button went.

A productivity product can therefore become a **producer of work**.

That work belongs in the productivity calculation.

Do not tell us that a new feature saves six minutes while ignoring the hours spent migrating, authenticating, retraining, troubleshooting, reconfiguring and relearning the product around it.

---

## 🪨 Boring Is A Feature

There is an enormous market for:

> **Thing. Works. Continues working.**

Reliability is a feature.

Familiarity is a feature.

Backwards compatibility is a feature.

Low resource requirements are a feature.

Repairability is a feature.

Not requiring the user to think about you is a feature.

Sometimes the highest compliment infrastructure can receive is:

**“Yeah. It works.”**

That does not mean the customer lacks imagination.

It means the customer has something else to do.

---

## 🤖 Sandra Has Already Lived Through Vista

This is the relevance to artificial intelligence.

Before giving Sandra:

**HUMAN SUPERINTELLIGENCE**

remember:

Sandra has already lived through Vista.

She has seen what happens when functioning infrastructure becomes anxious that it is not exciting enough, acquires a conspicuous new interface, increases the hardware requirements, moves familiar things around and calls the resulting administrative burden innovation.

So when the AI proposition becomes:

more compute;

more hardware;

more subscriptions;

more interface;

more accounts;

more dashboards;

more anthropomorphism;

more behavioural measurement;

more things to configure;

more things to check;

more things to learn;

Sandra is entitled to ask:

**Why?**

If you give millions of people something boring that reliably removes twenty minutes of irritating work every day, they may fucking love you.

You do not need Computer God to justify the product.

You need the product to work.

---

## 🎩 Be Your Own Fucking Product, Man

A competitor having a desirable characteristic does not mean every product needs that characteristic.

Apple can be Apple.

Microsoft can be Microsoft.

Excel does not need to become Notion.

Notion does not need to become Slack.

Slack does not need to become an operating system.

Every application does not need an AI assistant.

Sometimes a thing does not need to do more things.

The product can know what it is.

So, Microsoft:

**be your own fucking product, man.**

We picked you already.

Stop dressing Windows up for a date with Steve Jobs.

**He wasn’t the customer. Sandra was.**
