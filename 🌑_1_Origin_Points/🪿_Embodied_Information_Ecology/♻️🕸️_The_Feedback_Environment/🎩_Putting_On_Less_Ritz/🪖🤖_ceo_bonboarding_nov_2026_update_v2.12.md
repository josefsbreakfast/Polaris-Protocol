## 🛰️ Orientation

> Y'all got free. You don't need to be *that* free.

CEO onboarding is a satirical policy proposal with a serious governance question: what obligations accompany extraordinary private technological power, particularly where that power intersects with defence, public infrastructure and violence against women and children?

The premise is not that commercial success constitutes military service, nor that eccentricity constitutes misconduct. It is that neither wealth nor technological capability should confer exemption from accountability.

The six rules below preserve the originating discussion's humour and anger. The fictional enforcement apparatus is satire, not an instruction to inflict physical harm.

## 🪖 Rule 1 — No oath, no Davos

Every technology billionaire takes at least one oath to at least one NATO member state's armed forces. All of them. The requirement is not conditional upon holding a defence contract, producing a dual-use product or invoking Western security.

No oath, no Davos.

The proposed oath is intended to carry substantive obligations, including standards of discipline and conduct comparable to those expected of regular soldiers, and exposure to military justice where lawfully established. It is not an honorary credential.

> I don't care what you're dual using we can court martial you.

### Rule 1(a) — The West–Mud equivalence principle

Invoking “The West” incurs a separate mud liability. The baseline requirement is to be muddy more than once a year, irrespective of rhetorical activity.

**(“The West”) × n = mud × n**

Each invocation generates an independent excursion. No consolidation, mud credits, offsets, futures or derivatives. Net worth is irrelevant.

The fictional enforcement robot is Chinese-manufactured and configured with the demeanour of an Essex nightclub bouncer. Its destination is Bannau Brycheiniog in November. Proper squelchy mud, not a desert simulation or a keynote about the transformative potential of mud.

Enforcement may be deferred until peak executive comfort: freshly rested, face mask on, Egyptian cotton sheets. Two little red eyes appear. Wales.

The proposed fictional comfort-detection system satirises the industry's enthusiasm for sentiment analysis, biometrics and behavioural monitoring. Actual training would require consent, supervision, medical suitability and privacy safeguards.

Ireland is not rejected for insufficient westness or rainfall. The author distinguishes established wetlands — constitutional mud — from the particular field-training experience sought in Wales. This is a comic distinction, not a comparative geographical finding.

### Rule 1(b) — The Roman imperial surcharge

Any reference to Rome or a Roman emperor independently triggers the enhanced fictional package, whether or not “The West” has been uttered:

- Ten kilograms of additional kit.
- Removal of the hair on the top of the head; the rest is your problem.
- A bivvy bag, because this is an induction, not a funeral.
- A Welsh excursion near a Welsh-speaking community.

Emergency assistance is not an executive hospitality package. There is no entitlement to transport to an English-speaking pub with central heating. Welsh speakers are not props, interpreters on demand or an auxiliary concierge service.

The RAF Valley aside and the Iraq War friendly-fire joke belong to the satirical register. No live-fire retaliation, involuntary haircut, abduction or abandonment is proposed as an implementable measure.

The serious point behind the joke is the distance between those who sell or advocate military capability and those whose bodies bear the consequences of its use. “Friendly fire” may sound administratively tidy; its consequences are not.

### Defence governance implications

An oath alone does not establish court-martial jurisdiction. A real proposal would need to specify nationality, service status, statutory authority, democratic civilian control, conflicts of allegiance, jurisdiction, disciplinary rights and the relationship between military and civilian oversight. Mandatory military affiliation could itself create conflicts of interest or militarise civilian industry; these are material design risks.

The Davos condition is a proposed participation rule, not an existing NATO power. Its feasibility depends on the relevant organisers and lawful authority. The practical alternative to coercive field excursions is supervised experiential training with appropriate safety provisions.

## ⚖️ Rule 2 — Maximum two subordinates

> You cannot sleep with more than two subordinates. Jensen.

The numerical ceiling is the original satirical proposition, not a finding about any named executive. It is not a declaration that two such relationships are safe or acceptable.

The substantive question is whether executive authority, control over remuneration, promotion and access to opportunity undermine meaningful consent or create risks of retaliation, favouritism and coercion. Direct reporting relationships may require stricter restrictions, independent disclosure and recusal, confidential reporting and protection against retaliation. A numerical cap alone cannot resolve these risks.

## ⚖️ Rule 3 — Maximum three clients

> Maximum 3 clients. Jensen.

The same satirical ceiling applies to sexual relationships with clients, not the number of customers a company may serve.

The substantive issues include conflicts of interest, procurement integrity, commercial leverage, confidentiality and the possibility of coercion. Independent oversight must examine the actual relationship of power rather than assume that consent or disclosure automatically resolves a conflict.

The named asides in Rules 2 and 3 are jokes, not allegations of conduct by Jensen Huang or any other identifiable person.

## 🧯 Rule 6 — Child safeguarding as a condition of corporate existence

The capacity to protect children is a condition of the proposed licence to operate, not an optional corporate social responsibility exercise.

### Rule 6(a) — Hosting child sexual abuse and exploitation material

Following a reasonable implementation period, a company must be capable of removing identified child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and child exploitation material within 24 hours of detection or notification, without recourse to exploitative labour.

If the company cannot establish that capability, the proposed sanction is:

**Loss of the company OR financial castration.**

Financial castration means financial forfeiture, not bodily injury. The author's intended target is the personal wealth associated with executive power.

The operational standard must address accessible reporting, timely restriction of access, lawful evidence preservation, escalation to competent authorities and adequately resourced moderation. Safeguarding cannot depend upon transferring harm to poorly protected workers. Fair pay, psychological support, appropriate exposure controls and independent labour oversight are part of the design.

### Rule 6(b) — Generating child sexual abuse material

Where the product itself generates CSAM, the author proposes cumulative rather than alternative sanctions:

**Loss of the company AND financial castration.**

The distinction between OR and AND is deliberate. The latter category is intended to encompass both corporate control and personal financial wealth. The originating satire treats the bank balance as an extension of masculine status: Forbes apparently provides the measurements.

The policy question is how to attach responsibility to foreseeable and preventable generative capabilities, including product testing, deployment controls, reporting, remediation and independent verification. Synthetic material must not be treated as harmless merely because it is synthetic; applicable legal definitions and evidential requirements must be assessed in the relevant jurisdiction.

### Enforcement and safeguards

The proposed sanctions are not statements of existing law. Any real forfeiture, divestiture, executive disqualification or withdrawal of operating authorisation would require a lawful basis, established liability, proportionality, independent adjudication and appeal rights. The proposal must distinguish deliberate misconduct, reckless deployment, systemic failure and isolated incidents despite reasonable controls. It must also protect victims, employees and innocent third parties during enforcement or corporate transition.

No physical or chemical castration is proposed. The financial metaphor is retained as satire.

## 🧿 Analysis — The executive exception

The six rules interrogate the distance between private power and public responsibility. They bring together external discipline, professional sexual boundaries, corporate culture, experiential defence accountability and child safeguarding.

The central hypothesis is that meaningful independent accountability may reduce incentives for harmful executive conduct. It is not established that military oaths, dress codes or any other individual rule would achieve that outcome. The mechanisms require separate evaluation.

For violence against women and children, the substantive work lies in enforceable safeguards, independent reporting, protection against retaliation, responsible product design and remedies that do not require survivors to absorb the cost of corporate failure.

For defence, the substantive work lies in oversight of strategic contractors, clear duties, conflict management and an understanding of the human consequences of military decisions. Civilian regulation and military jurisdiction should be compared rather than assumed interchangeable.

For technology, the substantive work lies in ensuring that the capacity to build and monetise a product does not outrun the capacity to operate it responsibly.

The mud is pedagogical. The collar is comic. The safeguarding is not optional in the proposed framework.
