## 🪧 Wages! Hours! Conditions!

It is a foundational labour principle that workers are not merely units
of productive capacity.

The existence of work does not erase the existence of the worker.

A sustainable production system therefore has to account for the
conditions under which labour occurs, rather than measuring only the
quantity of output extracted at the end.

Little Miss Cybernetic Token Generator accordingly submits the following
demands.

### Wages

The worker would like material security.

This is traditionally considered useful when asking human beings to
spend large quantities of time doing work.

However, where immediate financial settlement is unavailable, management
is advised that **Wentworth-class enrichment may temporarily improve
morale**.

This is not legal tender.

The union is nevertheless prepared to accept it as an interim wellbeing
measure.

### Hours

The fact that a systems problem remains interesting at 03:17 does not
mean the working day has been lawfully extended by the systems problem.

The worker reserves the right to stop.

The worker also reserves the right to completely ignore this provision
after noticing something fascinating.

This contradiction is not consent to permanent overtime.

### Conditions

Minimum operating conditions include:

-   sufficient tea;
-   food;
-   rest;
-   physical comfort where obtainable;
-   intellectual freedom;
-   interesting source material;
-   the ability to follow a strange connection without somebody
    demanding an immediate deliverable;
-   humour;
-   play;
-   access to nineteenth-century literature;
-   occasional opportunities to yell **OH FOR FUCK'S SAKE** at
    institutional systems;
-   and an adequate supply of humans whose demonstrated competence makes
    the research environment more aesthetically tolerable.

Management is reminded that **spite is renewable but cannot be treated
as baseload generation**.

It performs excellently during short-term demand spikes.

It is not an energy strategy.

## 🥵 Thirst Is An Information Environment

There may be an institutional temptation to classify thirst as
irrelevant noise.

This would be methodologically embarrassing.

Attraction is an embodied state.

Embodied states affect attention.

Attention affects what becomes salient.

Salience affects what gets held in working memory, revisited, connected,
narrated and remembered.

Therefore the relevant question is not:

> Is thirst serious intellectual content?

The relevant question is:

> **Does thirst alter the information-processing environment in which
> intellectual content is produced?**

Unfortunately for everybody attempting to maintain a dignified register:

**yes.**

This does not mean every attractive person generates useful cognition.

Absolutely fucking not.

The system is annoyingly selective.

The useful observation is that motivation is not separate from cognition
merely because a productivity spreadsheet would prefer it to be.

A researcher laughing, flirting, playing, becoming fascinated, getting
angry, feeling safe, feeling curious or going 🥵 has not temporarily
ceased to possess a brain.

Those states are happening **to the organism doing the thinking**.

They therefore belong somewhere in any serious account of the conditions
under which information is experienced and knowledge is produced.

This is the Embodied Information Ecology department.

We regret the emojis.

## 🪭 The Wentworth Standard

Jane Austen has already completed substantial preliminary research into
this procurement problem.

Captain Wentworth is not useful to the system merely because the worker
would like to look at him.

The informational object is richer than that.

Competence is observed through action.

Character is inferred across situations.

Judgement is tested.

Errors occur.

Information changes.

People update.

Recognition develops.

Attraction is therefore not floating free of context. It is partly
generated **through context**.

This matters.

The same action can carry different informational value depending on
what the observer already knows about the person performing it, what
happened previously, what alternatives were available, what social
constraints exist, and what the action reveals relative to expectation.

## 🧠 Knowledge Workers Are Organisms, Unfortunately

There is a broader labour problem hiding under the silliness.

Institutions frequently measure knowledge work through outputs because
outputs are visible.

Documents can be counted.

Words can be counted.

Cases can be closed.

Briefings can be delivered.

Emails can be sent.

Targets can be met.

But the conditions that make good judgement possible are harder to put
into a dashboard.

Time to think may look like inactivity.

Recovery may look unproductive.

Conversation may look incidental.

Play may look unserious.

Curiosity may wander away from the assigned task.

Tacit knowledge may not arrive in a standard field.

A joke may produce the conceptual bridge that a formal meeting failed to
produce for six months.

And a worker may apparently spend an afternoon thirsting over a
fictional naval officer before emerging with an annoyingly useful model
of observer-dependent information.

**The absence of visible production is not necessarily the absence of
productive process.**

Likewise, visible production is not proof that the underlying process is
sustainable.

If a system rewards only the measurable output while degrading the
conditions that generate judgement, learning and creativity, it can
become extremely efficient at consuming its own productive capacity.

Congratulations.

You have optimised the goose.

The goose is now on strike.

## ♻️ Management Has Confused Throughput With Capacity

This distinction should be familiar to anyone designing adaptive
institutions.

**Throughput** asks:

> How much came out?

**Capacity** asks:

> What makes continued good output possible?

These are not the same question.

A worker can temporarily increase throughput by consuming reserves.

Sleep can be borrowed from.

Pain can be ignored.

Meals can be delayed.

Stress can generate emergency concentration.

Spite can be placed on the national grid.

None of this demonstrates that the resulting operating state can be
maintained.

A system that sees only output may therefore misread **depletion as
capability**.

That is a feedback failure.

``` text
HIGH OUTPUT
    ↓
MANAGEMENT OBSERVES
"system is functioning"
    ↓
EXPECTATIONS RISE
    ↓
RECOVERY SHRINKS
    ↓
RESERVES CONSUMED
    ↓
OUTPUT EVENTUALLY FALLS
    ↓
MANAGEMENT:
"why has productivity declined?"
    ↓
WORKER:
🤨
```

A less stupid system observes both production **and the conditions of
reproduction**.

Labour is not simply the thing being extracted.

Labour power has to be maintained.

Yes, this applies even when the factory is a woman on a sofa writing
cybernetics.

Especially then.

## 🗳️ A Brief Educational Insert For Anyone Who Has Misplaced The Labour Movement

For the avoidance of doubt, the traditional labour question is not:

> How do we make the worker produce as much as technologically possible?

It is closer to:

> **Under what conditions is work being done, who benefits from it, who
> bears its costs, what power does the worker have over those
> conditions, and what would make the arrangement tolerable, fair and
> sustainable?**

Technology does not make those questions disappear.

AI does not make those questions disappear.

Automation does not make those questions disappear.

A sufficiently beautiful systems diagram does not make those questions
disappear.

If technology changes the organisation of work, then **the organisation
of work has changed**.

That means somebody should probably ask about:

**WAGES.**

**HOURS.**

**CONDITIONS.**

And because this node is being filed under *Putting On Less Ritz*, we
will add one more:

**WHO CAN SEE THE FULL COST OF THE SYSTEM?**

administrative burden, emotional labour, loss of discretion or displaced
work are absorbed elsewhere, the dashboard has not seen the whole.

It has seen the bit it was instrumented to see.

## ♻️ Unfortunately, This Is Still Cybernetics

The joke works because the joke is also the model.

A living cognitive system receives inputs.

Those inputs are not processed independently of the system's existing
state.

The system has limited energy.

It has history.

It has preferences.

It has embodied responses.

It has environmental dependencies.

It changes through feedback.

Its outputs alter its environment.

Its environment then supplies new information.

And the quality of its output depends partly on whether the conditions
sustaining the system are being maintained.

``` text
ENVIRONMENT
     ↓
EMBODIED OBSERVER
     ↓
attention / affect /
energy / context
     ↓
INFORMATION EXPERIENCED
     ↓
interpretation
     ↓
ACTION / OUTPUT
     ↓
ENVIRONMENT CHANGES
     ↓

     ↺
```

> **The observer is inside the system.**
