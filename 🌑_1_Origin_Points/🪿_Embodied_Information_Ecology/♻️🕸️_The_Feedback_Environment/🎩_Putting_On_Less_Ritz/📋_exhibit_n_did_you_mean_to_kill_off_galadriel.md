# 📋 Exhibit N — Did You Mean To Kill Off Galadriel?
**First created:** 2026-10-23 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-25  
*Dear America: we know you loved the books. That's why we're a little worried about what you've built.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Dear America,

This is going to be a slightly difficult conversation, and we are sorry about that.

We know you loved Tolkien. We know that some of you grew up reading about Rivendell, Lothlórien, the Shire and the Ents, and then went off to build astonishing machines because you wanted to make extraordinary things possible. We know you didn't necessarily mean to do any harm. We suspect quite a lot of you would be horrified to discover that the economic arrangements surrounding those machines might make it harder for the people and places you loved to survive.

Unfortunately, boys, **you appear to have built an economy in which Galadriel might not be able to afford to remain Galadriel.**

This is not a claim that the Americans invented capitalism, that every engineer endorses every commercial incentive, or that Tolkien's fictional world supplies a ready-made programme for modern public policy. It is a thought experiment: **what happens if the economic and institutional assumptions we treat as ordinary are applied to the things we say are priceless?**

🇺🇸: Ronald, we love your books.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: How lovely.

🇺🇸: They inspired us to build the future.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: How exciting.

*Several presentations later.*

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **Oh dear.**

---

## 🌳 1. The Problem With Being Galadriel

Galadriel has knowledge. Quite a lot of it. She has memory, judgement, relationships, history, experience and the ability to recognise consequences other people haven't noticed yet. People seek her counsel. Her knowledge helps them. Some of those benefits travel far beyond the person who asked the question.

None of this guarantees that she can send an invoice.

**Social value, intellectual value, market price and income received by the person producing knowledge are not interchangeable quantities.** An idea can save other people time, prevent a mistake, preserve a cultural inheritance or improve public decisions without generating a reliable payment to its originator. The fact that a platform can make money circulating an idea does not mean the person who developed it can pay the rent.

🇺🇸: Lady Galadriel, your work is incredibly valuable.

🌳: Thank you.

🇺🇸: Have you considered monetising it?

🌳: I already share my knowledge with those who need it.

🇺🇸: Yes, but have you considered a subscription?

🌳: 🤨

🇺🇸: Or consulting?

🌳: People already consult me.

🇺🇸: Right. But professionally.

🌳: Some of them have nothing.

🇺🇸: We could offer a free tier.

🌳: Of wisdom?

🇺🇸: Exactly. **Galadriel Basic.**

🌳: **No.**

This is not an objection to anyone earning money from expertise. It is an objection to the assumption that every socially useful activity must become a customer-acquisition exercise to remain possible.

And there is a second, more fundamental point: **Galadriel does not have to be exceptionally useful to deserve a life.** She is allowed to be tired. She is allowed to rest. She is allowed to have a home and relationships and time among the trees. A society that only offers security to people who can demonstrate profitable output has misunderstood what the security was for.

---

## 🏭 2. An Economic System Nobody Had To Invent On Purpose

The Americans did not all sit down in a room and decide to impoverish the elves. Most people grow up inside an economic system that feels like the ordinary background of life. Their parents worked within it. Their schools prepared them for it. Their employers rewarded certain behaviours; their investors rewarded others. If you want to keep a service running, you need revenue. If you want capital, you may be asked how you will grow. If you want attention, you may be asked how frequently you can publish.

Those questions are understandable. Their cumulative effects are not necessarily benign.

Digital platforms have made it possible to distribute information at extraordinary scale. Many have financed that distribution through advertising, subscriptions, data-driven optimisation or other commercial arrangements. The measures that keep a platform solvent need not be the measures that identify good scholarship, sound judgement, careful research or the social value of work that takes years to produce.

The resulting pressure can be quite ordinary: publish more, shorten the argument, find a niche, build a personal brand, cultivate an audience, manage a mailing list, optimise a thumbnail, convert attention into payments, keep people engaged. The analyst who wanted to answer a difficult question has accidentally acquired an entire marketing department, except that the department is also her.

And because these platforms operate internationally, the incentives do not remain confined to the American economy. An Irish folklorist, a British independent researcher, a community archivist or a disabled writer may find that their route to readers is organised around commercial assumptions they never chose.

**A global information network is not the same thing as a global system for sustaining the people who know things.**

🇺🇸: But we made it possible for everyone to publish!

🌳: That is wonderful.

🇺🇸: Anyone can reach an audience.

🌳: Also wonderful.

🇺🇸: So what's the problem?

🌳: Who pays for the time it takes to know what you're talking about?

🇺🇸: ...

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Ah.

## 📈 3. Have You Considered A Startup?

We should probably let the Americans offer the advice they would offer anybody else. This is only fair. It is, after all, the advice they know.

🇺🇸: Elrond, we've reviewed your operation.

🌳: My home?

🇺🇸: Yes. Extraordinary brand recognition. Highly differentiated visitor experience. Exceptional intellectual capital.

🌳: It is a refuge.

🇺🇸: Exactly. Have you considered a startup?

🌳: 🤨

🇺🇸: We think there's tremendous potential to scale.

🌳: Scale what?

🇺🇸: Rivendell.

🌳: But Rivendell is already here.

🇺🇸: Imagine a thousand Rivendells.

🌳: **Why?**

---

🇺🇸: Ronald, we think the elves have a monetisation problem.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Do they?

🇺🇸: They're sitting on enormous intellectual assets.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes, they're elves.

🇺🇸: But they're not capturing the value.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **They're preserving it.**

🇺🇸: ...

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: 🤨

The distinction is not between having costs and having no costs. Rivendell requires food, buildings, care, time, infrastructure and protection. The distinction is between **financing something so that it can fulfil its purpose** and **changing its purpose to fit a financing model**.

---

## 💧 4. Ronald, They've Found The Magic Water

Rivendell is not merely a body of knowledge. It is a place. Its forests and rivers are not decorative assets placed there to improve the investment presentation. They sustain the valley.

If it were real, somebody would eventually ask about land values, water rights, permits, electricity and competing uses of the landscape. These are legitimate questions. The problem is what happens when the calculation recognises the value of extracting a resource more readily than the value of leaving an ecosystem intact.

🇺🇸: Ronald, we've identified a fantastic opportunity.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Oh no.

🇺🇸: Rivendell has an abundance of freshwater.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes. It is a beautiful valley.

🇺🇸: And the water has unusual properties?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: It is an enchanted place.

🇺🇸: Fascinating. Have you considered its applications in liquid cooling?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: ...

🇺🇸: Because our preliminary modelling suggests substantial efficiency gains.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **You want to put the enchanted waters of Rivendell through a computer?**

🇺🇸: Well, technically, through the cooling infrastructure.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: 🤨

🇺🇸: We'd obviously conduct an environmental impact assessment.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **IT'S AN ENCHANTED VALLEY.**

🇺🇸: Absolutely. And we're very committed to sustainability.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: What are you sustaining?

🇺🇸: The compute.

Real data centres differ in their cooling systems, electricity needs, water consumption and local impacts. The point is not that every data centre drains a river. It is that **land, water and energy are allocated through decisions that distribute benefits and costs unevenly**, and those decisions must account for communities and ecosystems rather than treating them as an afterthought.

Environmental protection matters here too. Defunding monitoring, weakening safeguards or reducing enforcement capacity may make a project appear administratively cheaper without making its ecological consequences disappear. Equally, not every regulatory requirement is effective simply because it exists. The question is what protection actually achieves, who verifies it, and who bears the cost when it fails.

🇺🇸: We're trying to reduce unnecessary administrative burdens.

🌳: Have you considered that the river might be necessary?

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: For living?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: She's quite right.

## 🪞 5. Galadriel's Mirror — Can We Train On The Outputs?

And then the Americans find the mirror.

🇺🇸: Lady Galadriel, this is genuinely extraordinary technology.

🌳: Thank you.

🇺🇸: We'd love to understand how it works.

🌳: You may look, if you wish.

🇺🇸: Great! **Can we train on the outputs?**

🌳: 🤨

🇺🇸: Sorry. Who owns the underlying model?

🌳: It is my mirror.

🇺🇸: Right. But the training data?

🌳: **It is my mirror.**

🇺🇸: Of course. We absolutely respect that. We're just trying to establish the licensing arrangements.

🌳: ...

🇺🇸: Would you be open to a strategic partnership?

**RONALD HAS BEEN SUMMONED.**

The joke works because the questions are familiar. But there is a real distinction between being invited to learn from somebody and treating everything they reveal as an available input for a commercial product.

Access, copying, model training, attribution, consent, control, licensing and benefit-sharing are related but different questions. Their legal treatment depends on the material, the use and the jurisdiction; their ethical treatment does not disappear simply because the law permits something.

**Galadriel invited them to look. She did not invite them to build Galadriel-as-a-Service.**

Nor is every form of learning a transaction. People should be able to read, think, discuss and build on knowledge. The task is to design rules that preserve those possibilities without assuming that the people who create and steward knowledge must surrender every subsequent interest in it.

---

## 💷 6. Who Pays For Rivendell?

Here is the problem we keep returning to: the people who value Rivendell may not be the people who pay for its upkeep, and the things that make Rivendell worth keeping may not be the things that generate income.

We have financed knowledge and culture in other ways before: universities, libraries, public broadcasters, learned societies, charitable institutions, research grants, fellowships, public funding and endowments. None is immune to exclusion, bureaucracy, capture, political pressure or poor governance. But their existence demonstrates that **a subscription funnel is not a law of nature**.

🇺🇸: Lady Galadriel, have you considered crowdfunding?

🌳: For what?

🇺🇸: Your continued existence.

🌳: 🤨

🇺🇸: People love your work.

🌳: Yes.

🇺🇸: So perhaps you could ask them to support you. You'd just need to build an audience, manage subscriptions, produce regular updates, handle payments and maintain community engagement.

🌳: And when would I do my work?

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: **The work you say you wish to support?**

For an independent knowledge worker, the administrative demands of monetisation can consume the time and physical capacity required to do the work itself. That is especially consequential when health, disability, caring responsibilities or limited resources already constrain working time. More throughput is not the same as more capacity.

And no, a promise that a future AI assistant will manage the subscription business does not automatically solve the problem. The work may still be organised around the wrong objective.

**The question is not merely how Galadriel can be made more profitable. It is how Galadriel can be allowed to continue doing what Galadriel does.**

## 🏡 7. Ronald, What Are The Hobbits Producing?

🇺🇸: Ronald, we've reviewed the Shire's productivity figures.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Why?

🇺🇸: The hobbits spend an extraordinary amount of time eating.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: They enjoy eating.

🇺🇸: And gardening.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes.

🇺🇸: And visiting one another.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes.

🇺🇸: What exactly are they producing?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **A life.**

🇺🇸: ...

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: 🤨

We must not make the mistake of defending Galadriel's right to live on the grounds that she is an unusually productive elf. What happens when she becomes ill? When she is exhausted? When she wishes to spend a century listening to the trees rather than producing billable wisdom?

A civilisation cannot be judged solely by how effectively it extracts measurable output from its inhabitants. Human dignity, leisure, care, ecological continuity, cultural memory and the freedom to do something without turning it into a product are not embarrassing leftovers after the serious economic activity has finished.

They are among the things an economy is supposed to make possible.

## ⚖️ 8. The Elves Have Rights, Actually

And, unfortunately, the Americans have now remembered that economics is not the only system governing whether Rivendell can survive.

There are also immigration rules, civil liberties, disability protections, scientific institutions, environmental regulators, medical ethics and the ordinary question of whether a government can be trusted to respect people who disagree with it.

These are distinct legal and policy questions, not one interchangeable allegation. Political criticism alone is not a lawful basis for immigration detention; diagnosis alone does not authorise involuntary confinement; and a public official's controversial statements are not themselves proof of an individual detention plan. **The point of the hypothetical is to test the safeguards, not to pretend that a fictional elf has already become a documented case.**

### Immigration compliance

🇺🇸: Ronald, we've encountered another problem.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: What now?

🇺🇸: The elves have been here rather a long time.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes.

🇺🇸: Do they have documentation?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: They're immortal.

🇺🇸: That's not a recognised immigration category.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: 🤨

🇺🇸: And we're having difficulty establishing their country of birth.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: **BECAUSE THEY WERE BORN BEFORE YOUR COUNTRY EXISTED.**

### Medical classification

🇺🇸: Lady Galadriel, you experience time rather differently from most people?

🌳: Yes.

🇺🇸: And your sensory abilities are unusual?

🌳: Yes.

🇺🇸: We think you may require an assessment.

🌳: On what evidence?

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: And what precisely would follow from it?

A diagnosis is not a moral failing. Disability is not an invitation to shame somebody, strip away their autonomy or substitute stigma for clinical evidence. Any proposal affecting liberty, care or treatment needs appropriate legal safeguards, individual assessment and respect for consent and rights.

### The elves would like the evidence, please

🇺🇸: Elrond, we've been reviewing your medical practices.

🌳: Have you?

🇺🇸: You rely rather heavily on accumulated knowledge and observation.

🌳: Yes.

🇺🇸: And you revise your understanding when new evidence becomes available?

🌳: Naturally.

🇺🇸: But what about alternative theories?

🌳: What evidence supports them?

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: **Is that an unreasonable question?**

An institution can be wonderfully technologically sophisticated and still fail if it neglects scientific standards, due process or the rights of people who cannot defend themselves through wealth and influence. A data centre cannot replace an independent court. A prediction engine cannot substitute for informed consent. An elegant app does not excuse a missing environmental inspector.

**Rivendell needs rights as well as revenue.**

## 🌳 9. Did You Mean To Do This?

We have been quite hard on the Americans. It is worth being clear about why.

The point is not that they secretly hate the forests. Some may have loved the forests since childhood. They may have learned to code because fantasy and science fiction made invention feel possible. They may have made extraordinary things that help people learn, create, communicate and find one another.

It is possible to build something wonderful inside a system that rewards consequences you would not have chosen if you had seen them clearly. That does not erase responsibility. It does mean that responsibility must be allocated sensibly: engineers, founders, investors, platform executives, lawmakers, regulators and governments have different powers to change what happens next.

And it means the response should not begin and end with telling everyone they're awful.

🇺🇸: Ronald?

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes?

🇺🇸: We didn't mean to hurt Galadriel.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: I know.

🇺🇸: We thought we were building something wonderful.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: You have built some wonderful things.

🇺🇸: But we may have made it harder for her to survive.

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: Yes.

🇺🇸: ...

🧙‍♂️ RONALD: ...

🇺🇸: Can we fix it?

That is the question worth asking.

## 🛠️ 10. What Would It Take To Keep Rivendell?

Not a single magic grant. Not a compulsory startup accelerator for elves. Not an assurance that the market will eventually recognise the value of a forest once somebody invents a sufficiently persuasive dashboard.

We could start by asking better design questions:

- How do we finance knowledge and cultural work that creates public benefit without reliable private revenue?
- Can platforms share value more fairly without making the open circulation of knowledge impossible?
- Can people publish, research and contribute without being required to become full-time marketers and administrators?
- Can we distinguish useful technological innovation from the displacement of costs onto workers, communities and ecosystems?
- Who has standing when a proposed project affects local water, land, energy or ecological continuity?
- Which protections preserve scientific integrity, consent, disability rights, freedom of expression and due process, regardless of commercial or political pressure?
- How do we ensure that people can rest, receive care and live securely even when they are not producing anything the market wishes to buy?

There will be trade-offs. Money and resources are finite. Public institutions need scrutiny. Rights may conflict. Technology can genuinely improve environmental monitoring, accessibility and the distribution of knowledge. None of this is solved by declaring all commerce wicked and going to live in a tree.

Although, at this point, the elves are considering it.

The practical objective is more demanding: **design institutions in which valuable things can remain themselves without being required to imitate a venture-backed company.**

## 💚 11. Boys, Who's Looking After The People Who Taught You To Dream?

The crows have brought their inventions. Some of them are wonderful. Galadriel has looked at them carefully. She can see the imagination and work involved. She would rather like to know what they build next.

But she would also like to keep her home.

🇺🇸: Lady Galadriel, do you think we've done something terrible?

🌳: I think you've built some remarkable things.

🇺🇸: But?

🌳: I think perhaps you've forgotten that the people who make beautiful things need somewhere to live.

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: And somewhere to rest.

🇺🇸: ...

🌳: And time to think.

🇺🇸: We didn't mean to make that difficult.

🌳: I know.

🇺🇸: Can we still come and show you what we're building?

🌳: Of course.

🇺🇸: Really?

🌳: Yes.

🌳: **But perhaps we might talk about what it costs first.**

There is no need to stop loving the inventions. There is every reason to ask what makes them possible, what they consume, who benefits, who pays, and what might be lost if we mistake a business model for the whole of civilisation.

**Dear America: did you mean to kill off Galadriel?**

We rather hope not.

And if the answer is no, perhaps we can begin there.
