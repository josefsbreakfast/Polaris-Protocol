# 🐳 See Our New Deterrence Capability
**First created:** 2026-09-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-17  
*A wholly unserious British deterrence proposal for when ordinary burden-sharing has failed and America must instead assume custody of one (1) Tony Blair.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

There comes a point in every special relationship when one party has to ask whether the burdens are, in fact, being shared.

Britain has heard a great deal about deterrence, interoperability, strategic depth, burden-sharing, resilience and the importance of maintaining credible capabilities.

Fine.

We have developed one.

It does not require a submarine. It does not require a warhead. It does not require anybody to press a button.

It requires a crate.

```text
UNITED STATES
      ↓
does something which causes
Britain an intolerable quantity
of administrative nonsense
      ↓
UNITED KINGDOM
      ↓
reviews escalation ladder
      ↓
decides against conventional retaliation
      ↓
      📦
ONE (1) TONY BLAIR
      ↓
transatlantic transfer
```

This is not a threat.

It is a gift.

**HERE. HAVE HIM. YOU WILL LOVE HIM.**

## 🐳 Deterrence Through Custody

Traditional deterrence tries to make another state believe that the cost of an action will exceed its benefit.

This system works differently.

The relevant uncertainty is not:

> What will Britain do?

It is:

> **Will Britain make Tony our administrative responsibility?**

The ambiguity matters.

```text
Pentagon:
"Is that a threat?"

Britain:
"Deterrence works best when nobody has to find out."

Pentagon:
"But are you actually—"

Britain:
"HERE. HAVE HIM."

Pentagon:
"We haven't agreed to—"

Britain:
"YOU WILL HAVE HIM."
```

The receiving state must now calculate not only the probability of transfer, but the potentially indefinite downstream requirement for panels, institutes, strategic visions, keynote appearances and conversations beginning with the phrase *historic opportunity*.

This is deterrence through **unwanted institutional inheritance**.

## 🧠 What The Joke Is Actually Doing

Underneath the Tony delivery mechanism sits a recognisable feedback problem.

Powerful states frequently describe international relationships through the language of mutual obligation while the costs generated by policy, technology, security choices and institutional dependencies can travel unevenly.

The joke reverses the direction of travel.

```text
YOU EXPORT
political / technological /
security externalities
        ↓
WE ACQUIRE
consequences
        ↓
VERY WELL
        ↓
WE HAVE AN EXPORT TOO
        ↓
        🎪
   ┌─────────┐
   │  TONY   │
   │  🪙 → 💬 │
   └─────────┘
```

The object being exported is deliberately absurd. That is the point.

Instead of imagining the UK–US relationship only through grand strategy, military hardware and polished diplomatic language, the joke asks what **burden-sharing looks like when the burden is institutional inheritance**: people, habits, policy fashions, advisory ecosystems, assumptions about power and recurring ideas that cross borders long after anybody remembers ordering them.

The deterrent therefore succeeds when America asks:

> “Is this a threat?”

and Britain can truthfully reply:

> “No. We said he was a gift.”

## 🎩 Putting On Less Ritz

The Ritz version of the special relationship is flags, podiums, state visits, historic friendship and solemn declarations about shared values.

The less-Ritz version asks:

```text
WHAT MOVES?
      ↓
people
ideas
money
technology
institutional habits
policy assumptions
security dependencies
advisory networks
      ↓
WHO ABSORBS THE CONSEQUENCES?
```

The Tony Blair Zoltar Machine is not an answer to that question.

It is what happens when Britain gets tired and begins proposing **strategic burden-sharing by pet adoption**.

No actual foreign-policy doctrine should be constructed around giving former prime ministers to other countries.
