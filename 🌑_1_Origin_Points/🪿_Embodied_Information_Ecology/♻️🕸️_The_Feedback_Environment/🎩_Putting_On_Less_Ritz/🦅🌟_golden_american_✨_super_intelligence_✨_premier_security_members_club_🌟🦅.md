## 3. 🎒 This Is The Duke Of Edinburgh Award For Corporations

The ten stars are **not interchangeable points**.

This matters.

A company cannot accumulate ten units of goodness by doing the same convenient thing ten times.

The conceptual model is closer to the Duke of Edinburgh Award: achievement requires activity across different categories. You cannot announce that you were extremely good at volunteering and have therefore decided not to bother with the expedition.

## 4. 🎯 Stable Domains, Moving Priorities

The broad categories do not need to be reinvented every year.

The **focus** does.

Each assessment cycle should identify where the evidence indicates that AI-related activity is currently producing the largest, fastest-growing, most concentrated, or most neglected harms.

That annual process might identify:

- particularly affected populations;
- particular identities or groups experiencing concentrated harms;
- particular harm mechanisms appearing across many populations;
- rapidly emerging technical risks;
- unusually large disparities;
- neglected or poorly measured problems;
- systemic failures appearing across multiple companies;
- new consequences created by changes in capability or deployment.

The eventual method should probably use a hybrid of **who is being affected** and **what is happening to them**.

Why?

Because identity-only analysis can miss mechanisms that cut across groups.

Harm-only analysis can flatten the fact that some populations are bearing much more of the damage.

So the annual process asks both:

> **Where is the harm?**

and:

> **Who is carrying it?**

The result becomes an annual **American Excellence Priority Set**.

Companies do not get to select their favourite five priorities from a catalogue of things they already do well.

America identifies what currently needs attention.

Companies choose how brilliantly they respond.

## 12. 🚫 Corporate Philanthropy Bullshit Simulator

Prosocial reinvestment cannot become:

# CORPORATE PHILANTHROPY BULLSHIT SIMULATOR 2027

The following should not automatically qualify:

- ordinary legal compliance;
- ordinary operating expenditure;
- advertising;
- sponsorship primarily designed to market the company;
- naming-rights vanity projects;
- circular payments to controlled entities;
- purchasing services from your own subsidiary and calling it community investment;
- programmes whose principal measurable beneficiary is the company itself;
- activity already fully counted towards another advanced award without additionality;
- commitments with no evidence that money actually moved;
- announcements;
- vibes.

Qualifying reinvestment therefore needs standards around:

- **additionality** — would this have happened anyway?
- **materiality** — is the commitment meaningful relative to the company and claimed achievement?
- **independence** — who verifies the work?
- **beneficiary relevance** — does the intervention respond to an evidenced need?
- **community participation** — where appropriate, did affected people have meaningful influence?
- **conflicts of interest** — who benefits financially?
- **related-party transactions** — where did the money actually go?
- **outcomes** — what happened after the announcement?
- **durability** — did the intervention persist long enough to matter?
- **transparency** — can the public inspect the evidence?

You cannot buy a cape.

You can fund qualifying work.

You can demonstrate what happened.

The accreditation recognises the verified result.

This distinction is the entire fucking point.

## 23. 🧩 Anti-Gaming Rules

The moment this system exists, highly paid people will begin attempting to optimise it.

That is fine.

The objective is to make the easiest route to optimisation:

**do the useful thing.**

The architecture therefore needs explicit protection against:

### Star farming

Repeatedly performing the cheapest activity in one domain cannot generate endless credit.

### Double counting

The same expenditure or achievement cannot be claimed across multiple categories unless the framework explicitly permits proportionate allocation.

### Corporate-group laundering

Moving an activity between subsidiaries cannot make the same underlying work newly qualifying.

### Philanthropic substitution

Prosocial investment cannot excuse failure to meet ordinary legal or regulatory duties.

### Evidence laundering

A glossy external report is not independent verification merely because somebody outside the company received money to write it.

### Acquisition laundering

Buying a company with an advanced accreditation does not automatically transfer its cape to the purchaser.

**You cannot acquire another company and inherit its cape.**

Absolutely not.

The accreditation belongs to the verified institutional performance represented by the evidence.

### Regulatory cosplay

A company cannot create a private badge with a confusingly similar eagle, give itself ten stars, and hope everybody gets bored before reading the small print.

Official marks need controlled issuance and machine-verifiable provenance.

### Strategic neglect

Excellent performance in several domains cannot indefinitely compensate for severe failure in another mandatory domain.

No:

> three accessibility stars therefore one complimentary bribery.

The system is not a Tesco Clubcard for misconduct.

## 24. 🛠️ Failure, Recovery, And Not Teaching Companies To Hide Problems

A serious accreditation system must avoid creating incentives to conceal incidents.

If any reported failure automatically destroys advanced status, companies may learn that the safest route to keeping the shiny thing is not telling anyone what happened.

That would be catastrophic mechanism design.

The framework therefore needs to distinguish between:

- the occurrence of an incident;
- institutional negligence;
- reasonable prevention;
- speed of detection;
- quality of disclosure;
- cooperation with investigation;
- remediation;
- treatment of affected people;
- recurrence;
- concealment;
- retaliation;
- falsification.

A company which identifies a problem, reports it promptly, protects affected people, cooperates, fixes the underlying mechanism, shares useful learning, and prevents recurrence may demonstrate something valuable about institutional resilience.

A company which hides the same problem until somebody else discovers it has demonstrated something else.

Advanced accreditation should therefore reward **good sensing and honest correction**, not fantasy perfection.

The goal is not:

> never admit anything went wrong.

The goal is:

> build institutions capable of detecting, reporting, correcting, and learning before the same failure becomes normal.

There may eventually be prestige achievements associated with exceptional recovery or sector-wide learning.

Not because failure is glamorous.

Because **institutional learning is a capability**.

## 25. 🌍 International Interoperability

The eagle is American.

The underlying assurance does not have to be.

Other jurisdictions do not need to copy America's exact visual vocabulary to recognise equivalent verified achievements.

International interoperability can operate beneath the presentation layer.

Countries or regulatory blocs might mutually recognise defined elements such as:

- assessment criteria;
- assurance levels;
- verification standards;
- evidence requirements;
- continuity periods;
- remediation status;
- incident-reporting quality;
- advanced prosocial achievements;
- interoperability or security marks.

America can display:

**✨🌟🌟🌟🌟🌟🦅🌟🌟🌟🌟🌟✨**

Another country can use its own symbol.

Britain will presumably make something that looks like it was designed by a committee in 1987 and then, after six months, quietly introduce a surprisingly excellent technical standard underneath it.

The EU will produce an extremely polished mark accompanied by 146 pages of specifications.

Japan will inevitably make the accreditation object look better than everybody else's.

The underlying machine-readable assurance can still map across systems.

The nerds internationally will understand this.

That is, frankly, one of the stronger interoperability assumptions available to us.
