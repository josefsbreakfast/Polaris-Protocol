# ✌️ Thanks for the Robit, Don

**First created:** 2026-09-30 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-30\
*An historical-irony node about information control, artificial
intelligence, the economics of the haystack, and the profound
inconvenience of eventually giving the public better tools for reading
the archive.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🛰️ Orientation

First:

Donald Trump did **not** invent artificial intelligence.

He did not cause machine learning.

He did not personally cause large language models.

The United States did not develop AI merely because one orange man
became pathologically preoccupied with hostile media, leaks, "fake
news", political narratives and people saying things he did not like.

That would be an absurd causal history.

Unfortunately, there is a much funnier and more defensible version.

During Trump's first presidency, the United States explicitly elevated
artificial intelligence as an economic and national-security priority.

The administration's **American AI Initiative**, established by
Executive Order 13859 in February 2019, directed a coordinated federal
strategy around AI research, data and computing resources, standards,
workforce development and international engagement.

The order itself described continued US leadership in AI as important to
both economic and national security.

At the same time, American defence research was already treating the
modern information environment as a computational problem.

DARPA's Media Forensics programme sought automated ways to assess
whether images and video had been manipulated.

Its successor, Semantic Forensics, expanded the problem into detecting,
attributing and characterising falsified multimodal media and
large-scale automated disinformation.

None of this means:

``` text
TRUMP GOT PARANOID
      ↓
AMERICA INVENTED ROBIT
```

But it does give us the historical irony:

``` text
STATE + INDUSTRY
BECOME EXTREMELY INTERESTED IN
AI, INFORMATION, AUTHENTICITY,
DISINFORMATION, ATTRIBUTION,
SCALE AND AUTOMATED ANALYSIS

                ↓

several years pass

                ↓

🤖

PUBLIC:
oh excellent

we can use this to read
your fucking paperwork
```

So:

> **Thanks for the Robit, Don.**

## 🍊 1. The Man Was Very Interested In The Information Environment

Trump's political rise occurred inside an extraordinary struggle over
information.

The period surrounding the 2016 election and his first presidency
included arguments about:

-   "fake news";
-   leaks;
-   social media;
-   platform power;
-   foreign influence;
-   election interference;
-   disinformation;
-   manipulated media;
-   intelligence assessments;
-   anonymous sources;
-   government secrecy;
-   press legitimacy;
-   online political mobilisation;
-   and who could establish what was real.

Those phenomena had many causes and many actors.

They should not be collapsed into:

``` text
TRUMP DID IT
```

But Trump became one of the central political objects around which these
arguments organised themselves.

The information environment was no longer merely where politics
happened.

The information environment became a major subject of politics.

## 🤖 2. Meanwhile: America Discovers Robit

On 11 February 2019, Trump signed Executive Order 13859:

> **Maintaining American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence.**

The American AI Initiative treated AI as strategically important to:

-   economic competitiveness;
-   national security;
-   scientific discovery;
-   federal research and development;
-   technical standards;
-   workforce development;
-   access to federal data and computing resources;
-   public trust;
-   and international competition.

NIST was directed to develop a plan for federal engagement in AI
technical standards.

The administration later described five principal lines of effort:

``` text
AI research investment
federal AI computing + data
technical standards
AI workforce
international engagement
```

So no:

> **Donald Trump invented ChatGPT because he hated being criticised.**

But yes:

> **The Trump administration formally helped institutionalise AI as a
> strategic federal priority.**

Thank you for the Robit, Don.

## 🛰️ 3. This Was Bigger Than Trump

The important correction is that US government interest in computational
information environments predates Trump.

DARPA's **Social Media in Strategic Communication** programme had
already been studying:

-   social networks;
-   patterns of information flow;
-   misinformation;
-   deception campaigns;
-   linguistic cues;
-   sentiment;
-   opinion;
-   and how ideas propagate through social media.

DARPA's **Media Forensics** programme began in 2016.

Its aim was to develop automated methods for detecting manipulation in
digital imagery and video.

Then came **Semantic Forensics**.

By 2019--20, the research problem explicitly included:

``` text
detect
attribute
characterise
```

falsified multimodal media.

The programme aimed to help analysts reason about:

``` text
WHAT is manipulated?
WHO produced it?
WHY?
HOW?
```

This matters for Polaris because those are not merely "AI questions".

They are **information-forensics questions**.

## 🧠 4. The State Had Discovered The Scale Problem

Humans are very good at reading one document.

Humans are less good at:

``` text
3,000,000 documents
```

especially when the relevant relationship is:

``` text
page 43 of document A
        ↕
paragraph 7 of document B
        ↕
a different spelling of the same name
        ↕
an interview conducted six years earlier
        ↕
a court exhibit filed four administrations later
```

Modern states generate enormous archives.

Modern journalism generates enormous archives.

Modern courts generate enormous archives.

Modern digital communication generates enormous archives.

The obstacle increasingly becomes:

> **Can anybody hold enough of the information environment in working
> memory to notice the relationship?**

This is where Robit becomes irritatingly useful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌾 5. The Economics Of The Haystack

Historically, obscurity did not always require destruction.

Imagine:

``` text
NEEDLE
```

inside:

``` text
HAYSTACK
```

If the haystack contains ten pieces of hay, somebody finds the needle.

If it contains ten million:

``` text
GOOD LUCK, BABES
```

The information can technically be public while remaining practically
obscure.

That gives us an important distinction:

``` text
LEGAL ACCESSIBILITY
        ≠
PRACTICAL LEGIBILITY
```

A court record may be public.

A congressional report may be public.

A FOIA production may be public.

A government archive may be public.

But if understanding the story requires one human to:

-   find all of them;
-   download all of them;
-   read all of them;
-   remember all names;
-   reconcile all dates;
-   recognise aliases;
-   identify duplicated documents;
-   notice changed institutional terminology;
-   reconstruct administrative transfers;
-   distinguish allegations from findings;
-   and retain the whole structure for ten years,

then:

``` text
PUBLIC
```

does not necessarily mean:

``` text
KNOWABLE
```

## 🤖 6. Robit Changes The Cost Of Looking

Machine-assisted research changes some of those economics.

A machine can help:

-   search large corpora;
-   extract dates;
-   identify names;
-   normalise entities;
-   compare versions;
-   find repeated phrases;
-   identify apparent contradictions;
-   build timelines;
-   classify documents;
-   map institutional relationships;
-   generate candidate joins;
-   preserve provenance;
-   keep track of unresolved questions;
-   and repeatedly return to old material when new information appears.

The human remains responsible for:

-   deciding what matters;
-   checking sources;
-   evaluating evidence;
-   understanding context;
-   recognising legal distinctions;
-   detecting bullshit;
-   deciding whether two things really belong together;
-   and refusing to let a statistical relationship become a factual
    claim merely because a machine noticed it.

So the useful architecture is:

``` text
🤖
SCALE + RETRIEVAL + RELATIONAL MEMORY

             ↕

🧠
CONTEXT + JUDGMENT + EVIDENCE + MEANING
```

Not:

``` text
ASK ROBIT WHO DID CRIME
```

Robit does not get to decide who did crime.

Robit gets to say:

> **Oi. These two documents appear to be talking about the same
> meeting.**

Then we investigate.

## 🧷 7. Robit Is Particularly Good At Being Annoying About Join Keys

Suppose one archive says:

``` text
Interview conducted 14 March
```

Another says:

``` text
302 prepared 17 March
```

Another says:

``` text
information received from source three days earlier
```

Another contains:

``` text
same unusual phrase
same agent
same field office
```

A human researcher may never encounter those documents in the same week.

Machine-assisted research can keep the candidate relationship alive.

Useful join keys include:

-   dates;
-   docket numbers;
-   Bates numbers;
-   file numbers;
-   names;
-   aliases;
-   email addresses;
-   job titles;
-   agency offices;
-   investigators;
-   prosecutors;
-   classification markings;
-   distinctive phrases;
-   attachment names;
-   timestamps;
-   locations;
-   document metadata.

The machine does not establish the relationship.

It makes the relationship **cheap enough to check**.

## 🔔 8. New Information Can Wake Old Information Up

This is where the node rejoins the Feedback Environment.

From the Savile problem:

> **New information should be able to wake old information up.**

AI-assisted research makes that substantially easier.

Imagine a document read in 2018.

Nothing obvious.

Then a document appears in 2026 containing a new name.

Robit can ask:

``` text
WHERE HAVE WE SEEN THIS BEFORE?
```

And suddenly:

``` text
2018 DOCUMENT
      ↓
WAKE UP
```

The meaning of an old information object can change because the
surrounding information environment changed.

That is cybernetic memory.

## ✌️ 10. The Joke Is Not That AI Knows The Answer

The joke is that AI can help reconstruct the question.

That distinction matters enormously.

Bad use:

``` text
USER:
was there a giant conspiracy?

🤖:
yes probably here is a thrilling narrative
```

No.

Good use:

``` text
USER:
this institutional behaviour looks incoherent

🤖:
here are the 47 relevant records
here are their dates
here are the agencies
here are the contradictions
here are the ordinary explanations
here are the missing handoffs
here are the comparators we still need
```

Then:

``` text
HUMAN:
oh for fuck's sake
```

But now it is an **informed** oh for fuck's sake.

## 🪞 11. AI Can Also Manufacture A Much Better Conspiracy Theory

This deserves a warning.

The same capabilities that help reconstruct real information
environments can produce magnificent nonsense.

Give a language model:

``` text
10,000 documents
+
instruction:
PROVE THEY COVERED IT UP
```

and the machine has an enormous search space from which to construct
something persuasive.

Coincidences become joins.

Ambiguities become clues.

Missing records become evidence.

Ordinary bureaucratic language becomes sinister.

Contradictions become proof.

Absence becomes proof.

Presence becomes proof.

Everything becomes proof.

Therefore Polaris needs the opposite instruction:

``` text
DO NOT PROVE HYPOTHESIS

MAP INFORMATION ENVIRONMENT
```

Maintain competing explanations.

Preserve evidence status.

Search for disconfirmation.

Require comparators.

Distinguish:

``` text
CONNECTED
```

from:

``` text
CORRELATED
```

from:

``` text
SIMILAR
```

from:

``` text
COINCIDENT
```

from:

``` text
WE DO NOT KNOW
```

Robit requires epistemic manners.

## 🧪 12. The Anti-Paranoia Protocol

For machine-assisted historical reconstruction:

### Step 1 --- State the hypothesis

Precisely.

### Step 2 --- Generate competing hypotheses

Including boring ones.

Especially boring ones.

### Step 3 --- Derive predictions

What evidence should exist if each hypothesis is true?

### Step 4 --- Search by information object

Not merely by narrative keyword.

### Step 5 --- Preserve provenance

Every factual claim needs an origin.

### Step 6 --- Preserve evidence status

``` text
allegation
testimony
intelligence
documentary evidence
official finding
judicial finding
conviction
disputed interpretation
unknown
```

### Step 7 --- Find comparators

Was the behaviour actually unusual?

### Step 8 --- Search for disconfirmation

What would make our preferred explanation weaker?

### Step 9 --- Record unresolved contradictions

Do not force them closed.

### Step 10 --- Let the human remain responsible

Robit is research infrastructure.

Not oracle.

## 🏛️ 13. This Is Also A Democratic-Scale Problem

Modern transparency regimes produce astonishing quantities of material.

That is good.

But disclosure at enormous scale creates a second-order problem:

``` text
HOW DOES THE PUBLIC READ IT?
```

A democratic right to records is weakened if only:

-   governments;
-   intelligence services;
-   enormous corporations;
-   specialist litigation teams;
-   major newsrooms;
-   and wealthy research institutions

possess the computational resources required to make those records
legible.

Accessible machine analysis changes that balance.

Potentially.

It can give:

``` text
small newsroom
academic
survivor
researcher
activist
member of public
```

capabilities previously requiring a research team.

That does not make the machine correct.

It changes who can afford to ask complicated questions.

## 📚 14. The Archive Starts Behaving Differently

Once machine analysis becomes cheap enough, the archive is no longer
passive storage.

It becomes queryable relational memory.

Instead of:

``` text
HERE ARE 500 BOXES
```

we can ask:

``` text
show every appearance of this person

show every document transferred between these offices

show every reference to this case number

show every occasion the stated reason for withholding changed

show every document created before 20 January 2017
but reviewed after it

show every later document that cites an earlier interview

show every contradiction in stated custody

show every place where a person is described under
different evidential categories

show me what would disconfirm this hypothesis
```

The haystack has become searchable by relationships.

That is a major epistemic change.

## 🌾 15. You Do Not Need To Destroy The Needle If Nobody Can Read The Haystack

This proposition needs care.

It is **not** a claim that governments deliberately create enormous
disclosures to conceal particular records.

Sometimes large archives are large because reality is large.

Sometimes disclosure is large because transparency law worked.

Sometimes duplication is just bureaucracy.

Sometimes bad indexing is just bad indexing.

But as a general information principle:

> **Information abundance can create practical obscurity even without
> deliberate concealment.**

Therefore:

``` text
MORE INFORMATION
```

does not automatically produce:

``` text
MORE KNOWLEDGE
```

The missing operation is:

``` text
RELATIONAL PROCESSING
```

Robit helps supply it.

## 🧠 16. This Is Why Context Windows Matter Politically

A human reading one document asks:

> **What does this say?**

A relational research system can additionally ask:

> **What does this change?**

That is much closer to the real information problem.

Because:

``` text
DOCUMENT A
```

may mean little alone.

Then:

``` text
DOCUMENT B
```

appears.

Now A changes meaning.

Then:

``` text
DOCUMENT C
```

reveals that B was sent to another agency.

Then:

``` text
DOCUMENT D
```

reveals that agency had already possessed A.

The research object is no longer the document.

It is:

``` text
A ↔ B ↔ C ↔ D
```

That is the information ecology.

## 🍊 17. So Where Does Don Actually Fit?

Carefully.

Trump's first administration did not create the underlying AI research
trajectory.

DARPA information-analysis programmes predated his presidency.

Commercial machine learning had its own enormous scientific and economic
history.

The technologies underlying modern language models emerged from a global
research ecosystem involving universities, private companies,
governments and researchers across many countries.

But Trump did preside over a period when the United States increasingly
treated AI as strategic infrastructure.

His 2019 executive order formally established the American AI
Initiative.

The National AI Initiative Act of 2020 subsequently codified major
elements of federal AI strategy.

And the political period surrounding him intensified public and
institutional concern about information authenticity, manipulation,
platforms, foreign influence and digital political communication.

So the joke survives contact with the evidence if we phrase it properly:

> **Donald Trump did not give us AI.**

He did, however, occupy the Presidency while the United States was
accelerating its institutional commitment to AI and computational
responses to a profoundly contested information environment.

And now:

``` text
WE HAVE ROBIT
```

## 🦆 18. Unintended Affordances Are Very Funny

Technology rarely remains confined to the purpose imagined by the
institutions funding it.

The internet.

Encryption.

Satellite imagery.

Open-source intelligence.

Digital archives.

Search engines.

Machine translation.

Large language models.

Tools developed for:

``` text
commerce
defence
administration
competition
productivity
```

can acquire entirely different civic uses.

The important concept is:

> **Unintended affordance.**

A system capable of analysing enormous heterogeneous information
environments can also be used by people trying to understand the
institutions that produced those environments.

That is not technological destiny.

It is an affordance.

And, occasionally, a punchline.

## 📰 19. Robit Does Not Replace Journalists

Quite the reverse.

The better Robit gets at retrieval and synthesis, the more important
human investigative judgment becomes.

Journalists still need to:

-   obtain records;
-   authenticate documents;
-   cultivate sources;
-   understand institutions;
-   challenge official explanations;
-   protect vulnerable people;
-   understand legal context;
-   distinguish allegation from evidence;
-   interview humans;
-   notice what was never written down;
-   and publish responsibly.

Robit can help a journalist notice:

``` text
THIS DOESN'T MATCH THAT
```

It cannot safely conclude:

``` text
THEREFORE CRIME
```

The ideal relationship is:

``` text
🤖:
weird

📰:
how weird?

🤖:
here are the records

📰:
compared with what?

🤖:
excellent question
```

## 🔎 20. Questions Journalists Should Ask About Machine-Readable Government

As governments release larger archives:

-   Are records released in machine-readable form?
-   Are original metadata preserved?
-   Are stable document identifiers supplied?
-   Are duplicate documents identified without deleting provenance?
-   Are redaction codes machine-readable?
-   Are page numbers stable?
-   Are OCR layers accurate?
-   Can datasets be downloaded in bulk?
-   Are indexes published?
-   Are previous versions preserved?
-   Are corrections documented?
-   Are agency transfers recorded?
-   Can document lineage be reconstructed?
-   Are disclosure APIs available?
-   Are scanned images unnecessarily preventing computational analysis?

A government can technically disclose information while making
computational analysis unnecessarily difficult.

That does not prove intent.

It is nevertheless a transparency-design problem.

## 🧷 21. Ask For Better Haystacks

The democratic response should not merely be:

``` text
BUILD BIGGER AI
```

It should also be:

``` text
BUILD BETTER ARCHIVES
```

Public records should, where lawful and safe, have:

-   stable identifiers;
-   provenance;
-   creation dates;
-   modification dates;
-   custodial history;
-   document relationships;
-   machine-readable text;
-   structured redaction reasons;
-   version histories;
-   source agency;
-   transfer history;
-   and clear evidential status where appropriate.

Then both humans and machines can audit the information environment.

The objective is not:

``` text
AI KNOWS EVERYTHING
```

It is:

``` text
PUBLIC RECORDS BECOME
MORE LEGIBLE TO THE PUBLIC
```

## 🪁 22. New Information Should Wake Old Information Up --- Automatically, But Not Decide It

Imagine a public archive with governed relational tooling.

Newly released document arrives.

System identifies:

``` text
same case number
same witness
same event date
same official
same document quoted elsewhere
```

It alerts researchers:

> **This may alter the context of six older records.**

Perfect.

What it must **not** say is:

> **This proves the six older records were fraudulent.**

Wake-up:

yes.

Verdict:

human evidence process.

## 🧯 23. Robit Needs To Show Its Work

Machine-assisted accountability becomes dangerous if it creates another
black box.

For every important synthesis, we need:

``` text
SOURCE
DATE
QUOTE / FACT
DOCUMENT LOCATION
INFERENCE
CONFIDENCE
ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION
```

The ideal Robit output is not:

> **Trust me. I read three million pages.**

That is simply replacing:

``` text
TRUST THE INSTITUTION
```

with:

``` text
TRUST THE MACHINE
```

No thank you.

The machine should make the archive **more inspectable**, not become a
new priesthood standing between the human and the archive.

## ✌️ 25. Thanks For The Robit, Don

So, no.

We are not giving Donald Trump credit for artificial intelligence.

Steady on.

The title means something narrower and much more annoying.

During a political period saturated with disputes about information,
authenticity, leaks, disinformation, foreign influence and narrative
control, the United States also accelerated the institutional treatment
of artificial intelligence as strategically important infrastructure.

The resulting technological ecosystem now includes tools capable of
helping humans analyse archives at a scale that previously made
meaningful public reconstruction brutally expensive.

So if the historical problem turns out to involve:

``` text
ten years
multiple administrations
millions of pages
several courts
several agencies
changing classifications
changing custodians
```

## 🧿 26. What This Node Does Not Claim

This node does not claim that Donald Trump caused the development of
artificial intelligence.

It does not claim that US AI research was created to suppress criticism
of Trump.

It does not claim that DARPA programmes were personal political
projects.

It does not claim that government investment in AI establishes
wrongdoing by any political actor.

It does not claim that machine-generated relationships between records
are evidence of criminal conduct.

It does not claim that large archives are deliberately designed to
conceal information.

It does not claim that AI can determine historical truth autonomously.

It makes a narrower observation:

> **The same technological transition that made information environments
> vastly more complex also produced tools increasingly capable of
> reconstructing those environments.**

And there is some historical irony in who occupied the White House while
the United States formally accelerated its strategic AI programme.
