# 😶‍🌫️ Why Would A Fascism Hide The Root Cause?

**First created:** 2026-09-30 \| **Last updated:** 2026-09-30\
*A Feedback Environment node about causal substitution: why an
authoritarian or fascist information system may become better at
identifying enemies who justify its preferred interventions than causes
that would require the system to change itself.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🛰️ Orientation

This node begins with a slightly different question from:

> **Why would fascists lie?**

That is too easy.

Propaganda lies.

Governments lie.

Political movements lie.

People lie.

The more interesting systems question is:

> **If a political system is organised around authoritarian or fascist
> responses to social disorder, what happens when an accurate
> explanation of that disorder would implicate the institutions,
> hierarchies, myths or distributions of power the system is trying to
> preserve?**

Does it:

``` text
identify root cause
      ↓
change system
```

Or does the information environment become better at producing:

``` text
alternative cause
      ↓
compatible intervention
```

?

This node calls that:

# **causal substitution**

The harm can remain visible.

The explanation moves.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧭 1. First: What Do We Mean By "A Fascism"?

Not:

``` text
THING I DISLIKE
=
FASCISM
```

The term has a history and should retain analytical meaning.

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum describes fascism as a
far-right political philosophy or movement that places the nation, often
defined in exclusive racial or ethnic terms, above the individual;
opposes pluralist democracy and liberal individual rights; and commonly
embraces authoritarian leadership, national rebirth, exclusion and
violence against designated enemies.

Historical fascist movements were not identical.

Italian Fascism and German Nazism were not interchangeable.

Nor should every authoritarian government be collapsed into fascism.

The formulation:

> **a fascism**

is therefore deliberate.

We are examining a **systemic tendency** that can be tested against
historical fascist cases and against movements displaying particular
fascistic mechanisms.

We are not using "fascist" as punctuation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧩 2. The Root-Cause Problem

Imagine a society experiencing:

``` text
economic insecurity
violence
social fragmentation
institutional distrust
crime
sexual violence
public-health failure
declining services
political instability
```

There are many possible causal explanations.

Some may implicate:

-   economic structure;
-   state policy;
-   institutional failure;
-   elite impunity;
-   corruption;
-   poor regulation;
-   weak safeguarding;
-   labour conditions;
-   inadequate healthcare;
-   policing failures;
-   family structures;
-   powerful organisations;
-   inequality;
-   discrimination;
-   or failures accumulated over decades.

Those explanations can be politically expensive.

Because they may imply:

``` text
redistribute resources
regulate powerful actors
investigate respected institutions
limit elite discretion
strengthen independent oversight
protect marginalised people
improve welfare provision
accept institutional responsibility
change policy
change ourselves
```

An authoritarian political project may instead prefer explanations
producing interventions such as:

``` text
punish
exclude
deport
silence
police
censor
detain
purify
discipline
restore hierarchy
```

The explanatory environment is therefore not politically neutral.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🪞 3. Historical Fascism Did Not Merely Conceal Problems

The Nazi case gives us a very clear historical mechanism.

Germany after the First World War and during the Weimar Republic
experienced genuine crises:

-   political instability;
-   severe economic disruption;
-   unemployment;
-   social conflict;
-   the consequences of war;
-   the Great Depression;
-   anxiety about changing social and sexual norms;
-   and declining confidence in democratic institutions.

Those problems were real.

The Nazi explanation was not.

Nazi ideology falsely blamed Jews for an extraordinary range of
Germany's political, social, economic and cultural problems.

It blamed Jews for Germany's defeat in the First World War.

It associated Jews simultaneously with capitalism and communism.

It depicted Jews as conspiratorial outsiders undermining the national
community.

The propaganda therefore performed an informational operation:

``` text
REAL SOCIAL DISTRESS
        ↓
FALSE CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION
        ↓
DESIGNATED ENEMY
```

The regime did not need to persuade everybody that the distress itself
was imaginary.

It could acknowledge distress and **reassign its cause**.

That is more useful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 😶‍🌫️ 4. Causal Substitution

Define:

> **Causal substitution occurs when a system redirects attention from
> mechanisms producing a problem toward an alternative explanation that
> better supports the system's preferred distribution of power or
> preferred intervention.**

In simplified form:

``` text
ROOT CAUSE
    ↓
SOCIAL HARM
    ↓
PUBLIC DISTRESS
```

becomes:

``` text
PUBLIC DISTRESS
    ↓
DESIGNATED ENEMY
    ↓
AUTHORITARIAN SOLUTION
```

The middle of the causal chain disappears.

Or, more precisely:

``` text
it gets foggy
```

😶‍🌫️

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🏛️ 5. Why Would The System Prefer The Wrong Cause?

Because explanations contain implied interventions.

Suppose explanation A is:

``` text
weak institutions permit powerful people
to act with impunity
```

Possible intervention:

``` text
strengthen oversight
investigate powerful people
redistribute institutional power
```

Explanation B:

``` text
dangerous outsiders are corrupting society
```

Possible intervention:

``` text
exclude outsiders
increase policing
increase executive power
```

If your political project already wants:

``` text
more hierarchy
more policing
less pluralism
more exclusion
more executive discretion
```

then explanation B is extraordinarily convenient.

The causal model legitimises the intervention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🔄 6. The Intervention Can Then Validate The Explanation

This produces a feedback loop.

``` text
SOCIAL PROBLEM
      ↓
enemy blamed
      ↓
authoritarian intervention
      ↓
enemy increasingly visible as political category
      ↓
state measures conflict with enemy
      ↓
conflict becomes evidence enemy is dangerous
      ↓
more intervention
```

The intervention generates observations which appear to validate the
model that justified the intervention.

This is epistemically dangerous.

Because the system begins manufacturing its own confirming evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🚨 7. "Restoring Order" Is An Important Phrase

Historical Nazi propaganda repeatedly presented the regime as defending
or restoring order against supposed enemies.

The US Holocaust Memorial Museum notes that propaganda campaigns helped
create acceptance of anti-Jewish measures by depicting the Nazi
government as stepping in to restore public order.

That is an important mechanism.

The political sequence becomes:

``` text
disorder
   ↓
enemy
   ↓
emergency
   ↓
extraordinary power
   ↓
order
```

But if the enemy did not cause the original disorder, the intervention
cannot solve the root cause.

So:

``` text
ROOT CAUSE
still there
```

The resulting persistence of social harm can then justify:

``` text
MORE AUTHORITARIANISM
```

because the designated enemy has apparently not yet been sufficiently
controlled.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🕳️ 8. The Root Cause Can Become Politically Dangerous Information

Suppose an authoritarian movement says:

> **The nation would flourish if we could remove the corrupting
> outsiders.**

Now imagine evidence shows:

``` text
the harm is being generated
inside respectable institutions
```

That information does more than challenge one policy.

It challenges the explanatory architecture.

Because suddenly:

``` text
OUTSIDE THREAT
```

becomes:

``` text
INTERNAL SYSTEM FAILURE
```

And:

``` text
PURITY
```

becomes:

``` text
ACCOUNTABILITY
```

And:

``` text
REMOVE THEM
```

becomes:

``` text
CHANGE US
```

That is a much more threatening informational transition for a politics
organised around hierarchy, purity and external enemies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧒 9. CSA Is An Especially Difficult Root-Cause Problem

Child sexual abuse is particularly hostile to simplistic enemy models.

Why?

Because abuse can occur through ordinary social structures.

The relevant environments can include:

-   families;
-   schools;
-   religious organisations;
-   children's homes;
-   sports;
-   healthcare;
-   entertainment;
-   political organisations;
-   youth organisations;
-   workplaces;
-   policing failures;
-   local government;
-   care systems;
-   and informal networks of trust.

The risk is not reducible to:

``` text
STRANGER
```

or:

``` text
OUTSIDER
```

or:

``` text
DEVIANT POPULATION OVER THERE
```

Safeguarding often requires examining:

``` text
access
trust
authority
dependency
institutional reputation
professional status
family power
adult credibility
child credibility
information sharing
reporting pathways
institutional incentives
```

That is structurally awkward for political models that prefer danger to
arrive from outside the trusted social order.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🏠 10. The Respectability Problem

Imagine the desired political picture:

``` text
GOOD FAMILY
GOOD CHURCH
GOOD SCHOOL
GOOD COMMUNITY
GOOD POLICE
GOOD MEN
BAD OUTSIDER
```

Now introduce safeguarding evidence showing that harm can occur inside:

``` text
family
church
school
community
police
respected professions
```

The information does not mean those institutions are inherently abusive.

It means:

> **Trusted institutions require safeguarding precisely because trust
> and authority can create access and suppress challenge.**

That is a very different model of social safety.

Safety comes not from identifying a pure in-group.

It comes from:

``` text
accountability
feedback
independent reporting
distributed scrutiny
survivor credibility
records
institutional memory
limits on power
```

That is almost the opposite informational architecture.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 👾 11. The Monster Is Politically Useful

A society understandably wants sexual violence to be monstrous.

But there are two meanings of monster.

### Monster as moral description

Some acts are horrifying.

Fine.

### Monster as social category

``` text
THE KIND OF PERSON
WHO DOES THIS
IS OBVIOUSLY DIFFERENT
FROM NORMAL PEOPLE
```

Danger.

Because if the perpetrator must look like:

``` text
MONSTER
```

then:

``` text
respectable
successful
charitable
married
religious
professional
patriotic
popular
institutionally trusted
```

can become false negative indicators.

The more politically invested a system becomes in respectable hierarchy,
the harder it may become to process evidence that harm is being
generated within respectable hierarchy.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🎯 12. Scapegoating Is A Causal Technology

Scapegoating is not merely:

``` text
BE MEAN TO MINORITY
```

It performs explanatory work.

It says:

``` text
WHY IS THIS HAPPENING?

THEM.
```

That answer is computationally cheap.

It compresses:

``` text
economics
history
institutions
policy
culture
power
feedback
material conditions
```

into:

``` text
ENEMY
```

It is therefore a kind of **causal compression**.

Terrible model.

Excellent slogan.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧠 13. Fascistic Information Environments Reward Low-Complexity Explanations

Complex systems produce uncomfortable answers.

They say:

``` text
several things are true
```

or:

``` text
different mechanisms interact
```

or:

``` text
this policy helped here and harmed there
```

or:

``` text
we do not yet know
```

or:

``` text
our own institution contributed
```

Authoritarian political communication tends to benefit from much cleaner
structures:

``` text
US
vs
THEM

ORDER
vs
CHAOS

PURE
vs
CORRUPT

LOYAL
vs
TRAITOR

STRONG
vs
WEAK
```

This is not simply rhetorical style.

It affects which causal models survive political selection.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🪜 14. The Preferred Explanation Produces The Preferred Tool

If your preferred state tool is:

``` text
POLICE
```

then problems become legible as:

``` text
CRIME
```

If your preferred tool is:

``` text
BORDER
```

problems become:

``` text
FOREIGNER
```

If your preferred tool is:

``` text
CENSORSHIP
```

problems become:

``` text
DANGEROUS SPEECH
```

If your preferred tool is:

``` text
EXECUTIVE POWER
```

problems become:

``` text
EMERGENCY
```

This is the political version of:

> **When you have a hammer, everything starts looking like a nail.**

Except the hammer has a propaganda department.

## 🪿 15. The Feedback Environment Can Select The Wrong Explanation Without A Meeting

This is important.

We do not require:

``` text
SECRET ROOM

FASCISTS:
let us conceal root cause
```

Instead:

``` text
politician proposes outsider explanation
        ↓
fits ideology
        ↓
fits existing prejudice
        ↓
produces emotionally clear story
        ↓
supports preferred intervention
        ↓
receives media attention
        ↓
political base rewards it
        ↓
institutions collect data around intervention
        ↓
more information about designated enemy
        ↓
enemy becomes increasingly measurable
        ↓
alternative structural causes remain poorly measured
```

Eventually:

> **The system has more evidence about the thing it chose to look at.**

That does not mean the chosen thing caused the problem.

It means observation followed power.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📊 16. Measurement Can Harden The Causal Substitution

Suppose the state believes group X causes problem Y.

It increases surveillance of X.

Now the state possesses:

``` text
MORE DATA ABOUT X
```

It detects more offences involving X.

Then:

``` text
LOOK
MORE DATA
```

But:

``` text
MORE OBSERVATION
```

is not automatically:

``` text
MORE UNDERLYING INCIDENCE
```

This is why denominators, detection rates, reporting rates, enforcement
intensity and comparator populations matter.

A feedback environment can make its target look increasingly causally
important simply by making the target increasingly observable.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🔦 17. Searchlight Bias

Call this:

> **Searchlight bias.**

``` text
STATE POINTS SEARCHLIGHT HERE
            ↓
FINDS THINGS HERE
            ↓
CONCLUDES HERE IS WHERE THINGS ARE
            ↓
POINTS MORE SEARCHLIGHT HERE
```

Meanwhile:

``` text
DARK AREA
```

remains poorly measured.

The dark area can then appear comparatively safe.

Not because it is.

Because nobody brought the fucking torch.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📰 18. Journalism Can Accidentally Inherit The Searchlight

Journalists often depend on institutional outputs:

-   arrests;
-   prosecutions;
-   police statistics;
-   government reports;
-   official briefings;
-   court proceedings;
-   parliamentary statements;
-   leaked investigations.

If state attention is systematically concentrated on one population or
explanation, journalism can inherit that sampling frame.

Then:

``` text
STATE OBSERVES X
      ↓
MEDIA REPORTS X
      ↓
PUBLIC ASSOCIATES PROBLEM WITH X
      ↓
POLITICIANS DEMAND MORE ATTENTION TO X
      ↓
STATE OBSERVES MORE X
```

A closed informational loop.

Journalism therefore has to ask:

> **What would this dataset look like if institutional attention were
> distributed differently?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧷 19. Questions Journalists Should Ask

Whenever a political actor attributes a complex social harm to a
designated group:

-   What exactly is the claimed causal mechanism?
-   What evidence establishes causation rather than correlation?
-   What is the denominator?
-   How does detection intensity differ between populations?
-   How does reporting differ?
-   How does policing differ?
-   What equivalent harms occur outside the targeted group?
-   What data are not collected?
-   What institutions are absent from the dataset?
-   What changed before the claimed cause appeared?
-   Does the proposed intervention address the causal mechanism?
-   What would we expect to observe if the explanation were wrong?
-   Who gains power under the proposed solution?
-   Which institutions avoid scrutiny if this explanation dominates?
-   What alternative explanations have been tested?
-   What evidence would falsify the government's preferred explanation?

And especially:

> **What is happening outside the searchlight?**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧒 20. Questions Journalists Should Ask About CSA Narratives

When CSA is attached to a demographic, cultural, religious, ethnic,
migrant or political category:

-   What proportion of total CSA is represented by the phenomenon being
    discussed?
-   What definition of CSA is being used?
-   Are recorded offences being confused with prevalence?
-   Are group-based cases more likely to receive dedicated
    investigations?
-   Are equivalent networks in other populations classified differently?
-   What proportion occurs within families or trusted relationships?
-   What institutional access enabled offending?
-   Which safeguarding failures recur across demographic groups?
-   Which warning signs were missed?
-   Which institutions received reports?
-   Which institutions failed to connect information?
-   Did professional or social respectability suppress suspicion?
-   Did the offender's status affect credibility judgments?
-   Are politicians discussing perpetrator identity more than access
    mechanisms?
-   Does the proposed policy improve safeguarding for children outside
    the politically salient category?
-   What happens to the explanatory model when the perpetrator belongs
    to the preferred in-group?

That final question is particularly useful.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🔄 21. Root Causes Threaten Scapegoats

Suppose careful research finds:

``` text
access
+
authority
+
weak reporting
+
institutional reputation management
+
poor information sharing
+
victim disbelief
```

predict harm across multiple settings.

That model is politically inconvenient for scapegoating because it
travels.

It applies to:

``` text
THEM
```

and:

``` text
US
```

It therefore replaces identity policing with systems engineering.

Instead of:

``` text
WHO ARE THE BAD PEOPLE?
```

it asks:

``` text
WHAT ENVIRONMENT LETS BAD ACTS
CONTINUE UNDETECTED?
```

That is a much more powerful safeguarding question.

And a much less convenient propaganda question.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🏗️ 22. Root-Cause Analysis Redistributes Responsibility

Scapegoating concentrates responsibility.

``` text
THEY DID IT
```

Systems analysis distributes responsibility across:

``` text
perpetrator
institution
regulator
records system
reporting pathway
leadership
professional culture
resource allocation
law
oversight
```

This does **not** dilute perpetrator responsibility.

The offender remains responsible for offending.

It asks an additional question:

> **Why was the offender able to continue?**

That second question often implicates power.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 😶‍🌫️ 23. Fog Can Be Produced Without Falsehood

A system does not always need to say something false.

It can produce fog through:

``` text
selective emphasis
```

``` text
category choice
```

``` text
missing denominator
```

``` text
repeated anecdote
```

``` text
different naming conventions
```

``` text
selective enforcement
```

``` text
selective publication
```

``` text
administrative fragmentation
```

``` text
measurement choices
```

``` text
silence about comparators
```

Every individual statement may be technically true.

The resulting causal picture may still be badly distorted.

That is why fact-checking individual sentences is necessary but
insufficient.

We also need:

> **model-checking**

What causal model is the collection of true statements teaching?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧠 24. Manufactured Causality Does Not Require A Manufacturer

Polaris already has:

> **Manufactured ignorance does not require a manufacturer.**

Add:

> **Manufactured causality does not require a manufacturer.**

If institutions repeatedly:

-   observe the same populations;
-   publish the same categories;
-   reward the same explanations;
-   ignore the same comparators;
-   and fund the same interventions,

the resulting information environment can converge on a causal story
nobody centrally designed.

That story can still be wrong.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🪞 25. And Sometimes It Is Deliberate

Do not let systems language erase agency.

Historical fascist propaganda was not merely an accidental emergent
property.

The Nazi regime deliberately controlled media and propaganda.

It deliberately defined enemies.

It deliberately promoted false antisemitic causal explanations.

It deliberately used propaganda to create acceptance of persecution.

It deliberately concealed atrocities.

The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum documents Nazi efforts to
deceive both German and international publics about political aims and
mass murder, including portraying Germany as the victim of Jewish and
Allied aggression and disguising genocidal policies as "resettlement".

So we need both models:

``` text
EMERGENT CAUSAL DISTORTION
```

and:

``` text
DELIBERATE PROPAGANDA
```

They can coexist.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧪 26. How Do We Distinguish Them?

Look for mechanism.

### Emergent distortion predicts:

-   inconsistent explanations;
-   local incentives;
-   measurement artefacts;
-   jurisdictional fragmentation;
-   different actors reproducing similar biases without common
    instructions;
-   path dependency;
-   institutional inertia.

### Deliberate causal substitution predicts:

-   messaging instructions;
-   coordinated terminology;
-   central propaganda strategy;
-   deliberate suppression of contrary evidence;
-   knowingly false claims;
-   targeting decisions;
-   policy documents identifying propaganda goals;
-   strategic repetition across channels.

### Mixed systems predict:

``` text
central narrative
        +
decentralised institutions
that learn what narrative is rewarded
```

That may be the most powerful arrangement of all.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧯 27. Do Not Reverse-Engineer Every Bad Policy Into Fascism

Important.

Bad causal analysis is common in democracies.

Scapegoating is older than fascism.

Governments of many ideological traditions protect institutions.

Police over-observe some populations.

Media create moral panics.

Bureaucracies measure what is easy to measure.

Politicians prefer simple stories.

None of those observations alone establishes fascism.

The relevant question is whether they sit inside a broader political
architecture involving features such as:

-   anti-pluralism;
-   authoritarian leadership;
-   exclusionary nationalism;
-   designated internal enemies;
-   systematic attacks on democratic constraints;
-   political violence or its legitimation;
-   suppression of independent institutions;
-   and a politics of national purification or rebirth.

Do not let the label replace the analysis.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🔍 28. The Root-Cause Test

For any movement claiming:

``` text
WE WILL RESTORE ORDER
```

ask:

### 1. What disorder?

Define it.

### 2. What caused it?

Show evidence.

### 3. What alternative causes were tested?

Not merely mentioned.

### 4. What does the proposed intervention act upon?

Cause or symbol?

### 5. What happens if the intervention succeeds?

Should the measured harm decline?

### 6. Does it?

If not:

### 7. Does the movement revise its causal model?

Or demand:

``` text
MORE
```

of the same intervention?

That final step is revealing.

A system capable of learning should update.

A system organised around an enemy may instead conclude:

> **We have not fought the enemy hard enough.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## ♻️ 29. The Cybernetic Difference

A healthy feedback system says:

``` text
MODEL
  ↓
INTERVENTION
  ↓
OBSERVATION
  ↓
MODEL UPDATE
```

A closed ideological system says:

``` text
MODEL
  ↓
INTERVENTION
  ↓
FAILURE
  ↓
ENEMY MORE POWERFUL THAN EXPECTED
  ↓
SAME MODEL, HARDER
```

That is anti-learning.

The feedback channel exists.

The system refuses the correction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🌫️ 30. Why Hide The Root Cause?

So, finally:

Why would a fascism hide the root cause?

Sometimes because leaders consciously know the truth would undermine
their programme.

Sometimes because propaganda requires an enemy.

Sometimes because accurate causation would implicate powerful
institutions.

Sometimes because the real solution requires redistribution, regulation,
accountability or institutional reform rather than coercion.

Sometimes because an external enemy is easier to mobilise against than
an internal system failure.

Sometimes because the preferred political tool selects the explanation.

Sometimes because measurement follows enforcement.

Sometimes because institutions learn what information their superiors
reward.

Sometimes because everybody keeps looking where the light already is.

And sometimes the system may not know it is hiding anything at all.

It has simply become extremely good at seeing:

``` text
THE CAUSES
IT KNOWS HOW TO PUNISH
```

and extremely bad at seeing:

``` text
THE CAUSES
THAT WOULD REQUIRE IT
TO CHANGE ITSELF
```

😶‍🌫️

That is the root-cause problem.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧿 31. What This Node Does Not Claim

This node does not claim that every authoritarian policy is fascist.

It does not claim that every instance of scapegoating is fascism.

It does not claim that every institutional failure is deliberate.

It does not claim that governments never correctly identify demographic
patterns relevant to policy.

It does not claim that group characteristics can never be analytically
relevant.

It does not claim that every social harm has one simple "root cause".

It does not claim that systems analysis removes individual
responsibility.

It does not use Nazi Germany as a direct equivalence for contemporary
states.

It uses documented historical fascist mechanisms to construct testable
questions about:

``` text
causal attribution
enemy construction
measurement
institutional incentives
propaganda
feedback
and power
```

The point is not:

> **Everything is fascism.**

The point is:

> **If an information environment repeatedly explains social harm
> through enemies whose punishment strengthens the existing hierarchy,
> while explanations implicating that hierarchy remain persistently
> under-observed, ask why.**
