# 🍓 Austen Cybernetics 202: Wingman Anne
**First created:** 2026-09-21 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-21  
*Sometimes the thing you are trying to detect changes the behaviour of the system so effectively that your sensor appears to report the opposite.*
---
## 🧭 Orientation
Welcome back to *Persuasion*.
In 201, we established the basic systems failure:
- Anne Elliot and Captain Wentworth loved each other;
- Anne broke their engagement;
- eight years passed;
- Wentworth returned successful, wealthy, attractive and still extremely fucking annoyed;
- Anne remained in love with him;
- neither had reliable access to the other's internal state;
- both therefore acted according to models assembled from incomplete information.
Now we get to make the problem worse.
Because Anne does not merely fail to tell Wentworth she loves him.
Her love for Wentworth is one of the reasons she behaves in ways that Wentworth can interpret as evidence that she **doesn't**.
This is:
# The Wingman Anne Problem.
---
## 🪽 1. Wingman Anne
The quickest version of the plot:
> Anne Elliot has become the most legendary fucking wingman Captain Wentworth has ever had because she still loves him and has mistakenly concluded that the kindest thing she can do is help him marry somebody else.
This sounds ridiculous.
It is also an excellent model of a difficult information problem.
Anne observes:
- Wentworth has returned;
- Wentworth appears angry or distant towards her;
- Wentworth is receiving attention from younger women;
- Wentworth appears particularly interested in Louisa Musgrove;
- Anne was the person who rejected him;
- Anne therefore does not consider herself entitled simply to march back into his emotional life.
Anne's resulting model is roughly:
```text
Wentworth no longer wants me.
+
Wentworth appears to want Louisa.
+
I still love Wentworth.
+
I want Wentworth to be happy.
+
I do not want to interfere with his choices.
=
SUPPORT WENTWORTH'S APPARENT CHOICE.

🫡

Wingman Anne has received the mission.

Unfortunately:

the mission is not real.

⸻

📡 2. Latent State Is Not Observable State

Suppose we want to know:

Does Anne love Wentworth?

The actual state is:

ANNE_LOVES_WENTWORTH = extremely yes

But Wentworth cannot inspect that variable directly.

He has access only to outputs:

* what Anne says;
* where Anne goes;
* how Anne behaves around him;
* whether Anne seeks him out;
* whether Anne appears jealous;
* how Anne responds to Louisa;
* what Anne allows herself to communicate.

This distinction matters well beyond Austen.

A system can possess an internal state that is only imperfectly represented by its observable outputs.

The mistake is to assume:

observable signal = underlying state

when the actual relationship may be:

underlying state
        ↓
other internal variables
        ↓
environmental constraints
        ↓
control strategy
        ↓
observable signal

Anne’s behaviour is not generated by love alone.

It is generated by:

love + history + guilt + judgement + social convention + uncertainty + respect for Wentworth’s apparent autonomy.

⸻

🔇 3. The Signal Disappears Because It Matters

Here is the particularly beautiful problem.

If Anne cared less about Wentworth, she might have fewer reasons to respect what she believes are his wishes.

But because she loves him:

she does not want to interfere with the happiness she thinks he has found elsewhere.

Therefore:

strong attachment
        ↓
greater concern for Wentworth's wishes
        ↓
greater restraint
        ↓
weaker observable romantic pursuit

The variable Wentworth wants to detect is participating in the process that suppresses its own signal.

That means:

Signal absence is not necessarily state absence.

And sometimes:

A stronger underlying state can generate a weaker observable signal.

This is one reason simple inference from behaviour is dangerous.

⸻

⚓ 4. Meanwhile, Captain Wentworth Has Had An Idea

Wentworth is not passively observing this system.

That would already be difficult enough.

He is acting inside it.

And Wentworth has unresolved feelings, wounded pride and several attractive young women enthusiastically paying attention to him.

He therefore becomes conspicuously friendly with Louisa.

There are several things happening here, and we should resist reducing every Wentworth–Louisa interaction to a deliberate laboratory experiment on Anne.

But from the perspective of the Anne–Wentworth information system, the result is disastrous.

Wentworth produces behaviour that Anne can reasonably interpret as:

CAPTAIN_WENTWORTH_ROMANTIC_PREFERENCE = Louisa

Anne updates.

This is the crucial transition.

Wentworth’s behaviour is not merely evidence about the system.

It is an input into the system.

⸻

♻️ 5. Congratulations, You Have Created Feedback

Anne now acts according to the new information.

Wentworth appears interested in Louisa
        ↓
Anne concludes Wentworth may want Louisa
        ↓
Anne decides not to interfere
        ↓
Anne supports/facilitates the apparent trajectory
        ↓
Anne displays less jealousy and romantic pursuit

Wentworth can then observe Anne’s behaviour.

And what does that behaviour look like?

Potentially:

Anne seems perfectly willing for me to be with Louisa.

Oh no.

So:

Wentworth still cares about Anne
        ↓
Wentworth behaves warmly toward Louisa
        ↓
Anne infers genuine Wentworth → Louisa interest
        ↓
Anne suppresses Anne → Wentworth signal
        ↓
Anne facilitates apparent Wentworth → Louisa trajectory
        ↓
Wentworth observes Anne's apparent lack of objection
        ↓
Wentworth receives weaker evidence that Anne loves him

SIR.

You have interacted with the sensor.

⸻

🪞 6. Second-Order Observation

This is where the 200 series moves beyond:

Person A misunderstands Person B.

Anne is not merely modelling Wentworth.

She is modelling Wentworth’s model of Anne.

She knows:

* she rejected him;
* he remembers that;
* he may believe she did not love him sufficiently;
* he may resent her;
* he may consider their relationship finished.

Wentworth is also modelling Anne’s model of Wentworth.

He can ask:

* Does Anne regret rejecting me?
* Does she recognise what I became?
* Does she still care?
* What does she think about Louisa?
* What does she think I think about her?

We now have:

Anne
↓
Anne's model of Wentworth
↓
Anne's model of Wentworth's model of Anne

and:

Wentworth
↓
Wentworth's model of Anne
↓
Wentworth's model of Anne's model of Wentworth

This is why human social systems become computationally annoying very quickly.

Everyone is running simulations of everyone else’s simulations.

And nobody has root access.

⸻

🫡 7. Anne Is Not Behaving Irrationally

This point matters.

It would be easy to describe Anne’s behaviour as absurd because the audience knows more than Anne does.

But Anne is not randomly producing nonsense.

Given her information, her behaviour has a coherent logic.

She believes:

1. she rejected Wentworth;
2. she hurt him;
3. she has no automatic claim upon him eight years later;
4. he appears interested in Louisa;
5. Louisa appears interested in him;
6. Wentworth deserves to choose his own happiness;
7. Anne’s own continuing love does not entitle her to obstruct that choice.

Therefore:

Wingman Anne.

The decision process can be coherent while the situational picture is wrong.

That distinction matters enormously.

If we only inspect the decision:

Why the fuck are you helping the man you love get another woman?

Anne looks ridiculous.

If we inspect the model generating the decision:

Oh.

The behaviour becomes legible.

⸻

🗺️ 8. Shared Situational Picture: Fucked

There is a useful distinction here between:

* individual reasoning quality;
* information quality;
* shared situational awareness.

Anne can reason competently from bad information.

Wentworth can reason competently from bad information.

Both can therefore produce locally sensible actions which make the shared environment worse.

This is one reason distributed systems cannot be evaluated merely by asking:

Was each individual actor behaving rationally?

Sometimes:

yes.

And the collective result is still:

absolutely fucking disastrous.

The system-level question becomes:

What information was available to each participant when they acted, and how did their actions subsequently alter the information available to everybody else?

⸻

🪽 9. Legendary Fucking Wingman

The joke deserves its own analytical moment.

Anne does not sabotage Louisa.

She does not treat Louisa as an enemy simply because Louisa appears to be receiving something Anne desperately wants.

Instead, Anne’s loyalty to Wentworth generalises into concern for the person she believes Wentworth prefers.

And when the situation becomes genuinely dangerous at the Cobb, this becomes even clearer.

Whatever Anne privately feels about Wentworth:

Louisa is hurt.

The romantic competition model becomes irrelevant.

Human being requires help.

Anne helps.

This tells us something important about Anne’s objective function.

She is not optimising:

MAXIMISE_ANNE_ACCESS_TO_WENTWORTH

Her behaviour is much closer to:

protect people I care about
+
respect other people's autonomy
+
behave according to my judgement
+
do not make my private desire everybody else's administrative problem

Which is admirable.

It is also, under current conditions, catastrophic romantic telemetry.

🚨 10. The Cobb Changes The Information Environment

The full Cobb analysis belongs in 203 and 204.

For 202, we only need one consequence.

Before the accident, Wentworth has mostly been interpreting Anne through a romantic-social environment.

Then the system experiences a serious perturbation.

Louisa is badly hurt.

Anne responds.

Wentworth receives a class of information about Anne that his Louisa strategy was never designed to generate.

Her:

* judgement;
* steadiness;
* usefulness;
* care for Louisa;
* ability to respond rather than merely perform;

become much harder to fit inside his old Anne model.

And there is a particularly devastating piece of information embedded in all of this:

Anne is helping Louisa even though Anne believes Louisa may become Wentworth’s wife.

Captain.

Anne has basically been the most legendary fucking wingman for you, bruv.

Perhaps we should revisit the constancy assessment.

⸻

🧪 11. Sensor Error Or Interpretation Error?

This gives us another useful distinction.

Sometimes a sensor is faulty.

It reports something inaccurately.

But sometimes the sensor is functioning perfectly and the observer has misunderstood what the reading means.

Wentworth sees Anne:

* not competing;
* not declaring herself;
* not interfering;
* helping Louisa.

Those observations are not necessarily false.

The inference is the problem.

He can read:

Anne does not interfere
=
Anne does not care

when the actual causal chain is closer to:

Anne cares enormously
        ↓
Anne believes Wentworth wants somebody else
        ↓
Anne respects Wentworth's autonomy
        ↓
Anne does not interfere

Captain Wentworth does not necessarily need a better sensor.

He needs a better model of what the sensor output means.

⸻

🔁 12. Self-Confirming Systems

Now we can name the nastier problem.

Suppose Wentworth begins with:

Anne does not care enough.

He behaves accordingly.

His behaviour makes Anne believe he wants somebody else.

Anne withdraws further.

Wentworth observes her withdrawal.

That observation appears compatible with:

Anne does not care enough.

The model has helped produce the evidence that appears to confirm the model.

That does not mean every belief automatically creates reality.

It means that in interactive systems, an observer’s assumptions can influence the behaviour subsequently being observed.

This is especially dangerous when nobody notices that the observation process is itself part of the causal chain.

⸻

💌 13. The Missing Channel

Why does the Harville conversation matter so much later?

Because Wentworth finally receives information that is difficult to reconcile with his existing model.

Anne discusses constancy.

Wentworth hears her.

Suddenly:

* restraint can be reinterpreted;
* apparent acceptance can be reinterpreted;
* Wingman Anne can be reinterpreted;
* eight years of behaviour can be reconsidered under a different causal model.

The historical observations do not necessarily change.

Their meaning changes.

That is an important information lesson:

New information can update not merely our model of the present but our interpretation of previously observed data.

Wentworth suddenly has to rerun the logs.

Oh.

OH.

THAT’S WHAT SHE WAS DOING.

Aggressive quill noises commence.

⸻

🧠 14. What Wingman Anne Teaches Us

1. Internal state is not directly observable

Behaviour is produced through multiple interacting variables.

2. Signal absence is not state absence

A state may exist without producing the output an observer expects.

3. Strong states can sometimes suppress their own expected signals

Anne’s love contributes to her restraint.

4. Observers are participants

Wentworth’s behaviour changes Anne’s behaviour.

5. Feedback can distort inference

Anne’s response becomes new evidence for Wentworth.

6. Locally rational behaviour can produce globally ridiculous outcomes

Neither participant needs to be stupid for the system to fail.

7. Interpretation requires a causal model

Observing behaviour is not enough.

We need some understanding of what could have generated it.

8. New information can alter old information

The event remains the same.

Its interpretation changes.

⸻

🍓 15. The 202 Problem Set

When observing another person, institution or system:

* What state are you actually trying to infer?
* Can you observe it directly?
* Which outputs are you using as proxies?
* What else could generate those outputs?
* Does the underlying state itself alter how strongly it is expressed?
* Has your own behaviour changed the system you are observing?
* Is the system responding to you rather than merely revealing itself to you?
* Are participants modelling one another’s models?
* Could several individually coherent decisions be creating a collectively incoherent result?
* What evidence would genuinely falsify your current model?
* If new information arrived, would you reinterpret old observations differently?

And, crucially:

Have you accidentally created Wingman Anne?
