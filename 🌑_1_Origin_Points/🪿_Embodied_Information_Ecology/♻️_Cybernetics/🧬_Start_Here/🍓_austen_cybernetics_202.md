# 🍓 Austen Cybernetics 202: Wingman Anne

**First created:** 2026-09-21 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-21 *Latent state, observable signal, observer effects, and the catastrophic consequences of becoming an extremely effective wingman for the man you love.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Sometimes the thing you are trying to detect changes the behaviour of the system so effectively that your sensor appears to report the opposite.

This is the **Wingman Anne Problem**:

> **A system can respond perfectly to the state it has inferred while
> being completely wrong about the state that actually exists.**

Anne still loves Wentworth.

Wentworth still loves Anne.

This should be straightforward.

It is not.

## 🧮 Latent state is not observable state

Anne's internal state is approximately:

```text
STILL CATASTROPHICALLY IN LOVE WITH CAPTAIN WENTWORTH
```

Wentworth cannot directly inspect that state.

He sees outputs:

- restraint;
- politeness;
- apparent acceptance of his attention to Louisa;
- lack of overt jealousy;
- practical assistance.

The mistake would be to treat:

```text
observable behaviour
=
internal state
```

Behaviour is generated by internal state **plus** beliefs about the environment, constraints, goals and social rules.

Anne's love is one of the reasons her love becomes difficult to observe.

## 🕸️ Wingman Anne

Anne believes Wentworth wants Louisa.

Anne also believes she has forfeited any right to interfere.

Therefore:

```text
Anne loves Wentworth
        ↓
Anne thinks Wentworth wants Louisa
        ↓
Anne wants Wentworth to be happy
        ↓
Anne suppresses her own claim
        ↓
Anne helps the apparent courtship proceed
```

Captain Wentworth sees this and may reasonably infer:

```text
Anne is helping me with Louisa
        ↓
Anne does not care
```

No.

**Anne has become such an effective wingman that she is actively degrading the observability of her own thirst.**

## 📡 The signal disappears because it matters

This is the important bit.

Anne is not failing to emit the expected signal despite loving Wentworth.

Her love helps cause the behaviour that suppresses the signal.

Formally:

> The variable under observation contributes causally to a control
> strategy which suppresses the output being used as its proxy.

Polaris:

> **Anne has basically been the most legendary fucking wingman for you,
> bruv.**

## 🪞 Meanwhile, Wentworth is also affecting the sensor

Wentworth is not passively observing Anne.

He behaves.

He flirts with Louisa.

He praises firmness.

He performs being over Anne with a level of commitment normally associated with a naval campaign.

Anne observes those outputs and updates her own model:

> He wants Louisa.

Then she behaves accordingly.

Wentworth:

> *flirts with Louisa to demonstrate how extremely over Anne he is*

Wingman Anne:

> **I understand the mission. 🫡**

Wentworth:

> wait no

Wingman Anne:

> **Pretty young woman identified. Social obstacles being cleared.**

Captain.

You interacted with the fucking sensor.

This is more than a simple interpretation error.

Wentworth does not merely observe Anne's behaviour and assign the wrong meaning to it. His own attempt to produce evidence that he is over Anne changes Anne's model of the situation. Anne then adapts to the state he has performed.

Wentworth observes that adaptation as though it were independent evidence about Anne's original state.

It is not.

```text
Wentworth wants Anne
        ↓
Wentworth performs NOT WANTING ANNE
        ↓
Wentworth flirts with Louisa
        ↓
Anne observes apparent interest
        ↓
Anne updates:
"Frederick wants Louisa"
        ↓
Anne #helps
        ↓
Wentworth observes Anne helping
        ↓
Wentworth updates:
"Anne does not want me"
        ↓
WRONG, CAPTAIN
```

> **Captain Spreadsheet has accidentally A/B tested Anne's generosity
> and interpreted the successful result as rejection.**

The technical payload underneath the joke matters:

> **The observer has interacted with the sensor. The resulting behaviour
> is not independent evidence of the system's prior state.**

Wentworth has put his thumb on the scale and then treated the reading as though the scale had been untouched.

Or, in Wingman Anne:

> **Captain. You interacted with the fucking sensor. 🫡**

## 🧪 The successful test is not the result you think it is

The experiment, insofar as Wentworth has accidentally constructed one, does reveal something real about Anne.

Unfortunately, it is not the variable he thinks he has measured.

His implicit test is approximately:

```text
IF I appear interested in another woman
AND Anne loves me
THEN Anne should display jealousy / obstruction / distress visibly enough
for me to detect it.
```

Anne fails that predicted output.

Wentworth can therefore read the result as:

```text
no detectable possessiveness
        ↓
no love
```

But Anne's actual control policy is closer to:

```text
IF Frederick wants another woman
AND I love Frederick
THEN I should not obstruct his happiness.
```

So the stronger Anne's generosity, restraint and concern for Wentworth's welfare are, the more successfully she can produce the very output he has classified as evidence against love.

The test has worked.

The interpretation of what it tested has not.

That distinction will matter repeatedly across the wider cybernetics cluster:

> **A successful measurement procedure can still answer the wrong
> question.**

Wentworth has obtained evidence about Anne's response to what she believes he wants.

He has mistaken it for direct evidence about whether she wants him.

Wingman Anne, meanwhile, is #helping.

> **Pretty young woman identified. Social obstacles being cleared.
> Captain. 🫡**

## ♻️ Congratulations, you have created feedback

The loop now looks roughly like:

```text
Wentworth still loves Anne
        ↓
Wentworth performs availability elsewhere
        ↓
Anne infers he wants someone else
        ↓
Anne suppresses her own interest
        ↓
Wentworth sees less evidence Anne loves him
        ↓
Wentworth continues behaving as though she does not
        ↺
```

Both agents are responding to locally available information.

The system-level result is terrible.

> **Both controllers are functioning. The shared situational picture is
> fucked.**

## 👁️ Second-order observation

Anne is not merely observing Wentworth.

She is observing **what she thinks Wentworth wants** and adjusting herself around that model.

Wentworth is not merely observing Anne.

He is observing **Anne's behaviour after his own behaviour has affected it**.

That is why social systems become recursive very quickly.

We do not simply model one another.

We model:

- what the other person wants;
- what they think of us;
- what they think we want;
- how they may respond if we reveal something.

The observer is inside the loop.

## ⚖️ Anne is not behaving irrationally

It is important not to turn the lesson into:

> Anne should simply have announced everything immediately.

Anne has history, social constraints, uncertainty and a plausible belief that Wentworth wants somebody else.

Her behaviour makes sense from her local model.

Wentworth's interpretations also have some local logic.

The failure is relational.

This is one reason cybernetics is useful: it lets us examine how individually intelligible actions can combine into a malfunctioning system without needing one participant to be stupid or malicious.

## 🪨 The Cobb changes the information environment

Then Louisa falls.

Wingman Anne stops being primarily a courtship-information problem and becomes extremely useful in an emergency.

Wentworth receives a different class of signal about Anne.

This does not immediately solve the romantic inference problem.

But it destabilises the model supporting it.

We will handle that properly in 204.

For now:

Wingman Anne:

> **PROTECT THE ASSET. 🚑**

Wentworth:

> **FOR FUCK'S SAKE I WAS TRYING TO MAKE YOU JEALOUS.**

Wingman Anne:

> *cannot receive transmission; feminine decorum enabled*

## 🧠 Sensor error or interpretation error?

Wentworth does not necessarily need better eyes.

He is observing real behaviour.

The problem is what he thinks the behaviour means.

This distinction matters:

```text
observation: Anne helps Louisa
interpretation: Anne does not love me
```

The observation can be correct while the conclusion is wrong.

A good system therefore needs not only sensing but a causal model adequate to interpret the sensor output.

## 🔁 Self-confirming systems

Bad models can help generate the evidence that appears to confirm them.

Wentworth thinks Anne does not care.

He behaves as though she does not care.

Anne sees him behaving as though she is irrelevant and withdraws further.

Wentworth sees withdrawal.

Model confirmed.

Except the model helped produce the output.

More precisely, Wentworth's **performed state** altered Anne's model; Anne's altered model changed her behaviour; and Wentworth then treated that changed behaviour as evidence about the state that existed before his intervention.

```text
performed signal
      ↓
changes observer's model
      ↓
changes observer's behaviour
      ↓
changed behaviour returns as evidence
      ↓
original model appears confirmed
```

That is feedback contamination.

The system is now manufacturing some of the evidence from which its participants are attempting to infer the system.

This is why reflexive human systems are dangerous to analyse as though behaviour were independent evidence.

**Frederick, my boy, you have contaminated your own dataset.**

## 💌 The missing channel

Eventually the system improves because Anne and Wentworth obtain higher-quality information.

Anne's conversation with Harville allows Wentworth to reinterpret the history.

Wentworth's letter supplies direct information about his own state.

The lesson is not:

> everyone must always say everything immediately.

It is:

> **systems relying heavily on indirect inference need some route by
> which models can be corrected.**

Otherwise Wingman Anne can run indefinitely.

## 🧿 What Wingman Anne teaches us

A useful checklist:

- internal state is not directly observable;
- behaviour is generated under constraints;
- the variable being measured may alter the signal;
- observers affect what they observe;
- an observer's performed state can change another observer's model;
- the resulting behaviour may therefore be contaminated as evidence
    of the prior state;
- a successful measurement procedure can answer the wrong question;
- agents model one another recursively;
- correct observations can support incorrect conclusions;
- locally rational behaviour can produce system-level failure;
- model correction requires an information channel.

Or:

> **If the woman you love is helping you court somebody else, first
> establish whether she is indifferent or simply catastrophically
> considerate.**
