# 🍓 Austen Cybernetics 203: Bro, Your Model Has One Fucking Feature
**First created:** 2026-09-21 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-21  
*If your model of human reliability contains one variable called FIRMNESS, the problem may not be the humans.*
---
## 🧭 Orientation
In 201, Captain Wentworth arrived carrying an eight-year-old model of Anne Elliot.
In 202, we discovered that Anne's behaviour was making that model unusually difficult to correct because:
# Wingman Anne.
Now we need to inspect the model itself.
Wentworth has a theory.
It is not a completely stupid theory.
That is important.
His original engagement to Anne ended because Anne allowed herself to be persuaded against marrying him.
Wentworth was devastated.
He therefore places enormous value on something he understands as:
**firmness.**
Someone who knows their own mind.
Someone who does not allow other people to redirect them.
Someone who remains committed.
Someone who can be relied upon.
All reasonable things to value.
Unfortunately, Captain Wentworth has compressed several different human characteristics into approximately one fucking variable.
```text
FIRMNESS
   ↓
strength
   ↓
constancy
   ↓
judgement
   ↓
reliability
   ↓
GOOD

Captain.

BRO, YOUR MODEL HAS ONE FUCKING FEATURE.

⸻

⚓ 1. Why Wentworth’s Model Makes Sense

Do not begin by laughing at him.

We will get to that.

Wentworth’s model has a history.

Young Wentworth believed:

* Anne loved him;
* they had chosen each other;
* their commitment mattered;
* they could build a future together.

Then Anne changed her decision after receiving advice from somebody she trusted.

From Wentworth’s position, the causal lesson is easy to understand:

When external pressure arrived, Anne’s commitment did not hold.

That hurts.

It also generates a model.

PAST EVENT:
Anne was persuaded against marrying me.
INFERRED FAILURE:
Insufficient firmness.
DESIRED FUTURE TRAIT:
Firmness.
MODEL CONFIDENCE:
Unfortunately high.

The problem is therefore not:

Wentworth values a completely irrelevant characteristic.

The problem is:

Wentworth has allowed one painful historical event to make one characteristic carry far too much explanatory weight.

That is a much more interesting systems failure.

⸻

🧠 2. Compression Is Necessary

Models are simplifications.

They have to be.

The world contains too much information to represent every feature of every system at full resolution.

We therefore compress.

Instead of modelling every possible human response under every possible condition, Wentworth can use categories:

* firm;
* weak;
* reliable;
* unreliable;
* decisive;
* persuadable.

Useful.

Efficient.

Potentially disastrous.

The question is not:

Is the model simplified?

All models are.

The question is:

Which distinctions disappeared during compression?

In Wentworth’s case, several different things are beginning to collapse into one another:

firmness
constancy
stubbornness
courage
judgement
decisiveness
reliability
resistance to influence
strength

These are not the same variable.

⸻

🪞 3. Enter Louisa Musgrove

Louisa appears to offer Wentworth an attractive contrast.

Anne was persuadable.

Louisa presents herself as firm.

She is:

* lively;
* energetic;
* confident;
* decisive;
* enthusiastic;
* unwilling to be easily talked out of things.

Wentworth likes this.

And because Wentworth’s existing model gives firmness enormous weight, Louisa’s behaviour becomes especially legible to him.

He can observe:

LOUISA:
knows what she wants
+
acts upon it
+
resists being persuaded
=
STRONG CHARACTER

Meanwhile:

ANNE:
was persuaded
=
FAILED IMPORTANT CHARACTER TEST

There is a seductive simplicity here.

The model is easy to apply.

Unfortunately:

reality has more columns.

⸻

📊 4. One Proxy Is Doing Too Much Work

Wentworth cannot directly observe abstract qualities like:

* lifelong reliability;
* courage under serious pressure;
* good judgement across changing environments;
* relational constancy;
* ability to revise a decision intelligently;
* capacity to distinguish good advice from bad advice.

Those are difficult variables.

So observable behaviour becomes a proxy.

Does this person change their mind when other people advise them?

Wentworth’s model begins treating the answer as highly informative.

But a proxy is not the thing itself.

For example:

resistance to persuasion ≠ good judgement

and:

willingness to listen ≠ weak judgement

and:

changing a decision ≠ lack of constancy

and:

refusing to change a decision ≠ strength

Context matters.

A useful system needs enough variety to distinguish among those cases.

Wentworth’s model currently does not.

⸻

🧮 5. Bro, There Are Other Stats

Suppose we tried to represent the relevant human characteristics separately.

Very crudely:

judgement
constancy
adaptability
courage
emotional regulation
responsiveness to evidence
self-command
reliability
empathy
decisiveness
capacity to accept advice
capacity to reject bad advice
competence under pressure

Suddenly:

FIRM / NOT FIRM

looks slightly underpowered.

This is the recurring problem of low-dimensional evaluation.

A complex system is being judged through a very small number of observable dimensions.

The model may therefore become extremely confident while remaining badly specified.

The problem is not necessarily that the selected feature tells us nothing.

The problem is that it does not tell us enough.

Or:

BRO, THERE ARE OTHER STATS.

⸻

🪨 6. Unfortunately, There Is A Cobb

Then Austen does something extremely useful.

She stress-tests the proxy.

Louisa wants Wentworth to catch her while she jumps down the steps at the Cobb.

She does it.

She wants to do it again.

Wentworth objects.

Louisa persists.

Her determination does not disappear when somebody gives her a reason to reconsider.

Excellent firmness.

Then she falls.

Badly.

Suddenly:

refuses persuasion

has ceased to look automatically like:

excellent character trait

The same observable characteristic can produce different consequences under different environmental conditions.

That should bother us.

⸻

💥 7. The Proxy Has Failed In Production

The Cobb does not prove:

firmness bad.

Nor does it prove:

persuasion good.

That would simply replace one stupid model with another stupid model.

The lesson is more precise.

Louisa’s accident exposes a distinction Wentworth’s model has not been representing adequately:

rigidity is not reliability.

A person who refuses to alter course may be:

* courageous;
* committed;
* principled;

or:

* poorly calibrated;
* reckless;
* unable to incorporate new information;
* performing consistency after circumstances have changed.

Similarly, a person who changes course may be:

* weak;
* easily manipulated;

or:

* attentive;
* responsive to evidence;
* capable of incorporating another person’s expertise;
* exercising contextual judgement.

The observable action alone does not resolve the question.

We need the surrounding system.

⸻

🔄 10. Adaptability Is Not The Opposite Of Strength

This is the larger lesson.

Robust systems do not necessarily hold the same output regardless of input.

Sometimes that is precisely what makes a system brittle.

A capable controller may need to:

* maintain course when conditions remain appropriate;
* alter course when conditions change;
* distinguish noise from meaningful information;
* distinguish pressure from useful advice;
* preserve important goals while changing tactics.

Human judgement works similarly.

The useful distinction is not simply:

changes mind / does not change mind

It is closer to:

When should this person update?
When should they refuse to update?
What information changes their behaviour?
How do they evaluate that information?
What remains stable while tactics change?

Anne can be influenced.

The more interesting question is:

How does Anne decide what influence deserves weight?

That is a much richer model.

⸻

🎛️ 11. Constancy Is Not Behavioural Rigidity

This matters particularly because Wentworth cares about constancy.

But constancy does not necessarily mean repeating the same observable action forever.

A system can preserve a higher-order objective while changing lower-order behaviour.

Anne’s behaviour changes enormously across eight years.

Something else does not.

Her attachment to Wentworth.

This gives us a useful distinction:

surface behaviour:
variable
underlying commitment:
remarkably persistent

Meanwhile Louisa can display considerable behavioural firmness while her romantic attachment itself later changes.

That is not an insult to Louisa.

It is evidence that:

observable decisiveness and deep temporal constancy are not the same property.

Captain Wentworth has been using one to infer the other.

That inference needs work.

⸻

📡 12. Proxy Failure Does Not Mean Stop Measuring

A common response to a failed proxy is:

Well, clearly measurement is useless.

No.

The lesson is not:

never infer anything from behaviour.

We have to infer from behaviour.

The lesson is:

* know that you are using a proxy;
* understand its limitations;
* use multiple observations;
* preserve distinctions between related variables;
* test the model under different conditions;
* look for contradictory evidence;
* revise when the proxy stops predicting what matters.

Wentworth’s failure is not that he observes firmness.

His failure is that firmness has become an overloaded variable.

It is carrying far too much of the model.

⸻

🧩 13. Requisite Variety

This gives us a gentle route into a larger cybernetic principle.

A controller facing a complex environment needs enough internal variety to respond meaningfully to the variety in that environment.

For our purposes:

If your model contains fewer meaningful distinctions than the environment requires, different situations will collapse into the same category.

Wentworth’s model struggles to distinguish:

being persuaded because you are weak
from
being persuaded because somebody gave good advice

and:

remaining firm because you are courageous
from
remaining firm because you are reckless

Those distinctions matter.

The environment contains more variety than his model currently represents.

Therefore:

BRO, YOUR MODEL HAS ONE FUCKING FEATURE.

Not literally.

Pedagogically.

Please do not write to the Admiralty.

⸻

🪽 14. And Wingman Anne Makes It Worse

Remember 202.

Wentworth is not evaluating Anne under clean laboratory conditions.

Anne’s continuing attachment to him is generating restraint.

She appears less possessive precisely because she cares about his apparent wishes.

So the low-dimensional model and the feedback problem interact.

Wentworth sees:

Anne was persuadable eight years ago
+
Anne does not currently compete for me
+
Anne appears willing to facilitate Louisa

His existing model can fit these observations into:

Anne lacks sufficient constancy.

But a richer model can produce:

Anne was persuadable in one major youthful decision
+
Anne believes I no longer want her
+
Anne believes I want Louisa
+
Anne remains deeply attached to me
+
Anne therefore refuses to obstruct what she thinks will make me happy

Same observed person.

Different model complexity.

Very different inference.

⸻

🧯 15. Do Not Replace One Single-Axis Model With Another

This is important enough to make explicit.

The lesson is not:

Anne good because flexible.

Louisa bad because firm.

That would miss the entire point.

There are situations in which firmness is vital.

There are situations in which adaptation is vital.

There are situations in which listening to other people is wise.

There are situations in which everybody around you can fuck off.

The desired capability is not permanent compliance or permanent resistance.

It is:

context-sensitive judgement capable of both updating and holding course.

The system needs both.

Anne’s strength is not:

she can be persuaded.

It is:

persuasion does not exhaust the description of how she makes decisions.

🧪 17. The 203 Problem Set

When using a model to assess a person, organisation or system:

* Which properties do you actually care about?
* Which of those can you observe directly?
* Which observable features are acting as proxies?
* How many different underlying states could produce the same proxy?
* Have several distinct characteristics been collapsed into one category?
* Is one historical event carrying too much explanatory weight?
* Does your model contain enough variety for the environment?
* What happens when the system is perturbed?
* Which observations contradict the model?
* Are you explaining contradictory evidence away rather than updating?
* Have you confused consistency with reliability?
* Have you confused adaptation with weakness?
* Have you confused decisiveness with judgement?
* Have you confused resistance to influence with independence?

And finally:

Bro. Are there other stats?
