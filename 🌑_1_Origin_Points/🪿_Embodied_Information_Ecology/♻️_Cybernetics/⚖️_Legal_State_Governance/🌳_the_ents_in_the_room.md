# 🌳 The Ents in the Room  
**First created:** 2025-12-26 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-13  
*On slow power, ancient processes, and decisions that arrive centuries too late.*  

> *“We have decided… to do nothing.”*

---

## What This Is (and Is Not)

This node is **satire**.

It uses allegory to describe familiar institutional behaviours, not to accuse individuals or departments of misconduct.

If you are looking for:
- procurement analysis
- legal critique
- policy recommendations  

those live elsewhere.

This node exists to help readers **recognise patterns** they already feel but struggle to name.

---

## The Ent Problem

Some institutions are not captured.  
They are not malicious.  
They are not even especially incompetent.

They are **slow**.

They deliberate.
They consult.
They commission reviews.
They request further evidence.
They await consensus.
They schedule the next meeting.

Years pass.

The world changes.
Infrastructure embeds.
Harms accumulate.

Eventually, a decision is reached — about a system that has already become unavoidable.

These institutions are Ents.

---

## Why Ents Are Not Villains

Ents are:
- principled
- process-oriented
- historically grounded
- deeply cautious
- allergic to rash action

They value:
- precedent
- proportionality
- legitimacy
- and being *seen* to act correctly

In isolation, these are virtues.

In fast-moving technological and securitised environments, they become liabilities.

---

## Ent-Time vs Platform-Time

Platforms move in:
- weeks
- sprints
- pilots
- emergency authorisations

Ents move in:
- inquiries
- frameworks
- white papers
- “we are considering this carefully”

By the time the Ents speak:
- the platform is embedded
- the contract is renewed
- the workflow has changed
- staff have been trained
- reversal is “irresponsible”

The Ents are not overruled.

They are **outpaced**.

---

## The Room Problem

The danger is not that Ents exist.

The danger is that **Ents are often the only visible adults in the room** — and everyone assumes that if something were truly wrong, they would have stopped it.

This creates a comforting illusion:
> “If this were dangerous, surely someone serious would have intervened.”

The Ents *are* intervening.

They are just doing so on a timescale incompatible with infrastructure.

---

## When Ents Finally Move

When Ents finally act, it is usually to:
- add guidance
- request safeguards
- issue principles
- recommend oversight
- suggest future review

Rarely to:
- halt deployment
- unwind infrastructure
- or say “this should not exist”

Their movement is real — but incremental.

And infrastructure does not pause to watch.

---

## Ents and Responsibility Diffusion

Ents rarely hold the whole picture.

They see:
- legality
- proportionality
- precedent

They do not see:
- cumulative harm
- distributional impact
- epistemic drift
- who absorbs friction first

So when harm occurs, it appears:
- accidental
- unfortunate
- nobody’s fault

This is how responsibility dissolves into moss.

---

## Why Ents Thrive in Westminster

Ent-like institutions thrive where:
- caution is rewarded
- risk is punished
- delay feels safer than action
- and legitimacy matters more than speed

Westminster has many such spaces.

They are essential.

They are also easily bypassed by:
- emergency logic
- operational framing
- and “just technical” systems.

---

## The Tragedy of the Ents

The tragedy is not that Ents are useless.

It is that they are **right too late**.

They often arrive at the correct conclusion —
after the decision has already been made irreversible by infrastructure.

They defend the door.
The wall has already been built around them.
