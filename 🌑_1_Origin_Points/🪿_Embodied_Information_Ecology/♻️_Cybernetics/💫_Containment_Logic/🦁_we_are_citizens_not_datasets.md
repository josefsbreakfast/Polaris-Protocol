# 🦁 We Are Citizens, Not Datasets  
**First created:** 2025-11-16 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-13  
*How governance now hears statistical abstractions instead of human beings.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  
In contemporary governance, citizens are no longer encountered as people.  
They are encountered as **profiles**, **segments**, **risk scores**, **needs assessments**, **sentiment aggregates**, **behavioural predictions**, and **engagement metrics**.

The citizen has been replaced by the dataset.

This node maps how that shift occurred, what it obscures, and why democracies become unmoored when representation is mediated exclusively through data.

---

## ✨ Key Features  

- **Statistical Imaginaries**  
  Governance increasingly relies on numerical proxies: “public opinion,” “risk cohort,” “engagement segment.”  
  These abstractions stand in for actual voices.

- **Unembodied Constituencies**  
  Decision-makers encounter “youth,” “minorities,” “survivors,” “disabled people,” or “risk groups” only through reports and dashboards — never the living individuals behind them.

- **Platform-Mediated Public**  
  Commercial platforms filter, rank, and distort visibility, meaning the “public voice” that reaches policymakers is shaped by algorithms, not people.

- **Edgeless Citizenship**  
  Citizens become objects of analysis rather than participants in negotiation.  
  Representation becomes a one-way observation pipeline.

- **Metric-Driven Priorities**  
  Governance optimises for what is measurable — not what matters.

---

## 🧿 Analysis  
When datasets replace citizens, three failures emerge:

### 1. **Epistemic Failure — What is heard is not real.**  
Metrics capture noise patterns, not lived reality.  
Algorithmic summaries can flatten context, suppress nuance, and render vulnerable people statistically invisible.

### 2. **Relational Failure — No texture, no trust.**  
Representatives cannot build trust with abstractions.  
Governance becomes brittle, suspicious, and detached from the humans it claims to serve.

### 3. **Moral Failure — Accountability without encounter is theatre.**  
If politicians never sit with constituents, listen to testimony, or see the cost of policy in human terms, they cannot act with integrity.

In this architecture, the dataset speaks for the citizen —  
and the citizen is left without a voice.

Reversing this trend requires reinserting **embodiment**, **presence**, and **relationship** into governance.  

A democracy that hears only data cannot hear dissent, vulnerability, or complexity.  
It can only hear what is visible to its instruments.
