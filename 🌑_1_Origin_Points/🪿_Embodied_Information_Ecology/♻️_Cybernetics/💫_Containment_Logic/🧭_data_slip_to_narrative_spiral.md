# 🧭 Data Slip → Narrative Spiral
**First created:** 2025-12-02 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-13
*How an AI-generated falsehood entered a football-safety assessment, hardened inside institutional process, and became a political story larger than the error itself.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

In October 2025, Birmingham City Council’s Safety Advisory Group decided that away supporters would not be admitted to Aston Villa’s Europa League fixture against Maccabi Tel Aviv on 6 November 2025.

West Midlands Police supplied the principal safety advice. One document presented to the group referred to a supposed West Ham United–Maccabi Tel Aviv match on 9 November 2023. No such match took place. The force later confirmed that the claim came from an unauthorised Microsoft Copilot query.

That false fixture was not merely a comic clerical blemish. Nor can it honestly carry the entire decision on its back. It is useful because it makes a wider failure visible: uncertain information entered an operational process, acquired institutional authority, survived inadequate challenge, and was defended publicly before its provenance was properly established.

---

## 🗺️ The Documented Sequence

### The fixture and the decision

- Aston Villa were due to host Maccabi Tel Aviv on **6 November 2025**.
- West Midlands Police assessed the fixture as high risk and advised the Safety Advisory Group.
- On **16 October 2025**, the group decided that away supporters would not be admitted.
- Responsibility for the formal decision rested with Birmingham City Council through the event’s safety-certification process, although police advice was its main driver.

### The relevant Amsterdam match

The international comparator was Ajax v Maccabi Tel Aviv in Amsterdam in **November 2024**, not an incident in 2023.

The public record describes a complex sequence: some initial disorder involving a minority of Maccabi supporters, followed by organised violence with an antisemitic character against Israeli supporters and others. Flattening that sequence in either direction produces poor intelligence.

### The false fixture

The police paper later included a fictitious West Ham match. Senior officers initially gave Parliament inaccurate accounts of the source, referring first to social-media scraping and then to a Google search. The former chief constable subsequently confirmed that Microsoft Copilot had generated the information and said its use had not initially been disclosed to senior command.

The problem was therefore not a country-code slip or an innocent autofill error. It was an AI-generated assertion admitted into operational material without elementary verification, followed by uncertainty inside the organisation about how it had arrived there.

---

## 👾 Why One False Datum Mattered

The fictitious match may not have been the sole or decisive reason for the restriction. The wider assessment also considered local tensions, protest, possible disorder, international events, prior football violence and policing capacity.

But a datum can matter without being the final trigger.

It matters when it:

- reveals weak source discipline;
- appears alongside other disputed claims;
- gives invented history the appearance of corroboration;
- enters a document used to justify a restrictive decision;
- survives review by officers and a multi-agency group;
- produces shifting institutional explanations after challenge.

The correct question is not, “Did this one sentence mechanically cause the ban?”

It is:

> **What does the sentence reveal about the reliability, provenance and challenge applied to the assessment as a whole?**

---

## 🌀 From Data Slip to Narrative Spiral

The spiral developed in several stages:

1. **Generation** — a technical tool produced a plausible-looking falsehood.
2. **Admission** — the claim entered police material without adequate verification.
3. **Institutional inheritance** — later decision-makers encountered it with the authority of an official document.
4. **Public defence** — senior accounts of its provenance changed under scrutiny.
5. **Political compression** — debate narrowed toward whether one false fixture invalidated everything.
6. **Symbolic escalation** — the policing decision became a referendum on antisemitism, Israel, Palestine, protest and institutional competence at once.

This is how a small data failure becomes a narrative engine. Every participant selects the fragment that confirms their prior story, while the harder governance questions become strangely unavailable.

---

## 🔍 What the Wider Reviews Found

The later scrutiny did not support either comforting extreme.

It did not establish that there was no genuine public-order risk. The Home Affairs Committee accepted that the fixture presented real risk and complexity.

It also did not treat the fictitious match as an isolated typo floating inside an otherwise pristine assessment. The Committee found poor due diligence, inaccurate claims about the Amsterdam disorder, confirmation bias, inadequate retention of contemporaneous notes, unbalanced presentation of threats, and insufficient early consultation with Birmingham’s Jewish community. It found no evidence that antisemitism motivated the decision, while recognising why the process damaged trust and created that perception.

The useful conclusion is more demanding than partisan vindication:

> **Real risk does not excuse unreliable evidence. Unreliable evidence does not prove that every identified risk was imaginary.**

---

## 🪬 Public Order Without Demographic Profiling

Birmingham’s Muslim and Jewish communities are not themselves risk factors.

Operational assessment should examine evidenced conduct and threats, including:

- credible intelligence about planned violence or disorder;
- risks to visiting supporters;
- risks to residents, protesters and worshippers;
- antisemitic, anti-Muslim, anti-Palestinian and anti-Israeli targeting;
- the conduct of identifiable high-risk individuals or groups;
- available separation, transport and ticketing measures;
- police capacity, necessity and proportionality;
- the effect of political publicity on the threat environment.

Population identity must not become a proxy for dangerousness. Equally, fear of discriminatory appearance must not prevent police from recording a specific, evidenced threat. The discipline is to identify the conduct, source and confidence level—and not let “community tension” become a polite box into which every distinction disappears.

## ⚖️ The Legal Boundary

This was a public-order and event-safety decision. It was not a legal determination that genocide had occurred, that a supporter cohort shared genocidal intent, or that permitting attendance would make the United Kingdom complicit in genocide.

Genocide is a precisely defined international crime requiring prohibited acts committed with intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a protected national, ethnic, racial or religious group. Proceedings and provisional measures in the International Court of Justice case concerning Gaza do not by themselves determine the final merits or convert association with a state, army or football club into individual culpability.

The International Criminal Court Act 2001 provides for genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes in domestic law. It does not create a football-specific duty to exclude supporters, and it does not replace ordinary requirements of individualised evidence.

The more immediate governance duties concern:

- lawful and proportionate public-order decision-making;
- accurate, reviewable evidence;
- non-discrimination and the public sector equality duty;
- reasoned safety advice and accountable certification;
- practical protection for all affected communities.

Atrocity-prevention literacy still matters. It matters most when used with legal precision, rather than as a rhetorical solvent poured over an evidential gap.

## 🤖 The AI-Governance Lesson

Generative AI did not make the decision. Human and institutional systems decided whether to ask it, trust it, copy it, circulate it and defend the resulting material.

High-consequence use requires at minimum:

- an identifiable human owner for every factual claim;
- verification against authoritative sources;
- preservation of prompts, outputs and source trails;
- explicit labelling of AI-assisted research;
- separation of intelligence from open-source commentary;
- review proportionate to the consequence of error;
- prompt correction throughout every downstream record.

“The AI said it” is not provenance. It is a confession that provenance was never secured.

---

## 🧭 Diagnostic Questions

When an error appears inside a politically sensitive risk assessment, ask:

- Where did the claim originate?
- Who checked it, and against what?
- Which later claims depended on it?
- Was uncertainty preserved or stripped away?
- What other evidence independently supported the decision?
- Were affected groups consulted symmetrically?
- Could a less restrictive measure manage the evidenced risk?
- Were corrections transmitted to every recipient?
- Did publicity itself alter the operational risk?

The aim is not to turn one hallucination into a conspiracy. It is to stop institutional authority from turning a hallucination into history.
