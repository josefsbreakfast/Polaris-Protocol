# ✈ Containment by Crash — Risk Management and Symbolic Deletion  
**First created:** 2025-11-06 | **Last updated:** 2026-09-12  
*Disaster as the reset function of authoritarian infrastructure.*  

---

## 🛰 Orientation  
A crash is never only mechanical in its consequences. Whether accidental, enabled by negligence, or deliberately exploited after the event, it can become an administrative convenience — erasing a ledger, silencing dissenting contractors, or opening a clean-slate budget cycle beneath the language of condolence. To model risk in such systems is to map **where deletion masquerades as fate**.  

---

## ✨ Key Features  
- **Systemic risk as policy:** tolerated vulnerability or alleged engineered failure used for bureaucratic renewal.  
- **Symbolic deletion:** catastrophe replacing audit.  
- **Containment loop:** crash → mourning → reconstruction → amnesia.  

---

## 🔍 Analysis / Content  

### 1. The Economy of Loss  
Major disasters can trigger procurement rounds, loyalty tests, and leadership reshuffles. In that institutional economy, risk can come to fund its own renewal.  

### 2. Crash as Compliance Tool  
Fear of catastrophe keeps institutions in alignment. The system survives by demonstrating what happens when one node fails.  

### 3. Deletion and Dataflow  
Digital systems replicate this pattern: corrupted logs, overwritten registries, “archival rot” treated as inevitability rather than intervention. Containment by crash is the offline ancestor of metadata sabotage.
