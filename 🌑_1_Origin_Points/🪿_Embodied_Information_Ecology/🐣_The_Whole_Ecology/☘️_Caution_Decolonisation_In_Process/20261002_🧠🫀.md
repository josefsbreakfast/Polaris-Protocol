# 🧠🫀 Caution: Decolonisation In Process --- 2 October 2026

**First created:** 2026-10-02 \| **Last updated:** 2026-10-02\
*Loose excavation notes. Not a finished position on Ireland,
republicanism, Britain, Palestine, or decolonisation. I appear to have
found a bit of my own information ecology that has been running without
giving me the documentation.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

> “And you dare to call me a terrorist,  
> while you looked down your gun…”  

(Even an indirect reference was a hesitation; may his memory be s blessing. No one deserves to die like that; no state is validated or dignified when it imprisons its apparent sons and daughters, to die through neglect of its own laws.)  

> “…When I think of all the deeds that you had done:  
> You had plundered many nations divided many lands,  
> You had terrorised their peoples you ruled with an iron hand,  
> And you brought this reign of terror to my land.”

> “I am deeply patriotic; I never want to believe anything bad about our country; I never want to call into question the behaviour of our soldiers and our Army, which I believe to be the finest in the world.  
> 
> And I have seen for myself the very difficult and dangerous circumstances in which we ask our soldiers to serve.  
>
> But the conclusions of this report are absolutely clear: there is no doubt; there is nothing equivocal; there are no ambiguities.  
>
> What happened on Bloody Sunday was both unjustified and unjustifiable. It was wrong.”

It just doesn’t feel like me moved forward; it felt like we slipped back. And I watched men who supposedly had brought in our collective freedom, bring it all back.  

I hope to G-d we move forward with haste, and leave these rotten methods in the past.  

We let them die in 1981, and we came so close again; I cannot fully articulate what this did for my relationship to the concept of the nation, here.

## 🪟 I think I found the glass

-   I have spent years finding extremely good reasons not to touch this
    bit of my own history.
-   Not really my history.
-   Other people were closer.
-   Other people were harmed more directly.
-   I do not know enough.
-   I am British.
-   I am English.
-   I am too far removed.
-   I should not overstep.
-   I should listen to people who actually lived it.
-   All individually reasonable statements.
-   Collectively: an extremely efficient escape hatch.
-   I thought this was a bit of a niggle.
-   Turns out there is actually quite a big wound under there.
-   Probably need to clean that out and get it so that it can heal up
    again.
-   There is a strange glass wall around Ireland for me.
-   I can apply moral and historical reasoning to other places and other
    conflicts.
-   I can understand that violence has context without believing all
    violence is justified.
-   I can understand that states create categories, that "law and order"
    is not a neutral starting point, that the choice of time zero
    matters, that the construction of territory matters, that power
    matters.
-   Then I turn towards Ireland and something in me goes:

> **No. Careful. Do not proceed.**

-   The inhibition arrives before the conclusion.
-   That is important.
-   It is not the same thing as having reached a moral conclusion and
    then respecting it.
-   It feels much more like a pre-existing boundary around what I am
    allowed to investigate.

## 🧠 The intellectualisation is extremely efficient, unfortunately

-   One of the more annoying discoveries is that I am very good at
    hiding from this in ways that look exactly like doing excellent
    intellectual work.
-   I get close to a proposition that feels dangerous --- for example,
    whether some particular act of republican violence might have been
    understood as necessary, justified, effective or defensible in its
    own historical conditions --- and immediately disappear upwards
    into:
    -   proportionality;
    -   target selection;
    -   alternatives;
    -   legitimacy;
    -   efficacy;
    -   discrimination;
    -   chronology;
    -   comparative cases;
    -   constitutional questions;
    -   historiography.
-   All useful.
-   All genuinely things I care about.
-   Also, apparently, a fantastic way of not noticing that three seconds
    earlier I became frightened.
-   The brain does not change the subject to Pokémon.
-   It changes the subject to **methodology**.
-   Extremely inconvenient.
-   A thought occurring in my mind does not necessarily represent a
    conclusion I have reached.
-   An inhibition occurring in my mind does not necessarily represent a
    moral boundary I have chosen.

## 👻 Bits of spirits or ghosts

-   "Cultural inheritance" feels almost too tidy.
-   Inheritance sounds like somebody hands you an object and tells you
    what it is.
-   This feels more like fragments surviving after the explanatory layer
    disappeared.
-   Bits of language.
-   Turns of phrase.
-   Humour.
-   Music.
-   References.
-   Comfort.
-   Danger.
-   Little rules about what not to say.
-   Ways of responding to authority.
-   Ways of making yourself unobtrusive.
-   Things the adults around you did that only become legible decades
    later.
-   Other people occasionally recognising something in you that you do
    not quite know how to name.
-   I am not trying to establish:

> **Actually, Surprise, I Am Irish™.**

-   I am discovering that I seem to be close enough for the inheritance
    to have reached me, but far enough away that nobody gave me a map of
    what I inherited.
-   It feels like I am carrying bits of spirits or ghosts, but not in
    touch enough to hear them.
-   There are two distances at once:
    -   far enough away that I do not want to appropriate a
        straightforward identity or pretend to an experience I did not
        have;
    -   not far enough away for everything to have disappeared.
-   Some of it made the journey.
-   The question may not actually be **"Am I Irish?"**
-   The questions may be:
    -   **What reached me?**
    -   **What happened to people I came from that made this the thing
        they passed on?**
    -   **What did they pass on through silence?**
-   There is grief in discovering traces of people I actually knew
    inside myself after they are no longer here to explain them.

> **I recognise this object.**\
> **I have been carrying it my whole life.**\
> **I did not know it came from you.**\
> **And now I cannot ask you what it meant.**

## 👵 The people I would ask are dead

-   Two people who cared for me when I was little were first-generation
    UK-born to Irish parents.
-   They had different relationships with Britain, the British state,
    religion, Irishness and authority.
-   I do **not** know their private politics and I do not want to invent
    them now because they are dead and cannot correct me.
-   What I remember much more clearly is the behavioural environment.
-   There were things that were discussable.
-   There were things that were not.
-   There were things that might be discussed differently depending on
    who was in the room.
-   There was a strong impression that some conversations were simply
    not safe conversations to have in this country.
-   I do not know whether silence meant agreement, disagreement,
    caution, exhaustion, respectability, professional necessity, family
    history, or some mixture.
-   Silence is information.
-   It is not enough information to reconstruct somebody's beliefs.
-   That distinction matters.
-   I am sad that I cannot ask them now.
-   I am angry that I did not have enough historical equipment while
    they were alive to know what I wanted to ask.
-   New information is making lots of tiny things retrospectively
    legible.
-   That is lovely in one way.
-   It is also fucking awful.

## 🏭 And then there is the extremely inconvenient Belfast question

-   A very basic historical question --- why those six counties? ---
    appears to have kicked a hole through the floor.
-   I started asking about partition, territory, industrial capacity,
    Belfast, political viability and how the box itself was made.
-   This is exactly the kind of question I am perfectly comfortable
    asking elsewhere:
    -   Who drew the boundary?
    -   Why this population unit?
    -   What material interests sat inside it?
    -   What political outcome did the boundary make possible?
    -   What gets treated as the natural starting point afterwards?
-   Applying the same questions to Ireland felt weirdly transgressive.
-   That is the interesting part for today's purposes.
-   The historical answers need their own sourced work.
-   The internal response was immediate enough to notice.

## 🪖 I do not have one opinion on "the armed struggle"

-   "Armed struggle 1922--1997" is not a useful single moral object.
-   I do not want to flatten decades of different conditions, actors,
    targets, strategies and available alternatives into one emotional
    response.
-   I also do not want "it was complicated" to become another trapdoor
    through which I disappear every time I get near a difficult
    judgement.
-   Explanation is not endorsement.
-   Condemnation of a particular act does not require retroactively
    legitimising every feature of the political settlement around it.
-   Understanding why somebody believed violence was necessary does not
    require agreeing that it was.
-   Time matters.
-   Information environments matter.
-   Available routes matter.
-   Power matters.
-   Targets matter.
-   Outcomes matter.
-   Changed conditions matter.
-   I am allowed to reach different conclusions about different acts.
-   I am also allowed not to know yet.
-   What I am trying to remove is the feeling that merely asking the
    question makes me dangerous.

## 🇵🇸 Palestine activism did not create the fear from scratch

-   This may be one of the biggest things I have realised today.
-   The last three years forced me to accelerate work I might otherwise
    have taken another decade to approach.
-   I had to think hard about:
    -   occupation;
    -   colonialism;
    -   state violence;
    -   resistance;
    -   political violence;
    -   legitimacy;
    -   terrorism categories;
    -   whose violence becomes "security";
    -   whose violence becomes "terrorism";
    -   where the historical clock starts;
    -   who gets to define the relevant population;
    -   how cultural memory and present information interact.
-   Palestine gave me analytical machinery.
-   Then some of that machinery rotated home.
-   And my brain went:

> **Oh. Absolutely fucking not.**

-   Watching the British state's handling of Palestine activism was
    deeply upsetting to me.
-   I am deliberately separating that statement from claims about the
    legality of particular decisions or the private intentions of
    particular decision-makers.
-   There are institutional questions I want to understand properly
    later:
    -   who knew what;
    -   what powers they believed they had;
    -   what legal advice constrained;
    -   what could or could not be said publicly;
    -   why attention appeared concentrated in some places and absent in
        others;
    -   what was happening privately while public positions appeared
        static;
    -   how later records, litigation, inquiries, archives or memoirs
        may change the picture.
-   I do not need to invent those answers today.
-   What I **can** record today is what the process did inside me.
-   Some of what I saw activated cultural memory around Irish prisoners
    and hunger strikes.
-   It frightened me.
-   It felt horribly familiar even before I had worked through exactly
    why.
-   The crucial line is:

> **Palestine activism did not create that fear from scratch. It appears
> to have found it.**

-   That is different.
-   I was not encountering Palestine with an empty information
    environment.
-   New information hit an existing network:
    -   Irish family fragments;
    -   Britishness;
    -   Englishness;
    -   Jewishness;
    -   terrorism discourse;
    -   political violence;
    -   respectability;
    -   belonging;
    -   state authority;
    -   silence;
    -   fear of becoming legible as the wrong kind of person.
-   The recognition may have happened before I consciously constructed
    the comparison.

## 🏛️ "I thought we were better than that"

-   This is uncomfortable.
-   I had bought into an idea that Britain was somehow better than its
    imperial history now.
-   Not perfect.
-   But **past that**.
-   Magically, apparently.
-   Without doing the work.
-   There was an assumption that certain political and social mechanisms
    belonged safely to History.
-   The last few years shook that assumption very badly.
-   Again, this is not me saying:
    -   every present British action is colonial;
    -   modern Britain is identical to the Empire;
    -   Ireland and Palestine are the same;
    -   every security decision is illegitimate.
-   It is me saying that I realised I had been relying on a story of
    automatic moral progress.
-   Time passing is not institutional maintenance.
-   "We are better now" is not itself a safeguard.
-   I do not want to replace **Britain Is Inherently Good™** with
    **Britain Is Inherently Evil™**.
-   Both are too easy.
-   I want to be able to stay inside the "we" and ask:

> **I thought we were better than this. Why weren't we? What work had I
> assumed had already been done? What work actually has to keep being
> done?**

-   I just felt like **we were still the colonisers**.
-   That sentence is an account of my experience.
-   It is not, by itself, a complete historical or legal analysis.
-   The "we" matters.
-   I am British.
-   This is not The Colonisers Over There™.
-   That may be part of why it hurt so much.

## 🧱 The fear is not necessarily "somebody will disagree with me"

-   It is more like:
    -   somebody will classify me;
    -   somebody will decide what kind of person I am;
    -   somebody will decide I am unsafe;
    -   somebody will decide I am beyond the boundary of respectable

-   It also explains why the response is often pre-emptive.
-   Make sure I am reasonable.
-   Make sure I am careful.
-   Make sure I qualify.
-   Make sure I demonstrate that I understand all the complications.
-   Make sure I am not too visible.

## 🔉 Turning myself down

-   This may be the clearest phrase from today.
-   It is an ambient way of:
    -   apologising for myself;
    -   turning myself down;
    -   making sure I do not say certain things;
    -   making sure I do not overstep;
    -   making sure I do not put too much of myself out there.
-   There is a huge degree of anxiety in the background.
-   It is not always experienced as **fear** because it is so ordinary.
-   It can look like:
    -   consideration;
    -   humility;
    -   carefulness;
    -   nuance;
    -   professionalism;
    -   intellectual rigour;
    -   not wanting to take up too much space.
-   Some of those are genuine values of mine.
-   The problem is when they become the respectable clothing worn by
    automatic self-erasure.
-   There is a difference between:

> **I am choosing to make room for somebody else.**

and:

> **I must make myself smaller in advance in case being fully legible
> causes somebody to downgrade or punish me.**

-   I do not want to make myself smaller.
-   That is possibly the least complicated conclusion in this entire
    fucking folder.
-   I do not want to become bigger than other people.
-   I want to **stop performing the subtraction**.

## 🎻 Rebel songs and the problem of positive Irishness

-   In the last couple of decades, a lot of the positive Irish identity
    I have personally encountered has come through trad music, including
    material that is explicitly republican, rebel, memorial or otherwise
    politically difficult.
-   That creates a strange bind when the respectable British cultural
    frame around Irishness has often felt to me like it has much more
    room for:
    -   entertaining;
    -   charming;
    -   drunk;
    -   silly;
    -   stupid;
    -   sentimental;
    -   safely successful;
    -   or dangerous/terrorist.
-   The rebel material can feel like Irishness speaking in the first
    person and refusing shame.
-   That does **not** mean every song is a historical textbook.
-   It does **not** mean liking a song is a comprehensive endorsement of
    every action associated with its politics.
-   It means music can carry:
    -   memory;
    -   anger;
    -   courage;
    -   belonging;
    -   grief;
    -   humour;
    -   refusal.
-   There are lines in rebel songs that have given me solace because
    they reverse the direction of judgement.
-   They ask who gets to name whom.
-   I play *Come Out Ye Black and Tans* before hard things.
-   This is funny.
-   It is also probably not unrelated to anything else in this file.

### 🎶 The playlist is probably slightly OTT for a reason

-   I genuinely do not think I would have survived this period since
    2023 in quite the same way without the Irish music register.
-   I cannot tell anybody how many times *Come Out Ye Black and Tans*
    and *Joe McDonnell* have been on.
-   Sometimes I needed grief.
-   Sometimes I needed defiance.
-   Sometimes I needed somebody else to hold anger for a few minutes.
-   Sometimes I needed the sheer fucking cheek of it.
-   Sometimes I needed humour badly enough that humour itself became a
    survival tool.
-   Sometimes I just needed **not to be crushed by the shame**.
-   The playlist is probably slightly OTT because it has not been
    functioning as an ordinary playlist.
-   It has been a place to put things.
-   A survival archive, maybe.
-   A register I could move into when the emotional language available
    elsewhere was making me smaller.
-   There is something important in being able to meet frightening or
    humiliating material with mockery rather than only horror.
-   There is something important in grief that does not require
    humiliation.
-   There is something important in hearing a cultural voice that does
    not begin by apologising for being in the room.
-   This does not make the songs historical authorities.
-   It does not turn listening into blanket endorsement of every
    politics, person or act represented in them.
-   It tells me something about what the music was **doing** in my
    information ecology.
-   While one stream of information was saying:
    -   be careful;
    -   be respectable;
    -   do not become legible as the wrong kind of person;
    -   do not be too angry;
    -   do not put too much of yourself out there;
the music could carry something more like:
    -   you do not have to accept somebody else's description of you;
    -   you are allowed to laugh;
    -   you are allowed to be angry;
    -   grief can still have dignity;
    -   shame does not automatically belong to you;
    -   **fuck them, sing.**
-   I want to acknowledge the people who made, preserved, performed and
    passed on that culture and that music.
-   I do not know how to explain everything it has meant to me.
-   I do know that some of it reached me when I needed it.
-   Whatever this period of my life eventually turns out to have been,
    I owe them thanks for helping me survive it.

> **Before I understood what the wound was, I had apparently already
> found some of the medicine.**

## 📺 Who was allowed to narrate Irishness?

-   I am noticing a difference between Irishness being represented
    **for** a British audience and Irishness speaking in its own voice.
-   This is partly why apparently very wholesome mainstream
    representations can feel oddly distant from the Irish culture I
    experienced around actual people.
-   This is not an accusation against individual entertainers.
-   It is about what forms of Irishness feel easily legible and safe
    inside British cultural space.
-   Then something more abrasive, political, regional, diasporic or
    unashamed arrives and my response is:

> **Oh. There you are.**

-   Which is then immediately followed by:

> **Am I allowed to say that?**

-   There is the glass again.

## 🛂 A generation too late for the passport is not the same thing as no inheritance

-   Administratively, I am a generation too late for the passport.
-   Culturally, the line is obviously messier.
-   People have independently assumed I am Irish from

-   I find that flattering.
-   I do not mind the assumption.

-   Citizenship eligibility, cultural inheritance and authority to speak

-   Cultural transmission can attenuate unevenly.
-   Explicit identity can fade while other things survive:
    -   vocabulary;
    -   syntax;
    -   humour;
    -   family habits;
    -   music;
    -   religion;
    -   attitudes to authority;
    -   ideas of rude/polite;
    -   ideas of safe/unsafe;
    -   things one is careful about.

## 🧑‍🏭 Class, region and the other kinds of not-quite-normal white person

-   There is probably overlap here with:
    -   working class;
    -   North East;
    -   accent;
    -   regional identity;
    -   Irish family background;
    -   Catholic cultural proximity;
    -   Jewishness;
    -   schooling;
    -   respectability.
-   Not equivalent histories.
-   Not interchangeable prejudices.
-   But there is a recognisable experience of:

> **I am white, but I am not quite the default kind of white in this

-   Britain can do hierarchy through:
    -   accent;
    -   school;
    -   postcode;
    -   clothes;
    -   religion;
    -   name;
    -   cultural reference;
    -   manners;
    -   whether somebody is "polished";

## 🛤️ Old prejudice is a low-visibility pathway

-   This may be one of the most important additions.
-   New harms can be difficult to detect when they travel through old biases, old scapegoats and old forms of discrimination.
-   A completely novel category may make people stop and ask why everybody is suddenly being treated differently.
-   An old groove already feels culturally legible.
-   Worse: the person experiencing it may already know the choreography.
-   So instead of thinking:

> **Something discriminatory is happening around me.**

-   the output can be:

> **I need to be more careful.**  
> **I probably should not say that.**  
> **Maybe I am overreacting.**  
> **I should explain myself better.**  
> **I should make sure they know I am reasonable.**  
> **I should turn myself down.**

-   The discriminatory information has been converted into self-correction.
-   Old prejudice therefore has an advantage over novel prejudice: **it comes with pre-trained users**.
-   Not because the users consciously agree with it.
-   Because the culture has already supplied expectations about who is respectable, threatening, embarrassing, excessive, credible, loyal, normal, or entitled to anger.
-   Possible mechanism:

> **novel harm + familiar prejudice → familiar feeling → self-regulation → delayed recognition of the novel harm**

-   Which gives me another Whole Ecology proposition:

> **Familiarity is not evidence of safety. Sometimes familiarity is evidence that the system has encountered the pathway before.**

-   And another:

> **Old prejudices can conceal new harms because the person experiencing them may initially experience the harm as an instruction to regulate themselves.**

## 🐣 This is The Whole Ecology

-   This is why this ended up under:

`🌑_1_Origin_Points/🪿_Embodied_Information_Ecology/🐣_The_Whole_Ecology/`

-   The information ecology of a person is not only the information they
    can consciously retrieve.
-   It includes:
    -   what was said;
    -   what was not said;
    -   who became tense around what;
    -   which identities were conspicuous;
    -   which identities were kept quiet;
    -   what television repeatedly associated with danger;
    -   what school omitted;
    -   what songs supplied that respectable culture did not;
    -   what adults treated as safe conversation;
    -   what somebody learned about authority without anybody explaining
        the lesson;
    -   what the body anticipates before the conscious mind has
        constructed an argument.
-   The big theoretical thing:

> **Information can survive after its explanation has been lost.**

-   You do not necessarily inherit:

> **Here is the threat, here is what happened, and here is why you
> should respond this way.**

-   You can inherit:

> **Be careful.**

-   Then the ecology keeps processing new information through that
    instruction.
-   That instruction can influence output even when the system no longer
    has conscious access to the information that originally produced the
    adaptation.
-   But today's correction is important:
-   **the ecology does not only transmit the injury.**
-   It can also transmit or preserve counter-information.
-   Humour.
-   Music.
-   Refusal.
-   Dignity.
-   Collective memory.
-   Ways of metabolising humiliation without accepting the humiliation
    as truth.
-   Ways of being angry without disappearing into the anger.
-   Ways of grieving without surrendering the right to speak.
-   Cultural material can therefore be doing regulatory work before the
    person consciously understands why they keep reaching for it.
-   I found the ghosts in the caution and silence.
-   Apparently some of the ghosts were singing back.
-   So the inherited ecology may contain both:

> **Be careful.**

and:

> **Fuck them, sing.**

-   That changes the model.
-   The same cultural transmission that carries injury, caution or
    adaptation may also carry resources for surviving and revising those
    adaptations.
-   I did not need to understand the whole wound before I could use some
    of what the culture had preserved against it.
-   I also apparently did not need to understand the threat model consciously before the ecology began looking for people, music and histories that could help me interpret it.
-   This makes **interpretive community** part of the ecology too.
-   Sometimes the missing information is not another fact. It is another person in whom a configuration of experience is already legible.
-   And old prejudice can itself become infrastructure: a familiar pathway through which new information is interpreted, normalised or converted into self-policing before conscious recognition catches up.
