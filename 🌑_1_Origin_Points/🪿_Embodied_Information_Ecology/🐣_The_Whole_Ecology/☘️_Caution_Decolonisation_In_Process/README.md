# ☘️ Caution: Decolonisation In Process

**First created:** 2026-10-02 \| **Last updated:** 2026-10-02\
*First-person working notes from inside an unfinished process. This is
not a guide to being Irish, a theory of Irish identity, or an
instruction that anybody else should experience decolonisation this
way.*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧭 Orientation

This folder records something I did not realise I was carrying.

I am British and English, with Irish cultural and family inheritance
that reached me unevenly. I grew up close enough to some of it for it to
shape me, but far enough from it --- and with enough silence around
parts of it --- that I did not have a map for what I had inherited.

While working through Irish history, British state power, Palestine,
political violence, cultural representation and my own responses to
Gaeilge, I started noticing an unusually strong pattern of inhibition.

Apologising.

Qualifying.

Intellectualising.

Worrying about overstepping.

Worrying about being associated with the wrong politics.

Feeling that ordinary expressions of Irish cultural connection required
explanation.

Feeling undeserving when Irish people were simply nice to me about
trying to learn Gaeilge.

Making myself smaller.

The notes in this folder document that process as I encounter it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## ☘️ This is not a model of Irishness

Irish readers may recognise absolutely none of this.

Good.

Other people may have grown up securely Irish, securely British-Irish,
Irish-speaking, republican, unionist, nationalist, neither, diasporic in
completely different ways, or with much clearer family narratives than
mine.

Some people will have done considerably more decolonial work than I
have.

Some will reasonably look at something I am painfully excavating and
think:

> **...yes? We know?**

Also good.

This folder is not here to turn my delayed recognition into everybody
else's revelation.

It records **one information ecology**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🐣 Why this lives in The Whole Ecology

The useful question for Embodied Information Ecology is not whether my
particular inheritance is universal.

It plainly is not.

The useful observation is that information can remain active after its
explanation has been lost.

A person may inherit not:

> *Here is what happened, here is what it meant, and here is why we
> behave this way.*

but simply:

> **Be careful.**

That information can survive through silence, behaviour, cultural
representation, family adaptation, anticipated consequences, missing
histories, social feedback and bodily threat responses.

It can continue affecting what someone approaches, avoids, says, wears,
learns, apologises for and believes they are entitled to know.

That is why these messy first-person notes belong here.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## ⚠️ What "decolonisation" means in this folder

It does **not** mean replacing one compulsory set of political
conclusions with another.

It does not mean deciding in advance what I must think about Britain,
Ireland, republicanism, unionism, political violence, Palestine, or
anybody involved in them.

For me, at this stage, it means trying to identify where inherited
hierarchy, fear or shame may be deciding what I am allowed to
investigate **before I have investigated it**.

The desired outcome is not predetermined agreement.

It is greater freedom to think.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🫀 Proximity still matters

Recognising that something has affected me does not make me the person
most affected by it.

These notes should not be used to substitute my experience for people
who grew up in Ireland or Northern Ireland, people directly affected by
conflict and state violence, Irish speakers and language communities, or
anybody carrying histories substantially closer than mine.

Listening remains part of the work.

But listening to somebody else does not require disappearing myself.

Both things can be true.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🪟 Read accordingly

Expect:

-   unfinished thoughts;
-   uncertainty;
-   things I later revise;
-   emotional responses recorded before I fully understand them;
-   distinctions between what I know, what I suspect and what I still
    need to research;
-   jokes, because unfortunately I remain me;
-   occasional encounters with an invisible pane of glass followed by
    **oh for fuck's sake, there it is again**.

This is a process log, not a verdict.

Some readers will be much further through this work.

Some will never have needed this particular work at all.

Some may recognise pieces of it through entirely different histories.

You do not need to reproduce my conclusions, my route, or my wounds for
the underlying observation to hold:

> **We process new information through whole ecologies --- including
> parts of those ecologies we may not yet know are there.**

That is what I am trying to observe here.

Without making myself smaller while I do it.
