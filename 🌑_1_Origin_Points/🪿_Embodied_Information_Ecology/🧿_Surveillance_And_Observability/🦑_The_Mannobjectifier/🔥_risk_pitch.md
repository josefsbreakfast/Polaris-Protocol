# 🔥 Risk Pitch

> **Prospective investors should carefully consider the following risks
> before investing in the future of computational dick measurement.**

Mannobjectifier™️ is a fictional product. Its risk profile is less
fictional than it first appears.

The obvious objection is ethical: a pair of smart glasses designed to
estimate the apparent length of an adult man's flaccid penis through his
trousers is an intrusive, objectifying and spectacularly unnecessary
consumer product.

That is not, however, the only problem an investor would encounter.

Once Mannobjectifier™️ is decomposed into its component technologies,
the joke becomes considerably less technologically exotic. Camera-based
measurement, body-landmark detection, pose estimation, garment and
contour analysis, wearable optics, edge inference, probabilistic
classification and data integration are not inventions that require
somebody first to decide to build a Dick Measuring Machine.

The obscenity of the use case can disguise the banality of the
technology.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 📉 Material risks to the business

### Intellectual-property exposure

Mannobjectifier™️ does not get to patent *computer vision, but for
dicks* and go home.

A real company attempting to build the product would have to establish
freedom to operate across a stack containing hardware, optics, sensors,
image processing, body measurement, pose estimation, wearable computing,
machine-learning inference, interface design and potentially biometric
or identity-related technologies.

The most commercially dangerous patent may therefore have nothing
whatsoever to do with penises.

It may cover a method for estimating dimensions from monocular imagery.
It may concern body landmarks, garment geometry, wearable-camera
calibration, edge inference or the way information is rendered to the
user.

The fictional company's distinctive application does not imply that the
underlying technical territory is unoccupied.

**Investor question:** *Which part of this company do we actually own?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Technical commoditisation

Mannobjectifier™️ may discover that it has founded a company around what
eventually becomes a feature.

If general-purpose smart glasses already contain cameras, processors,
spatial mapping and machine-vision capabilities, then a sufficiently
capable platform may be able to reproduce the relevant inference without
reproducing Mannobjectifier™️ as a standalone device.

That creates an uncomfortable strategic possibility:

> the clever part may not be the glasses, the camera or even the
> computer vision.\
> the clever part may merely be the particular inference we have chosen
> to make.

A novel use case is not automatically a durable technical moat.

**Investor question:** *Are we building a platform, or are we one
software update away from becoming a toggle?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Incumbent competition

Entering a new market does not mean entering an empty market.

A Mannobjectifier™️ launch could attract attention from companies with
stronger hardware supply chains, larger model-development teams,
existing wearable platforms, established distribution, deeper patent
portfolios and considerably more lawyers.

An incumbent need not believe that computational dick measurement is
itself a magnificent business.

It may merely conclude that the company has demonstrated a commercially
interesting capability, user behaviour, dataset, interface or market
segment.

The risk is therefore not only:

**somebody copies the product.**

It is also:

**somebody who already owns most of the infrastructure decides the
product should never have been a company.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Acquisition pressure

Successful startups do not encounter competition only through imitation.

They may become acquisition targets.

A larger company might value Mannobjectifier™️ because it wants the
team, patents, models, data, optics work, user base, brand, distribution
channel or simply the removal of a potential future competitor.

This can be attractive to investors.

It can also mean that the company's strategic independence lasts
approximately until somebody with a much larger balance sheet notices
it.

The acquisition question becomes especially interesting where the
fictional product sits close to an existing research-and-development
trajectory. A buyer need not want **Mannobjectifier™️**. It may want the
capability demonstrated by Mannobjectifier™️ before that capability
develops into somebody else's market position.

**Investor question:** *Are they buying our future, or preventing it?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Infrastructure dependency

Mannobjectifier™️ cannot manufacture an entire technological ecosystem
merely because the logo has a squid on it.

A real product could depend upon third-party chips, camera modules,
optics, operating systems, model frameworks, cloud infrastructure, app
distribution, mapping or identity services, manufacturing partners and
platform permissions.

Dependency creates leverage.

A supplier can change its terms. A platform can restrict functionality.
An API can disappear. A hardware partner can prioritise a larger
customer. A model provider can alter acceptable-use rules. A
distribution channel can decide that the product is reputationally
radioactive.

The company may therefore possess its own brand while remaining
structurally dependent upon infrastructure controlled by organisations
with different incentives.

**Investor question:** *Who can switch us off?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Regulatory and privacy exposure

There is a difference between observing something and having an
unrestricted right to computationally analyse, retain, combine or act
upon it.

A product performing intimate bodily inference in public would
immediately raise questions around privacy, data protection, biometric
or sensitive inference, transparency, consent, retention, profiling,
automated decision-making and the distinction between on-device
observation and persistent data collection.

Mannobjectifier+™️ makes this problem worse.

The Full Flu Shot™️ premise deliberately moves from one camera inference
toward the aggregation of relationship, behavioural, commercial and
identity signals. At that point the interesting question is no longer
whether a camera can see something.

It is what happens when observations become records, records become
profiles, profiles become classifications, and classifications become
interventions.

**Investor question:** *What exactly are we processing, and what gives
us the right to process it?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

### Stakeholder and reputational risk

Some stakeholders may object to the product because it is, technically
speaking, appalling.

Potential objections may come from customers, subjects, employees,
suppliers, platforms, regulators, advocacy groups, advertisers,
investors and people who simply do not wish to live in a civilisation
containing automated trouser analytics.

The company should not assume that disagreement will divide neatly along
gender lines, political lines or attitudes toward technology.

Nor should it assume that describing an inference as humorous,
empowering or technologically impressive resolves the underlying
question of whether the subject wanted to become a data surface.

**Investor question:** *What happens when the people being measured do
not think they are the customer?*

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 🧱 The uncomfortable moat problem

The greatest strategic problem may be that Mannobjectifier™️ is
simultaneously ridiculous as a product and ordinary as a technical
architecture.

Its fictional stack can be written without inventing magic:

-   camera-based observation;
-   body and pose estimation;
-   contour and garment analysis;
-   probabilistic measurement;
-   wearable display;
-   local or remote inference;
-   identity resolution;
-   data aggregation;
-   behavioural classification.

That matters.

The existence of a ridiculous application should not reassure us about
the underlying capability.

It should do the opposite.

If an obviously invasive joke can be constructed from relatively general
technical building blocks, the more important question is what happens
when the same capabilities are applied to purposes that are commercially
valuable, institutionally desirable, socially normalised and much less
visible to the person being inferred about.

Mannobjectifier™️ is funny because the inference is crude and the
objectification is obvious.

Many consequential systems enjoy neither disadvantage.

## 👞 The shoe was on the other foot

The Catching Print moment worked because the reversal was immediate.

Women briefly and conspicuously applied intimate visual assessment to
men, and some men discovered that being assessed according to a bodily
characteristic they had not volunteered for public evaluation felt
remarkably unpleasant.

That recognition is useful.

It does not require the conclusion that men are uniquely fragile, that
women are uniquely virtuous, or that reciprocal objectification is
desirable.

Men and women do not encounter public bodily scrutiny in identical ways.

For many women, visual assessment is not an unusual conceptual exercise.
Bodies can be read for attractiveness, age, weight, breasts, clothing,
pregnancy, sexuality, availability and conformity before the woman has
said anything at all. The precise experience varies enormously, but the
possibility of being looked at and socially interpreted is familiar
enough to become background calculation.

Catching Print briefly changed who was being asked to notice that
calculation.

The useful response was available:

> **Oh. This is unpleasant. Perhaps people have been trying to tell us
> something.**

Mannobjectifier™️ deliberately chooses the other response.

## 🇺🇸 The pitch

You found approximately two minutes of decentralised human
objectification unpleasant.

Excellent.

We have secured seed funding.

We have added cameras.

We have added computer vision.

We have added confidence scores.

We have added a subscription.

We have begun integration with the surveillance-capitalism ecosystem.

And, following consultation with counsel, we regret to inform investors
that the principal barrier to scaling computational dick measurement may
not be that civilisation considers the idea ethically indefensible.

It may be that somebody else already owns the patents.

**Mannobjectifier™️**

*The shoe was on the other foot for approximately two minutes, and
instead of learning anything from the experiment, we decided to scale
it.*
