# 🌺 The Opium Empire Has Telemetry

**First created:** 2026-10-02 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-02  
*The British Empire once organised land, labour, law and trade around an extractive commodity. The contemporary information economy has discovered something considerably stranger: the body can be source, market, sensor and feedback mechanism at the same fucking time.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation — Unfortunately The Poppy Field Can Now Look Back

There is a familiar way of telling the history of the Opium Wars:

Britain wanted Chinese tea.

China did not particularly want British manufactures.

Britain sold China opium.

China tried to stop it.

Britain sent gunboats.

All true enough at the scale of a school-history paragraph.

It is also missing most of the machinery.

The opium did not materialise spontaneously aboard British ships. The nineteenth-century trade depended upon an imperial system stretching through India and China: land, cultivators, colonial law, Company agents, processing, auctions, private merchants, Chinese intermediaries, silver, tea, taxation, war and political power.

One population did not simply get "exploited" in the same way as another.

Different territories and populations occupied **different positions inside the same extraction circuit**.

That is the useful historical lesson for Embodied Information Ecology.

Not:

> **SOCIAL MEDIA IS LITERALLY OPIUM AND MARK ZUCKERBERG IS THE EAST INDIA COMPANY.**

Please calm down.

The useful question is:

> **Who supplies the substrate? Who performs the labour? Who controls processing and infrastructure? Who becomes the market? Who carries the externalities? Who captures the value? And what happens when the system can observe the population responding to what it supplies?**

Because the contemporary information economy introduces one rather significant modification to the old imperial diagram.

The field can now look back.

The field can learn.

The field can become angry.

The field can become reassured.

The field can organise.

The field can leave.

The field can notice that you are measuring it and deliberately fuck with your measurements.

The field can also be the customer.

And the factory.

And part of the commodity-production process.

And the sensor telling the system whether yesterday's intervention worked.

There is, unfortunately, a body in the loop.

## 1. 🌺 First, Britain Actually Did This

Before borrowing history as an analogy, we need the history.

Opium cultivation and consumption in India long predated British rule. The East India Company did not invent the poppy, Indian opium, Asian opium markets or state attempts to control them.

What British imperial expansion did was acquire the political and administrative capacity to reorganise a large existing economy around Company interests.

After the Company's rise to territorial power in Bengal and Bihar, and particularly after the 1765 grant of *diwani* revenue rights in Bengal, Bihar and Orissa, the Company increasingly controlled the opium trade. Warren Hastings brought the trade under Company control in 1773. By the end of the century, the Company had moved from contracting arrangements towards direct administration through opium agencies.

Regulation VI of 1799 is particularly useful because it makes the architecture unusually visible.

Private poppy cultivation was prohibited within the regulated system. Cultivation was tied to specified land and Company arrangements; contracted cultivators were required to deliver opium to Company agents at fixed prices, and illicit cultivation, sale and diversion were punishable.

The exact experience of cultivators varied across place and time. Historians have found coercion, evasion, bargaining, local markets and regional differences that do not fit neatly into a single picture of every cultivator being physically forced into every field.

We do not need to flatten that history.

The structural point is stronger when stated accurately:

> **The colonial state used law, territorial authority, purchasing arrangements, administrative knowledge and monopoly power to determine who could produce opium, under what conditions, where much of it could be sold, and who captured the resulting fiscal value.**

Nor did Britain simply prohibit Indians from ever consuming opium while cheerfully exporting it elsewhere.

Domestic Indian consumption existed. Regional markets existed. Regulation changed over time.

The sharper hypocrisy is not **INDIANS WERE FORBIDDEN TO TOUCH THE DRUG**.

It is that colonial government sought extensive control over **production, distribution, possession and revenue**, while turning opium into an extraordinarily valuable imperial export.

By 1855–56, opium contributed 16.9 per cent of British Indian government revenue.

That is not a marginal sideline.

That is fiscal architecture.

## 2. 🫖 Meanwhile, Britain Has A Tea Problem

Britain wanted Chinese goods.

A lot.

Tea became enormously important to British consumption. Silk and porcelain mattered too.

The problem was that Qing China did not provide Britain with a conveniently symmetrical market for British goods. The resulting trade imbalance meant silver flowed towards China.

Indian opium offered an answer.

The Company could control production and auction opium in Calcutta. Private merchants could then carry it onwards towards the Chinese market. Chinese purchasers paid for opium with silver. That silver could circulate back through the trading system and help finance purchases of Chinese goods.

This institutional separation was extremely convenient.

The Company did not need to stand personally on the Chinese coast wearing a hat marked:

> **HELLO I AM THE GOVERNMENT OPIUM SMUGGLER**

It could control the profitable upstream architecture through India and auction the commodity to private merchants who carried it onwards.

China had prohibited opium. British and other merchants nevertheless continued the illegal trade.

By the early nineteenth century the scale had become enormous. Britain's own National Archives describes British merchants as smuggling more than 1,000 tonnes of opium into China annually by the period immediately preceding the First Opium War.

In 1839, Imperial Commissioner Lin Zexu moved against the trade at Canton, confiscating and destroying large quantities of opium and requiring traders to undertake not to bring illegal goods into China.

Britain responded within a wider escalating confrontation with military force.

The First Opium War ended in British victory and the 1842 Treaty of Nanking. Among other things, China ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain, opened the commercial settlement further and paid indemnities including compensation connected with the destroyed opium.

A second war followed in the 1850s, producing further coercive opening of China.

There is no need to improve this history with a grand psychological conspiracy.

We do not need to claim:

> Britain devised opium because it had scientifically calculated that addiction would psychologically subjugate the entire Chinese population.

The documented system is quite sufficiently grim.

Britain benefited from an Indian opium economy organised through colonial power.

British and associated commercial networks sold a prohibited addictive commodity into China.

The trade helped address Britain's commercial problem with Chinese imports and generated substantial revenue.

When Qing authorities attempted to suppress the trade, imperial power became military power.

That is enough.

## 3. 🗺️ One Empire, Several Positions In The Circuit

The historical system can be simplified like this:

**Indian land**  
↓  
**Indian cultivators**  
↓  
**colonial production regime**  
↓  
**Company processing and control**  
↓  
**Calcutta auction**  
↓  
**private merchant networks**  
↓  
**Chinese market**  
↓  
**consumption**  
↓  
**silver and commercial value**  
↓  
**British imperial fiscal and trading system**

That simplification loses local complexity.

It gains one useful analytical property.

It forces us to stop using **exploitation** as though it describes one position.

The cultivator is not occupying the same position as the consumer.

The consumer is not occupying the same position as the merchant.

The merchant is not occupying the same position as the colonial administrator.

The administrator is not occupying the same position as the government receiving revenue.

They are connected.

They are not interchangeable.

So when Embodied Information Ecology encounters a contemporary extraction system, we can ask the same questions:

- What is the substrate?
- Who performs the labour?
- Who controls the infrastructure?
- Who determines the conditions of participation?
- Who processes the material?
- Who receives the product?
- Who becomes the market?
- Who carries the externality?
- Who captures the value?
- Who possesses enough power to change the rules?

This is already better than:

> tech company bad because data.

But we have one further problem.

The contemporary positions do not stay politely separated by several thousand miles.

## 4. 🛢️ Data Is Not The New Oil, Please Stop Saying That

"Data is the new oil" survives because it captures something real.

Oil can be:

**extracted → processed → refined → combined → transported → sold → used**

Data can also pass through extraction, processing and commercialisation.

Lovely.

Unfortunately oil does not respond to the refinery.

Oil does not become anxious because you showed it something.

Oil does not calm down because its friend sent it a message.

Oil does not buy a handbag because you correctly inferred that its relationship has ended.

Oil does not learn.

Oil does not radicalise.

Oil does not unionise.

Oil does not decide it has had enough of the fucking pipeline and delete the app.

Oil does not notice that you are measuring it and deliberately alter its behaviour.

People do.

That difference is not decorative.

It changes the system.

Behavioural information is generated by organisms capable of perception, interpretation, memory, learning, adaptation and action.

The source is not a passive deposit.

The source has a nervous system.

## 5. 🫀 The Raw Material Is Not Data

Consider an ordinary day.

A person:

wakes up.

Checks the time.

Messages a friend.

Walks somewhere.

Searches for a headache.

Looks at shoes.

Does not buy the shoes.

Reads an article.

Watches half a video.

Closes it.

Opens another.

Laughs.

Gets angry.

Checks a train.

Changes route.

Deletes a draft.

Buys lunch.

Sends a photograph.

Ignores a notification.

Returns to it three hours later.

Goes home.

From inside the person, these are parts of a life.

They have meanings.

Some are trivial.

Some are intimate.

Some are accidental.

Some are deliberate.

Some are responses to bodily states.

Some are responses to other people.

Some make sense only because of everything that happened yesterday.

A sufficiently instrumented digital system does not receive **the life**.

It receives representations produced from observable events.

Depending upon the system and permissions involved, these might include clicks, searches, viewing duration, purchases, device information, interactions, social connections, location-related information, language or other behavioural traces.

Then processing begins.

**embodied activity**  
↓  
**observable event**  
↓  
**recorded data**  
↓  
**aggregation**  
↓  
**inference**  
↓  
**prediction / classification / ranking**  
↓  
**commercial, administrative or strategic use**

This is why saying **data is extracted from people** is sometimes conceptually too late.

Data is already a representation.

The deeper substrate is:

> **human beings living lives from which observable information can be produced.**

The raw material is not "data" in the same way crude oil waits underground.

The informational material is generated through embodied activity.

That is why the body belongs in the diagram.

## 6. 🏭 Congratulations, The Poppy Is Doing Unpaid Data Entry

The contemporary participant can occupy several positions simultaneously.

They may be a customer because they receive a service.

They may be a content producer because they upload something.

They may be an observed system because their behaviour produces telemetry.

They may be a contributor because their corrections, ratings, reactions or other interactions improve a system.

They may become part of an audience segment.

They may generate signals used to predict future behaviour.

But precision matters.

Not all information enters the system in the same way.

There is a difference between:

- information a person deliberately provides;
- behaviour observed while providing a service;
- information obtained from another source;
- information inferred from multiple observations;
- information required for the service to function;
- information retained because it has additional commercial value.

Those distinctions matter legally.

They matter technically.

They matter ethically.

And they matter informationally.

The FTC's 2024 examination of major social-media and video-streaming companies found extensive collection and monetisation of personal information, including data obtained from brokers and information concerning users and non-users. It also found personal information being fed into algorithms, analytics and AI systems, while some automated uses offered little or no meaningful opt-out.

That does not mean every click is literally sold as a tiny parcel to a mysterious man called Advertiser Dave.

The more interesting commercial output may be something derived from the observations.

Which brings us to the factory.

## 7. 📦 The Commodity May Be A Model Of The Person

There is a persistent folk model of surveillance capitalism:

> Company takes **MY DATA**.
>
> Company sells **MY DATA**.
>
> Advertiser Dave now possesses a spreadsheet containing **MY DATA**.

Sometimes data is transferred or shared.

Sometimes that description is directionally useful.

But it can obscure the more sophisticated product.

The commercially valuable output can be:

- an inferred interest;
- an audience category;
- a propensity estimate;
- a recommendation;
- a ranking;
- a prediction;
- a personalised advertising opportunity;
- an aggregate behavioural model;
- access to a population selected according to inferred characteristics.

So the pipeline may look more like:

**human activity**  
→ **measurement**  
→ **statistical relationship**  
→ **inference**  
→ **prediction**  
→ **actionable informational product**

The value is not necessarily the observation itself.

It may be the system's increased ability to answer:

> **Given what we have observed, what is likely to happen next?**

Or:

> **Which output should we present next?**

And that second question changes everything.

Because the processed commodity can return to the source.

## 8. 🔴 And Then The Commodity Comes Back

A conventional extractive diagram can end comfortably at the market.

**resource → extraction → processing → commodity → sale**

Our diagram cannot.

A recommender system observes behaviour partly in order to determine what to recommend.

An advertising system uses information partly in order to determine what advertising to display.

A notification system can use information partly in order to determine when or what to send.

A ranking system determines what appears first.

A personalised feed changes what the person encounters.

So:

**person**  
↓  
**behaviour**  
↓  
**observation**  
↓  
**data / inference / model**  
↓  
**prediction or ranking**  
↓  
**returned informational environment**  
↓  
**person**

And the person does something.

Perhaps nothing changes.

Perhaps they ignore it.

Perhaps they click.

Perhaps they buy something.

Perhaps they learn something useful.

Perhaps they find a friend.

Perhaps they become furious.

Perhaps they leave.

Perhaps they remain for another hour.

Whatever happens next can itself become observable.

So the full circuit is:

**embodied state**  
→ **behaviour**  
→ **observation**  
→ **model**  
→ **intervention**  
→ **changed informational environment**  
→ **embodied response**  
→ **new behaviour**  
→ **new observation**

♻️

This does not establish that every recommendation is manipulation.

It does not establish that personalisation determines behaviour.

It does not abolish human agency.

It establishes something much more modest and much more important:

> **Observation can become intervention.**

The system does not merely measure an environment.

Its output can become part of that environment.

## 9. 🪞 The Field Has Started Responding To Agricultural Policy

Now we have a measurement problem.

Suppose a system observes:

> People who behave like this tend to remain engaged when shown X.

So it shows more X.

That changes what those people encounter.

Their subsequent behaviour occurs in the environment partly constructed by the recommendation.

The system observes that subsequent behaviour.

It updates.

The next environment changes again.

This is cybernetics.

It is also why behavioural telemetry cannot automatically be read as a pristine sample of some pre-existing independent human preference.

Observed behaviour is produced through a coupled system:

**organism + history + social environment + interface + affordances + constraints + available information + feedback**

This does not make the preference fake.

It makes the environment relevant.

The European Commission's 2026 preliminary Digital Services Act findings concerning TikTok, Instagram and Facebook are useful here precisely because they identify concrete design mechanisms rather than invoking spooky mind control: infinite scroll, autoplay, push notifications and highly personalised recommender systems. The Commission's findings remain preliminary, not final adjudications.

The important systems point does not depend upon proving that every person is helpless before these mechanisms.

If interface design, ranking and recommendation had no capacity whatsoever to alter behaviour, there would be very little commercial reason to optimise them.

Design exists because environments participate in behaviour.

Humans still participate too.

We are allowed to discuss both at once.

## 10. 🌾 LOOK HOW MUCH EVERYONE LOVES POPPIES

Imagine we construct a field.

We optimise the soil for poppies.

We provide poppy seed.

We reward poppy yield.

We make poppy cultivation unusually easy.

We continually test which agricultural interventions produce more poppies.

We measure successful poppy production.

We discard interventions that produce fewer poppies.

We scale interventions that produce more.

Several years later, we stand proudly beside the field and announce:

> **Extraordinary news.**
>
> **Our measurements reveal that this ecosystem naturally produces a shitload of poppies.**

Bro.

You built a poppy field.

This is the epistemological problem with treating engagement as though it were a transparent measurement of unconstrained human preference.

A system optimises for engagement.

It tests which outputs produce engagement.

Successful engagement-producing designs propagate.

People engage.

The engagement is measured.

And then:

> **Look how much people want engagement.**

Perhaps they do.

But the observation alone cannot tell us what the same population would have done inside a differently constructed environment.

This is not an argument against measuring behaviour.

It is an argument for remembering **what system generated the measurement**.

The observer has not necessarily stood outside the field.

The observer may have installed the irrigation.

## 11. 🧠 The Extraction Target Has Expanded

Advertising has long cared about a basic behavioural question:

> **What might this person buy?**

Contemporary computational systems can make many additional regularities valuable.

What captures attention?

What predicts return?

Which items are treated as similar?

Which language patterns recur?

Which signals correlate with a preference?

Which output tends to produce another action?

How do people perform particular informational tasks?

How are concepts associated across enormous collections of human-produced material?

Machine learning and generative AI make this especially important.

Human-produced texts, images, code, annotations, classifications, corrections, interactions and other informational artefacts can contain statistical regularities produced through human cognition, culture and social behaviour.

Be precise.

We have not:

> **downloaded human cognition.**

The soul is not sitting in AWS waiting for a licence agreement.

The defensible proposition is more interesting:

> **Human informational artefacts and behavioural traces can contain learnable regularities produced by embodied human cognition, culture and social interaction.**

A system capable of learning from those regularities therefore makes valuable not merely:

**what humans bought**

but potentially:

**patterns in what humans made, said, selected, corrected, associated and did.**

The extractable informational terrain expands.

And the people producing it remain alive inside the environment to which computational outputs return.

## 12. 🧑‍🌾🧑‍💻🛒 Unfortunately You Are India And China Before Breakfast

Now return to the nineteenth-century diagram.

Its populations occupied different positions.

Indian cultivators produced much of the commodity.

Company institutions controlled important parts of production and revenue.

Private merchants transported it.

Chinese consumers constituted the principal external market.

Silver and commercial advantage moved through the imperial system.

The positions were connected but analytically distinguishable.

A contemporary digital participant can collapse several positions into one body.

Before breakfast, one person may be:

### Producer

They create text, photographs, video, code, conversation or other informational material.

### Contributor

Their ratings, corrections, selections, reports or interactions may improve a service or its models.

### Observed system

Their behaviour produces telemetry.

### Raw-material source

Observed or supplied information becomes input to analysis, inference or modelling.

### Consumer

They receive the service, feed, model output, entertainment or information.

### Market

Advertisers or other actors may pay to reach people matching particular characteristics.

### Experimental environment

Interfaces and outputs may be tested against observable responses.

### Feedback sensor

Their next action supplies information about the previous output.

The nineteenth-century extraction circuit sprawled across territories, ships, warehouses, auction rooms, bureaucracies and markets.

The contemporary circuit can compress an astonishing number of functions into:

> **one phone.**

And behind the phone:

> **one body.**

The plantation, factory, auction house, market and consumer have not literally become identical.

But their informational functions can now overlap inside the same embodied participant.

That is the thing worth noticing.

## 13. ⚠️ Where The Opium Analogy Breaks

Historical analogy becomes useless when it starts eating the history.

So:

The British Indian opium economy involved colonial territorial rule.

It involved monopoly and coercive legal authority.

It involved an addictive physical drug.

The China trade involved large-scale illegal commerce.

The Opium Wars involved military violence and imperial treaty imposition.

Contemporary digital systems are not automatically equivalent to any of those things.

Using Instagram is not being colonised by the East India Company.

A push notification is not a gunboat.

An advertising auction is not the Treaty of Nanking.

A recommender system is not chemically identical to opium.

Users receive enormous real benefits from digital technologies.

People use them deliberately.

People resist their affordances.

People leave platforms.

People organise through them.

People form friendships through them.

People learn through them.

People make art through them.

People also use the same infrastructure to challenge powerful institutions.

Good.

Keep all of that.

The analogy is not:

**these things are morally identical.**

It is:

> **Both systems become more legible when we identify the substrate, positions in the extraction circuit, control of infrastructure, direction of value, distribution of externalities and power to alter the environment.**

And the contemporary case adds:

> **What happens when the source of valuable information is itself an adaptive participant receiving outputs from the system processing that information?**

That is the new bit.

Do not ruin it by pretending TikTok invaded Canton.

## 14. ♻️ The Body Is Not Outside The Information System

The crude information model looks like:

**person → data → computer**

The person produces information.

The machine processes it.

Finished.

But an embodied information ecology looks more like:

**body ↔ interface ↔ computational system ↔ institutions ↔ other bodies ↔ wider environment**

Information travels in several directions.

The body receives information.

The body experiences it.

The body interprets it through prior knowledge, physiology, memory, social position and immediate context.

The body changes state.

The person acts.

That action changes the environment.

The changed environment becomes information for other bodies and systems.

Some of those systems observe the response.

Some adapt.

New information returns.

Again:

**information → embodied experience → response → environmental change → new information**

The body is therefore not merely the endpoint of information delivery.

Nor is it merely the origin of data.

It is an active component in a recursive system.

This is the central proposition of **The Body In The Loop**:

> **When information systems observe human behaviour and return outputs capable of changing the environment in which subsequent behaviour occurs, the human body belongs inside the system model.**

You cannot safely draw the box around the computer and leave the person outside it.

The person is one of the places where the feedback happens.

## 16. 🛡️ Once The Environment Is Observable, Other People Can Learn It Too

There is one further consequence.

A commercially useful feedback environment does not exist in geopolitical isolation.

NATO's 2024 approach to countering information threats explicitly treats the openness, interconnectedness and digitalisation of Allied societies as characteristics hostile actors may exploit. It identifies social media and online services as part of the environment in which hostile information activity occurs and describes information-environment assessment in terms of actors, behaviour, content, degree and effect.

Commercial platforms and hostile information operations are not the same thing.

Do not collapse them.

A company optimising recommendations for engagement is not thereby conducting an enemy information operation.

But an environment that makes behaviour highly observable, populations highly networked, information rapidly distributable and responses measurable may create capabilities that more than one kind of actor can learn to use.

That gives us a security question downstream of the political-economy question:

> **When a society builds infrastructure capable of observing behavioural response at scale, who else can learn from the resulting environment?**

The opium empire had ships.

The feedback environment has telemetry.

Different mechanism.

Same reason to map the infrastructure before assuming only its intended operator matters.

## 17. 🧭 Questions For Embodied Information Ecology

When examining an information system, ask:

- What human activity produces the information?
- What becomes observable?
- What is deliberately disclosed?
- What is passively observed?
- What is obtained from elsewhere?
- What is inferred?
- Which distinctions between those categories are being hidden by the generic word **data**?
- Who performs the informational labour?
- Who owns or controls the infrastructure?
- Who determines the conditions of participation?
- Who processes the observations?
- What is the commercially, administratively or strategically valuable output?
- Is the valuable object the raw observation, an inference, a prediction, a model, access to an audience, or something else?
- Who receives the value?
- Who carries the externalities?
- What output returns to the observed population?
- Does that output alter what the population encounters?
- Can the altered environment change subsequent behaviour?
- Is that subsequent behaviour observed?
- Does it become training information for the next intervention?
- Is behaviour produced inside an optimised environment subsequently presented as evidence of what people naturally prefer?
- What counterfactual environment are we comparing it with?
- Can participants inspect or contest important inferences?
- Can they receive the useful function without supplying unnecessary information?
- What happens when people know they are being observed?
- What happens when they deliberately change behaviour because they know they are being observed?
- What happens when another commercial, political or hostile actor learns the same environment?

And finally:

> **Are we looking at information extracted from a population?**
>
> **Or are we looking at a coupled system in which extraction itself has become one of the forces shaping the population from which the next information will be produced?**

Those are not the same system.

The second one has telemetry.

And a pulse.

### Evidence boundary

The historical sources establish the component architecture of British Indian opium production, imperial revenue, illegal trade into China and military-commercial coercion.

The contemporary sources establish component mechanisms including extensive behavioural-data collection and monetisation, automated processing, personalised recommender systems, engagement-oriented design and the strategic significance of digital information environments.

They do **not** establish that contemporary technology companies are equivalent to the East India Company, that behavioural data and opium are equivalent commodities, or that every personalised information system deliberately seeks to subjugate its users.

The comparison in this node is **systems analysis**.

Its purpose is to identify positions in an extraction circuit and then ask what changes when the source of valuable information is an adaptive embodied participant capable of receiving and responding to the system's outputs.

That distinction stays in.
