# ✉️ No One Writes Letters Like They Used To
**First created:** 2026-10-22 | **Last updated:** 2026-10-22  
*On parliamentary pigeons, gateway actors, safeguarding, anti-money-laundering, infrastructure sovereignty, and the awkward fact that observation changes the thing being observed.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

An American technology contractor writes to British parliamentarians who have criticised it. Some recipients interpret the letters as pressure. The letters become news. The news raises further questions about the contractor's political relationships. And a British voter, who might reasonably have preferred to ask the governing party about its decisions, finds herself discussing the contractor's stationery instead.

**No one writes letters like they used to.**

This is not primarily a node about whether Palantir is good or bad, nor an attempt to attribute every Labour decision to Peter Mandelson. It is about **observation becoming intervention**: how attempts to manage political information change the information ecology, and how lobbying regulation itself becomes something lobbyists adapt to.

The question is not whether influence can be abolished. It is whether the public can identify, interrogate and contest the influence exerted upon its government **before the resulting contracts become practically irreversible**.

Because the infrastructure may hold children's information. Because its owners may be politically connected. Because its financing may be complicated. Because national-security restrictions can reduce what the public sees. And because a public body that cannot migrate its systems may have little practical leverage when a serious concern finally surfaces.

**SCRUTINISE THEM ALL.** Not only the contractor that wrote a letter. Not only Labour. Not only the Conservatives. Not only the people whose names appear in an Epstein file. All the relevant owners, suppliers, intermediaries, financial arrangements and decision-makers — using consistent standards.

---

## ✨ Key Features

- A letter is an intervention, not merely a container for propositions.
- Recipients have contexts, audiences and routes for redistribution that senders do not control.
- Political parties, consultants, contractors and departments are distinct actors; their relationships require reconstruction rather than assumption.
- Political donations, legal pressure, expertise, employment relationships and procurement can interact without being identical activities.
- Disclosure changes behaviour. A workable transparency system anticipates displacement rather than merely banning a label.
- The right to petition government is worth defending; unequal capacity to influence it is worth investigating.
- Gateway actors can connect political, commercial and international networks without representing the entirety of lobbying.
- AML, safeguarding, beneficial ownership and national-security scrutiny are complementary duties, not competing hobbies.
- The state must be able to exit an infrastructure contract **in practice**, not merely on paper.
- Credible documentary leads can warrant proportionate due diligence without establishing wrongdoing.

## ✉️ The Post Has Arrived

Reporting on 22 October 2026 described letters sent on 3 June by Palantir UK chief executive Louis Mosley to Jeremy Corbyn and Rachael Maskell. The letters reportedly rehearsed parliamentary criticisms and offered meetings. Corbyn characterised the correspondence as an attempt to intimidate critics. Palantir's position, as reported, was that it sought to correct inaccurate claims. These are competing accounts of purpose and effect, not interchangeable findings.

Clive Lewis's separate correspondence with former Labour deputy leader Tom Watson, now associated with Palantir, raises an adjacent but distinct question about political networks and the movement of personnel between party leadership and commercial advocacy. Do not merge different letters, authors or recipients into one imaginary document. Obtain the complete texts before performing close rhetorical analysis.

There is nothing inherently improper about a company disputing an MP's account of its business. MPs are not exempt from correction. But the form matters. Individually addressed letters which itemise someone's public criticisms can carry an additional message: *we are paying attention to you*. Whether that is welcome engagement, an attempt at persuasion, or perceived pressure depends partly on the recipient and the relationship.

And, inconveniently for any sender who expected privacy, MPs are capable of showing people their post.

**Source:** [Anadolu Agency: ‘UK lawmakers say letters from Palantir were intended to intimidate critics’](https://aa.com.tr/en/europe/uk-lawmakers-say-letters-from-palantir-were-intended-to-intimidate-critics/4097386).

## 🧿 You Have Been Observed. Congratulations.

An actor observes criticism; selects a recipient; chooses a channel; sends a response. That response becomes an observation for the recipient, who brings different prior knowledge, political obligations, personal experience and social relationships to the exchange.

The sender's intended meaning is not identical to the recipient's interpretation. Neither is identical to what a journalist, constituent, parliamentary colleague or campaign organiser subsequently extracts from it.

**Transmission ≠ interpretation ≠ behavioural outcome.**

A corporate letter may aim to correct the record, establish a relationship, discourage further criticism, create a defensible paper trail, or communicate to a wider audience. We cannot infer which objective dominated from its existence alone. We can, however, examine what happened after it entered the receiving system.

### 🪿 The pigeon has friends

An MP with established relationships across grassroots organising and independent journalism is not an isolated mailbox. A message may travel:

**Company → MP → reporter → campaigning networks → public discussion → parliamentary questions → further company response.**

This is a *possible propagation pathway*, not a verified map of every transmission in this case. The point is structural: **the letter becomes fresh evidence about the sender's conduct, regardless of whether the sender wanted that outcome**.

A communication intended to narrow controversy can widen it. Alternatively, a sender may welcome publicity. The outcome alone cannot settle intent.

## 🏛️ Excuse Me, Who Is Actually Governing?

A British voter has questions for the government. How did a contractor become embedded in public functions? Who selected it? Who advised ministers? What changed between Conservative and Labour administrations? Which contractual, technical and procurement dependencies constrained subsequent decisions?

Those are questions for the state and its political leadership, not only for the supplier.

The MoPAC/Palantir controversy belongs in this reconstruction, but its legal findings and precise contractual history must be stated from the judgments and procurement records, not compressed into a slogan. A decision inherited from an earlier administration is not automatically evidence of improper lobbying; equally, inheritance does not make a later government's decisions unaccountable.

**The democratic principal remains the public.** Ministers and public bodies must explain the decisions for which they are responsible, including why a supplier was retained or a process lawfully departed from open competition.

## 🕸️ One Man Cannot Personally Be the Entire Lobbying Industry

Peter Mandelson, Global Counsel, the Tony Blair Institute, political donors, advisers and commercial suppliers may all be relevant to particular investigations. They are not a single organisation, and association is not proof of a coordinated scheme.

The proper unit of inquiry is a **documented network of representations and decisions**, not a celebrity villain.

For each proposed connection, ask:

1. **Access:** Who met whom, in what capacity, and when?
2. **Representation:** Which client's or institution's interests were being advanced?
3. **Content:** What specific policy, contract or regulatory outcome was sought?
4. **Transmission:** What papers, meetings, donations or personnel relationships carried the argument?
5. **Decision:** What did the responsible institution actually decide, and why?
6. **Attribution:** Is there evidence that the representation affected the outcome, or only that the positions align?
7. **Continuity:** What survived the transition from opposition to government, or between administrations?

Government-published Mandelson appointment material expressly identifies public interest in references to Global Counsel and Palantir, and describes differing redaction rationales. That is a reason to inspect the documentary record carefully—not permission to fill redactions with invented conclusions.

## 🕸️ Mandelson as a Gateway, Not the Whole Building

Mandelson is interesting here not because he is somehow *all of lobbying, UK edition*. He is interesting as a **potential gateway actor**: someone whose longstanding political relationships and commercial activities may connect networks that would otherwise have fewer direct routes to one another.

A gateway is a question about *position and transmission*, not a moral verdict. The relevant concepts are brokerage, access, introductions, professional credibility, movement between offices, and the persistence of relationships after a formal role ends. An investigation might ask which contacts were made, on whose behalf, with what disclosures, and whether those contacts affected a public decision. It cannot jump from *he knows people* to *he procured the outcome*.

Global Counsel matters as one potential institutional channel. So do party advisers, former ministers, donors, consultants, policy institutes and public bodies. The point is to map **specific documented edges**, not draw one very dramatic spider and label it *British Politics*.

The same methodology must be applied to Conservative networks and to influence structures that have no equivalent single gateway actor. One person may help an investigator find the door. **The door is not the building.**

## 🏗️ The Other Contractors Would Like You to Keep Looking at Palantir

Palantir is conspicuous because it wrote letters. Other technology suppliers need not write anything nearly as interesting to acquire a significant position in the British state's operating environment.

Consider the differences among suppliers:

| Supplier or sector | Questions that deserve examination |
|---|---|
| Palantir | Data integration, analytics, public-sector deployments, access and procurement |
| Oracle | Databases, enterprise applications, cloud, legacy systems, migration costs |
| Microsoft | Licensing, operating systems, productivity platforms, cloud and identity dependencies |
| Amazon Web Services | Hosting concentration, infrastructure dependencies, portability and subcontracting |
| Google | Cloud and AI services, data handling, interoperability and public contracts |
| UK and European suppliers | Barriers to entry, domestic technical capability, ownership and procurement access |

These firms do not all sell the same thing. That is the point. A state can become dependent on a vendor without that vendor owning the building, the fibre or the physical servers. It may depend on a database schema, an identity layer, proprietary integrations, staff trained on one platform, or a procurement architecture that makes alternatives implausible.

**Intensity of publicity is not a measure of the scale of dependency.** Scrutiny must follow public exposure and public risk, not simply the most entertaining corporate stationery.

## 🦈 The Software Wars Did Not End When Government Bought a Subscription

Enterprise technology has a history of aggressive competition, consolidation, licensing disputes and attempts to retain customers. Microsoft's antitrust history and Oracle's acquisition history — including PeopleSoft, Siebel and Sun — belong in this background. Their details require proper historical sourcing; the fact of competition is not proof of misconduct in any present contract.

But the economics matter. Once a public service has configured workflows, databases, interfaces, training and contractual terms around a supplier, replacement may require far more than buying a new product. Data export is not necessarily functional migration. A contractual right to leave is not a staffed, tested, funded exit capability.

A state that cannot credibly switch supplier cannot negotiate from the same position as one that can. **Vendor lock-in is therefore a governance issue, not merely a technical inconvenience.**

## 🕸️ Blair, Ellison, Oracle — and the Difference Between a Relationship and a Finding

The Tony Blair Institute, Larry Ellison's philanthropy, Oracle, Tony Blair's continuing political relationships, Euan Blair's Multiverse and UK government technology procurement are **distinct subjects of investigation**. The relevant public reporting about Ellison-associated funding to the Institute warrants a sourced financial and governance map: amounts, dates, donor entities, declared partnerships, policy work, government engagements and conflict safeguards.

Oracle's UK contracts and technical footprint need their *own* register. Multiverse's ownership, public-sector engagements and publicly funded skills provision need a separate register. A father–son relationship is not a corporate merger. Nor does a philanthropic donation automatically demonstrate control over a government decision.

Nevertheless, the fact that a relationship is lawful, familial or philanthropic does not place it outside ordinary due diligence. The right question is: **what interests, access, representations, conflicts or dependencies are material to the public decision?**

For each proposed connection, record the date, source, entities, actual relationship, relevant public decision, and whether any influence is evidenced or merely hypothesised. Do not fill gaps with assumptions. Do not use gaps as an excuse to stop asking.

## 🔎 The Epstein Documents: A Due-Diligence Lead Is Not a Conviction

Publicly released Epstein records contain several leads relevant to investigating the nature, if any, of relationships among Jeffrey Epstein, Larry Ellison, Oracle-associated individuals and financial institutions. **Document identifiers matter** because they allow a reader to test the underlying claim rather than inherit an atmosphere.

| Identifier | What the record raises |
|---|---|
| `EFTA01976881` | A 2 November 2012 email in which Boris Nikolic asks Epstein how well he knows Larry Ellison. The question does not supply Epstein's answer. |
| `EFTA01460390` | A June 2013 Deutsche Bank CRM communication listing a **Jeffrey Epstein of Oracle Corp** as an active lead, alongside other Oracle-associated names. The identity and accuracy of the corporate affiliation require verification; a CRM lead is not proof of employment. |
| `EFTA01255972` / `EFTA01255973` | A multi-account banking transaction report containing a roughly $45.7m line referencing the Lawrence Ellison Foundation. The displayed record does not establish that Epstein managed or controlled the transaction. |
| `EFTA01880159` | An email joking about Larry Ellison's purchase of an island in comparison with Epstein's island. A reference is not proof of a personal relationship. |
| `EFTA00711362` | A forwarded wealth-ranking article that mentions Ellison. This establishes a documentary mention, not direct contact. |

A separately reported conversation between Epstein and Ehud Barak discussing Tony Blair should be retrieved in its original audio or transcript, with publisher, date, surrounding conversation and wording verified before the node asserts what it shows. Familiar references to a person in a conversation are not automatically evidence that the person participated in it.

**These records are sufficient to formulate due-diligence questions. They are not sufficient to allege criminal conduct by Ellison, Blair, Oracle, their children or associated organisations.** Nor does an individual named Jeffrey Epstein in a bank CRM automatically establish that the entry identifies the convicted financier.

The standard is not *prove every relationship before investigating it*. It is *investigate documentary leads proportionately and record what the evidence actually establishes*. That is the entire point of due diligence.

## 🛢️ Andy Burnham, Why Did You Sign Off on Jackdaw Like You Did?

A very timely illustration arrived on **21 October 2026**.

The UK government announced that the North Sea Transition Authority had granted production consent for the already-licensed Jackdaw gas field after Energy Secretary **Miatta Fahnbulleh agreed** that consent could be granted. Prime Minister **Andy Burnham publicly welcomed and defended** the decision. Strictly speaking, the published decision-making roles matter: the Prime Minister's political endorsement should not be silently substituted for the Secretary of State's statutory agreement or the regulator's consent.

The government's case included domestic gas supply, energy security, jobs and a proposed Energy Wealth Fund intended to channel North Sea revenues into the energy transition. The government stated that the decision was compatible with climate obligations; opponents dispute the wisdom and climate consequences of further fossil-fuel production. A prior approval had been revisited following litigation and the requirement to consider downstream emissions.

This is precisely where the question about lobbying becomes concrete:

- Which oil and gas interests made representations, directly or through intermediaries?
- What did the public consultation receive, and how were those submissions evaluated?
- Which representations concerned jobs, security of supply, domestic prices, taxation, climate impacts and alternative energy investment?
- What evidence did ministers rely upon, and what alternatives were considered?
- What relationship, **if any**, did party donations, professional lobbying or prior consultancy contacts have to the decision-making process?
- Which records permit a voter to distinguish commercial advocacy from independent departmental analysis?

**The coincidence of a policy decision with an industry's preferred outcome does not establish that the industry caused it.** But it is reasonable to ask how the decision was reached, particularly where the public has outstanding questions about political access and intermediary networks.

A voter can disagree with the outcome and still want the complete reasons. A voter can agree with the outcome and want exactly the same thing.

The timing makes Jackdaw a useful contemporary worked example, not evidence of an unproven Global Counsel–Jackdaw causal chain.

## 💷 The Carrot, the Stick and the Very Expensive Lunch

Lobbying is not one instrument. It is a repertoire: meetings, evidence, employment, policy advice, donations, publicity, legal challenge and sometimes threats of litigation. UKLFI's legal and advocacy interventions and the less visible machinery of energy-sector lobbying offer comparative lines of inquiry, but their mechanisms and legal statuses must be documented separately.

The same organisation can offer valuable expertise while arguing for a commercially favourable outcome. The same political actor can receive both useful information and pressure. A government may genuinely agree with a lobbyist's policy case. Those possibilities must remain open.

The question is not simply whether a particular contact was 'positive' or 'negative'. It is **which costs, benefits, assumptions and options entered the decision-maker's environment**, and whether the public can reconstruct their significance.

## 🗳️ The Right to Petition Is Not a Corporate Invention

Start earlier than the public-affairs consultancy.

Petitioning the Crown and Parliament, organised reform movements, abolitionism, suffrage, trade unionism and disability-rights campaigning all illustrate how people have sought to influence institutions. Commercial lobbying, patronage and chartered-company influence have their own long British histories; the East India Company makes an instructive, if rather alarming, example of the entanglement of commercial and state power.

It would be an extraordinary democratic own goal to respond to corporate lobbying by constructing rules that also make it harder for a disabled constituent, trade union, local campaign or small voluntary organisation to be heard.

**The right to seek influence is broadly shared. The capacity to purchase sustained access is not.**

That asymmetry—not the mere act of petitioning—is the constitutional design problem.

## 🪄 Good News, Chaps. We Have Abolished Lobbying.

Parliament abolishes lobbying.

By Tuesday, everyone is engaged in *strategic stakeholder relations*.

By Wednesday, the lobbyists have become *independent policy advisers*.

By Thursday, somebody has discovered an entirely new form of lunch.

Nobody is lobbying anymore. The stationery has been updated. Democracy is saved.

Except, of course, it isn't.

If regulation targets a job title or narrow channel rather than the **function of attempting to influence public decisions**, some activity will migrate to other channels. This does not mean all research, consultancy, litigation or advocacy is covert lobbying. It means definitions must distinguish legitimate activity from attempts to evade disclosure without pretending that a change of label changes the underlying incentives.

Political donation reform has the same problem: caps, eligibility, corporate funding, overseas connections, cryptocurrency and third-party spending must be considered together. Closing one route may increase the attractiveness of another.

## 💷 Anti-Money-Laundering: Who Owns It, Who Paid, Who Benefits?

The AML point is not a decorative footnote to procurement. It is a question about whether the state can trace the money and identify the people who exercise effective control over infrastructure it may soon be unable to replace.

A risk scenario deserves examination:

**Private control → complex ownership and financing → strategically essential service → restricted public visibility → reduced practical ability to challenge or exit.**

If an owner or intermediary attempted to use national-security confidentiality as cover for financial opacity, would independent authorities still be able to inspect the beneficial ownership, source of funds, related-party transactions, significant subcontracting and payment flows? What if the ownership changed five years into the contract? What if money moved through several jurisdictions? Who notices? Who can compel records? Who is institutionally independent of the people who awarded the contract?

A national-security classification **does not itself confer immunity from AML law, criminal investigation or authorised regulatory scrutiny**. Nor is an opaque public website evidence that regulators lack access. The risk lies in fragmented information, weak verification, overbroad confidentiality claims, institutional dependence and gaps between nominal authority and practical oversight.

This is why a useful register needs more than a logo and a contract value:

- Verified beneficial owners and persons exercising significant control, with relevant ownership changes tracked over time.
- Material lenders, investors, parent companies, subsidiaries and significant subcontractors.
- Source-of-funds and source-of-wealth checks where proportionate to the risk.
- Relevant politically exposed persons, family members and close associates, assessed under applicable risk-based standards rather than presumed culpable.
- Material related-party payments, unusual transaction structures and cross-border exposure.
- A clearly identified independent body able to inspect sensitive records under secure arrangements.
- Real remedies if a supplier obstructs lawful scrutiny, including workable continuity and exit arrangements.

The state does not need to publish a classified network diagram to establish that someone independent can audit who owns the network and where the money goes.

**Classify operational secrets where necessary. Do not classify away financial accountability.**

## 🧒 Children Are Not a Procurement Externality

If a system processes children's information, safeguarding cannot be confined to a supplier's glossy cyber-security slide. It must include what information is collected, whether it is necessary, who can access it, where it travels, who can compel disclosure, how long it is retained, and what happens if the supplier becomes unsuitable or the contract fails.

The distinction between *sensitive personal data* in everyday speech and the legal categories of *special-category data* or *criminal-offence data* matters. So do the roles of controller and processor. The precise legal duties depend on the deployment, not the company name on the contract.

Relevant checks should include staff and privileged-access controls, subcontractor permissions, audit logs, incident response, international transfers, data minimisation, retention, safeguarding escalation and a realistic migration plan. Where credible allegations concern a relevant owner or controlling decision-maker, assess the actual relationship to the system, the available evidence and the proportionate protective steps. **Allegations are not convictions; safeguarding is not required to wait for a conviction before assessing risk.**

Children and families did not negotiate these contracts. Their inability to choose the supplier is one reason public authorities must exercise greater care.

## 💸 The Buffett Test: Apparently We Can Pause the Money

A pointed comparison arose in the discussion of Warren Buffett's charitable giving and public questions surrounding Bill Gates's past association with Jeffrey Epstein. The precise status of any pause, its timing, and Buffett's stated reasons **must be verified from attributable reporting or primary statements before this is presented as an established event**. Do not invent a donor's motive from the existence of a controversy.

But the governing question does not depend on a celebrity anecdote being exactly as remembered:

**If a private donor can decide that serious unresolved questions justify further checks before committing discretionary funds, what level of precaution should the state exercise before placing children's information and essential public functions into long-term commercial systems?**

The comparison is about *decision thresholds*, not an allegation that the Gates Foundation, Microsoft, a particular cloud contractor and any individual named in the Epstein records are the same legal actor. They are not.

The #MeToo lesson relevant here is also narrower and more serious than guilt by association: credible concerns deserve a process that can examine them without automatically dismissing them because someone is influential — and without automatically treating an allegation as a finding. A procurement authority should be able to explain what it checked, what risks it identified, what safeguards it required and who can review its reasoning.

The costs of waiting may be asymmetrical. A philanthropic commitment may be deferred. A live public system with millions of records and no tested exit path may be much harder to move.

The punchline is that **due diligence is cheaper while you can still say no**.

## ♻️ The Observer Has Entered the System

Transparency is not a window positioned outside politics. It is an intervention within politics.

**Activity → observation → disclosure → altered incentives → adaptation → changed activity → renewed observation.**

There is a second loop: **procurement → technical integration → switching costs → reduced exit options → supplier leverage → renewed procurement dependence.**

And a third: **visible controversy → concentrated scrutiny → quieter channels receive less attention → influence adapts → the visibility pattern changes.**

These are possible dynamics to test, not a claim that every contractor follows the same strategy.

A meeting register may improve accountability while encouraging some actors to prefer less formal channels. A donation rule may improve traceability while increasing incentives to use intermediaries. A procurement disclosure may improve scrutiny while encouraging contractors to frame their claims differently. These are possible adaptations, not inevitable outcomes.

This is the central cybernetic lesson: **the measurement regime becomes part of the environment to which the measured actors respond**.

An effective system therefore needs feedback of its own: independent review, anomaly detection, anti-avoidance provisions, public accessibility, enforcement and periodic revision. Merely accumulating disclosures is not the same as making influence intelligible.

And a government cannot infer from a tidy register that all consequential influence is registered.

## ⚖️ What Could Britain Actually Do Differently?

The following are questions for public deliberation, not a preselected party programme.

| Design area | Options worth examining | Avoidable failure |
|---|---|---|
| Donations | Individual and aggregate caps; verified beneficial ownership; donor eligibility; cryptocurrency traceability | Influence moves to opaque third parties |
| Lobbying | Activity-based registration; client identification; subject and outcome sought; proportionate contact disclosures | Ordinary constituents face corporate-scale compliance |
| Opposition parties | Disclosure of substantial paid policy advocacy and material support before entry into government | Legitimate political association becomes indiscriminately surveilled |
| Revolving door | Cooling-off periods, declarations, independent approval and enforcement | Informal relationships remain unexamined |
| Procurement | Traceable supplier contact, conflict declarations, reasons for exceptions and extensions | Commercial confidentiality swallows the decision record |
| AML and ownership | Verified control, financing and risk-based ongoing checks | Ownership changes or intermediaries become untraceable |
| Safeguarding and data | Risk assessment, access controls, independent audit and continuity planning | Children bear risks they could not consent to or avoid |
| Strategic infrastructure | Tested portability, interoperability, funded exit plans and internal expertise | A legally terminable contract becomes practically permanent |
| National security | Restricted but independent scrutiny of sensitive financial and governance records | Secrecy becomes a shield against accountability |
| Transparency infrastructure | Searchable, interoperable, timely datasets with stable identifiers | Thousands of technically public fragments cannot be connected |
| Public participation | Citizens' deliberation, civil-society consultation and parliamentary response duties | Incumbent parties define acceptable influence on their own terms |

Regulation must be proportionate to the resources and influence involved. A citizen emailing their MP is not equivalent to a contractor retaining a professional influence operation. But both are entitled to lawful political participation.

## 🏗️ Sovereignty Is Not a Procurement Checkbox

A minister may be legally entitled to end a contract and yet have no safe operational way to do so. A department may nominally own its data but lack the expertise to move, interpret or maintain it outside the supplier's systems. A public service may retain the legal power to govern while becoming practically dependent on one commercial architecture.

Five distinct capacities matter:

| Dimension | Test |
|---|---|
| Legal | Can the state make and enforce the relevant decisions? |
| Technical | Can public institutions inspect, maintain, modify and migrate the systems? |
| Operational | Can essential services continue through disruption or supplier exit? |
| Commercial | Can the state credibly negotiate and change supplier? |
| Democratic | Can the public understand and contest the arrangement, through appropriate oversight? |

A data-export clause is not an exit plan. An exit plan is not credible until its assumptions have been costed, exercised and tested against operational reality. Independent technical capacity is not wasted duplication if it is what makes democratic choice executable.

**Procurement can be an intervention in the state's future ability to intervene.**

## 🛡️ Classified, Confidential, or Just Embarrassing?

A criminal investigation, regulatory review, national-security restriction, commercial confidentiality claim and political decision not to comment are different things.

Where an investigation genuinely restricts disclosure, explain what can lawfully be said without compromising it. Where a particular operational detail must remain secret, consider whether the institutional process can still be independently scrutinised. Where no lawful restriction exists, the mere fact that an answer would be awkward is not a public-interest exemption.

There are real limits: publishing sensitive defence specifications or prejudicing legal proceedings can cause harm. **But the existence of a legitimate exception should not be allowed to erase the distinction between protected details and accountable decisions.**

The public should not be asked to accept an implied law-enforcement or national-security explanation where none has been established.

**National-security designation is not a blanket AML exemption.** A proper oversight design can restrict public access to genuinely sensitive operational material while giving suitably authorised, independent reviewers access to ownership, financing, procurement, safeguarding and conflict records. If that separation is impossible in practice, the authority should explain why and identify the compensating safeguard.

Otherwise, secrecy can become an information bottleneck: the public cannot see, the auditor cannot connect, the regulator cannot obtain, and the government cannot leave. That is not sovereignty. That is a dependency with a security label.

## 📮 Return to Sender

We began with letters.

The letters are not the entire investigation. They are the thing that made an intervention visible. A state may be considerably more affected by the contract that never becomes a headline, the beneficial owner who is difficult to identify, the financial relationship no one checks, or the platform that cannot be replaced.

And when that platform holds children's records, **the state does not get to call safeguarding an optional extra**.

**SCRUTINISE THEM ALL:** Palantir, Oracle, Microsoft, AWS, Google, smaller suppliers, politically connected intermediaries, Labour networks, Conservative networks, and any other actor whose position is materially relevant. Scrutiny is not a finding of guilt. It is how a government avoids mistaking lack of visibility for lack of risk.

The fox has discovered due diligence. The pigeon has friends. The department, ideally, has retained the ability to migrate its data.



The sender knew what it wanted to say. The recipient knew what the sender represented. The public acquired a new object of scrutiny. And the discussion expanded from a company's reputation to the relationships through which commercial interests interact with political parties and the state.

Then Jackdaw arrived, reminding us why the bigger question matters. A government decision can have substantive arguments in its favour and still require public examination of who sought it, how it was evaluated, and who was accountable for approving it.

The British public does not need to be shielded from knowing that organisations lobby its government. Nor does it need the governing party to decide, without meaningful independent participation, exactly how much of that relationship it is comfortable disclosing.

**We cannot abolish the desire to influence power. We can decide how visible, contestable and accountable that influence must be.**

And perhaps, before sending another carefully composed letter to a parliamentarian, someone might consider what happens when the parliamentarian writes back.

*Or forwards it to a journalist.*
