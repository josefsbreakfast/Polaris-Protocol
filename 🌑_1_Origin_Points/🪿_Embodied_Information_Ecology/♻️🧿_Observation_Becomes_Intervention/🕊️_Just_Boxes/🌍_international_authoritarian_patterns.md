# 🌍 International Pattern Library  
**First created:** 2025-12-13 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-17  
*A comparative library of recurring structural patterns through which authoritarian capture emerges across different regimes, eras, and political systems.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

This node assembles **comparative historical cases** to demonstrate a central Polaris claim:

> Authoritarian capture follows repeatable structural patterns, regardless of ideology, culture, or era.

The purpose is not moral equivalence or historical flattening.  
It is **pattern recognition**.

By examining where and how similar dynamics recur, we can distinguish:
- structural mechanisms from ideology,
- institutional failure from individual pathology,
- and early warning signals from late-stage collapse.

---

## 🧭 How to Read This Library  

Each case is treated as:
- a **structural example**, not a total history,
- a source of **mechanisms**, not justification,
- a lens on *how* institutions fail, not *why people believed*.

The focus is on:
- policing and coercive authority,
- oversight breakdown,
- stress, attrition, and exception logic.

## 🇩🇪 Weimar Germany → Nazi Consolidation  

### Relevant Patterns  
- Policing weakened by political instability and street violence  
- Paramilitary groups overwhelming enforcement capacity  
- Selective non-enforcement justified as “pragmatic”  
- Legal exceptionalism normalised before regime change  

### Structural Insight  
Authoritarian consolidation was enabled not only by ideology, but by:
- exhausted institutions,
- habituation to emergency powers,
- and loss of public faith in neutral enforcement.

## 🇺🇸 Jim Crow–Era United States  

### Relevant Patterns  
- Law existing formally but unenforceable against coercive social power  
- Armed intimidation overriding neutral policing  
- Selective enforcement framed as “keeping the peace”  
- Oversight rendered impossible by local retaliation  

### Structural Insight  
This was not only racist policing by belief, but policing **outmatched by coercive social force**.

Law did not disappear.  
It was made dangerous to apply.

- ⚖️ *Schmittian Logic in Policing Practice*  
- 🔥 *Asymmetric Overload & Legal Insulation*

## 🇪🇸 Francoist Spain  

### Relevant Patterns  
- Early purification of pluralist and community-embedded officers  
- Loyalty prioritised over competence  
- Police framed as moral guardians of order  
- Oversight subordinated to regime stability  

### Structural Insight  
Ethical restraint was treated as political risk.  
Professionalism was redefined as obedience.

🔗 Related nodes:
- 🧬 *Authoritarian Personnel Purification*  
- 🧠 *Ethical Attrition via Asymmetric Burden*

## 🌍 Contemporary Democratic Contexts  

### Relevant Patterns  
- Prolonged austerity hollowing institutions  
- Burnout-driven enforcement asymmetry  
- Expansion of legal grey zones (terrorism, protest, migration)  
- Oversight bodies stretched beyond capacity  

### Structural Insight  
Authoritarian *outcomes* can emerge without authoritarian *intent* when:
- stress is chronic,
- incentives are misaligned,
- and oversight is symbolic.

🔗 Related nodes:
- 🏛️ *Oversight Collapse Under Systemic Attrition*  
- 🧠 *Why “Neutral Institutions” Fail Under Sustained Stress*

## 🔁 Pattern Repetition Across Regimes  

Across these cases, the same mechanisms recur:

- Early targeting of policing and coercive authority  
- Removal or exit of ethical and complex actors  
- Normalisation of exception and emergency  
- Burnout and enforcement ricochet  
- Oversight failure under shared scarcity  
- Public trust erosion preceding consolidation  

Ideology varies.  
**Infrastructure does not.**
