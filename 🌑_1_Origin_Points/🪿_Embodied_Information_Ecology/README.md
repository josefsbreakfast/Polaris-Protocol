# 🪿 Embodied Information Ecology
**First created:** 2026-08-09 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-09  
*An interdisciplinary framework for studying embodied human beings inside information environments that can observe, respond to, regulate and adapt around them.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

Human beings do not encounter information as abstract rational agents.

We encounter it through bodies.

We perceive through sensory systems. We allocate finite attention. We remember imperfectly. We become tired, frightened, reassured, overloaded, habituated and sensitised. Our environments respond to what we do. Some systems observe us. Some classify us. Some alter what happens next according to what they observe.

Those processes are often studied separately.

**Embodied Information Ecology** asks what becomes visible when they are studied together.

The working object is the interacting environment formed by:

- embodied human beings;
- information environments;
- feedback and regulatory processes;
- and systems of observation and surveillance.

The framework therefore begins with four domains:

```text
🫀 EMBODIMENT

♻️ CYBERNETICS

🕸️ INFORMATION ENVIRONMENTS

🧿 SURVEILLANCE AND OBSERVABILITY
```

But the purpose of the cluster is not simply to put four literatures beside one another.

It is to study their **interfaces**.

What happens when information encounters a body?

What happens when behaviour becomes feedback?

What happens when an embodied person knows they may be observed?

What happens when an information environment adapts to behaviour?

What happens when observation becomes an input into intervention?

And what happens when all of those processes begin operating recursively?

That is the ecology.

---

## 🧭 Why Ecology

The word **ecology** matters.

It does not require every relationship inside the system to be:

- intentional;
- centrally controlled;
- stable;
- linear;
- harmful;
- beneficial;
- or even fully understood.

An ecology can contain organisms, environments, pressures, adaptations, dependencies, competition, amplification and feedback at several scales.

The same caution is useful here.

A change in one part of an information ecology may alter another without anyone designing the complete result.

Several independent systems may interact.

A feedback mechanism may produce an outcome its designers did not anticipate.

Observation may change behaviour.

Changed behaviour may alter subsequent observation.

Information intended for one purpose may become input into another system.

The framework therefore does **not** begin from an assumption of central control.

It begins from interaction.

---

## 📚 The Intellectual Neighbourhood

**Embodied Information Ecology** is a Polaris working framework rather than a claim that a new academic discipline has already been established under this exact name.

It sits at the intersection of several existing traditions.

### 🫀 Embodied and situated cognition

These traditions resist treating cognition as something occurring entirely inside an isolated abstract mind.

Bodies, environments, sensory systems, action and context matter.

### ♻️ Cybernetics and systems theory

These provide languages for feedback, regulation, adaptation, control, recursion and the way the output of one process can become input into what happens next.

### 🕸️ Media and information ecology

These examine communication technologies and informational conditions as environments capable of shaping perception, social organisation, behaviour and culture.

### 🧿 Surveillance studies

These examine observation, visibility, legibility, classification, data collection and the power relationships created when some actors can observe others.

### 🧩 Sociotechnical systems

These provide a broader way of understanding humans, institutions and technologies as interacting components rather than independent objects.

None is identical to Embodied Information Ecology.

Together they provide much of its intellectual toolkit.

# 🧱 The Four Domains

## 🫀 Embodiment

**Primary object:** the embodied human organism.

This domain includes:

- perception;
- sensory processing;
- attention;
- cognition;
- affect;
- memory;
- fatigue;
- pain;
- stress and arousal;
- nervous-system responses;
- bodily regulation;
- disability;
- adaptation;
- and embodied or situated cognition.

A note belongs primarily here where the phenomenon can substantially be explained by examining the embodied organism without requiring an information system, feedback mechanism or observer as part of the central explanation.

> **Routing question:** What is happening in or through the embodied organism?

→ [`🫀_Embodiment/`](./🫀_Embodiment/)

---

## ♻️ Cybernetics

**Primary object:** feedback, regulation and adaptation.

This domain includes:

- feedback loops;
- control;
- regulation;
- signalling;
- homeostasis;
- reinforcement;
- adaptation;
- recursive processes;
- learning systems;
- system response;
- and outputs becoming subsequent inputs.

The important distinction is:

```text
something changes
≠
cybernetics

something changes
and that change affects what happens next
=
potentially cybernetic
```

> **Routing question:** Is the central phenomenon a feedback or regulatory process?

→ [`♻️_Cybernetics/`](./♻️_Cybernetics/)

---

## 🕸️ Information Environments

**Primary object:** the informational conditions in which humans and systems operate.

This domain includes:

- communication environments;
- information availability;
- information scarcity and abundance;
- networks;
- platforms;
- transmission;
- discoverability;
- attention environments;
- media ecology;
- epistemic conditions;
- and the organisation and circulation of information.

Information merely existing is not enough to make every phenomenon an information-environment problem.

The domain concerns the **structure and properties of the informational environment itself**.

> **Routing question:** What kind of informational environment exists, and how is information organised, available or encountered within it?

→ [`🕸️_Information_Environments/`](./🕸️_Information_Environments/)

---

## 🧿 Surveillance And Observability

**Primary object:** observation, visibility and legibility.

This domain includes:

- surveillance;
- monitoring;
- observability;
- visibility;
- data collection;
- legibility;
- classification through observation;
- asymmetries between watcher and watched;
- and awareness of possible observation.

The framework distinguishes:

```text
data exists
≠
surveillance

something can be observed
=
observability

observation is organised or exercised
=
surveillance
```

Observability therefore matters even where active surveillance has not been established.

> **Routing question:** Who or what can observe whom, through what mechanism, and with what degree of visibility?

→ [`🧿_Surveillance_And_Observability/`](./🧿_Surveillance_And_Observability/)

# 🔗 The Six Interfaces

The interface folders exist for phenomena whose explanation depends upon the **relationship between two domains**.

The governing test is:

> **If removing either domain destroys the phenomenon the note is trying to explain, the interface may be the correct home.**

---

## 🫀♻️ The Body In The Loop

**Embodiment × cybernetics**

The body, its state or its behaviour becomes part of a feedback or regulatory process.

```text
stimulus
→ embodied response
→ response affects subsequent input or intervention
→ new embodied response
```

This interface can include conditioning, reinforcement, physiological feedback, adaptive regulation and human-in-the-loop systems.

→ [`🫀♻️_The_Body_In_The_Loop/`](./🫀♻️_The_Body_In_The_Loop/)

---

## 🫀🕸️ Information Is Experienced

**Embodiment × information environments**

Information does not simply arrive.

It is perceived by an organism.

This interface examines how informational conditions become:

- attention;
- perception;
- cognition;
- emotion;
- sensory load;
- memory;
- stress;
- expectation;
- and embodied experience.

```text
information environment
        ↓
attention / perception
        ↓
embodied experience
```

→ [`🫀🕸️_Information_Is_Experienced/`](./🫀🕸️_Information_Is_Experienced/)

---

## 🫀🧿 The Observed Body

**Embodiment × surveillance**

This interface examines what actual or anticipated observation does to embodied people, and how embodied behaviour becomes observable.

It may include:

- chilling effects;
- hypervigilance;
- self-monitoring;
- self-presentation;
- behavioural modification;
- bodily legibility;
- and physiological consequences of anticipated observation.

A useful test is:

> **Would the bodily phenomenon materially change if the possibility of observation disappeared?**

→ [`🫀🧿_The_Observed_Body/`](./🫀🧿_The_Observed_Body/)

---

## ♻️🕸️ The Feedback Environment

**Cybernetics × information environments**

This interface examines information environments that respond to behaviour and thereby alter subsequent informational conditions.

```text
information
→ behaviour
→ system response
→ changed information environment
→ behaviour
→ ...
```

Recommendation systems are one obvious example.

They are not the whole category.

→ [`♻️🕸️_The_Feedback_Environment/`](./♻️🕸️_The_Feedback_Environment/)

---

## ♻️🧿 Observation Becomes Intervention

**Cybernetics × surveillance**

Surveillance becomes cybernetic where observation is used as input into a system that subsequently responds, regulates or adapts.

```text
observe
→ classify
→ respond
→ observe response
→ update
→ respond again
```

A system that merely records may primarily present a surveillance question.

A system in which observation changes subsequent treatment presents a feedback question as well.

→ [`♻️🧿_Observation_Becomes_Intervention/`](./♻️🧿_Observation_Becomes_Intervention/)

---

## 🕸️🧿 The Observed Information Space

**Information environments × surveillance**

This interface examines observation of informational environments and the effects that observability can have upon communication.

It may include:

- monitored communication spaces;
- audience awareness;
- visibility of communicative behaviour;
- observation of networks;
- information asymmetries;
- and informational environments altered by the possibility of observation.

```text
someone watches a person
→ 🧿

being watched changes embodied behaviour
→ 🫀🧿

someone observes an information environment
→ 🕸️🧿

observation drives adaptive intervention
→ ♻️🧿
```

→ [`🕸️🧿_The_Observed_Information_Space/`](./🕸️🧿_The_Observed_Information_Space/)

# 🐣 The Whole Ecology

The whole-system layer has the highest admission threshold.

A note does **not** belong here merely because it mentions three or four domains.

It belongs here where the phenomenon is an **emergent or recursive property of their interaction** and cannot adequately be explained by one domain or one pairwise interface.

For example:

```text
🕸️ information environment
        ↓
🫀 embodied perception
        ↓
behaviour
        ↓
🧿 observation
        ↓
♻️ classification / response
        ↓
changed 🕸️ information environment
        ↓
changed 🫀 experience
        ↓
...
```

No individual arrow explains the whole process.

The loop does.

This folder therefore contains work on:

- emergence;
- multi-domain recursion;
- ecological adaptation;
- interacting feedback loops;
- system-level effects;
- and phenomena produced by relationships among several parts of the ecology.

> **Routing question:** Is the thing being explained an emergent property of the ecology rather than a property of one component or one interface?

→ [`🐣_The_Whole_Ecology/`](./🐣_The_Whole_Ecology/)

# 🧭 Where Does A Note Go?

The basic routing process is:

```text
                         NEW NOTE
                            │
                            ▼
             Can ONE domain explain it?
                    │                 │
                   YES                NO
                    │                 │
                    ▼                 ▼
              DOMAIN FOLDER      Does the explanation
                                 depend on the relationship
                                  between TWO domains?
                                      │          │
                                     YES         NO
                                      │          │
                                      ▼          ▼
                                  INTERFACE    Does it require
                                   FOLDER      multi-domain
                                               interaction or
                                                recursion?
                                                   │
                                                   ▼
                                              🐣 WHOLE
                                               ECOLOGY
```

The most important rule is:

> **Route by what the note is trying to explain, not by everything the note happens to mention.**

---

## 🪜 The Lowest-Sufficient-Level Rule

Prefer the lowest level of ecological complexity sufficient to explain the phenomenon.

```text
ONE DOMAIN EXPLAINS IT
→ domain

RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN TWO DOMAINS EXPLAINS IT
→ interface

EMERGENT / RECURSIVE MULTI-DOMAIN PROCESS
→ whole ecology
```

Do not promote a note to an interface merely because another domain appears in it.

Do not promote a note to the whole ecology merely because several domains are mentioned.

Complex vocabulary does not require a complex classification.

The explanatory mechanism does.

---

## ✂️ The Removal Test

Where two locations seem equally plausible, remove one domain mentally.

Ask:

> **Does the argument still work?**

If yes, the removed domain probably does not determine the note's home.

If no — if removing it destroys the phenomenon being explained — the interface is probably doing real analytical work.

The same test applies to the whole ecology.

If one pairwise interface adequately explains the mechanism, the note does not need to live at whole-system level.

---

## 🚫 Mention Is Not Membership

A note about surveillance may discuss stress.

That does not automatically make it `🫀🧿`.

A note about algorithms may discuss information.

That does not automatically make it `♻️🕸️`.

A note about information environments may mention surveillance.

That does not automatically make it `🕸️🧿`.

A note about all four domains may still fundamentally be about only one.

The rule is:

```text
MENTION
≠
MEMBERSHIP
```

Cross-link liberally.

Classify conservatively.

---

# ⚠️ What This Framework Does Not Claim

Embodied Information Ecology does not claim that:

- every information environment is deliberately engineered;
- every feedback loop is intentional;
- every observable person is actively surveilled;
- every system response is personalised;
- every behavioural change is caused by information exposure;
- every correlation between domains establishes causation;
- interacting systems necessarily share a controller;
- complexity itself proves coordination;
- technological systems operate independently of human institutions;
- human responses are mechanically predictable;
- or one framework can replace the specialised disciplines from which it borrows.

The framework is intended to **separate mechanisms before reconnecting them**.

It should make causal claims harder to smuggle across disciplinary boundaries, not easier.

---

## 🧪 Evidence Before Ecology

Whole-system explanations are particularly vulnerable to overclaiming.

A coherent loop on paper is not evidence that every stage occurred in a real case.

Where the framework is applied empirically, each relevant relationship should therefore be established separately.

```text
A can affect B
≠
A affected B here

B can be observed by C
≠
C observed B here

C can alter D
≠
C altered D here

all four mechanisms are technically possible
≠
one integrated system caused the observed outcome
```

Mechanism, capability, observation, inference and demonstrated causation must remain distinguishable.

The ecology is a tool for asking better questions.

It is not permission to fill missing arrows.

---

## 🪿 The Working Proposition

The central proposition of this cluster is simple:

> **Human beings are embodied participants in information systems, not abstract recipients of information.**

Once information environments can observe behaviour, respond to it and alter subsequent conditions, the useful unit of analysis may no longer be only:

- the message;
- the platform;
- the observer;
- the algorithm;
- or the body.

Sometimes the useful object is the **relationship between an embodied organism and an information environment capable of observing, responding to and adapting around it**.

The four domains explain components.

The six interfaces explain relationships.

The whole ecology explains what can emerge when those relationships become recursive.
