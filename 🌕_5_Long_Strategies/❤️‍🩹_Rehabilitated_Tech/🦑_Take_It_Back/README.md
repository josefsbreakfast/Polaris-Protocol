# 🦑 Take It Back
**First created:** 2026-08-22 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-22
*Rehabilitating technical culture by refusing the hierarchies, prestige games, and inherited measures that made technology smaller than it needed to be.*

## 🛰️ Orientation

> **#WomenInSTEM? The girls, the gays, and the theys do not need your imperial metrics.**

This folder is not an application for admission.

It is not a diversity brochure for institutions that first narrowed technical authority, then congratulated themselves for permitting selected outsiders back into the room. It is not interested in proving that women, queer people, disabled people, working-class people, or anyone else can meet a standard whose construction is itself part of the problem.

**Take It Back** begins somewhere more useful.

Technology is a human inheritance. So are mathematics, engineering, computation, systems thinking, making, repairing, explaining, experimenting, breaking things carefully, and putting them back together better.

Nobody gets permanent ownership because they successfully turned competence into a status aesthetic.

## 🧿 The Problem Is Not Admission

A great deal of inclusion language quietly preserves the hierarchy it claims to challenge.

The structure remains:

1. somebody defines technical seriousness;
2. somebody defines the metrics;
3. somebody decides what counts as expertise;
4. somebody else is invited to demonstrate that they qualify.

That is still somebody else's game.

**Rehabilitated Tech asks a different question: what if the game is badly designed?**

If a technical culture rewards opacity over explanation, prestige over usefulness, extraction over stewardship, narrow optimisation over system health, and gatekeeping over transferable understanding, then increasing the diversity of people permitted to succeed inside it does not by itself rehabilitate the technology.

Representation matters.

So does **what everyone is being represented inside**.

## 🦑 Take It Back

Taking it back does not mean returning technology to an imaginary golden age.

It means recovering the parts worth having:

- curiosity without humiliation;
- expertise without priesthood;
- rigour without deliberate obscurity;
- experimentation without treating other humans as disposable test environments;
- technical ambition without imperial fantasies of control;
- security without macho theatre;
- accessibility without intellectual dilution;
- explanation as evidence of understanding rather than evidence of inferiority;
- systems that serve people rather than requiring people to become convenient inputs for systems.

The point is not to become better at performing somebody else's version of technical authority.

The point is to build a better one.

## 🔬 A Different Measure of Technical Seriousness

Technical excellence should survive translation.

If an idea can only retain its authority while surrounded by specialist vocabulary, institutional prestige, inaccessible interfaces, or twenty years of assumed background knowledge, some of what is being protected may be status rather than complexity.

A stronger technical culture should be able to move between registers:

**mechanism → model → consequence → explanation → use**

without destroying the underlying precision.

That means the expert should find enough depth to interrogate.

It also means the newcomer should be able to understand why the problem matters.

Those are not competing requirements.

Making difficult things legible is itself a technical skill.

## 🛠️ Rehabilitation, Not Respectability

This project is not interested in demonstrating that marginalised people can be respectable technologists.

Respectability is another metric somebody else gets to administer.

The more interesting demonstration is that people traditionally treated as peripheral to technical power can produce work that is:

- more generative;
- more humane;
- more interdisciplinary;
- more resilient;
- more comprehensible;
- more historically literate;
- more alert to externalities;
- and, where it counts, simply **better**.

Not better *for a woman*.

Better.

## 🌐 For The Normies Too

A rehabilitated technical culture cannot depend upon ordinary people remaining intimidated by it.

The person arriving without twenty years in defence, cyber, software, engineering, policy, or intelligence should be able to look at serious technical work and say:

**Oh. I understand what this is doing.**

And then:

**Oh God. Why did everybody make this sound so complicated?**

That is not dumbing down.

That is removing unnecessary toll booths from knowledge.

Experts remain necessary. Specialist knowledge remains necessary. Precision remains necessary.

Mystification does not.

## ⚖️ Refuse The Imperial Metric

Metrics are never merely numbers once institutions attach status, resources, authority, legitimacy, or human worth to them.

A metric can illuminate.

It can also smuggle in the assumptions of whoever chose the ruler.

So this folder will keep asking:

- Who selected the measure?
- What does it reward?
- What disappears when performance is compressed into it?
- Who benefits from being legible to it?
- Who is classified as deficient?
- What behaviour does optimisation produce?
- What happens if we simply refuse to accept the ruler?

Sometimes the most technically serious response to a metric is not to score higher.

It is to demonstrate that the metric is shit.
