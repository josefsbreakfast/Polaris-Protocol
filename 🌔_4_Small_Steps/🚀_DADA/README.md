# 🚀 Defence Against Digital Authoritarianism
**First created:** 2026-07-20 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-20  
*Small, practical steps for regaining agency when digital systems feel confusing, intrusive, or impossible to challenge.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

You may have reached the point where you are no longer trying to survive the next five minutes, but the situation still feels enormous, technical, invasive, and difficult to explain.

You may be:

- frightened;
- exhausted;
- vulnerable;
- technically outmatched;
- unsure what is ordinary;
- unsure what is malicious;
- unsure what can be proved.

Digital interference can feel uniquely disorienting because:

- devices behave unpredictably;
- systems are opaque;
- ordinary explanations remain possible;
- serious capabilities also exist;
- logs and records may be inaccessible;
- several small failures may combine into one overwhelming experience.

Confusion is not proof that nothing is happening.

It is also not proof of the most frightening explanation.

DADA begins after the immediate crisis, when you are steady enough to ask what is happening but may still be frightened, exhausted, vulnerable, or technically outmatched.

It does not promise that one checklist can remove the threat.

It helps you begin:

- reducing confusion;
- preserving evidence;
- protecting what matters;
- making better decisions;
- regaining some practical power.

---

## 🌔 Why DADA Sits Under Small Steps

DADA belongs inside `🌔_4_Small_Steps` because it is post-crisis but pre-resolution.

It is not primarily for the moment when someone needs to:

- ground themselves immediately;
- get through the next hour;
- reduce acute risk;
- restore basic safety.

That work belongs in `🌓_3_In_The_Moment`.

It is also not yet the full terrain of:

- litigation;
- institutional reform;
- public campaigning;
- infrastructure change;
- long-term political accountability;
- sustained collective defence.

DADA sits between those two phases.

It begins when someone can say:

> I am not safe enough to ignore this, but I am steady enough to begin understanding it and doing something small.

A small step may mean:

- preserving one useful record;
- securing one important account;
- protecting one trusted contact;
- checking one explanation;
- reducing one avoidable exposure;
- preparing one clearer complaint;
- refusing one unsafe process.

A small step is not a claim that the problem is small.

It is a claim that power can be rebuilt incrementally.

- immediate safety;

- regaining practical control;
- learning enough to make one better decision;
- preserving evidence;
- reducing avoidable exposure;
- protecting one account, relationship, device, or record at a time.

The question is:

> What can I do next that gives me a little more power without making me less safe?

Longer-term work may involve:

- litigation;
- regulation;
- institutional reform;
- campaigning;
- collective infrastructure;
- public accountability;
- durable political change.

The question is:

> What would have to change so that this cannot keep happening?

DADA does not collapse these phases into one task.

It gives the reader permission to begin where they actually are.

## 🧿 What DADA Means

**Defence Against Digital Authoritarianism** means protecting people and communities against systems or practices that use digital infrastructure to:

- observe;
- classify;
- rank;
- restrict;
- intimidate;
- isolate;
- manipulate;
- interfere with association;
- narrow access to institutions;
- make challenge difficult.

“Authoritarianism” here does not refer only to one named regime type.

It includes recurring tendencies such as:

- secrecy without accountability;
- expanding surveillance;
- weak appeal routes;
- political profiling;
- concentrated data power;
- exceptional powers becoming routine;
- coercion hidden inside administration;
- technical systems treated as beyond democratic challenge.

Not every technical failure is authoritarian interference.

Not every adverse decision is coordinated.

Not every powerful capability has been used in every case.

DADA exists to hold both truths at once:

- serious capabilities exist;
- claims about actual use still require evidence.

## 🌍 Why This Matters Now

Governments and institutions face growing incentives to expand:

- surveillance;
- data integration;
- behavioural prediction;
- automated risk assessment;
- digital intervention;
- information control.

Commercial systems already collect enormous amounts of behavioural data.

State and commercial infrastructure increasingly overlap through:

- platforms;
- contractors;
- data brokers;
- banks;
- employers;
- insurers;
- public bodies;
- shared vendors.

Periods of:

- political instability;
- war;
- public-health emergency;
- protest;
- information conflict;
- economic pressure;

often increase demand for monitoring and control.

The world has also moved further into an age of:

- data saturation;
- information warfare;
- automated classification;
- algorithmic visibility;
- political fragmentation;
- militarised uncertainty.

Democratic restraint does not strengthen automatically merely because the technology becomes more powerful.

That does not mean:

- every country is operating one unified system;
- every state uses every available capability;
- every anomaly is evidence of state action.

It means capability, incentive, governance, and accountability must be examined rather than assumed.

## 🧠 Theory And Practice Belong Together

Basic practical advice is not enough on its own.

Without theory, people may:

- endlessly reset passwords;
- blame themselves;
- chase every anomaly;
- miss institutional causes;
- mistake symptoms for mechanisms;
- treat every problem as an individual device failure.

Without practice, theory may:

- increase fear;
- make the threat feel total;
- leave the reader with no usable action;
- turn every possibility into paralysis;
- encourage constant investigation without relief.

DADA therefore pairs theory with practice.

### Theory

Theory asks:

- What is technically possible?
- What data exist?
- How may systems connect?
- What forms of intervention are plausible?
- Where can error enter?
- What would actual use look like?

### Practice

Practice asks:

- What can be checked?
- What can be secured?
- What should be recorded?
- Which relationships need protection?
- What should not be changed impulsively?
- When is specialist help needed?
- What can wait?

Theory names the terrain.

Practice gives you somewhere to put your feet.

## 🪜 The DADA Method

A recurring method for this branch is:

> **Stabilise → observe → preserve → reduce → test → escalate**

### 1. Stabilise

Do not begin major technical action during acute panic where avoidable.

Preserve immediate access to:

- medication;
- care;
- money;
- trusted contacts;
- essential accounts;
- accessibility tools.

### 2. Observe

Record what happened.

Separate:

- fact;
- interpretation;
- fear;
- hypothesis.

### 3. Preserve

Keep useful materials such as:

- screenshots;
- dates;
- error messages;
- headers;
- logs;
- device details;
- institutional correspondence.

### 4. Reduce Exposure

Make proportionate changes.

Avoid:

- destroying evidence;
- locking yourself out;
- removing care;
- breaking accessibility;
- changing everything at once.

Prioritise high-value accounts, devices, and relationships.

### 5. Test The Smallest Claim

Identify one mechanism that can be checked.

Preserve rival explanations.

Avoid jumping from anomaly to attribution.

### 6. Escalate Carefully

Decide whether the next route is:

- technical;
- legal;
- safeguarding;
- institutional;
- collective;
- public.

Escalation should follow the mechanism, not merely the fear.

## 🔧 What Small Steps Can Achieve

Small steps may help a person:

- reduce account compromise;
- preserve evidence;
- identify patterns;
- stop one form of leakage;
- protect one trusted contact;
- separate devices or roles;
- recognise an unsafe process;
- avoid an impulsive destructive response;
- prepare a clearer complaint;
- regain some confidence.

Small steps cannot always:

- identify the responsible actor;
- remove state-level capability;
- force institutional disclosure;
- undo already-shared data;
- guarantee safety;
- resolve every anomaly;
- repair every damaged relationship.

Partial control is still control.

A small action may not end the threat.

It may still preserve:

- access;
- evidence;
- dignity;
- choice;
- time.

## 🌫️ Avoiding Totalisation

Several things may coexist:

- genuine interference;
- ordinary bugs;
- institutional incompetence;
- commercial tracking;
- algorithmic instability;
- trauma responses;
- malicious individuals;
- unrelated failures;
- fragmented systems.

A complicated environment may contain several causes at once.

DADA should resist two failures.

### Failure One: Dismissal

Assuming that because ordinary explanations exist, serious interference cannot.

### Failure Two: Totalisation

Assuming that every anomaly belongs to one hidden system.

Both failures reduce useful agency.

The aim is to:

- preserve uncertainty;
- test mechanisms;
- reduce harm;
- avoid forcing every event into one theory.

## 💾 Capability Is Not Allegation

The `💾_Capabilities` branch asks:

- What is technically possible?
- What data would be required?
- What systems could perform it?
- What decisions could it support?
- What traces might it leave?
- What evidence would establish actual use?
- What rival explanations remain?

Capability tells us what could happen.

Evidence tells us what did happen.

Capability analysis is useful because it:

- prevents naïveté;
- improves questions;
- identifies records worth requesting;
- supports better safeguards;
- clarifies technical mechanisms;
- avoids premature accusation.

The strongest capability model is one that clearly marks the boundary between:

- possibility;
- plausibility;
- evidence;
- attribution;
- causation.

## 🎯 Proportionate Threat Models

Defence depends on the likely threat.

Possible threat actors may include:

- abusive partner;
- stalker;
- criminal group;
- hostile employer;
- platform;
- data broker;
- public authority;
- intelligence or security service;
- unknown or mixed actor.

Different actors have different:

- access;
- resources;
- legal powers;
- persistence;
- incentives;
- technical capacity.

The strongest imaginable attacker should not automatically become the default explanation.

Powerful actors should also not be excluded merely because they are uncomfortable to consider.

A proportionate threat model asks:

- What is being protected?
- From whom?
- With what likely access?
- At what cost?
- What evidence supports the model?
- What ordinary explanation remains?

## 🩺 Protect Function Before Perfect Security

Digital security measures can themselves create harm.

A person may accidentally:

- lose essential account access;
- break accessibility tools;
- disconnect care;
- delete evidence;
- remove a trusted helper;
- spend money on unnecessary equipment;
- increase isolation;
- lose access to benefits, banking, or healthcare.

DADA should prioritise:

1. safety;
2. continuity of essential function;
3. evidence;
4. proportionate security;
5. attribution later.

A defensive measure that removes your care, income, communication, or evidence may leave you less safe than before.

Perfect security is not the goal.

Safer function is.

## 🫂 Relationships Are Part Of Digital Security

Digital defence is not only about devices.

It also includes:

- trusted contacts;
- emergency routes;
- professional confidentiality;
- chosen family;
- carers;
- community support;
- legal access;
- medical access.

A system may weaken a person by weakening:

- trust;
- introductions;
- credibility;
- support;
- mutual protection;
- access to institutions.

Your security includes the people who help you remain a person.

This means defensive planning should ask:

- Who must remain reachable?
- Who should not receive sensitive data?
- Which relationship is essential to care?
- Which contact needs an alternative route?
- Which professional relationship requires confidentiality?
- Which connection would leave the person more isolated if disrupted?

## 🤝 Collective Defence

Individual hygiene cannot solve infrastructural power.

Collective defence may include:

- shared technical knowledge;
- community evidence standards;
- trusted device support;
- secure referral routes;
- institutional accountability;
- mutual aid;
- collective bargaining;
- journalist and legal partnerships;
- public-interest technical research.

Collective defence should not require vulnerable people to:

- expose themselves publicly;
- become full-time investigators;
- publish private evidence;
- confront suspected actors alone;
- sacrifice care or livelihood.

The point of collective defence is to distribute burden.

It should make survival easier, not create another test of endurance.

## 🚫 What DADA Is Not

DADA is not:

- a promise of total security;
- a substitute for emergency support;
- a guarantee of attribution;
- encouragement to confront a suspected actor;
- amateur intelligence work;
- a loyalty-testing framework;
- a reason to isolate from everyone;
- a demand to document continuously;
- a claim that every anomaly is malicious;
- a claim that sophisticated interference is impossible;
- a requirement to become technically expert;
- a demand to sacrifice ordinary life to investigation.

Defence should expand your life, not consume it.

If a defensive process produces:

- more isolation;
- less care;
- less access;
- more panic;
- fewer trusted relationships;
- constant vigilance;

it should be reassessed.

## 🛡️ Minimum Safety Rules

- Do not delete possible evidence impulsively.
- Do not confront a suspected actor without considering safety.
- Do not make every account change at once.
- Do not remove essential accessibility or care systems without a replacement.
- Do not share sensitive evidence indiscriminately.
- Do not assume the most alarming explanation is proven.
- Do not allow uncertainty to prevent reasonable protection.
- Protect trusted human contact.
- Preserve function.
- Test the smallest claim first.

These rules are not perfect security.

They are guardrails against making an already difficult situation worse.
