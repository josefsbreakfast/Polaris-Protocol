# 🩸 Feed Me Data
**First created:** 2026-07-19 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-19  
*How fragments become relationships, relationships become risk, and uncertainty becomes appetite.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation

The database does not begin by asking for a whole life.

It begins by asking for one more record.

A name.

A phone number.

A payment.

A location.

A membership.

A family tie.

A message.

Then another.

The appetite grows because every uncertainty can be answered by requesting another feed.

The system may begin with:

- identity;
- communications;
- location;
- finance;
- affiliation;
- private relationships.

Its power does not come from any one dataset alone.

It comes from convergence.

Once joined, separate records can become:

- relationship graphs;
- institutional classifications;
- political inferences;
- operational decisions.

Feed Me Data examines what enters the plant before the next question is asked:

> What does the system do with what it has eaten?

## 🧩 What This Cluster Covers

This cluster examines four linked stages.

### 1. Records

The system may ingest:

- names;
- accounts;
- devices;
- transactions;
- memberships;
- movements;
- addresses;
- events.

### 2. Relationships

The records may be used to infer:

- who contacts whom;
- who supports whom;
- who belongs where;
- who lives with whom;
- who trusts whom;
- who can reach whom.

### 3. Interpretations

Those relationships may then be interpreted as:

- influence;
- dependency;
- coordination;
- allegiance;
- vulnerability;
- risk.

### 4. Decisions

The interpretation may support decisions to:

- monitor;
- rank;
- restrict;
- refer;
- exclude;
- disrupt;
- redirect;
- intervene.

The important shift is not merely from less data to more data.

It is from data to judgement.

## 🔗 Core Model

The cluster follows this progression:

> **record → edge → pattern → inference → classification → decision**

### Record

A call occurred.

A payment moved.

A device appeared somewhere.

A person registered with an institution.

### Edge

The system links two entities.

### Pattern

The system detects recurrence, clustering, timing, or overlap.

### Inference

The system estimates:

- trust;
- influence;
- dependency;
- coordination;
- allegiance.

### Classification

The relationship is labelled:

- important;
- risky;
- central;
- sensitive;
- foreign-linked;
- politically consequential.

### Decision

The classification affects:

- scrutiny;
- access;
- employment;
- money;
- movement;
- association;
- support.

Precision at the beginning of the chain does not guarantee truth at the end of it.

## 🔭 Facts, Proxies, And Guesses

The cluster repeatedly distinguishes three categories.

### Confirmed Fact

A directly observed or documented record.

Examples:

- a call took place;
- a payment occurred;
- a person is formally employed;
- a device was detected near a location.

### Proxy

A measurable feature used to stand in for something harder to observe.

Examples:

- contact frequency used as a proxy for trust;
- centrality used as a proxy for influence;
- shared address used as a proxy for intimacy;
- remittance used as a proxy for political direction.

### Prediction

A model estimates that something probably exists.

Examples:

- a hidden relationship;
- likely allegiance;
- future influence;
- coalition potential.

A database becomes dangerous when its guesses inherit the authority of its records.

Facts, proxies, and predictions should never be displayed as if they carry equal certainty.

## 📚 Why More Data Does Not Automatically Mean More Truth

More data may:

- increase coverage;
- reproduce the same error;
- collapse context;
- create suspicious-looking coincidences;
- repeat one source across several systems;
- make correlation appear independent.

Several databases may contain the same wrong record.

Several institutions may rely on the same vendor.

Several refusals may descend from one designation.

Repetition is not corroboration when every system copied the same mistake.

Convergence can increase confidence.

It can also make error look authoritative.

The key question is not only:

> How many systems agree?

It is also:

> Where did the claim begin?

---

## 🫗 Context Collapse

Records are often created for one purpose and reused for another.

Examples include:

- payroll becoming political dependency;
- event attendance becoming allegiance;
- emergency contact becoming intimate partner;
- remittance becoming foreign direction;
- religious membership becoming ideology;
- shared address becoming political coordination;
- care becoming control.

The record may remain accurate while the interpretation becomes false.

A payment happened.

A person attended.

Two people share an address.

The political story attached to those facts may still be wrong.

Context must travel with the record.

---

## 🪴 The Appetite Problem

Every ambiguity may generate demand for another dataset.

- Unclear contact? Obtain messages.
- Unclear meeting? Obtain location.
- Unclear support? Obtain finance.
- Unclear allegiance? Obtain institutional affiliation.
- Unclear trust? Map family and intimacy.

The system may treat uncertainty as a data deficit.

But some uncertainty is not a technical failure.

It is an interpretive limit.

Some uncertainty should remain uncertainty.

The appetite becomes dangerous when the system assumes that enough data must eventually produce complete meaning.

Human relationships do not work that way.

---

## 🪞 Description Versus Intervention

There is a difference between:

- describing a network;
- ranking relationships;
- deciding which relationships should survive.

A graph may begin by showing:

- contact;
- proximity;
- support;
- affiliation;
- intimacy.

It becomes politically consequential when it begins shaping:

- access;
- work;
- money;
- movement;
- trust;
- association.

The database stops being a mirror when it begins rearranging the people inside it.

The concern is not only what the system knows.

It is what the system makes possible or impossible.

---

## 🌡️ Relationship Risk

Relationship risk is not merely the sum of two personal risk scores.

A relationship may create new capacity.

It may produce:

- credibility;
- shelter;
- legal access;
- emotional resilience;
- financial stability;
- mobility;
- coalition potential;
- public legibility.

The system may fear not either person, but what becomes possible between them.

This is why the cluster repeatedly returns to:

- bridge figures;
- relationship ceilings;
- managed substitutes;
- practical possibility;
- formal freedom.

A system may not need to suppress either person directly.

It may only need to keep the relationship from forming, stabilising, or becoming useful.

## ⚠️ Cross-Cutting Harms

Across the cluster, recurring harms include:

- guilt by association;
- context collapse;
- political profiling;
- diaspora criminalisation;
- exposure of confidential relationships;
- outing and intimate harm;
- financial exclusion;
- chilling lawful association;
- professional pressure;
- institutional exclusion;
- unsafe contact with estranged relatives;
- false confidence from repeated errors;
- practical narrowing of support and trust.

The harm may be:

- direct;
- cumulative;
- distributed;
- difficult to attribute to one decision.

No single institution may appear decisive.

The combined system may still reshape a life.

---

## 🛡️ Cross-Cutting Safeguards

Safeguards should include:

- purpose limitation;
- data minimisation;
- current-status checks;
- confidence labels;
- clear distinction between:
  - fact;
  - inference;
  - prediction;
- protected professional relationships;
- special protection for religious and political data;
- safety and consent checks;
- correction and appeal;
- propagation of corrections;
- retention limits;
- independent oversight;
- smallest testable claim first.

A system should also be able to explain:

- where a relationship classification came from;
- how it was tested;
- whether it is disputed;
- which decision used it;
- how it can be corrected.

## 🔎 Rival Explanations

Patterns may arise through:

- ordinary platform effects;
- prejudice;
- gatekeeping;
- institutional caution;
- trauma;
- coincidence;
- commercial incentives;
- administrative error;
- fragmented decision-making;
- risk aversion.

Pattern does not prove coordination.

Several harms may occur without one controller.

Distributed harm does not require central command, and systemic capability does not prove a specific operation.

This distinction protects both:

- analytical accuracy;
- survivor credibility.

The smallest claim that can be tested should come first.

---

## 🧪 Minimum Investigative Questions

When examining a possible relationship-governance system, ask:

- What was the original record?
- Why was it collected?
- Who accessed it?
- What relationship was inferred?
- What confidence applied?
- Was the relationship current?
- Was consent considered?
- Was safety considered?
- Did another system repeat the same source?
- What decision used the inference?
- Could the subject challenge it?
- Did the classification travel?
- Did several institutions create a cumulative effect?
- What evidence could disprove the claim?
- What ordinary rival explanation remains?

These questions prevent a capability model from becoming an unsupported allegation.

## ⚠️ What This Cluster Is Not Claiming

This cluster does not claim that:

- data collection is inherently abusive;
- social-network analysis is inherently unlawful;
- capability proves use;
- pattern proves coordination;
- association proves agreement;
- error automatically proves malice;
- cumulative harm identifies one responsible actor;
- every adverse decision is centrally directed.

Specific allegations still require:

- source;
- route;
- mechanism;
- dates;
- decision;
- consequence.

The cluster describes what systems can do.

It does not assume that every capability has been used in every case.
