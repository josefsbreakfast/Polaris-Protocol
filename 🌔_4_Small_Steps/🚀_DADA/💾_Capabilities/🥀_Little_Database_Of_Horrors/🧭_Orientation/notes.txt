🧭 Orientation — README Planning Notes

First created: 2026-07-19 | Last updated: 2026-07-19
Planning notes for the entrance to the Little Database of Horrors orientation cluster.

⸻

🛰️ Purpose Of This README

This README should act as the first reliable foothold for readers entering the Little Database of Horrors cluster.

Its job is not to prove the entire model.

Its job is to explain:

* what problem the cluster is examining;
* why the problem is plausible enough to examine now;
* what the cluster is and is not claiming;
* why relationships, rather than merely individuals, are the important unit of analysis;
* why diaspora communities may be particularly exposed;
* how the Little Shop of Horrors metaphor describes institutional escalation;
* how the five orientation nodes divide the initial argument.

The reader should leave the README understanding the central concern without being required to believe that one complete and centrally operated database has already been proven to exist.

The opening proposition should be something close to:

States already possess many of the tools required to map people, relationships, influence, dependency, movement, communication, and political activity. This cluster asks what becomes possible when those tools are combined—and what happens when institutions begin trying to shape not only what people do, but who may safely know whom.

⸻

🥀 Core Framing

The cluster is about a foreseeable capability convergence involving:

* social-graph analysis;
* risk scoring;
* identity databases;
* communications and location data;
* commercial data brokerage;
* platform recommendation and visibility systems;
* embassy and consular networks;
* family and community pressure;
* transnational repression;
* legal, financial, immigration, and administrative leverage.

The README should explain that none of these components needs to be novel.

The novelty lies in their combination.

A system does not require a field marked:

dangerous_friendship = true

It may instead combine:

* who communicates;
* who attends the same events;
* who transfers money;
* who shares institutions;
* who has legal, journalistic, political, or technical influence;
* who appears capable of turning private distress into effective action.

The cluster therefore concerns the movement from:

recording relationships

to:

interpreting relationships

to:

scoring relationships

to:

shaping the environment in which relationships form or survive.

⸻

🪴 Why The Little Shop Metaphor Matters

The README should introduce the metaphor clearly enough that later branch names feel coherent rather than decorative.

The plant begins as something small, useful, and apparently manageable.

It may be presented as:

* safeguarding;
* community engagement;
* counter-extremism;
* hostile-state protection;
* consular welfare;
* social stability;
* foreign-interference prevention;
* vulnerability assessment.

Then the system fails to make people fully predictable.

Instead of questioning the system, institutions feed it more:

* more records;
* more categories;
* more intimate data;
* more relationships;
* more surveillance;
* more coercive outputs.

The central Little Shop proposition is:

The database keeps demanding more intimate data because the last batch did not make human beings predictable enough.

The metaphor should carry several ideas at once:

* institutional appetite;
* purpose creep;
* escalation through failure;
* administrative denial;
* commercial and political incentives;
* a system that eventually shapes the environment it was meant merely to observe.

The README should make clear that the humour is not intended to trivialise the subject.

It gives readers a memorable model for a genuinely frightening escalation pathway.

⸻

🕸️ The Governing Question

The orientation cluster should revolve around:

Who may safely know whom?

This moves the analysis beyond individual surveillance.

Traditional risk models often ask:

* Is this person dangerous?
* Is this person influential?
* Is this person connected to prohibited activity?

The Little Database model asks an additional question:

What becomes possible if these two people trust one another?

A relationship may be considered consequential because it combines:

* testimony and publication;
* evidence and legal expertise;
* funding and organisation;
* cultural legitimacy and external access;
* technical skill and political persistence;
* family trust and practical protection;
* local experience and international reach.

The README should introduce the idea that:

Relationship risk is not merely the sum of two personal risk scores.

Two people who appear individually manageable may become institutionally consequential together.

This is the core reason the subject belongs in DADA.

Digital authoritarianism may develop from monitoring isolated individuals into managing the social conditions under which collective agency becomes possible.

⸻

🌍 Why Diaspora Communities Matter

The README should explain why diaspora communities present a particular temptation for authoritarian states.

Relevant features may include:

* continuing family ties in the origin state;
* consular and passport dependency;
* remittances and financial connections;
* religious, cultural, linguistic, and professional institutions;
* political exile communities;
* opposition media;
* student and academic networks;
* activist organisations;
* relatives who remain vulnerable to pressure;
* host-country freedom combined with origin-country leverage.

Diaspora communities may also be relatively bounded from an administrative perspective.

A foreign ministry, embassy, intelligence service, or aligned community organisation may already possess partial records concerning:

* nationality;
* family;
* location;
* employment;
* travel;
* community participation;
* political activity;
* requests for consular assistance.

The README should not imply that diaspora communities are naturally suspicious or politically uniform.

The danger arises because authoritarian systems may treat ordinary relationships, cultural belonging, and family life as governable political infrastructure.

A useful line may be:

A diaspora is not a foreign state’s population-management problem merely because its members remain connected to people, places, or institutions elsewhere.

⸻

🔭 What The Cluster Is Claiming

The README should state the claim positively and carefully.

The cluster argues that:

1. many relevant technologies and administrative capabilities already exist;
2. transnational repression is already a documented practice;
3. states already monitor relationships, families, communications, organisations, and movements;
4. authoritarian regimes have strong incentives to identify consequential social connections;
5. capability integration makes relationship scoring and social shaping foreseeable;
6. systems placed under political pressure may expand beyond their original purpose;
7. legal and ethical safeguards should be developed before the most severe abuse is fully exposed.

The cluster is therefore a:

* capability warning;
* escalation model;
* comparative research programme;
* investigative framework;
* community-protection resource;
* governance and legal analysis.

⸻

🚫 What The Cluster Is Not Claiming

The README must contain an explicit boundary section.

It is not claiming that:

* every state has built the same system;
* every strange social event is centrally coordinated;
* every embassy is managing personal relationships;
* every similar-looking contact is a deliberately selected substitute;
* every failed friendship, romance, collaboration, or introduction is interference;
* the existence of social-graph software proves its use for diaspora containment;
* one person’s experience proves a whole architecture;
* community politics, prejudice, algorithms, class sorting, or ordinary conflict are irrelevant explanations.

The cluster should also reject the idea that uncertainty justifies accusing named individuals of acting as agents or intermediaries.

A strong formulation would be:

The purpose of the model is to make emerging capabilities investigable, not to turn every confusing relationship into evidence of conspiracy.

⸻

🔬 The Smallest Claim First

The README should introduce the project’s methodological rule:

Identify the smallest mechanism capable of producing the observed pattern.

A strange outcome may arise from:

* one platform;
* one institution;
* one contractor;
* one embassy;
* one community faction;
* one corrupted record;
* one policy;
* one risk category;
* several overlapping systems without a central controller.

The reader should not begin with:

A complete hidden state architecture controls the whole network.

They should begin with questions such as:

* Who made this particular decision?
* What information did they possess?
* What system did they use?
* What rule or category was applied?
* Did the same error appear elsewhere?
* Was there a shared intermediary?
* What ordinary explanation remains plausible?
* What evidence would disprove coordination?

The README should make this method central because it protects both accuracy and affected communities.

⸻

🧿 The Five Orientation Nodes

The README should introduce each child node with enough detail to explain why it exists.

🥀 what_is_the_little_database_of_horrors.md

This node will define the overall model.

It should explain:

* the convergence of surveillance, graph analysis, scoring, and transnational repression;
* the difference between monitoring people and managing relationships;
* why the database is described as “little” at the beginning;
* how the system becomes more dangerous through integration and purpose creep;
* the central Feed me data escalation mechanism.

This is the conceptual definition node.

It should give the reader the clearest compact answer to:

What exactly are we worried someone might build?

⸻

🪴 somewhere_thats_green.md

This node will examine the benign administrative promise.

It should describe the imagined clean and orderly system in which governments believe they can:

* understand diaspora communities;
* identify trusted representatives;
* allocate safeguarding resources;
* detect threats;
* improve engagement;
* maintain social stability;
* prevent hostile interference;
* resolve uncertainty through better data.

The node should be sympathetic enough to explain why officials might initially consider the project reasonable.

Its purpose is to show that severe systems often begin not with an openly monstrous objective, but with:

* optimism;
* managerial confidence;
* security pressure;
* a desire for legibility;
* failure to recognise the rights and complexity being compressed.

This is where the reader sees the attractive shop window before being shown the plant.

⸻

🕸️ who_may_safely_know_whom.md

This node will introduce the relationship as the object of governance.

It should explore:

* relationship risk rather than merely person risk;
* coalition potential;
* bridge figures;
* social connectors;
* access to lawyers, journalists, funders, institutions, and external communities;
* why some relationships absorb emotion while others alter material conditions;
* the temptation to permit low-consequence contact while obstructing consequential association.

It should provide the governing question for the whole pack:

Is the system trying to understand a community, or decide which relationships it can safely permit to become effective?

This is the node that most clearly joins digital authoritarianism to freedom of association.

⸻

🔭 capability_not_allegation.md

This node will establish the evidentiary discipline.

It should distinguish:

* capability;
* procurement;
* technical possibility;
* documented conduct;
* motive;
* inference;
* hypothesis;
* proof.

It should explain why the cluster can reasonably investigate foreseeable abuse without claiming that every component has already been assembled into one operating system.

It should also explain why capability analysis matters:

* safeguards are usually designed before full proof of catastrophic misuse;
* procurement and governance decisions precede public scandal;
* human-rights analysis must consider foreseeable use;
* the absence of a complete exposed case does not make the risk fanciful.

This node should protect the cluster from both exaggeration and premature dismissal.

⸻

🔬 the_smallest_claim_first.md

This node will provide the investigative starting rule.

It should teach readers to:

* isolate concrete incidents;
* identify named actors and systems;
* separate observations from inferences;
* compare ordinary explanations;
* look for repeated mechanisms;
* test whether several small systems produce the apparent whole;
* define what would weaken the hypothesis.

It should resist the emotional pull of total explanation.

The node should make clear that proving:

* one shared watchlist;
* one repeated data error;
* one contractor;
* one embassy route;
* one platform rule;
* one unlawful information-sharing practice;

may be more useful and defensible than trying immediately to prove a complete architecture.

This is the methodological guardrail that allows the rest of the cluster to remain serious.

⸻

🗺️ Proposed Reader Journey

The README should route the reader through the orientation nodes in this order:

1. 🥀 What is the Little Database of Horrors?
    Define the capability and escalation model.
2. 🪴 Somewhere That’s Green
    Understand the benign promise and institutional temptation.
3. 🕸️ Who May Safely Know Whom?
    See why relationships become politically important.
4. 🔭 Capability, Not Allegation
    Establish what can responsibly be claimed.
5. 🔬 The Smallest Claim First
    Learn how the model should be tested.

This sequence moves from:

concept → temptation → governing problem → evidentiary boundary → investigative method.

The reader should then be ready to proceed into:

🩸_Feed_Me_Data/

where the cluster begins examining the actual information and capabilities from which such a system could be built.

🎭 Tone

The README should balance:

* horror;
* dry humour;
* technical seriousness;
* legal restraint;
* practical clarity;
* respect for affected diaspora communities.

The Little Shop language should be present, but it should not overwhelm the argument.

Useful recurring lines may include:

Feed me data.

The plant begins as a dashboard.

The model’s hunger is not a validation metric.

The system does not need perfect control. It only needs to keep altering the probabilities.

Human beings are not datasets with inconvenient gaps.

Association is not a defect in the model.

The prose should remain readable for:

* affected community members;
* journalists;
* civil-society organisations;
* lawyers;
* policymakers;
* technical researchers;
* protective-security professionals.

⸻

⚖️ Principal Guardrails

The orientation README should establish these guardrails early:

Do not turn possibility into proof

Technical feasibility and state motivation are reasons to investigate, not evidence of a particular operation.

Do not erase ordinary explanations

Community politics, social clustering, platform effects, prejudice, bureaucracy, and interpersonal conflict may produce similar outcomes.

Do not accuse weakly evidenced intermediaries

The model must not become a tool for community purges, loyalty tests, or public accusation.

Do not reproduce authoritarian logic

Communities protecting themselves must not begin scoring one another for ideological purity or presumed infiltration.

Do not flatten states or regions

Country studies must distinguish documented conduct, capability, political incentive, and uncertainty.

Do not wait for catastrophe before discussing safeguards

Responsible capability analysis asks what could foreseeably go wrong before the system becomes impossible to dismantle.

🧌 Goblin Summary

The state bought a little database to help it understand the community.

The database noticed that people knew other people.

Then it wanted to know whether those people trusted each other.

Then it wanted to know what they might accomplish together.

Then it became concerned that reality was not behaving according to the dashboard.

So it asked for more data.

This orientation cluster explains why that is a governance problem before the plant has finished eating the shop.
