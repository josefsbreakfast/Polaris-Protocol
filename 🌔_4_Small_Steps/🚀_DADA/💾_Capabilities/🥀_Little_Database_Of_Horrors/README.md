# 🥀 Little Database Of Horrors  
**First created:** 2026-07-17 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-21  
*A DADA capability model for relationship mapping, diaspora management, social containment, and the systems that keep demanding more intimate data because human beings remain inconveniently unpredictable.*  

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

The government bought a plant.

At first, the plant wanted contact data.

Then it wanted family data.

Then political data.

Then relationship scores.

Then everybody continued behaving like human beings rather than stable variables in an administrative model.

So naturally the plant asked for more people.

`🥀 Little Database Of Horrors` examines a foreseeable convergence of:

- state and commercial databases;
- social-graph analysis;
- behavioural and political risk scoring;
- spyware and communications access;
- embassy and consular intelligence;
- family and community pressure;
- platform ranking and recommendation;
- administrative and legal leverage;
- transnational repression;
- systems designed to identify influential relationships.

Its central question is:

> What happens when institutions move from observing diaspora relationships to classifying, constraining, redirecting, or exploiting them?

The cluster does not begin by alleging that one complete system already exists in a particular case.

It begins with a capability-and-incentive warning:

- many necessary components already exist;
- many states and institutions have reasons to combine them;
- relevant safeguards remain fragmented;
- the consequences may become visible before the full architecture does.

The plant does not need perfect control.

It only needs to alter probabilities:

- fewer introductions;
- more warnings;
- reduced visibility;
- delayed opportunities;
- redirected attention;
- damaged trust;
- increased exhaustion;
- higher costs for consequential relationships.

It does not need to control every branch.

It only needs to keep pruning the ones it dislikes.

## 🪴 Somewhere That’s Green  

The system rarely introduces itself as an apparatus for governing relationships.

It arrives with a cleaner promise.

A safe, legible, well-coordinated community in which institutions can:

- identify needs;
- distribute support;
- prevent harm;
- engage representatives;
- counter hostile actors;
- reduce uncertainty;
- manage risk;
- make better decisions.

Many of those aims are legitimate.

The danger lies in the transition from:

> understanding the community

to:

> deciding which relationships within it are safe, useful, tolerable, or politically consequential.

The Little Shop of Horrors framing supplies the escalation model.

The system begins as a small and apparently useful administrative plant.

It asks for a little data.

Then more data.

Then more intimate data.

Its failure to predict people is treated not as a reason to stop, but as evidence that it has not yet been fed enough.

> Feed me data.

## 🕸️ Who May Safely Know Whom?  

A database built to understand, engage, safeguard, monitor, or assess a diaspora may begin answering questions such as:

- Who influences whom?
- Which relationships create political capacity?
- Who connects dissidents to lawyers, journalists, funders, institutions, or one another?
- Which family members can be pressured?
- Which contacts are considered safe?
- Which relationships remain tolerable while private or emotionally absorptive?
- Which relationships become dangerous when they produce evidence, protection, organisation, or escape?
- Who may safely know whom?

This is the governing question of the cluster.

The subject of risk is no longer only the person.

It may become the relationship.

Two people who appear individually manageable may become consequential together because they combine:

- evidence and publication;
- testimony and legal skill;
- money and organisation;
- cultural trust and institutional access;
- persistence and protection;
- political legitimacy and technical capacity.

The system may tolerate contact.

It may object to consequence.

## 🔭 Capability Is Not Allegation  

This cluster maintains a firm distinction between:

- documented capability;
- documented conduct;
- reasonable inference;
- foreseeable abuse;
- untested hypothesis;
- unsupported allegation.

The existence of social-graph software does not prove that a state is arranging personal relationships.

An unusual social pattern does not prove a central database.

A regime’s motivation does not prove a particular operation.

A contractor’s product does not prove every possible use.

An embassy contact does not prove foreign-state direction.

The pack should show:

1. what already exists;
2. what has already been done;
3. what becomes technically possible when capabilities converge;
4. what incentives might encourage escalation;
5. what evidence would establish actual use;
6. what would weaken or disprove the model.

> The smallest supportable claim should always be made first.

A useful investigation may begin with:

- one field added;
- one purpose expanded;
- one referral shared;
- one representative empowered;
- one category reused;
- one relationship threshold repeated;
- one correction that failed to travel.

The model becomes stronger when every claim has to earn its name.

## 🩸 What The Plant Eats  

Relationship governance does not require one extraordinary source of information.

It can emerge through the accumulation of ordinary records:

- phone and messaging metadata;
- device contents;
- contact lists;
- location and travel;
- border records;
- financial transfers;
- employment and education;
- protest and event attendance;
- religious and community participation;
- family records;
- immigration and consular files;
- social-media activity;
- platform engagement;
- commercial data;
- intermediary reports.

None of these needs to contain a field labelled:

> dangerous friendship

The relationship graph appears when records are joined.

Yet observable contact is not human meaning.

The database may know that two people communicate.

It may not know:

- whether they trust one another;
- whether one is helping, exploiting, investigating, or avoiding the other;
- whether the relationship is stable;
- whether it will produce political action;
- whether the underlying record is accurate;
- what the relationship means to either person.

That gap is foundational.

The graph contains edges.

It does not contain love, loyalty, courage, humour, grief, obligation, recognition, or moral commitment in forms the system can reliably measure.

## 🎶 When Records Become Scores  

The decisive shift occurs when the system stops asking:

> What relationships exist?

and begins asking:

> Which relationships are likely to produce outcomes the institution dislikes?

Possible scoring dimensions may include:

- dissent;
- reach;
- credibility;
- organising capacity;
- institutional access;
- technical skill;
- legal skill;
- cross-community connection;
- persistence;
- dependency;
- vulnerability;
- intervention cost;
- reputational consequence;
- coalition potential.

Relationship risk is not the same as person risk.

The system may also prefer contacts who provide:

- familiarity;
- attention;
- cultural recognition;
- emotional expenditure;
- companionship;
- prestige;

while lacking:

- independence;
- institutional leverage;
- cross-network access;
- willingness to act;
- durable solidarity;
- practical protection.

The claim is not that every similar person is deliberately inserted.

The model asks whether systems may prefer relationships that absorb distress while failing to alter material conditions.

A relationship may be tolerated while it remains:

- casual;
- private;
- decorative;
- emotionally consuming;
- institutionally minor.

Friction may increase when it becomes:

- corroborative;
- protective;
- financially useful;
- legally consequential;
- politically organised;
- publicly credible;
- capable of creating an exit route.

Mushnik does not need to understand the whole plant.

He needs the shop to function.

- commercial incentive;
- bureaucratic convenience;

- deference to security language;
- refusal to inspect the architecture too closely.

> The front counter remains clean because the teeth are distributed through the supply chain.

## 🦷 When The Plant Acts  

The system does not need a formal prohibition in order to alter relationships.

It may change:

- visibility;
- timing;
- reputation;
- access;
- cost;
- confidence;
- opportunity;
- emotional capacity.

Possible interventions include:

- suppressing introductions;
- omitting people from events or lists;
- discouraging collaboration;
- circulating warnings;
- controlling invitations;
- reducing platform visibility;
- directing approved intermediaries;
- increasing administrative friction;
- pressuring relatives;
- exploiting immigration or travel dependency;
- threatening employment or funding;
- making relationships exhausting or dangerous to maintain;
- creating misunderstanding and wedge narratives.

The person may remain surrounded by contact while lacking:

- effective support;
- credible witnesses;
- institutional access;
- durable trust;
- people willing to act;
- safe routes out.

> Social contact is not the same as social protection.

The plant does not need to eliminate every relationship.

It can tolerate relationships that absorb distress while obstructing those capable of changing material conditions.

## 🌱 Cuttings Travel  

Digital authoritarian capabilities travel well.

They can be sold, shared, adapted, and connected as modular tools:

- spyware;
- biometric identification;
- social-media monitoring;
- influence analysis;
- risk scoring;
- border systems;
- identity infrastructure;
- data fusion;
- protest monitoring;
- communications interception.

A state does not need to invent a complete diaspora-management architecture.

It may acquire components from:

- private contractors;
- allied states;
- security partners;
- commercial data markets;
- existing public systems.

Transnational repression may therefore be collaborative.

One state may identify a target.

Another may detain them.

A contractor may supply the data.

A platform may shape visibility.

A border system may record movement.

A community intermediary may supply context.

A diplomatic mission may preserve distance.

Shared technology does not automatically prove shared intent.

The routes still require evidence.

## 🌍 Comparative Capability Studies  

The cluster should not be reduced to one familiar state or one familiar region.

Every case should be asked the same questions:

1. What conduct is documented?
2. What technical capability exists?
3. What administrative infrastructure is available?
4. What political incentive is present?
5. What diaspora pressure is known?
6. What role do embassies or proxies play?
7. What cooperation exists with other states?
8. How has the host country responded?
9. What safeguards are publicly identifiable?
10. What confidence level does the evidence support?

State studies must distinguish:

- capability;
- intent;
- documented use;
- foreseeable temptation.

They must not:

- flatten political systems;
- confuse states with diasporas;
- treat regional identity as a risk marker;
- imply equivalence where conduct differs;
- treat absence of public evidence as proof of innocence or secrecy.

The point of comparison is not to produce a league table of horror.

It is to identify transferable mechanisms, varying safeguards, and documented routes.

## 🌡️ The System Under Pressure  

A database that appears limited or administrative under ordinary conditions may behave differently during:

- war;
- sanctions;
- protest;
- elections;
- succession;
- coup fear;
- diplomatic crisis;
- terrorism;
- leadership scandal;
- international litigation;
- economic collapse;
- mass defection.

Under pressure:

- more people are scored;
- categories widen;
- confidence thresholds fall;
- ambiguous conduct becomes suspicious;
- relationships become networks;
- networks become coordination;
- dissent becomes foreign alignment;
- requests for restraint become evidence of concealment.

When reality no longer fits the available model, officials may:

- add categories;
- integrate more records;
- lower thresholds;
- expand surveillance;
- increase intervention;
- blame targets for unpredictability.

The pot gets larger.

The plant grows with it.

Escalation is plausible, not inevitable.

Institutions can still:

- stop;
- review;
- narrow;
- delete;
- separate;
- close.

The question is whether they know when to refuse the plant another meal.

## 🥀 The Audrey II Problem  

Human relationships are not stable variables.

People change.

Loyalty, attraction, fear, courage, dependency, political belief, and willingness to act are contextual.

Relationships are not interchangeable.

A socially similar substitute may lack:

- shared history;
- trust;
- humour;
- recognition;
- practical solidarity;
- moral commitment;
- complementary skills.

Contact is not meaning.

Frequent communication may indicate:

- trust;
- conflict;
- obligation;
- investigation;
- exploitation;
- care;
- avoidance;
- dependency.

A clean score may hide:

- racism;
- class prejudice;
- sectarian assumptions;
- sexism;
- disability prejudice;
- political conformity;
- community gossip.

When people behave differently from the prediction, the system may conclude:

- they are deceptive;
- a hidden relationship exists;
- the network is more sophisticated;
- more surveillance is required.

The model may then create its own evidence.

Isolation, harassment, and exclusion may make people:

- angrier;
- more fearful;
- more secretive;
- more politically active;
- less trusting of institutions.

The system cites the response as confirmation.

> The model’s hunger is not a validation metric.

## 🔎 Plant Or Community?  

Many apparent signs of relationship management can arise without a central system.

Possible alternatives include:

- homophily;
- class sorting;
- geography;
- religious or professional clustering;
- institutional gatekeeping;
- platform recommendation;
- family conflict;
- gossip;
- factional politics;
- prejudice;
- exhaustion;
- coincidence.

A useful model must explain something these alternatives do not.

Relevant questions include:

- Are similar patterns appearing across disconnected settings?
- Does timing correspond to political or digital activity?
- Do unrelated institutions use identical language?
- Are the same errors repeated across systems?
- Are people acting on information they should not plausibly possess?
- Are comparable relationship thresholds producing comparable friction?
- Is there a shared actor, contractor, platform, or rule?
- What observation would disprove coordination?

Mixed explanations should remain available.

A platform, institution, embassy, contractor, and community faction may all contribute without one controlling centre.

> The architecture may be coherent enough to harm and fragmented enough that no single theory explains every part.

## 🚨 When The Plant Is Under Strain  

A strained system should become less elegant.

Its categories, interventions, and substitutes may begin to leak.

Possible signs include:

- contradictory classifications;
- stale, duplicated, or merged records;
- identical language across unrelated channels;
- recycled or unsuitable substitutes;
- repeated relationship ceilings;
- obvious collaborators kept adjacent but apart;
- people treated as both irrelevant and dangerous;
- intervention disproportionate to conduct;
- outsiders noticing the same strange pattern.

No sign proves a database.

The branch focuses on:

- repetition;
- comparison;
- timing;
- shared errors;
- documentary traces;
- independent observation;
- identifiable routes.

The management layer becomes visible through error.

## 🧪 Investigation Without Feeding Paranoia  

The investigation must produce better evidence than the system being criticised.

Records should separate:

- observation;
- report;
- inference;
- hypothesis;
- rival explanation;
- later outcome.

Useful methods may include:

- relationship incident logs;
- comparative graphs;
- threshold and timing trackers;
- repeated intermediary tracking;
- language comparison;
- natural experiments;
- secure evidence preservation.

The explicit safety rule is:

> Do not conduct amateur counter-intelligence.

Do not:

- bait suspected agents;
- confront people without evidence;
- publicly name alleged intermediaries;
- unlawfully access accounts or devices;
- endanger relatives;
- provoke a foreign state;
- create entrapment schemes;
- turn uncertainty into community purges.

The investigation kit should reduce paranoia, not formalise it.

## 🪓 Do Not Feed The Model  

A system causing repeated discriminatory or coercive harm should not answer every failure by demanding more data.

Some systems require:

- narrowing;
- suspension;
- deletion;
- separation;
- closure.

Potential stopping triggers include:

- repeated false positives;
- inability to explain outputs;
- discriminatory effects;
- incompatible reuse;
- coercive action based on weak inference;
- failure to correct data;
- political direction of categories;
- retaliation against critics;
- evidence of relationship or family interference;
- foreseeable risk of persecution.

The cluster considers whether some inferences should be prohibited entirely, including:

- political-risk scoring based on family;
- relationship scoring without evidence of unlawful conduct;
- ideological guilt by association;
- use of intimate data for political classification;
- suppression of lawful association;
- automated recommendations designed to obstruct protected activity.

> The plant does not need more data. The institution needs to learn when to stop.

## 🛡️ Community Protection  

The strongest community protection is not perfect secrecy.

It is a network that cannot easily be fractured by:

- one rumour;
- one intermediary;
- one lost account;
- one institutional refusal;
- one family conflict;
- one corrupted record.

Protective practice includes:

- direct verification before severing contact;
- multiple routes to legal, journalistic, financial, and political support;
- protection of family across borders;
- preservation of routes for repair;
- distributed knowledge;
- practical mutual aid;
- cross-community trust;
- refusal of public accusation on weak evidence.

Community defence must not become:

- loyalty testing;
- ideological policing;
- suspicion of outsiders;
- harassment;
- exclusion of difficult or traumatised people;
- retaliation against legitimate disagreement.

The cure must not reproduce the plant.

## ⚖️ Rights, Duties, And Escalation  

Association is not an administrative risk externality.

People retain rights to:

- privacy;
- family life;
- friendship;
- intimate life;
- expression;
- religion;
- political participation;
- association;
- movement;
- due process;
- non-discrimination.

Foreign-state coercion inside a host country may also raise questions of:

- sovereignty;
- diplomatic responsibility;
- state attribution;
- host-state protection;
- complicity;
- due diligence.

A database is not, by itself, a crime against humanity.

The legal question concerns whether infrastructure supports prohibited acts as part of a widespread or systematic attack against a civilian population, alongside the required knowledge and offence-specific elements.

Legal language should remain precise.

The metaphor may be funny.

The claims still need footnotes.

## 🎭 Language And Cover Stories  

A project may be described as:

- safeguarding;
- engagement;
- resilience;
- stability;
- extremism prevention;
- foreign-interference protection;
- community cohesion;
- protective monitoring;
- risk mitigation.

The issue is not that every benign phrase conceals abuse.

The issue is that benign phrases can prevent scrutiny of operational meaning.

The analytical move is to translate the phrase into the permission.

For example:

**Diaspora engagement**

may mean:

> Who is recognised as a legitimate community representative?

**Protective monitoring**

may mean:

> Whose contacts are being recorded and assessed?

**Social stability**

may mean:

> Which relationships are considered politically tolerable?

**Foreign-interference prevention**

may mean:

> Who is treated as a suspect bridge between communities?

The scandal may not lie in the programme’s title.

It may lie in the answer to:

> Who may safely know whom?

## 📚 The Evidentiary Spine  

A capability warning becomes credible when it is assembled from:

- documented transnational repression;
- known surveillance tools;
- procurement and contractor evidence;
- embassy and consular abuse;
- family coercion;
- legal and administrative leverage;
- authoritarian cooperation;
- platform and graph-analysis capability;
- international law;
- host-country policy.

Every major claim should record:

- exact proposition;
- jurisdiction;
- date;
- source;
- primary or secondary status;
- confidence;
- dispute;
- what it proves;
- what it does not prove;
- relevant nodes.

Preferred source order:

1. primary legal and official documents;
2. court records;
3. procurement and corporate records;
4. technical research;
5. established human-rights organisations;
6. high-quality investigative journalism;
7. testimony with clear provenance;
8. commentary and hypothesis.

Do not allow a memorable metaphor to outrun the evidence.

## 🔭 Working Definition  

**Little Database Of Horrors** is a DADA capability model examining how surveillance, risk scoring, social-graph analysis, platform systems, diplomatic networks, community intermediaries, and transnational repression could converge into infrastructure for classifying and shaping relationships within diaspora communities.

The cluster does not require proof that one complete architecture already exists.

It asks whether:

- the components exist;
- institutions have incentives to combine them;
- crisis would encourage expansion;
- errors would produce visible signs;
- affected communities and host states can recognise and interrupt the pathway before monitoring becomes persecution.

Human beings are not datasets with inconvenient gaps.

Association is not a defect in the model.

The plant does not need more data.

The institution needs to learn when to stop.
