🥀 Little Database of Horrors — Cluster Planning Notes

First created: 2026-07-17 | Last updated: 2026-07-17
Preparatory notes for a DADA capability cluster on relationship mapping, diaspora management, transnational repression, and systems that keep demanding more intimate data because human beings remain inconveniently unpredictable.

⸻

🛰️ Orientation

These notes preserve the intended structure and argument of:

🌔_4_Small_Steps/🚀_DADA/💾_Capabilities/🥀_Little_Database_Of_Horrors/

The cluster examines a foreseeable convergence of:

* state and commercial databases
* social-graph analysis
* behavioural and political risk scoring
* spyware and communications access
* embassy and consular intelligence
* family and community pressure
* platform ranking and recommendation
* transnational repression
* administrative and legal leverage
* systems designed to identify influential relationships

The central question is:

What happens when states move from monitoring diaspora relationships to classifying, constraining, redirecting, or exploiting them?

The cluster does not begin by alleging that one complete system already exists in a particular case.

It begins from a capability-and-incentive warning:

Many of the necessary components already exist.
Many regimes have strong incentives to combine them.
The relevant safeguards are fragmented, weak, or designed for less integrated systems.

The Little Shop of Horrors framing supplies the escalation model.

The system begins as a small and apparently useful administrative plant.

It asks for a little data.

Then more data.

Then more intimate data.

Its failure to predict people is treated not as a reason to stop, but as evidence that it has not yet been fed enough.

Feed me data.

⸻

🧿 Core Proposition

A database built to understand, engage, safeguard, monitor, or assess a diaspora can develop into infrastructure for social control when it begins to answer questions such as:

* Who influences whom?
* Which relationships create political capacity?
* Who connects dissidents to lawyers, journalists, funders, institutions, or one another?
* Which family members can be pressured?
* Which contacts are considered safe?
* Which relationships can be tolerated while they remain private or emotionally absorptive?
* Which relationships become dangerous when they produce evidence, protection, organisation, or escape?
* Who may safely know whom?

The danger does not require perfect control.

A system only needs to alter probabilities:

* fewer introductions
* more warnings
* reduced visibility
* increased exhaustion
* delayed opportunities
* redirected attention
* damaged trust
* higher costs for consequential relationships

The plant does not need to control every branch.

It only needs to keep pruning the ones it dislikes.

⸻

⚖️ Core Evidentiary Rule

This cluster must preserve a firm distinction between:

* documented capability
* documented conduct
* reasonable inference
* foreseeable abuse
* untested hypothesis
* unsupported allegation

The existence of social-graph software does not prove that a state is arranging personal relationships.

An unusual relationship pattern does not prove the existence of a central database.

A regime’s motivation does not prove a particular operation.

The pack should show:

1. what already exists;
2. what has already been done;
3. what becomes technically possible when capabilities converge;
4. what incentives might encourage escalation;
5. what evidence would be required to establish actual use;
6. what would weaken or disprove the model.

The smallest supportable claim should always be made first.

⸻

🧭 Orientation

Purpose

This cluster is the front door.

It introduces the Little Database of Horrors as a capability model and explains why relationship management is a logical next frontier of digital authoritarianism and transnational repression.

It should make the subject understandable without asking readers to accept the entire architecture at once.

What It Will Cover

The eventual nodes should establish:

* what the Little Database of Horrors is;
* why the botanical and Little Shop framing is useful;
* the difference between surveillance and social management;
* the meaning of “who may safely know whom”;
* why diaspora populations may be especially exposed;
* why the model is plausible without being treated as proven;
* how to begin with the smallest identifiable mechanism.

🪴_somewhere_thats_green.md should describe the benign promise: a clean, safe, administratively legible community in which engagement, risk, welfare, and influence appear manageable.

🥀_what_is_the_little_database_of_horrors.md should provide the central definition.

🕸️_who_may_safely_know_whom.md should introduce relationship permission as the cluster’s governing question.

🔭_capability_not_allegation.md should establish the claim limits.

🔬_the_smallest_claim_first.md should prevent researchers from beginning with a total architecture when a platform, embassy, contractor, institution, or community intermediary may explain the immediate pattern.

Why It Matters

Readers need a way to think about emerging abuse before a complete public proof case exists.

Waiting for a fully integrated system to be exposed may mean waiting until:

* the infrastructure is entrenched;
* the categories have hardened;
* affected communities have already been divided;
* the system has migrated into ordinary administration;
* people harmed by it are treated as unreliable because the mechanism is unfamiliar.

Boundaries

This cluster defines the model.

It should not carry the full technical evidence, regional comparisons, investigative method, or legal analysis. Those belong in later branches.

⸻

🩸 Feed Me Data

Purpose

This cluster maps what the plant consumes.

It should show that relationship management does not require one extraordinary source of information. It can emerge from the accumulation and integration of ordinary data sources.

Core Argument

A relationship-control system would be built from fragments:

* phone and messaging metadata;
* device contents;
* contact lists;
* location records;
* border and travel data;
* financial transfers;
* employment and education;
* protest and event attendance;
* religious and community participation;
* family records;
* immigration and consular files;
* social-media activity;
* platform engagement;
* commercial data;
* reports from intermediaries.

None of these alone needs to contain a field labelled “dangerous friendship.”

The relationship graph emerges when the records are joined.

What It Will Cover

The branch should explain:

* how social graphs are constructed;
* what metadata can reveal without reading every message;
* how weak signals become relationship inferences;
* how data from different legal purposes can be combined;
* how intimate, political, financial, and professional relationships become machine-readable;
* how data brokerage and contractors widen state capability;
* how uncertainty is hidden when a probabilistic inference is displayed as a clean dashboard result.

Important Distinction

The database may know that two people communicate.

It may not know:

* whether they trust one another;
* what the relationship means;
* whether one person is helping, exploiting, avoiding, or investigating the other;
* whether contact will produce political action;
* whether the relationship is stable;
* whether the underlying data are accurate.

This gap between observable contact and human meaning is foundational to the later Audrey II problem.

Evidence Needed

* technical literature on graph analysis;
* surveillance and spyware capabilities;
* data-broker and commercial analytics markets;
* public procurement material;
* consular and identity-database systems;
* examples of purpose creep;
* evidence of family and community mapping;
* platform recommendation and ranking systems.

Guardrail

The existence of data is not evidence that every possible inference has been made or operationalised.

⸻

🎶 Suddenly Surveillance

Purpose

This cluster marks the transition from collection to prediction and intervention.

It asks when an archive becomes a risk model.

Core Argument

The decisive shift occurs when the system stops asking:

What relationships exist?

and begins asking:

Which relationships are likely to produce outcomes the state dislikes?

The subject of assessment becomes not only the person, but the relationship.

What It Will Cover

The branch should develop possible scoring dimensions such as:

* individual dissent;
* institutional credibility;
* public reach;
* organising capacity;
* technical or legal skill;
* cross-community connection;
* access to money or media;
* persistence;
* dependency;
* vulnerability to pressure;
* intervention cost;
* likely reputational consequences;
* coalition potential.

It should explain why:

Relationship risk is not the same as person risk.

Two people who appear individually manageable may become consequential together because they combine:

* evidence and publication;
* access and persistence;
* legal knowledge and testimony;
* money and organisation;
* political legitimacy and technical skill;
* cultural trust and external protection.

Managed Substitutes

This branch should introduce the idea of socially or emotionally satisfying alternatives that do not offer the same political, institutional, or protective capacity.

The claim is not that every similar person is deliberately inserted.

The model asks whether a system might prefer contacts who provide:

* familiarity;
* attention;
* cultural recognition;
* emotional expenditure;
* prestige;
* companionship;

while lacking:

* independence;
* institutional leverage;
* willingness to act;
* cross-network access;
* protection;
* durable solidarity.

Relationship Caps

A cap is a functional threshold.

A relationship may be tolerated while it remains:

* casual;
* private;
* decorative;
* emotionally consuming;
* institutionally minor.

Friction may increase when it becomes:

* corroborative;
* protective;
* financially useful;
* legally consequential;
* politically organised;
* publicly credible;
* capable of creating an exit route.

Guardrail

This is a threat model, not a licence to reinterpret every failed friendship, professional relationship, or romance as an operation.

⸻

🪴 Mushnik’s Dashboard

Purpose

This cluster examines the respectable administrative environment in which the system might be proposed, procured, normalised, and defended.

It is the front counter of the shop.

Everything looks clean.

The plant is still in the back.

Core Argument

Intrusive capabilities are rarely introduced under names that clearly describe their most coercive potential.

They may enter institutions as:

* community safeguarding;
* diaspora engagement;
* vulnerability assessment;
* counter-extremism;
* hostile-state protection;
* foreign-interference prevention;
* consular welfare;
* public-order risk;
* influence monitoring;
* social stability;
* protective intelligence;
* service coordination.

Many of these are legitimate activities in principle.

The danger lies in:

* unclear purpose;
* hidden scoring;
* weak boundaries;
* reused data;
* unreviewable judgements;
* politically elastic categories;
* systems that expand during crisis.

What It Will Cover

The cluster should examine:

* who commissions the system;
* who supplies the software;
* who defines the categories;
* who supplies community intelligence;
* who can challenge a classification;
* whether embassies or foreign ministries retain operational distance;
* how contractors and intermediaries create plausible deniability;
* how host-country institutions may become unwitting participants;
* how “safe” intermediaries acquire gatekeeping power;
* how responsibility is dispersed until no one appears to have made the decision.

Mushnik’s Role

Mushnik does not need to understand every capability.

He needs the shop to function.

The metaphor should be used to explain institutional complicity through:

* commercial incentives;
* fear of reputational damage;
* deference to security language;
* bureaucratic convenience;
* unwillingness to inspect the plant too closely.

Evidence Needed

* procurement documents;
* privacy assessments;
* diplomatic and consular structures;
* contractor relationships;
* information-sharing agreements;
* safeguarding and counter-extremism programmes;
* whistleblower testimony;
* oversight and audit reports.

Guardrail

Do not assume that a benign label is automatically fraudulent.

The task is to test whether the operational system remains proportionate to the stated purpose.

🦷 The Plant Has Teeth

Purpose

This cluster describes the move from monitoring to active social shaping and coercion.

The plant is no longer merely consuming data.

It is acting on the environment.

Core Argument

Relationship management does not require formal bans.

A state or aligned network can alter social outcomes by changing:

* visibility;
* timing;
* reputation;
* access;
* cost;
* confidence;
* opportunity;
* emotional capacity.

What It Will Cover

Possible interventions include:

* suppressing introductions;
* omitting people from events or lists;
* discouraging collaboration;
* circulating warnings;
* controlling invitations;
* reducing platform visibility;
* directing approved intermediaries towards targets;
* increasing administrative friction;
* pressuring relatives;
* exploiting immigration or travel dependency;
* threatening employment or funding;
* using financial, legal, or reputational pressure;
* making relationships exhausting or dangerous to maintain;
* creating misunderstandings and wedge narratives.

Isolation Without Prohibition

A key node should explain how someone can remain surrounded by contact yet lack:

* effective support;
* credible witnesses;
* institutional access;
* durable trust;
* people willing to act;
* safe routes out.

The appearance of social inclusion may coexist with practical isolation.

What Makes a Relationship Consequential

The cluster should distinguish relationships that:

* absorb distress;

from relationships that:

* change material conditions.

The system may tolerate emotional discharge while obstructing protection, evidence-building, resource transfer, or coalition.

Guardrail

This branch must remain descriptive and defensive.

It should not become an operational guide for manipulating or isolating people.

🌍 Regime And State Capability Studies

Purpose

This cluster provides the comparative evidence base.

It should prevent the entire subject from being reduced to one familiar example—usually China—while also preventing regional generalisation from replacing evidence.

Core Argument

The relevant question should be asked consistently of every state:

What capability exists, what conduct is documented, what incentive is present, and what safeguards constrain use?

The Middle East and North Africa should receive detailed coverage because many states in the region combine:

* significant diaspora populations;
* highly centralised political authority;
* active security institutions;
* embassy and consular networks;
* surveillance procurement;
* political sensitivity to exile communities;
* family and economic links across borders.

This does not mean that all states have equivalent records or systems.

Comparative Method

Every state study should distinguish:

1. Documented conduct
2. Documented technical capability
3. Relevant administrative infrastructure
4. Political incentive
5. Known diaspora pressure
6. Embassy or proxy involvement
7. Authoritarian cooperation
8. Host-country response
9. Publicly identifiable safeguards
10. Confidence level

Possible confidence categories:

* strong documented record;
* multiple credible indicators;
* partial or contested evidence;
* capability without clear evidence of this use;
* foreseeable temptation;
* insufficient public information.

Regional Coverage

The planned regional branches include:

* Middle East and North Africa;
* East and Southeast Asia;
* Russia and the post-Soviet space;
* East and Central Africa;
* additional global cases;
* democratic-state temptations;
* allied complicity and selective blindness.

Middle East And North Africa

The branch should begin broadly rather than naming only adversarial states.

It should examine Iran, Israel, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Egypt, Türkiye, Bahrain, Qatar, Jordan, Syria, Iraq, Lebanon, Oman, Kuwait, Yemen, and Palestinian governance structures according to the evidence available.

Each state must be assessed separately.

Guardrails

* Do not flatten political systems.
* Do not imply equivalence where conduct differs.
* Do not treat a regional identity as a risk marker.
* Do not confuse a state with its diaspora.
* Do not treat absence of public evidence as proof of innocence or proof of secrecy.
* Preserve jurisdiction, date, source quality, and attribution.

🌡️ When The Pot Gets Too Small

Purpose

This cluster explains how the same system may behave very differently under ordinary and pressured conditions.

This is the principal escalation branch.

Core Argument

A database that appears limited, dormant, or administrative under normal conditions can become coercive when political leaders demand more certainty and control.

Relevant pressure conditions include:

* war;
* sanctions;
* protest;
* revolution;
* elections;
* succession;
* coup fear;
* diplomatic crisis;
* terrorism;
* leadership scandal;
* international litigation;
* economic collapse;
* mass defection.

The System At Rest

Under lower pressure, the system may be used primarily for:

* engagement;
* monitoring;
* grant decisions;
* community outreach;
* passive intelligence;
* routine risk assessment.

Errors may remain hidden because consequences are limited or dispersed.

The System Under Pressure

During crisis:

* more people are scored;
* categories widen;
* confidence thresholds fall;
* ambiguous conduct becomes suspicious;
* relationships are interpreted as networks;
* networks are interpreted as coordination;
* dissent is treated as foreign alignment;
* errors become operational facts;
* requests for restraint are treated as evidence of concealment.

The Pot Problem

Human communities remain more complex than the model.

When reality no longer fits the available categories, officials may:

* resize the model;
* add more categories;
* feed it more data;
* expand surveillance;
* increase intervention;
* blame targets for unpredictability.

The pot gets larger.

The plant grows with it.

Pathway To Severe Abuse

The cluster should show how mass harm can arise without one meeting in which officials consciously decide to commit an international crime.

The pathway may instead be:

1. create a database for a limited purpose;
2. integrate additional sources;
3. assign risk categories;
4. expand categories during crisis;
5. act on uncertain classifications;
6. treat resistance as confirmation;
7. weaken review;
8. increase coercion;
9. normalise discriminatory consequences;
10. continue despite systematic harm.

Guardrail

Escalation is plausible, not inevitable.

The cluster should identify where institutions can still stop, review, delete, narrow, or close the system.

🥀 The Audrey II Problem

Purpose

This cluster explains why the model is structurally unreliable and institutionally hungry.

It is the intellectual centre of the horror metaphor.

Core Argument

Human relationships cannot be made reliably predictable through accumulating data.

The model’s failures may nevertheless increase its appetite.

The system keeps demanding more intimate data because the last batch did not make human beings predictable enough.

What It Will Cover

Human relationships are unstable variables

People change.

Loyalty, courage, attraction, fear, political belief, dependency, and willingness to act are contextual.

Relationships are not interchangeable

A substitute may resemble someone socially or aesthetically while failing to reproduce:

* shared history;
* trust;
* humour;
* recognition;
* practical solidarity;
* moral commitment;
* complementary skills.

Contact is not meaning

Frequent communication may indicate:

* trust;
* conflict;
* investigation;
* obligation;
* exploitation;
* avoidance;
* care;
* dependency.

The graph does not contain the whole relationship.

Bias becomes risk

Operators may encode:

* class prejudice;
* racism;
* sectarian assumptions;
* sexism;
* political conformity;
* disability prejudice;
* community gossip;
* institutional reputation.

A clean score can hide a dirty judgement.

Error becomes concealment

When people behave differently from the prediction, the system may conclude that:

* they are deceptive;
* a hidden relationship exists;
* the network is more sophisticated;
* more surveillance is needed.

The model creates its own evidence

Isolation, harassment, or exclusion may make people angrier, more fearful, or more politically active.

The system then cites that response as confirmation of the original classification.

Administrative Narcissism

The cluster should examine the institutional vanity involved in believing that a dashboard can adequately model:

* love;
* loyalty;
* family;
* solidarity;
* political awakening;
* grief;
* moral courage.

Guardrail

The critique should not imply that all analysis is useless.

The problem is treating probabilistic and partial models as sufficient grounds for coercive action.

🔎 Is It The Plant Or The Community

Purpose

This cluster is the differential diagnosis.

It asks how to distinguish a shared electronic or administrative system from ordinary community politics and platform effects.

Core Argument

Many apparent signs of social management can arise without central coordination.

Possible alternatives include:

* homophily;
* class sorting;
* geography;
* religious or professional clustering;
* institutional gatekeeping;
* platform recommendation;
* family conflict;
* gossip;
* factional politics;
* prejudice;
* exhaustion;
* coincidence.

A useful model must explain something these alternatives do not.

Questions It Must Answer

* Are similar patterns appearing across disconnected settings?
* Does timing correspond to political or digital activity?
* Do unrelated institutions use identical categories or language?
* Are people acting on information they should not plausibly possess?
* Do the same data errors recur across systems?
* Are interventions triggered at comparable relationship thresholds?
* Is there a shared actor, contractor, platform, or institution?
* Does the pattern remain after ordinary explanations are tested?
* What observation would disprove electronic coordination?

Signs More Consistent With Community Politics

* local variation;
* personal language;
* known interpersonal routes;
* slow diffusion;
* faction-specific narratives;
* inconsistent outcomes;
* changes following visible disputes.

Signs More Consistent With Shared Data Or Rules

* repeated categories across institutions;
* rapid simultaneous changes;
* identical phrasing;
* stale or merged errors appearing in several places;
* improbable awareness of weak or private links;
* interventions across disconnected settings;
* consistent relationship ceilings;
* decisions that appear rule-based rather than personal.

Guardrail

The branch should actively resist turning uncertainty into accusation.

It must preserve room for mixed explanations.

A platform, institution, embassy, contractor, and community faction may all contribute without one controlling centre.

🍄 Signs The Plant Is Under Strain

Purpose

This cluster documents the visible fruiting bodies of a hidden or overloaded process.

The mushroom metaphor works because outsiders may notice surface anomalies after a longer period of unseen activity beneath the social environment.

Core Argument

A strained system should become less elegant.

Its categories, interventions, and substitutes should begin to leak.

The management layer becomes visible through error.

What It Will Cover

Contradictory classifications

A person is treated as:

* irrelevant and dangerous;
* isolated and highly connected;
* incompetent and strategically sophisticated;
* harmless in one context and intolerable in another.

Stale, duplicated, and merged records

Old relationships persist in current assessments.

Different people are conflated.

Corrections fail to propagate.

Identical language

Supposedly independent institutions repeat:

* the same unusual phrase;
* the same error;
* the same character judgement;
* the same unexplained risk category.

Recycled or unsuitable substitutes

The same narrow contact profile appears repeatedly despite poor fit.

The contact satisfies visible surface traits but lacks the same consequential qualities.

Repeated relationship ceilings

Different relationships progress normally until they approach:

* shared work;
* evidence exchange;
* institutional access;
* durable trust;
* public legitimacy;
* protection.

Then similar friction appears.

Obvious collaborators kept adjacent but apart

People share:

* events;
* institutions;
* networks;
* causes;
* mutual contacts;

yet repeatedly fail to meet or work together despite obvious relevance.

Disproportionate intervention

The apparent response is far greater than the conduct would ordinarily justify.

Outsider recognition

People outside the presumed target group begin asking:

* Why does this keep happening?
* Why are the same people always in the middle?
* Why do obvious introductions never occur?
* Why is everyone repeating the same warning?
* Why does the social environment feel curated but ineffective?

Evidence Standard

No sign proves a database.

The branch should focus on:

* repetition;
* comparison;
* independent observation;
* documentary traces;
* timing;
* shared errors;
* identifiable routes.

Guardrail

Do not encourage people to interpret every social disappointment as evidence of hidden management.

🧪 Investigation Kit

Purpose

This cluster turns the model into a safe and disciplined investigative method for researchers, journalists, lawyers, affected communities, and oversight bodies.

Core Argument

The investigation must produce better evidence than the system being criticised.

It should not contaminate witnesses, spread accusations, or create new wedges.

What It Will Cover

Observation, inference, hypothesis

Records should separate:

* what happened;
* what was directly observed;
* what another person reported;
* what is inferred;
* what explanation is being tested;
* what alternatives remain possible.

Relationship incident logs

Record:

* relationship type;
* relevant dates;
* change in access or behaviour;
* threshold reached;
* named actor;
* documentary evidence;
* ordinary explanation;
* suspected mechanism;
* later outcome.

Comparative graphs

Compare patterns across:

* active and inactive community members;
* cities;
* institutions;
* political periods;
* relationship types;
* insiders and outsiders;
* consequential and non-consequential relationships.

Timing trackers

Look for:

* changes after public statements;
* changes after private communications;
* changes around travel;
* changes around protests, litigation, elections, or crises;
* repeated delay intervals;
* simultaneous institutional responses.

Language comparison

Track recurring:

* labels;
* warnings;
* factual errors;
* unusual expressions;
* risk descriptions.

Natural experiments

Compare similar relationships where only one becomes institutionally or politically consequential.

Evidence preservation

Cover:

* contemporaneous notes;
* original files;
* metadata;
* secure storage;
* access control;
* lawful handling;
* source protection;
* escalation to professionals.

Explicit Safety Rule

Do not conduct amateur counter-intelligence.

Do not:

* bait suspected agents;
* confront people without evidence;
* publicly name alleged intermediaries;
* access accounts or devices unlawfully;
* endanger relatives;
* provoke a foreign state;
* create false identities or entrapment schemes;
* turn uncertainty into community purges.

Guardrail

The investigation kit should be designed to reduce paranoia, not formalise it.

Purpose

This cluster covers prevention, stopping rules, oversight, correction, deletion, and closure.

Its question is:

At what point should the institution stop feeding the plant?

Core Argument

A system causing repeated discriminatory or coercive harm should not be allowed to answer every failure by demanding more data.

Some systems require narrowing.

Some require suspension.

Some require deletion.

Some should never be built.

What It Will Cover

* purpose limitation;
* data minimisation;
* prohibited inferences;
* restrictions on intimate and associational data;
* mandatory review;
* sunset clauses;
* independent authorisation;
* human-rights assessments;
* appeal and correction;
* record separation;
* deletion;
* auditability;
* contractor oversight;
* whistleblowing;
* remedies;
* system closure.

Stopping Rules

Possible stopping triggers include:

* repeated false positives;
* inability to explain outputs;
* discriminatory impacts;
* use beyond the original purpose;
* coercive action based on weak inference;
* failure to correct data;
* political direction of categories;
* retaliation against critics;

* suppressing lawful association;
* automated recommendations designed to obstruct protected activity.

Core Line

The model’s hunger is not a validation metric.

🕸️ Community Protection

Purpose

This cluster focuses on preserving relationships, trust, redundancy, and routes to support where transnational repression or digital social control is feared.

It is the practical anti-isolation branch.

Core Argument

The strongest community protection is not perfect secrecy.

It is a network that cannot easily be fractured by:

* one rumour;
* one intermediary;
* one lost account;
* one institutional refusal;
* one family conflict;
* one corrupted record.

What It Will Cover

Anti-wedge protocols

Communities should develop habits such as:

* verify directly;
* slow down before severing contact;
* distinguish disagreement from infiltration;
* preserve routes for repair;

Multiple routes to support

No one actor should monopolise access to:

* legal help;
* journalists;
* funding;
* safeguarding;
* community leadership;
* mental-health support;
* political representation.

Family protection

The branch should address the reality that public activity may expose relatives in another jurisdiction.

Guidance must be careful, proportionate, and developed with specialist organisations.

Host-country routes

Map lawful routes through:

* police;
* protective-security bodies;
* lawyers;
* universities;
* employers;
* unions;
* civil society;
* parliamentarians;
* journalists;
* regulators;
* data-protection authorities.

Relationships The Model Cannot Easily Cap

This should focus on:

* cross-community trust;
* redundant connections;
* direct verification;
* shared records;
* practical mutual aid;
* distributed knowledge;
* links outside one gatekeeping institution.

Guardrails

Community defence must not become:

* loyalty testing;
* ideological policing;
* suspicion of outsiders;

* harassment;

The cure must not reproduce the plant.

⚖️ Ethics And International Law

Purpose

This cluster establishes the rights, duties, attribution problems, and escalation thresholds relevant to relationship mapping and diaspora management.

Core Argument

Association is not an administrative risk externality.

People retain rights to:

* privacy;
* family life;
* friendship;
* intimate life;
* expression;
* religion;
* political participation;
* association;
* movement;
* due process;
* non-discrimination.

A foreign state’s coercive activity inside a host country also raises questions of:

* sovereignty;
* diplomatic responsibility;
* state attribution;
* host-state protection;
* complicity;
* due diligence.

What It Will Cover

Human rights

Potentially relevant frameworks include:

* privacy and family life;
* expression and association;
* freedom of religion;
* non-discrimination;
* liberty and security;
* protection from torture and ill-treatment;
* movement;
* effective remedy.

Attribution and proxies

The branch should examine when actions by:

* embassies;
* consulates;
* contractors;
* community organisations;
* private individuals;
* allied states;

may be legally attributed to a state.

Plausible deniability

Operational distance may complicate proof without removing harm.

Data infrastructure and persecution

The existence of a database is not itself a crime against humanity.

The relevant legal question would concern whether it supports prohibited acts as part of a widespread or systematic attack directed against a civilian population, alongside the required knowledge and offence-specific elements.

The system may become relevant infrastructure for:

* persecution;
* arbitrary detention;
* deportation;
* enforced disappearance;
* torture;
* severe deprivation of rights;
* other qualifying acts.

Duty To Stop

Institutions must not continue a harmful system merely because no one originally intended its final use.

The duty to review grows when:

* purposes change;
* categories widen;
* political pressure rises;
* harms become systematic;
* errors are known;
* remedies fail.

Guardrail

Legal terminology must be used precisely.

Do not use “crimes against humanity” as a general synonym for frightening or authoritarian technology.

✨ Short Version

Little Database of Horrors examines how surveillance, risk scoring, social-graph analysis, embassy networks, platform systems, and transnational repression could converge into infrastructure for classifying and shaping relationships within diaspora communities.

The cluster does not require proof that one complete architecture already exists.

It asks whether:

* the components exist;
* regimes have incentives to combine them;
* pressure would encourage expansion;
* errors would produce visible signs;
* communities and host states can recognise and interrupt the pathway before monitoring becomes persecution.

Human beings are not datasets with inconvenient gaps.

Association is not a defect in the model.

The plant does not need more data.

The institution needs to learn when to stop.
