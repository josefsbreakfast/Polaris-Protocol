Yes. I’d give every cluster folder its own README.md, including the regional folders that do not currently show one. Each README should orient the reader, define scope, explain inclusion choices, and point to the comparative guardrails.

🌍 Regime_And_State_Capability_Studies/

README.md

Introduces the comparative purpose of the branch: identifying documented mechanisms, transferable capabilities, state incentives, diaspora effects, host-state responses, and safeguards without producing a simplistic ranking of “bad regimes.”

⸻

🧭 Comparative_Method/

README.md

Explains the common method applied across every country and regional study. Establishes consistent questions, evidence standards, confidence language, and safeguards against false equivalence or unsupported attribution.

🔍_questions_to_ask_of_every_state.md

Provides the standard research template: documented conduct, technical capability, administrative infrastructure, political incentives, diaspora pressure, embassy or proxy roles, foreign cooperation, host-state response, and identifiable safeguards.

📏_evidence_and_confidence_scale.md

Defines how evidence is graded, from confirmed primary documentation through strong corroboration, reasonable inference, plausible capability, and unsupported hypothesis. Clarifies what raises or lowers confidence.

⚖️_capability_intent_and_documented_use.md

Separates possession of a capability from incentive, authorised implementation, documented deployment, attribution, and demonstrated harm. Prevents political hostility or technical capacity from being treated as proof of use.

🧯_avoiding_false_equivalence.md

Explains how to compare recurring mechanisms without flattening differences in scale, coercion, legality, political structure, oversight, or evidentiary strength. Comparison identifies patterns; it does not declare all states morally or operationally identical.

⸻

🕌 Middle_East_And_North_Africa/

README.md

Introduces a region containing monarchies, republics, occupations, fragmented states, war economies, security partnerships, and substantial diaspora populations. Highlights spyware markets, diplomatic pressure, family leverage, regional intelligence cooperation, and major differences between cases.

🇮🇷_iran.md

Examines documented transnational repression, family coercion, surveillance, assassination and abduction plots, online intimidation, consular and intelligence routes, and the targeting of dissidents, journalists, minority groups, and opposition networks abroad.

🇮🇱_israel.md

Examines surveillance and data-fusion capability, military and intelligence technology, occupation-related population control, political and diaspora monitoring, commercial spyware exports, and the need to distinguish Israeli state institutions from Jewish communities abroad.

🇸🇦_saudi_arabia.md

Covers spyware, online influence, threats to dissidents, diplomatic and intelligence reach, family pressure, rendition risks, and documented violence against critics abroad, alongside the protection supplied by strategic alliances.

🇦🇪_united_arab_emirates.md

Examines commercial surveillance capability, foreign contractors, influence operations, political monitoring, cross-border targeting, financial leverage, and the UAE’s role as both a regional security actor and international business hub.

🇪🇬_egypt.md

Covers repression of exiled dissidents, family retaliation, digital surveillance, passport and consular pressure, security-service reach, diaspora intimidation, and cooperation with foreign governments under counter-terrorism or migration frameworks.

🇹🇷_turkiye.md

Examines overseas intelligence activity, diaspora and religious-institution networks, extradition and rendition efforts, political profiling, family pressure, and targeting linked to Kurdish politics, Gülenist allegations, journalism, and opposition activity.

🇧🇭_bahrain.md

Explores spyware use, retaliation against families, diplomatic pressure, prosecution of critics, regional security dependence, and the consequences of close cooperation with Saudi Arabia and Western security partners.

🇶🇦_qatar.md

Assesses surveillance and influence capacity, treatment of critics and workers, regional political competition, media and diplomatic reach, and where public evidence of systematic diaspora repression is stronger, weaker, or contested.

🇯🇴_jordan.md

Examines intelligence capacity, political monitoring, cyber and communications controls, pressure on activists, refugee and border-data infrastructure, and Jordan’s intermediary role within Western and regional security relationships.

🇸🇾_syria.md

Covers extensive diaspora surveillance, embassy reporting, family punishment, detention on return, intelligence networks, coercive consular practices, wartime fragmentation, and the difficulty of separating state, militia, and allied-state capability.

🇮🇶_iraq.md

Examines fragmented authority across federal bodies, regional institutions, militias, parties, and foreign-backed networks. Focuses on how overlapping security, identity, border, and political systems complicate attribution and protection.

🇱🇧_lebanon.md

Explores fragmented governance, party and militia intelligence capacity, sectarian and family networks, foreign-state influence, banking and mobility pressure, and the blurred boundary between community information and coercive political infrastructure.

🇴🇲_oman.md

Assesses domestic monitoring and political-control capability, diplomatic mediation roles, regional data and security relationships, and the comparatively limited public evidence concerning systematic overseas targeting.

🇰🇼_kuwait.md

Examines digital speech enforcement, citizenship and residency leverage, surveillance capacity, political monitoring, and pressures affecting critics or stateless communities, while carefully marking gaps in evidence about overseas operations.

🇾🇪_yemen.md

Maps capability across fragmented authorities, including internationally recognised institutions, Houthi governance, separatist forces, militias, and foreign sponsors. Emphasises telecommunications access, aid systems, detention, family pressure, and conflict-driven data vulnerability.

🇵🇸_palestinian_authorities_and_fragmented_governance.md

Examines surveillance, political policing, security coordination, factional governance, occupation, donor infrastructure, and the distinct capacities of the Palestinian Authority, Hamas authorities, local actors, and external powers without treating Palestinians as one governing entity.

🔗_regional_security_and_data_cooperation.md

Maps shared spyware vendors, intelligence partnerships, counter-terrorism frameworks, border and migration systems, diplomatic coordination, rendition routes, and the role of Western or regional allies in enabling cross-border capability.

🌏 East_And_Southeast_Asia/

README.md

Introduces state and regional models involving party control, digital censorship, identity systems, overseas policing, family pressure, economic leverage, and technology transfer. Distinguishes highly centralised systems from smaller states adopting selected tools.

🇨🇳_china.md

Examines overseas police activity, united-front networks, family coercion, digital monitoring, platform and identity infrastructure, return pressure, transnational harassment, and the targeting of dissidents, minorities, journalists, and diaspora organisations.

🇭🇰_hong_kong.md

Covers the territorial and extraterritorial effects of the National Security Law, arrest warrants and bounties, diaspora warnings, pressure on relatives, institutional retreat, and the integration of Hong Kong enforcement with mainland capability.

🇰🇵_north_korea.md

Examines punishment of relatives, overseas labour control, embassy and intelligence monitoring, forced return, abduction history, digital operations, and the severe evidence limitations created by an exceptionally closed political system.

🇻🇳_vietnam.md

Covers monitoring and coercion of exiled activists, online harassment, family pressure, abduction allegations and documented cases, diplomatic routes, cyber capability, and targeting connected to political, religious, or environmental dissent.

🇰🇭_cambodia.md

Examines digital repression, opposition surveillance, threats to critics abroad, diplomatic and family pressure, cross-border cooperation, and the importation or adaptation of larger authoritarian governance models.

🔗_regional_authoritarian_cooperation.md

Maps technology transfer, deportation and return arrangements, policing cooperation, extradition, shared censorship models, diplomatic support, and the regional normalisation of treating dissidents abroad as domestic security subjects.

🪆 Russia_And_Post_Soviet_Space/

README.md

Introduces a region shaped by Soviet institutional inheritance, contemporary Russian influence, security-service cooperation, migration dependence, extradition systems, digital control, and substantial variation among post-Soviet states.

🇷🇺_russia.md

Examines assassinations and attempted assassinations abroad, poisoning, surveillance, cyber operations, family and property pressure, diaspora infiltration, legal harassment, disinformation, consular routes, and the targeting of opposition, journalists, defectors, and anti-war networks.

🇧🇾_belarus.md

Covers cross-border pursuit of opposition figures, aviation and border coercion, family retaliation, digital repression, passport restrictions, cooperation with Russia, and pressure on exiled democratic institutions and activists.

🇦🇿_azerbaijan.md

Examines spyware, threats and violence against critics, family pressure, media targeting, diaspora monitoring, international lobbying, detention or return risks, and the protection created by energy and security relationships.

🇹🇯_tajikistan.md

Covers coercion of relatives, forced returns, extradition requests, diaspora surveillance, targeting of opposition and regional minorities, dependence on Russian security and migration systems, and transnational pressure through employment vulnerability.

🇺🇿_uzbekistan.md

Examines the historical and current pursuit of dissidents, security-service reach, family coercion, return risks, reforms and their limits, and the need to distinguish earlier documented practices from present capability and conduct.

🇹🇲_turkmenistan.md

Covers extreme information control, pressure on relatives, movement restrictions, consular leverage, overseas monitoring, forced return concerns, and the difficulty of establishing scope in a highly closed state.

🔗_extradition_rendition_and_security_cooperation.md

Maps formal extradition, informal rendition, migration policing, regional security organisations, shared watchlists, passport controls, and the use of allied or dependent states to extend repression beyond national territory.

🌍 East_And_Central_Africa/

README.md

Introduces cases shaped by liberation movements, conflict, authoritarian consolidation, refugee and diaspora politics, aid dependence, regional security cooperation, and the use of family, consular, financial, and return pressure.

🇷🇼_rwanda.md

Examines threats, surveillance, alleged and documented violence against exiles, diplomatic pressure, community monitoring, family vulnerability, refugee politics, and the international reluctance to scrutinise a strategically favoured government.

🇪🇷_eritrea.md

Covers diaspora taxation, coercive consular services, community monitoring, threats to relatives, political festivals and organisations, forced-return risks, and the use of overseas institutions to sustain control over exiled populations.

🇪🇹_ethiopia.md

Examines diaspora surveillance and influence, political and ethnic targeting, spyware allegations and evidence, family pressure, conflict-driven classification, online mobilisation, and changing practices across successive governments and wars.

🇸🇸_south_sudan.md

Assesses intelligence and political monitoring capacity, threats to critics and journalists, diaspora factionalism, family and return pressure, conflict networks, and the evidentiary difficulty created by fragmented and rapidly changing authority.

🔗_regional_return_and_surveillance_networks.md

Maps refugee return pressure, intelligence cooperation, airline and border data, regional organisations, diaspora taxation, cross-border detention, foreign military partnerships, and the role of host states in enabling or resisting coercion.

🌎 Other_Case_Studies/

README.md

Creates a controlled route for mechanisms and states that do not fit the main regional folders. Prevents the primary structure from becoming exhaustive while preserving democratic, allied, hybrid, and cross-regional cases.

🧭_cases_outside_the_primary_regions.md

Sets criteria for adding further studies based on documented conduct, transferable mechanisms, comparative value, emerging risks, or important host-state failures rather than geographic convenience alone.

🏛️_democratic_state_temptations.md

Examines how democratic states may adopt political profiling, bulk data integration, association mapping, emergency powers, border screening, covert disruption, proxy enforcement, and weak appeal routes without formally abandoning democratic institutions.

🤝_allied_states_and_selective_blindness.md

Explores how strategic alliances, trade, intelligence sharing, migration policy, arms relationships, and diplomatic convenience may cause host states to overlook, facilitate, minimise, or outsource repression by partner governments.
