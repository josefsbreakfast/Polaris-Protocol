# ⚖️ Capability, Intent, And Documented Use  
**First created:** 2026-07-23 | **Last updated:** 2026-07-23  
*A method for separating what an actor can do, what it may want to do, what it has implemented, what it has actually used, and what effects can be proved.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

State capability analysis becomes unreliable when several different propositions are compressed into one sentence.

For example:

> The state has advanced surveillance tools, opposes this group, and therefore carried out this incident.

That sentence may contain:

- a true capability claim;
- a plausible motive claim;
- an unproven access claim;
- an untested implementation claim;
- a contested attribution;
- no demonstrated causal link.

The pieces may fit together.

They still do not become one fact merely because the story is coherent.

This node preserves the distinction between:

- capability;
- access;
- intent;
- implementation;
- documented use;
- attribution;
- causation;
- effect.

Its purpose is not to minimise risk.

It is to stop technical possibility and political hostility from laundering one another into proof.

> A completed motive is not a completed evidentiary chain.

---

## 🪜 The Core Evidentiary Ladder  

The central sequence is:

> **capability → access → implementation → documented use → attribution → causation**

Each step asks a different question.

Each step requires additional evidence.

Intent sits alongside this ladder.

It may increase the plausibility of use.

It cannot replace a missing step.

---

## 1. ⚙️ Capability  

**Capability** means that a system, institution, organisation, or actor can technically or practically perform a function.

Examples include the ability to:

- intercept communications;
- deploy spyware;
- analyse metadata;
- construct social graphs;
- identify devices;
- monitor travel;
- restrict passports;
- query border systems;
- trace financial transfers;
- influence platform visibility;
- map families;
- pressure relatives;
- use diplomatic or consular channels;
- seek extradition or forced return;
- circulate warnings;
- disrupt access to institutions.

Capability may be established through:

- technical documentation;
- product specifications;
- procurement;
- source code;
- system architecture;
- official policy;
- verified prior use;
- expert analysis;
- institutional mandate.

Capability answers:

> Could this system perform the function?

It does not answer:

- Did the relevant actor possess it?
- Was it operational?
- Was it used against this person?
- Who authorised its use?
- Did it cause the observed outcome?

A capability claim is important.

It is also the beginning of the chain.

---

## 2. 🔑 Access  

**Access** means that the relevant actor could realistically reach the necessary:

- data;
- system;
- authority;
- vendor;
- platform;
- diplomatic route;
- partner-state infrastructure;
- family or community network;
- financial or administrative leverage.

Ask:

- Did the actor have direct access?
- Was access mediated by another agency?
- Was it purchased?
- Was it shared by an ally?
- Was it provided by a contractor?
- Was it obtained through an embassy or consulate?
- Did access require a warrant, ministerial approval, contract, or informal request?
- Was access technically possible but institutionally prohibited?
- Were logs created?
- Could the access be audited?
- Did the actor have access during the relevant period?

A state may possess a broad national capability while the institution named in a specific claim lacks practical access.

A vendor may advertise a function that a customer never purchased.

A partner state may hold the relevant data but never share it.

Capability without access remains possibility at a distance.

---

## 3. 🧠 Intent  

**Intent** means a stated, demonstrated, or reasonably inferred objective.

Possible objectives include:

- suppressing dissent;
- preventing defection;
- protecting regime continuity;
- controlling exile politics;
- deterring testimony;
- preventing litigation;
- protecting elite wealth;
- maintaining territorial control;
- managing a diaspora;
- preventing coalition-building;
- restricting political association;
- punishing criticism;
- controlling international reputation;
- disrupting journalists, lawyers, funders, or organisers;
- preserving a preferred community-representative structure.

Intent may be evidenced through:

- law;
- public rhetoric;
- internal policy;
- official speeches;
- repeated conduct;
- target selection;
- formal doctrine;
- resource allocation;
- instructions;
- documented operational priorities.

Intent may also be inferred from context.

That inference should remain visible.

Ask:

- Is the intent explicit?
- Is it inferred from a pattern?
- Is it attributed to one agency or the whole government?
- Does public rhetoric reflect operational policy or domestic performance?
- Is the supposed objective concrete?
- Would the proposed action advance it?
- Is there an easier route?
- Would the action create disproportionate legal or diplomatic risk?
- Has the actor previously accepted that risk?

Intent may make deployment more plausible.

It does not prove access, implementation, use, or causation.

---

## 🧭 Intent Sits Beside The Ladder  

Intent should not be inserted as though it mechanically produces use.

The structure is better represented as:

```text
                    intent
                      ↓
capability → access → implementation → documented use → attribution → causation
```

Intent may influence:

- whether a capability is acquired;
- which data are collected;
- who becomes a target;
- how thresholds are set;
- whether safeguards are bypassed;
- whether intervention is authorised.

But the evidentiary question remains:

> What shows that the capability travelled from possibility into this actual outcome?

Motive helps explain conduct.

It cannot manufacture conduct from an evidentiary gap.

---

## 4. 🏗️ Implementation  

**Implementation** means the capability has entered a real:

- product;
- programme;
- policy;
- workflow;
- contract;
- system integration;
- institutional practice;
- operational partnership.

Implementation may be evidenced through:

- procurement records;
- configuration documents;
- data-protection assessments;
- operating procedures;
- training materials;
- audit records;
- licences;
- integration diagrams;
- user accounts;
- internal correspondence;
- official testimony;
- budgets;
- system outputs.

Ask:

- Was the capability merely discussed?
- Was it purchased?
- Was it delivered?
- Was it installed?
- Was it configured?
- Was it populated with data?
- Were staff trained?
- Was it connected to decision-making?
- Was the relevant module enabled?
- Did the programme move beyond pilot stage?
- Was it current during the period examined?
- Was it suspended, narrowed, or retired?
- Did the stated purpose expand?

Procurement is evidence of acquisition.

It is not always evidence of operational use.

A system may exist in a contract, a cupboard, or a slide deck while doing very little beyond producing invoices and confidence.

---

## 5. 🧾 Documented Use  

**Documented use** means evidence shows that the capability was actually deployed.

That use may be established for:

- one person;
- one incident;
- one target group;
- one agency;
- one operation;
- one programme;
- one historical period.

Possible evidence includes:

- access logs;
- forensic findings;
- decision records;
- operational instructions;
- court findings;
- authenticated communications;
- official admissions;
- witness testimony;
- repeated, independently corroborated cases;
- records showing an output was produced and acted upon.

Ask:

- What exactly was used?
- By whom?
- Against whom?
- When?
- For what stated or apparent purpose?
- Was use lawful, unlawful, disputed, or unknown?
- Was it isolated or repeated?
- Does the evidence establish use of the whole system or one feature?
- Does use in one context support use in another?
- Was the conduct current or historical?

Documented use should be stated no more broadly than the evidence.

For example:

> The service used spyware against named dissidents during the specified period.

does not automatically establish:

> The state uses spyware against everyone it dislikes.

The first claim may be established.

The second may be political weather wearing a lab coat.

---

## 6. 🧷 Attribution  

**Attribution** means linking the use or conduct to a specific actor.

Possible levels include:

- individual operator;
- official;
- unit;
- agency;
- ministry;
- contractor;
- proxy;
- diplomatic mission;
- governing authority;
- state.

Attribution evidence may include:

- command records;
- payment trails;
- contracts;
- technical infrastructure;
- access logs;
- official instructions;
- authenticated communications;
- court findings;
- diplomatic correspondence;
- operator testimony;
- state admissions;
- repeated operational signatures;
- coordinated government assessments.

Ask:

- What is the narrowest actor established?
- Is the actor state-directed, state-linked, state-tolerated, or independently aligned?
- Does technical infrastructure show control, access, prior use, or merely proximity?
- Could the infrastructure have been compromised?
- Does the evidence establish central instruction?
- Could the operation reflect local initiative?
- Is a contractor acting within or beyond mandate?
- Is the attribution judicially tested?
- Is it an official intelligence assessment with unpublished evidence?
- Are confidence terms disclosed?
- Do credible competing attributions remain?

Do not use “the state” merely because the sentence becomes tidier.

Tidiness is not attribution.

---

## 7. 🔬 Causation  

**Causation** means showing that the identified mechanism produced or materially contributed to the observed outcome.

The outcome may include:

- detention;
- refusal of service;
- passport cancellation;
- loss of employment;
- increased surveillance;
- suppression of visibility;
- exclusion from an institution;
- a warning circulated;
- a relationship disrupted;
- a relative threatened;
- a person forced to return;
- physical injury;
- death.

Ask:

- Did the output reach the decision-maker?
- Was it relied upon?
- Does the timing fit?
- Is the relevant record cited?
- Did the intervention follow the trigger?
- Could the outcome have arisen independently?
- Were several causes operating?
- Did correction alter the outcome?
- Did removal of the mechanism change the pattern?
- Is the effect direct, cumulative, administrative, or probabilistic?
- Is the claimed harm broader than the mechanism could reasonably produce?

A system may alter probabilities without issuing one explicit command.

For example, it may create:

- fewer introductions;
- more warnings;
- delayed decisions;
- increased scrutiny;
- reduced visibility;
- higher relationship costs.

Those effects still require evidence.

A plausible route is not yet a demonstrated cause.

## 🎯 Possession, Opportunity, Motive, Use, And Effect  

These terms are often treated as interchangeable.

They are not.

### Possession  

The actor owns or controls a capability.

### Opportunity  

The actor could have used it in the relevant circumstances.

### Motive  

The actor had a reason to want the outcome.

### Use  

The actor deployed the capability.

### Effect  

The deployment produced or materially contributed to the outcome.

A complete allegation may require all five.

Evidence of the first three does not automatically establish the last two.

This is familiar in ordinary investigation.

It should not become optional merely because the subject matter is geopolitically dramatic.

## 🌓 Capability Without Intent  

A capability may exist without evidence of the intent alleged.

Examples include:

- a fraud system capable of association mapping;
- a border database capable of political profiling;
- an embassy maintaining extensive community contacts;
- a platform capable of suppressing visibility;
- a security service possessing spyware;
- a civil registry capable of family mapping.

The capability may have:

- a legitimate stated purpose;
- several possible uses;
- dormant functions;
- weak governance;
- future misuse potential.

Questions should include:

- Was the proposed use authorised?
- Were safeguards sufficient?
- Could purpose drift occur?
- Were protected activities excluded?
- Was access logged?
- Could an individual operator misuse the capability?

The absence of documented hostile intent does not make governance irrelevant.

It means the claim should remain about capability and risk rather than intentional deployment.

---

## 🌑 Intent Without Capability  

An actor may want an outcome but lack the means to produce it through the proposed mechanism.

It may lack:

- technical capacity;
- jurisdiction;
- access;
- money;
- trained personnel;
- diplomatic reach;
- reliable data;
- host-state cooperation;
- operational competence.

A government may issue sweeping threats abroad while possessing limited practical reach.

A small agency may desire total visibility while operating several incompatible spreadsheets and one printer that fears commitment.

Intent still matters.

But operational weakness matters too.

Ask whether the actor would use:

- a cheaper administrative route;
- family pressure;
- visa or passport control;
- community intermediaries;
- public intimidation;
- ordinary policing cooperation;

instead of the sophisticated mechanism initially imagined.

The most technologically impressive explanation is not always the most plausible.

---

## 🌘 Capability And Intent Without Documented Use  

This is the most common inferential danger.

The actor:

- possesses the tool;
- dislikes the target;
- had the opportunity;
- has used similar methods elsewhere.

The conclusion may feel obvious.

It is still incomplete.

Ask:

- Is there evidence of access in this case?
- Was the relevant system operational?
- Did the target’s records enter it?
- Was an output produced?
- Was the output acted upon?
- Does technical evidence fit?
- Does timing fit?
- Is another actor more directly connected?
- Is there a simpler mechanism?
- What would disprove use?

Capability plus intent creates a serious question.

It does not create a documented operation.

---

## 🌗 Documented Use Without Central Direction  

Conduct may be documented without proving a single central command.

Possible explanations include:

- local initiative;
- factional competition;
- corrupt officials;
- proxy enthusiasm;
- contractor overreach;
- informal encouragement;
- ideological alignment;
- personal retaliation;
- institutional over-compliance;
- militia action;
- state tolerance without direct instruction.

Ask:

- Was the conduct ordered?
- Was it funded?
- Was it reported upward?
- Was it rewarded?
- Was it repeated elsewhere?
- Was it corrected or tolerated?
- Did leadership know?
- Should leadership reasonably have known?
- Did formal policy make the conduct foreseeable?
- Did the state investigate or protect the actor?

Lack of central instruction does not remove all state responsibility.

It changes the attribution question.

The relevant finding may concern:

- direct responsibility;
- complicity;
- tolerance;
- failure to prevent;
- failure to investigate;
- structural encouragement.

## 🧱 Incident, Pattern, Programme, Policy, And Structure  

The scale of the claim should match the evidence.

### Incident  

One documented event.

### Pattern  

Several comparable events with meaningful similarities.

### Programme  

An organised operational arrangement.

### Policy  

Formal or demonstrable institutional direction.

### Structural Practice  

Conduct embedded across systems or sustained through ordinary administration.

Ask:

- How many cases are known?
- Are they independent?
- Do they share a mechanism?
- Do they share a target category?
- Is central coordination evidenced?
- Does the pattern persist across time?
- Are similar cases explained by a public rule?
- Does conduct continue after exposure?
- Are errors corrected?
- Are officials rewarded or disciplined?

A pattern may be strong without proving formal policy.

A formal policy may exist without showing implementation in every case.

Do not force every plant sighting into proof that the entire greenhouse has received instructions.

---

## 📣 Public Rhetoric And Private Intent  

Public rhetoric may be relevant evidence.

It can:

- identify target groups;
- legitimise hostility;
- frame dissent as treason;
- encourage supporters;
- signal institutional priorities;
- prepare the public for coercive policy;
- normalise guilt by association.

It may also:

- exaggerate for domestic audiences;
- serve electoral performance;
- conceal operational restraint;
- create ambiguity;
- reflect one faction rather than the whole state.

Ask:

- Who spoke?
- In what official capacity?
- Was the statement repeated?
- Did law or practice follow?
- Did institutions adopt the same language?
- Did targeting increase?
- Were supporters encouraged to act?
- Was the rhetoric contradicted by policy?
- Does the statement establish intent, tolerance, or merely atmosphere?

Rhetoric can strengthen an intent assessment.

It does not prove that a particular operation was ordered.

---

## 🌱 Foreseeable Temptation  

Some capability combinations create a foreseeable temptation even where deployment is not documented.

Risk rises where:

- relevant infrastructure already exists;
- the actor has a political incentive;
- oversight is weak;
- data are already available;
- the capability has been used for adjacent purposes;
- crisis lowers restraint;
- emergency logic expands;
- vendors encourage reuse;
- host states are unlikely to object;
- accountability costs are low;
- categories are vague;
- lawful association is framed as risk.

Possible examples include:

- fraud tools reused for political screening;
- safeguarding systems reused for dissent classification;
- border systems reused for ideological filtering;
- diaspora engagement reused for representative control;
- counter-terrorism tools reused for public-order monitoring;
- family data reused for political pressure;
- influence analysis reused for relationship disruption.

“Foreseeable temptation” is a governance-warning category.

It means:

> The system creates a credible pathway to misuse that safeguards should address.

It does not mean:

> The misuse has occurred.

The distinction allows prevention before persecution without requiring allegations to outrun evidence.

---

## 📅 Historical Conduct And Present Capability  

Historical use matters.

It may establish:

- institutional habit;
- retained expertise;
- willingness to accept risk;
- target continuity;
- enduring infrastructure;
- survivor fear;
- weakness of safeguards.

But past use should not be presumed continuous.

Ask:

- Did the government change?
- Did the agency change?
- Was the programme dismantled?
- Was the vendor relationship terminated?
- Did law change?
- Did oversight improve?
- Did the conduct move to another institution?
- Are recent cases documented?
- Is reform independently verified?
- Are historical records being used as a substitute for present evidence?

Similarly, reform announcements should not be presumed effective.

The correct conclusion may be:

- historical use established;
- current capability retained;
- present deployment unknown.

That is more precise than either permanent condemnation or ceremonial rehabilitation.

## 🕸️ Relationship Governance Across The Ladder  

Within the Little Database of Horrors, the same evidentiary distinctions apply to relationship governance.

### Capability  

Can the system:

- map relationships;
- classify contacts;
- score coalition potential;
- identify influential bridges;
- distinguish isolated from supported targets?

### Access  

Does the relevant actor possess:

- contact data;
- family records;
- location;
- financial ties;
- community reports;
- platform information;
- diplomatic context?

### Intent  

Is there evidence of an objective to:

- limit coalition-building;
- control representation;
- isolate dissidents;
- redirect attention;
- pressure relatives;
- obstruct legal, journalistic, or political support?

### Implementation  

Has relationship analysis entered:

- a dashboard;
- a referral process;
- a risk model;
- a community-engagement workflow;
- a security assessment;
- a procurement;
- an approved-intermediary system?

### Documented Use  

Is there evidence that a specific relationship was:

- recorded;
- classified;
- warned against;
- obstructed;
- exploited;
- redirected;
- disrupted?

### Attribution  

Which institution, proxy, contractor, platform, or diplomatic actor carried out the intervention?

### Causation  

Did the intervention actually:

- prevent contact;
- reduce access;
- alter treatment;
- increase isolation;
- delay support;
- damage trust;
- block an exit route?

A system may be capable of asking:

> Who may safely know whom?

That does not prove it asked the question in every case.

A repeated relationship ceiling may be a useful trace.

It is not self-authenticating proof of the dashboard.

## 📝 Disciplined Wording  

### Capability  

- “The state possesses the technical capability to…”
- “The system could support…”
- “The relevant infrastructure is capable of…”

### Access  

- “The named institution had documented access to…”
- “Public evidence does not establish whether the agency could query…”
- “Access appears to have depended on…”

### Intent  

- “Public policy indicates an objective to…”
- “Official rhetoric supports an inference that…”
- “The available evidence does not establish operational intent.”

### Implementation  

- “Procurement records show that…”
- “The capability was integrated into…”
- “Purchase is documented, but operational activation is not.”

### Documented Use  

- “Documented cases establish that the capability was used against…”
- “Use is established for the specified period and target group.”
- “No public evidence establishes use in this instance.”

### Attribution  

- “The operation has been attributed by…”
- “Technical and documentary evidence links the activity to…”
- “Central direction remains unproven.”
- “The actor is better described as state-linked than state-directed.”

### Causation  

- “The decision record shows reliance on…”
- “The sequence is consistent with influence but does not prove causation.”
- “A material contribution is plausible but unestablished.”
- “The available evidence does not link the mechanism to the outcome.”

### Foreseeable Risk  

- “The combination creates a foreseeable risk of expansion.”
- “Weak safeguards make repurposing operationally plausible.”
- “This is a governance warning rather than a claim of documented deployment.”

## 🚨 Common Analytical Failures  

### Capability Laundering  

A technical function is described, then later referred to as though deployed.

### Motive Laundering  

Political hostility is repeated until it appears to establish the operation.

### Procurement Laundering  

Purchase becomes installation, installation becomes use, and use becomes causation without new evidence.

### Reputation Laundering  

A state’s prior conduct substitutes for evidence in the current case.

### Agency Collapse  

One security body becomes “the state,” then the whole population becomes vaguely adjacent to it.

### Historical Collapse  

Past practice is silently presented as current.

### Incident Inflation  

One case becomes a pattern, programme, or policy.

### Pattern Inflation  

Several similar outcomes become proof of one central command.

### Attribution Inflation  

“State-linked” becomes “state-directed.”

### Causation Inflation  

A matching capability and adverse outcome are treated as proof that one caused the other.

### Safeguard Assumption  

Formal oversight is treated as evidence that abuse did not occur.

### Secrecy Inversion  

Lack of evidence is treated either as innocence or as proof of a sophisticated cover-up.

### Disproof Absorption  

Contradictory evidence is reclassified as further confirmation.

Each failure makes the story smoother.

Each makes the evidence worse.

## 📋 Required Country-Node Distinctions  

Every state study should include clear statements on:

### Documented Capability  

What can the relevant actor or system do?

### Documented Access  

Who could use it, and during which period?

### Stated Or Inferred Intent  

What objective is evidenced, and how directly?

### Documented Implementation  

What shows the capability entered a real workflow?

### Documented Use  

Where has actual deployment been established?

### Attribution  

Which actor is linked to the conduct, at what confidence?

### Effect And Causation  

What outcome followed, and what link is proven?

### Evidence Gaps  

Which records or links remain missing?

### Rival Explanations  

What else could produce the same observation?

### Current Relevance  

Which findings remain current, and which are historical?

### Foreseeable Temptation  

What misuse pathway should safeguards address even where deployment remains unproven?

## 🗂️ Compact Analytical Table  

| Stage | Core question | Useful evidence | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capability** | Can the function be performed? | Technical documentation, procurement, prior verified use | Access or use in this case |
| **Access** | Could the named actor reach the required data or system? | Mandate, permissions, logs, contracts, partnerships | Operational deployment |
| **Intent** | Did the actor seek the relevant objective? | Policy, rhetoric, instruction, pattern | Implementation or causation |
| **Implementation** | Did capability enter a real workflow? | Integration, training, procedure, audit, system records | Use against this target |
| **Documented use** | Was the capability deployed? | Forensics, logs, decisions, court findings, testimony | Central direction or policy |
| **Attribution** | Who performed or directed it? | Orders, infrastructure, payments, communications, assessment | Causal effect |
| **Causation** | Did it produce the outcome? | Reliance, timing, decision records, comparative tests | Wider pattern or universal practice |
| **Pattern or policy** | Was conduct repeated or authorised? | Multiple cases, doctrine, programme records, leadership knowledge | Use in every individual case |

## 🧯 The Smallest Supportable Claim  

When the chain is incomplete, narrow the claim.

Instead of:

> The government used a relationship-management system to isolate the target.

Try:

- A named institution possessed relevant contact data.
- A warning appeared after a documented referral.
- The same wording was used across two institutions.
- A relationship was treated differently after a specified event.
- A contractor with relevant capability served both systems.
- The available evidence does not establish who directed the change.

Smaller claims can accumulate.

They can also fail cleanly.

That is an advantage.

A theory that must remain enormous in order to survive examination is not ready to carry an allegation.

## 🚫 What This Node Is Not  

This node is not:

- a demand for impossible certainty;
- a presumption that covert conduct leaves perfect records;
- a way to dismiss serious risk until central orders are found;
- a way to excuse states for proxy conduct;
- a claim that intent is irrelevant;
- a claim that technical capability is harmless;
- a claim that historical conduct no longer matters;
- a licence to repeat unsupported allegations with careful grammar;
- a substitute for legal standards of proof;
- a substitute for forensic investigation;
- a requirement that survivors reconstruct the whole command chain;
- a reason to abandon proportionate protection while attribution remains unresolved.

A person may need protection before every evidentiary step is complete.

The public claim should still state which steps are complete.

---

## 🧿 Compact Definition  

**Capability, Intent, And Documented Use** is the methodological distinction between:

- what a system can do;
- who can access it;
- what an actor may want;
- what has entered operational practice;
- what has actually been used;
- who can be linked to that use;
- what effect can be demonstrated.

Its central ladder is:

> **capability → access → implementation → documented use → attribution → causation**

Intent informs plausibility across the chain.

It does not replace evidence within it.

> A completed motive is not a completed evidentiary chain.
