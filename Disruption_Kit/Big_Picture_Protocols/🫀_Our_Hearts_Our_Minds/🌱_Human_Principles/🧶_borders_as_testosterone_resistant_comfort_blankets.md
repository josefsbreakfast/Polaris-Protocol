# 🧶 Borders as Testosterone-Resistant Comfort Blankets  
**First created:** 2026-01-18 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-12  
*International borders as affective artefacts that unevenly regulate human threat responses*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

This node examines international borders not primarily as legal or administrative instruments, but as **emotional and affective technologies** — comfort objects that reliably regulate some nervous systems while failing to regulate others.

The central claim is not ideological or moral. It is biological, social, and structural:

> Borders function as effective comfort objects for expansive, patrol-oriented threat responses  
> and as ineffective or alienating objects for proximity- and continuity-oriented safety needs.

This mismatch matters because borders are defended, mythologised, and escalated **as if they worked equally for everyone**.

They do not.

---

## ✨ Key Features  

- Frames borders as affective infrastructure rather than neutral lines  
- Explains gender-skewed resonance without pathologising men  
- Links war escalation to unprocessed fear and grief  
- Clarifies why many women and non-male people experience borders as incoherent or absurd  
- Positions pacifism, labour withdrawal, and indirect resistance as rational strategies, not moral weakness  

---

## 🧿 Comfort Objects Are Body, And Nervous-System, Specific  

A comfort object only functions if it aligns with the body and threat-response system using it.

Across mammalian species — humans included — male-coded threat responses tend to be:
- expansive  
- boundary-focused  
- patrol-oriented  
- highly reactive to perceived intrusion  

Borders map cleanly onto this pattern. They:
- define a perimeter  
- externalise threat  
- promise safety through exclusion  
- soothe anxiety by hardening a line  

For bodies and identities not organised around patrol comfort, the same object often fails to land at all.

---

## 2. Testosterone-Resistant by Design  

For many women and non-male people, borders do not perform their implied function:  
**they do not make the inside feel safer**.

Common responses are not opposition but confusion:
- “Why does this line matter more than what’s happening inside it?”  
- “How does defending the border improve daily life?”  
- “Why is this treated as sacred while care systems collapse?”  

This is not idealism.  
It is a **mismatch between the tool and the nervous system**.

Borders regulate territorial anxiety, not relational or environmental safety.

---

## 🗺️ Patrol Comfort vs Continuity Comfort  

**Patrol-oriented comfort**
- Safety through defended boundaries  
- Identity anchored to territory  
- Threat imagined as invasion  
- Stability imagined as control  

**Continuity-oriented comfort**
- Safety through livability and access  
- Identity anchored to relationships and routines  
- Threat experienced as disruption, precarity, or harm  
- Stability imagined as care and continuity  

Borders overwhelmingly privilege the first mode.

This produces governance failure modes where:
- territory is defended while interiors rot  
- symbolic strength replaces material safety  
- escalation is treated as reassurance  

---

## 🌋 War as Escalated Border Logic  

War is the maximal expression of border-based comfort logic.

From a testosterone-resistant comfort profile, war appears less as defence and more as:
- ritualised destruction in service of abstraction  
- prioritisation of lines over lives  
- emotional regulation for some at catastrophic cost to others  

This helps explain why war is historically and structurally a losing proposition for women and non-male people — not only materially, but emotionally.

Holding the line does not compensate for the loss of:
- bodily safety  
- social continuity  
- care infrastructure  
- future viability  

---

## 💞 Female and Queer Anarchism as Non-Resonance  

Many women and queer people do not reject borders out of utopian optimism.

They reject them because **the object never worked**.

The refusal often appears as:
- confusion rather than conquest  
- disengagement rather than rebellion  
- “why are we doing this?” rather than “tear it down”  

You cannot threaten someone with the loss of a comfort blanket that never kept them warm.

---

## 🧭 Where This Leaves Us (Practical Interjection)  

This analysis is **not** an argument for abolishing borders overnight. Borders currently scaffold infrastructure, service delivery, and emergency response. Pretending otherwise would be unserious.

What *is* being argued is more specific:

> Much of our global political architecture is optimised around male-coded threat-response comfort,  
> while systematically under-serving other modes of safety, grief processing, and coexistence.

As gender emancipation expands — including women, queer people, and those not coded as male — this asymmetry becomes increasingly visible.

### Naming the Pattern Without Moralising It  

When threatened, male-coded groups tend toward:
- territorial expansion or hard defence  
- alliance formation against an externalised “other”  
- abstraction of fear into borders, flags, and fronts  

This does not make men uniquely violent or malicious.  
It makes them mammals under stress — often without social permission to express fear or grief directly.

Unexpressed fear does not disappear.  
It re-emerges as escalation.

---

### ❤️‍🩹 Why This Feels Different for Women  

Many women do not experience safety as something produced by:
- defended perimeters  
- symbolic dominance  
- territorial holding  

Safety is more often experienced as:
- continuity of daily life  
- protection of the immediate “nest”  
- access to care, food, and stability  

This helps explain why women-led resistance so often takes the form of:
- pacifism  
- labour withdrawal  
- mutual aid  
- indirect or targeted disruption  

These are not weaker strategies.  
They are strategies aimed at interiors, not borders.

## 🌈 Not Men vs Women: The Queer Theory Bridge  

This is not a simple sex binary.

Patriarchy enforces hierarchy by coding:
- “female”
- “gay”
- “weak”
- “submissive”

as interchangeable insults.

That is why biological essentialism — even when used defensively — raises alarm bells.  
It often reproduces the same hierarchy it claims to resist.

The real axis is not men vs women, but **male-coded dominance vs everyone else**.

---

## 🕯️ A Different Question for Governance  

The forward-facing question becomes:

> How much of what we call “necessary geopolitics”  
> is actually unprocessed fear and grief routed through territorial systems?

And further:
- Can fear and loss be ritualised without war?  
- Can threat-response be discharged without an enemy?  
- Can governance support multiple comfort logics at once?  

Animals with complex brains grieve.  
Humans are not exempt — we are simply undertrained.
