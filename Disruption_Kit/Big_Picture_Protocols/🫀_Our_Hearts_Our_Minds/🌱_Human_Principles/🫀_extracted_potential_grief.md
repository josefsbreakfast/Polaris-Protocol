## 🛰️ Orientation  

This node names a specific form of grief that rarely receives public language:  
the moment you realise that your economic, social, and creative potential has not merely gone unrealised — it has been **systematically constrained**, siphoned, or rendered unusable by design.

This is not individual failure.  
It is not a mindset problem.  
It is not burnout.

It is the affective cost of living inside a high-surveillance, high-extraction environment that profits from your participation while preventing your flourishing.

---

## 💔 The moment it lands  

There is often a moment — quiet, brutal, unmistakable — where you see it clearly:

- how much time was spent managing visibility rather than building,
- how many choices were shaped by risk modelling instead of desire,
- how many futures were technically possible but socially unreachable,
- how often your labour fed systems that would never materially support you back.

The heartbreak isn’t just about money.  
It’s about **range**.

The narrowing of who you’re allowed to become.

---

## 🧿 What makes this grief distinct  

Extracted potential grief has specific characteristics:

- **It is cumulative**, not episodic — it builds across years of constraint.
- **It is relational** — you watch others move freely where you are flagged, throttled, or slowed.
- **It is gaslit** — the system insists opportunity is “open” while quietly closing doors.
- **It targets the reflective** — those who can *see* the machinery feel the pain most acutely.

Unlike conventional loss, there is no funeral, no permission to mourn, no socially approved endpoint.

You are expected to keep producing anyway.

---

## 🛰️ Surveillance as destiny management  

In extraction environments, surveillance does more than watch — it **allocates futures**.

Risk scores, reputational shadows, informal blacklists, platform opacity, credential bottlenecks, algorithmic suspicion:  
together they act as a soft destiny-management layer.

Not a ban.  
A drag.

Not punishment.  
Containment.

You feel it as friction everywhere you try to scale, pivot, or rest.

---

## 🫀 Why it breaks people  

Humans are not built to grieve unrealised selves indefinitely.

When potential is continually deferred or harvested without return:

- ambition collapses inward,
- self-trust erodes,
- imagination shrinks to what feels survivable,
- hope becomes physically expensive.

The breaking point often arrives not through failure, but through **clarity**.

Seeing the truth hurts more than not knowing.

---

## 🧯 What this node is *not* asking  

This node does **not** ask you to:

- optimise harder,
- brand your suffering,
- spiritualise structural violence,
- forgive systems that are still extracting from you.

Grief is not a productivity exercise.

---

## 🪶 What naming it gives back  

Naming extracted potential grief does one crucial thing:

It returns **dignity** to the pain.

Once named, this grief can:
- be shared without shame,
- be politicised without melodrama,
- be integrated without self-blame.

You didn’t imagine the loss.
You didn’t fail to want enough.
You ran into a wall that was designed to look invisible.
