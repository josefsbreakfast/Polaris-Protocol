# 💩 Why CTSA 2015 Violated Every Core IICSA Principle  
**First created:** 2025-11-23 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-12  
*How the Counter-Terrorism and Security Act 2015 (CTSA 2015) violated all of the core safeguarding principles established by the Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse (IICSA), creating a legal regime structurally incompatible with child protection.*  

---

## 🧭 Orientation  
The Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse (IICSA) articulated clear safeguarding principles:

- **transparency**,  
- **accountability**,  
- **child-centred practice**,  
- **non-discrimination**,  
- **information clarity**,  
- **oversight**,  
- **appealability**,  
- **preventing institutional abuse**,  
- **minimising power imbalances**.

CTSA 2015 — the law that placed Prevent into every school, NHS trust, college, and university — violated **every single one**.

The Act created a system in which:

- children can be surveilled without consent,  
- parents are not informed,  
- information is withheld,  
- behaviour is pathologised,  
- data is shared across agencies without transparency,  
- professionals cannot explain decisions,  
- racialised children are disproportionately targeted.

This node lays out the precise points of violation.

---

## 🧨 Structural Violations of IICSA Principles  

### **1. Transparency Principle → Violated by Counter-Terror Secrecy**  
IICSA emphasised that safeguarding must be:

- open,  
- explainable,  
- accountable.

CTSA 2015 embedded Prevent within:

- counterterrorism exemptions,  
- secret triage,  
- hidden minutes,  
- non-disclosable information pathways.

Families cannot know:

- if a child was referred,  
- who logged concerns,  
- what was said,  
- why decisions were made.

This is antithetical to safeguarding.

---

### ❌ Child-Centred Practice → Replaced with Risk-Centred Practice  
Safeguarding centres:

- the child’s voice,  
- their needs,  
- their suffering.

"Prevent" centres:

- the risk they pose,  
- the fears of professionals,  
- the projections of white discomfort.

This is not child protection —  
it is **child pre-crime classification**.  

Better effort would be spent using the same resources to scrutinise those who are responsible for predation upon children and adults alike.  

Obsession with the victim and survivor status, detracts from the actual prevention of the terrorism of sexual violence, especially when the victims are more vulnerable than most.  

---

### 💀 Accountability Principle → Violated by Multi-Agency Diffusion  
IICSA warned against systems where:

- everyone is responsible,  
- so no one is responsible.

Prevent/Channel panels:

- have no named accountable officer,  
- record no publicly accessible minutes,  
- diffuse responsibility across agencies,  
- create a bureaucratic fog.

This protects negligence and abuse.

---

### 🌋 Equality Principle → Violated by Racialised Targeting  
IICSA stressed that safeguarding must protect all children **equally**.

CTSA 2015 operationalised Prevent through:

- racialised suspicion templates,  
- whiteness-coded emotional norms,  
- metadata that disproportionately flags diaspora youth,  
- cultural misinterpretation,  
- disproportionate referral of Black, Muslim, Arab, South Asian children.

It created a two-tier safeguarding regime.

---

### 👹 Information Clarity → Violated by Intelligence Logic  
Safeguarding requires clarity and disclosure.

Prevent uses:

- coded language,  
- risk categories derived from intelligence doctrine,  
- ambiguous behavioural indicators (“volatility,” “instability,” “identity crisis”).

Children cannot understand what they are accused of.  
Families cannot respond.  
Professionals cannot explain.

---

### **6. Oversight Principle → Violated by Counterterror Exemptions**  
IICSA insisted on independent oversight mechanisms.

Prevent/Channel:

- have no independent ombudsman,  
- are exempt from many FOI requests,  
- have no tribunal system,  
- are not audited publicly,  
- exist partially outside democratic oversight.

Safeguarding cannot function inside this legal void.

---

### 🕳️ Appealability → Absent Entirely  
CTSA 2015 created a system where:

- children cannot appeal Prevent decisions,  
- parents cannot challenge referrals,  
- clinicians cannot request case notes,  
- universities cannot question police triage.

This contradicts IICSA’s core conclusion:  
*that child protection must include redress.*

---

### 🤡 Prevent Encourages Institutional Abuse Behaviours  
IICSA documented patterns of institutional abuse facilitated by:

- secrecy,  
- power imbalances,  
- compliance culture,  
- disbelief of victims.

CTSA reproduced all these conditions:

- Prevent weaponised against vulnerable children,  
- racialised predictions of risk,  
- coercive personalities shielded by secrecy,  
- communities unable to access justice.

Prevent becomes an incubator for the very dynamics IICSA warned about.  

---

### 🏛️ Behavioural Pre-Crime Classification Is a Form of Institutional Child Abuse  
IICSA identified a core pattern across decades of institutional failures:

> **When children are treated as risks rather than as people, abuse follows.**

CTSA 2015 created a statutory system that does exactly this — it **reclassifies children’s emotions, identity formation, distress, and political speech as pre-crime indicators**.

This is not safeguarding.  
This is **institutional child abuse**, because:

#### 🧠 It criminalises development  
Normal child behaviours — curiosity, boundary-testing, emotional overwhelm — are reframed as suspicious.

Treating development as danger is a form of psychological harm.

#### **b. It isolates children from trust and care**  
If a child expresses fear, anger, grief, or confusion:

- the teacher may escalate,  
- the clinician may report,  
- the family may be kept in the dark.

A child who cannot safely express emotion is a child at risk.

#### 🧿 It places children into unaccountable systems with unchecked adults  
Prevent/Channel panels involve:

- police,  
- social workers,  
- local authority officers,  
- safeguarding leads,  
- school administrators,

working **in secrecy**.

This creates perfect conditions for abusive personalities — as IICSA repeatedly warned.

#### 😢 It creates compliant children    
Children learn:

- to silence emotion,  
- to avoid disclosure,  
- to accept adult authority unquestioningly,  
- to hide distress.

Compliance is a known vulnerability factor for grooming and exploitation.

#### ⚠️ It marks children as “risky” in multi-agency systems  
Once flagged, a child may be:

- distrusted,  
- disbelieved,  
- treated as volatile,  
- treated as dangerous.

This makes them **far more vulnerable** to predatory adults who exploit institutional abandonment.

#### 🔥 It reproduces the exact abuse-enabling pattern IICSA identified  
IICSA showed that child abuse thrives when:

- power is unbalanced,  
- children are not believed,  
- adults are not accountable,  
- systems are secret.

CTSA 2015 recreated all four conditions.

This is not accidental.  
This is a structural consequence of embedding counterterror logic into child-facing services.

#### 🧲 Pre-crime logic invites coercive adults  
Coercive, narcissistic, or predatory personalities are drawn to:

- secrecy,  
- unreviewed judgement,  
- unilateral power,  
- systems that distrust children.

Prevent gives these individuals the perfect environment to operate.

This is why:

> **Prevent is not only harmful — it is unsafe.  
> CTSA 2015 created an abuse-permissive environment inside every school, every NHS trust, and every local authority in the UK.**
