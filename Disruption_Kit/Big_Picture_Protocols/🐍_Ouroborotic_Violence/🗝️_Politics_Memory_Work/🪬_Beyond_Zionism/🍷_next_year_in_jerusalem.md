# 🍷 Next Year in Jerusalem  
**First created:** 2025-11-02 | **Last updated:** 2026-08-16  
*On Seder liturgy, metaphorical geography, and the violence of forcing ritual language into nationalist literalism.*

---

## 🛰️ Orientation  

“Next year in Jerusalem” is not a travel itinerary.  
It is not a real-estate claim.  
It is not a test of Jewish loyalty.  

It is a line of **ritual speech**, spoken at the end of the Seder — after slavery has been named, empire indicted, and liberation explicitly declared unfinished.

For over two millennia, Jews have understood this line as **aspirational, conditional, and ethical**, not territorial. The insistence that it must now be read as a statement of modern political sovereignty is a late imposition — and a coercive one.

## 🧿 Analysis / Content  

### 1. **Jerusalem as a condition, not a destination**  
In Jewish liturgy, *Yerushalayim* names a world-state:  
- justice restored,  
- violence ended,  
- power restrained,  
- dignity universal.

Like *Olam Haba*, it is not a map coordinate but a **moral horizon**.  
It has always functioned this way — precisely because Jews lived, prayed, and died across the world without assuming their lives were incomplete.

### 2. **The insult embedded in literalism**  
To insist that Jews who did not “make it” to Jerusalem somehow failed in their Jewishness is not only wrong — it is grotesque.

It would imply that:  
- Jews murdered in the Shoah died unfinished,  
- rabbinic sages and Hasidic masters failed their covenant,  
- diaspora life was a 2,000-year mistake.

This is not Jewish theology.  
It is nationalist retrofitting applied backwards in time.

### 3. **Judaism does not run on reincarnation logic**  
This argument quietly relies on a foreign metaphysics:  
- that Jews who didn’t arrive will somehow “complete” themselves later,  
- that history is a retry loop until geographic fulfilment.

Judaism does not teach this.  
There is no reincarnation-based fulfilment logic that would make this coherent.

This is New Age / Christian teleology smuggled into Jewish language — and it collapses under even minimal scrutiny.

### 4. **Why this question keeps getting asked**  
When people ask anti- or non-Zionist Jews  
> “But what will you say at Passover?”  

they are not asking about ritual.  
They are testing compliance.

This question treats Jewish prayer as a loyalty oath and diaspora Jews as conditional participants in their own tradition.

That coercion is itself antisemitic.

### 5. **Why this belongs Beyond Zionism**  
Because *Next year in Jerusalem* only makes sense if **this year is unacceptable**.

And that critique applies equally to empire, occupation, and any system that demands safety be purchased with someone else’s dispossession.
